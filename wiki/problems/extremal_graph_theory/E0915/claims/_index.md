# problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0915/claims

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0915/_index|..]]

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0915/claims/1960_01_01_bartfai|1960_01_01_bartfai]]: Bártfai's solution (Mat. Lapok 1960) shows that a graph on $2n+1$ vertices
with $3n+1$ edges has an even cycle, found through a theta subgraph. The theta
subgraph gives two vertices joined by three internally disjoint paths, which
is the case $m=3$.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0915/claims/1966_01_01_bollobas|1966_01_01_bollobas]]: Bollobás (Studia Sci. Math. Hungar. 1966) proves that $2n-1$ edges on $n$
vertices force two vertices joined by four internally disjoint paths, so
$k_4(3n+1)=6n+1$. This is the case $m=4$, under either reading.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0915/claims/1973_09_01_leonard|1973_09_01_leonard]]: Leonard (Period. Math. Hungar. 1973) gives a graph with 57 vertices and 141
edges in which no two vertices are joined by five internally disjoint paths.
These are the problem's parameters at $m=5$ and $n=14$, and this is the first
published disproof.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0915/claims/1973_09_01_mader|1973_09_01_mader]]: Mader (Math. Z. 1973) proves the edge-disjoint Bollobás–Erdős conjecture for
every $m$, with the exact threshold, and refutes the vertex-disjoint form for
every $m\ge5$. It is the one result that answers both readings of the
question.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0915/claims/1974_10_01_sorensen_thomassen|1974_10_01_sorensen_thomassen]]: Sørensen and Thomassen (J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 1974) determine $k_5(n)$ and
prove a lower bound for $k_m(n)$ with slope above $m/2$ for every $m\ge5$.
This refutes the vertex-disjoint reading of the conjecture for every such $m$.
