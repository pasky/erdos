# problems/unit_fractions/E0301/claims

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0301/_index|..]]

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0301/claims/2025_09_16_van_doorn|2025_09_16_van_doorn]]: An elementary argument, credited to Wouter van Doorn in the erdosproblems.com
commentary, that $f(N)\le(25/28+o(1))N$ for Problem 301, by counting disjoint
dilates of $\{2,3,4,6,12\}$. It exists only as erdosproblems.com commentary
and is pending.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0301/claims/2026_05_27_wang|2026_05_27_wang]]: An unrefereed manuscript of 27 May 2026 by Xinjun Wang claims
$f(N)\le(667/806+o(1))N$ for the extremal function of Problem 301. The
argument uses dilates of the divisors of $720$ and a finite certificate.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0301/claims/2026_07_30_della_pietra|2026_07_30_della_pietra]]: An AI-assisted partial proof claim of 30 July 2026 by Donald Della Pietra that
$f(N)\ge(1/2+\varepsilon)N$ for an absolute $\varepsilon>0$ and all large $N$.
This would answer the particular question of Problem 301 in the negative.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0301/claims/2026_07_30_della_pietra_upper|2026_07_30_della_pietra_upper]]: Donald Della Pietra's repository announced on 30 July 2026 the upper bound
$f(N)\le(15437/19344+o(1))N$ for Problem 301, with an exact certificate over
the divisors of $5040$. The work is AI-assisted and unrefereed, and is
formalized only at $M=60$.
