***

**Claim.** Let $f(n)$ be the number of solutions of $n=2^k+p$ with $p$
prime; the paper does not state the range of $k$, and allowing or excluding
$k=0$ changes $f(n)$ by at most one, which does not affect the theorem.
[[../library/primes/erdos_1950_integers_form_related_problems/theorem_1|Theorem 1]]
of P. Erdős, *On integers of the form $2^k+p$ and some related problems*
([[../library/primes/erdos_1950_integers_form_related_problems/_index|card]]),
proved in answer to a question of Turán, states that $\limsup f(n)=\infty$,
and in fact that $f(n)>c\log\log n$ for infinitely many $n$, with $c>0$ an
absolute constant. The set $A=\{2^k:k\ge0\}$ has about $\log_2N$ elements up
to $N$, so it satisfies the hypothesis
$\lvert A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}\rvert\gg\log N$ of
[[problems/primes/E0237/_index|Problem 237]], and the theorem answers the
problem's question yes for this $A$. The erdosproblems.com commentary credits
this case to the paper. The general question is settled by Chen and Ding, the
[[problems/primes/E0237/claims/2022_01_26_chen_ding|accepted full claim]],
whose Corollary 1.2 extends this theorem to every set of more than $\log x$
integers up to $x$ with the bound $\tfrac18\log\log x-1.6$.

**Covers.** The case $A=\{2^k:k\ge0\}$, answered yes. Not covered: any other
set $A$ with $\lvert A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}\rvert\gg\log N$; the general case
is settled on Chen and Ding's page, not on this one.

**Depends on.** Nothing on this wiki; the claim rests on the cited paper.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: P. Erdős, On integers of the form $2^k+p$ and some
related problems, Summa Brasil. Math. 2 (1950), fasc. 8, 113--123, the `paper`
link. The link points to the Rényi Institute's archive of Erdős's papers. The
erdosproblems.com commentary credits this case to the paper. The
erdosproblems.com label, however, settles the problem through Chen and Ding and
credits them, so no `reviewed` evidence is listed here. The page is dated by the
fascicle's issue month, November 1950, which is printed on the fascicle's cover
(Summa Brasil. Math. vol. 2, fasc. 8). The day in the page name is a
placeholder.
