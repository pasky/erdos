***

**Claim.** Theorem 1.1 of Plasma AI, *The normal order of the number of
iterations of Euler's function*, preprint (2026): there is a constant
$\alpha$ with $1/\log3\le\alpha\le1/\log2$ such that
$\sum_{n\le x}(f(n)-\alpha\log n)^2\ll x(\log x)^2/(\log\log x)^5$, and the
$n\le x$ with $|f(n)-\alpha\log n|>\log n/\log\log n$ number
$O(x/(\log\log x)^3+\sqrt x)$. Hence $f(n)/\log n\to\alpha$ on a set of
natural density one: $f(n)/\log n$ has a distribution function, the unit
step at $\alpha$, and is almost always constant in the normal-order sense of
the Formulation on
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0408/_index|the problem page]]. The paper
counts iterations from $\varphi_0(n)=n$, so its $f(1)=0$ where the problem
page's $f(1)=1$; the two agree for $n\ge2$, and no density changes. The
value of $\alpha$ is not determined. The proof runs the moment method of
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0408/claims/1990_01_01_erdos_granville_pomerance_spiro|Erdős, Granville, Pomerance and Spiro]]
without their Elliott--Halberstam-type hypothesis, which it replaces by a
quantitative estimate for tuples of prime factors of $p-1$ (its Theorem
2.1), extending Theorem 7.1 of OpenAI's Poisson--Dirichlet manuscript and
resting on OpenAI's zero-free half-plane $\operatorname{Re}s>7/8$ and its
weighted-dilation-graph results. The source card
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/plasma_ai_2026_normal_order_number_iterations_euler_s_function/_index|plasma_ai_2026_normal_order_number_iterations_euler_s_function]]
digests the paper and the formalization.

**Covers.** The first two questions, the parts `distribution` and
`constant`. The third question, which is open-ended and not a part, is not
covered by the claim; the paper's remark on it, a consequence of Ford, Konyagin and Luca
(2010, Theorem 5), is recorded on the source card.

**Formalization.** The repository
[plasma-ai/erdos-408](https://github.com/plasma-ai/erdos-408/tree/5b02bf9ab59fd813af8f19c604ba70254fced08d)
formalizes Theorem 1.1 in Lean 4 (`v4.35.0`, Mathlib `v4.35.0`).
`Challenge.lean` states it as `Erdos408.normal_order_rate`, together with
formal-conjectures' statements of the first two questions,
`erdos_408.parts.i` and `erdos_408.parts.ii` at its commit of 2026-10-14,
with `f` copied verbatim and the density inlined; `Solution.lean` proves the
three, and `comparator.json` permits only `propext`, `Quot.sound` and
`Classical.choice`. The repository's `formalization.yaml` names Jacob
Parish as the formalization's author, records that it was written with
Claude Code, and gives its review status as self-assessed, with a Comparator
check run on macOS without the `landrun` sandbox.

the repository's tree at 5b02bf9 without its `paper/` folder, at its own pinned
toolchain, `leanprover/lean4:v4.35.0` with Mathlib at `v4.35.0`, in a sandbox
with no network. Comparator, run before anything else built the solution,
accepted the three theorems against `Challenge.lean`. The kernel then replayed,
from an empty environment, every declaration each of the three depends on, Lean
