**Claim.** Linmiao Xu's Lean 4 development *Erdős #1139: large gaps between
almost-primes*, in the folder `erdos-1139` of the public repository
`linrock/math-proofs` (MIT license at the commit linked above, of 5 October
2026), proves the affirmative answer to
[[problems/primes/E1139/_index|Problem 1139]] from one published theorem
that it takes as a hypothesis. Its theorem
`Erdos1139.Palomar.erdos_1139_of_gtz` derives

$$
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{u_{k+1}-u_k}{\log k}=\infty
$$

from the proposition `HasFixedSignedMixedPublishedVonMangoldtAsymptotics`,
the Green--Tao linear-forms asymptotic for the fixed systems the proof uses,
as the paragraph on the hypothesis below explains. The route goes through
double covers: if residue classes modulo primes $p$ or prime squares $p^2$
cover every $h\in[1,y]$ at least twice, the Chinese remainder theorem gives
$N\in(Q,2Q]$, $Q$ the product of the moduli, with $\Omega(N+h)\ge3$ for
$1\le h\le y$ (`cover_crt_interval`), and covers with
$\log Q\le\varepsilon y$ for every $\varepsilon>0$ give the infinite limsup
(`limsup_top_of_sparse`). The development takes the class $0$ modulo $p^2$
for the primes $p\le z$ and modulo $p$ for $z<p\le y/z$, after which the
only targets with fewer than two hits are $1$, the primes, the semiprimes
$pq$ with $p<z$ and $q>y/z$, and the prime powers $p^e$ with $z<p\le y/z$
(`core_classification`); it covers these with typed prime patterns in dyadic
prime shells, counted by the hypothesis, and lets $z\to\infty$. Without the
hypothesis it proves, by omitting a positive proportion of unused reserve
primes from the double covering of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0689/_index|Problem 689]], that the limsup is
at least some $A>1$ (`limsup_strictly_gt_one`) and that below every large
$X$ some interval of length at least $c\log X$, for one fixed $c>1$,
consists of integers with $\Omega\ge3$ (`uniform_maximal_gap`). It also
proves that its hypothesis implies arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions
of primes (`prime_ap_of_gtz`). The development's metadata describes it as
AI-assisted, and names the candidate proof posted in the site's thread on 19
June 2026 ([[problems/primes/E1139/claims/2026_06_19_price|Price's claim
page]]) as an earlier proposal of the squared-prime core, the typed prime
patterns and the linear-forms route, while recording its own result as an
independent proof.

**The formal statement.** `Statement.lean` records the formal-conjectures
declaration `Erdos1139.erdos_1139`
([`FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1139.lean` at the catalog's commit of 2026-09-18](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/6fbb54f24ccc2e64dcfaffc28c58950e377110d2/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1139.lean))
with the answer `True`: with $u$ the increasing enumeration, from index $0$,
of the $n>0$ with $\Omega(n)\le2$, the limsup in the extended reals of
$(u(k+1)-u(k))/\log(k+1)$ is $+\infty$. Since $u(k)$ is the problem's
$u_{k+1}$, the ratio at $k$ is the problem's ratio at index $k+1$, so this
is the problem's statement in the reading of its Formulation (prime factors
counted with multiplicity, $1$ included), with the same polarity.
`AxiomAudit.lean` proves that `erdos_1139_of_gtz` yields this proposition.

**The hypothesis.** `HasFixedSignedMixedPublishedVonMangoldtAsymptotics`
(`Proofs/AdaptiveMixedPublishedGTZCapstone1139.lean`) has three fields, for
three kinds of fixed system of integral linear forms: one prime pattern, the
label form $P$ and target forms $A_dP+B_dC$ in two variables; two patterns
sharing the label, in three variables; and two patterns of the same type
sharing one target value, parametrized by three integer coordinates. For
each fixed system and each fixed bounded open convex set $K$ inside the
region where the forms are positive, a field asserts that the sum over the
integer points $n$ of $NK$ of $\prod_i\Lambda(\psi_i(n))$, divided by
$N^d$, tends as $N\to\infty$ to the volume of $K$ times a product over
primes $\ell$ of local densities, with a Jacobian factor for the
shared-target parametrization. For one pattern the density at $\ell$ is the
proportion of $(\mathbb Z/\ell)^2$ on which no form vanishes, times
$(\ell/(\ell-1))^t$ for $t$ forms. The development proves that the
forms of each system are pairwise non-proportional, so each system has
finite complexity. Each field is then a case of the asymptotic (1.7) of
Green and Tao's generalized Hardy--Littlewood conjecture (Conjecture 1.2 of
*Linear equations in primes*), which their Main Theorem proves for every
system of finite complexity assuming the conjectures GI(s) and MN(s), both
since proved (cited under Depends on below). The hypothesis asks for no
uniformity: each field is one limit for one fixed system, and the
kernel-checked deduction uses nothing more. So the hypothesis is a published
theorem taken as a premise, not an unproven conjecture, and the claim is
scoped `full`. This was checked here by reading the definitions: the
one-pattern lattice is the set of integer points of $NK$, the positivity of
the forms follows from the region, and its local density is Green and Tao's
factor $\beta_p$. A second reading compared the two paired systems with
Green and Tao's statement. The development proves in Lean that each of their
local factors is Green and Tao's $\beta_\ell$ for the system and equals the
product of the one-pattern factors
(`AdaptiveSharedLabelFactorization1139` and
`AdaptiveSharedTargetActualFactorization1139`). The shared-target sum runs
over a lattice of rank $3$ in $\mathbb Z^4$, which
`SharedTargetFiberCovolume1139` parametrizes bijectively by $\mathbb Z^3$; the
Jacobian factor of that field is the change of variables of this
parametrization, which is not a Lean theorem and was checked by hand. Green
and Tao's closed convex body against the hypothesis's open one, and the
bounded box containing it, change only the error term. These readings are
the corpus's own and are not an independent review.
