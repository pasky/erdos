# problems/factorials_binomials/E0699/claims

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0699/_index|..]]

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0699/claims/2026_07_18_price|2026_07_18_price]]: A GPT 5.6 Sol Pro argument submitted by Liam Price proves that some prime
$p\ge i$ divides both $\binom ni$ and $\binom nj$ whenever $j\le3i/2$ or
$n=2j$. These are the two cases the site's curator credits on the problem
page.

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0699/claims/2026_07_25_van_doorn_rocca|2026_07_25_van_doorn_rocca]]: Two unpublished 2026 manuscripts by Wouter van Doorn and Stefano Rocca prove
that no counterexample has $i=1$, $i=2$ or $i\ge1476$, and that each fixed
$i\ge4$ allows only finitely many. They leave $i=3$ open.
