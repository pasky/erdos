# problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1021/claims

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1021/_index|..]]

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1021/claims/1974_04_01_bondy_simonovits|1974_04_01_bondy_simonovits]]: Bondy and Simonovits (J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 1974, refereed) prove that more
than $100kn^{1+1/k}$ edges force every even cycle $C_{2l}$ with
$k\le l\le kn^{1/k}$. With $k=3$ this gives $\mathrm{ex}(n,C_6)\le300n^{4/3}$,
so $c_3=1/6$.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1021/claims/2018_07_13_conlon_lee|2018_07_13_conlon_lee]]: Theorem 5.1 of Conlon and Lee (Int. Math. Res. Not. IMRN 2021) bounds the
extremal number of the one-subdivision of $K_k$ by $C_kn^{3/2-6^{-k}}$ for
every $k\ge3$. This answers Problem 1021 with $c_k=6^{-k}$.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1021/claims/2018_09_03_janzer|2018_09_03_janzer]]: Theorem 3 of Janzer (Electron. J. Combin. 2019) bounds the extremal number of
the one-subdivision of $K_k$ by $C_kn^{3/2-1/(4k-6)}$ for every $k\ge3$. This
is the sharpest exponent recorded for Problem 1021.
