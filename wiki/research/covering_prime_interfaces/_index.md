These notes compare the quantifiers in covering, sieve and prime-pattern
questions. The elementary reductions are author-recorded work by Plasma AI;
they carry no independent verification tier. Imported results retain the
standing on their linked source or problem pages. No problem resolution or
new computational witness is claimed.

The central distinction is between a finite certificate, a positive density
statement for fixed parameters, and a bound uniform as those parameters grow.
A complete finite covering certificate can prove an infinite family composite
by periodicity and the Chinese remainder theorem. Positive density for each
fixed sieve does not bound its first surviving interval. A theorem for almost
every parameter does not settle a specified exceptional parameter.

The notes on [[research/covering_prime_interfaces/finite_certificates|finite
certificates]] give the exact two-generator certificate for Problem 203 and
explain where a finite covering is the wrong kind of witness. The
[[research/covering_prime_interfaces/density_quantifiers|density notes]]
compare conditioned tails, periodic densities and inverse covering functions.
The [[research/covering_prime_interfaces/prime_gap_statistics|gap statistics]]
notes identify the additional support, moment and adjacent-gap information
needed by the limiting questions. The
[[research/covering_prime_interfaces/prime_pattern_uniformity|prime-pattern
notes]] isolate growing-tuple, exceptional-set and short-interval gaps.

Each proposed use names a discriminating test and a stopping condition.
Failure of such a test rejects that implication at its stated strength; it
does not refute the underlying Erdős problem.
