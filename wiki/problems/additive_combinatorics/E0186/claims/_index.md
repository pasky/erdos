# problems/additive_combinatorics/E0186/claims

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0186/_index|..]]

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0186/claims/1989_03_01_bosznay|1989_03_01_bosznay]]: Bosznay proved in 1989 that the largest non-averaging subset of
$\{1,\ldots,N\}$ has more than $cN^{1/4}$ elements for large $N$, using the
set of $iq^3+i(i+1)/2$ for $1\le i\le q-1$. This refereed result, credited by
the site, is the lower half of the answer.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0186/claims/2024_10_18_pham_zakharov|2024_10_18_pham_zakharov]]: Pham and Zakharov proved that every non-averaging subset of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$
has at most $N^{1/4+o(1)}$ elements, which with Bosznay's bound gives
$F(N)=N^{1/4+o(1)}$. The paper appeared in Geom. Funct. Anal. in 2025, and the
site adopts it.
