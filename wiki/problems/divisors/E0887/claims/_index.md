# problems/divisors/E0887/claims

[[problems/divisors/E0887/_index|..]]

[[problems/divisors/E0887/claims/2013_03_08_chan|2013_03_08_chan]]: Chan proves that, for each $c\ge3$, every large perfect square $n$ has at most
five divisors within $cn^{1/4}$ of $\sqrt n$. This answers the question for
squares with $K=5$.

[[problems/divisors/E0887/claims/2014_06_09_chan|2014_06_09_chan]]: Chan proves that every large $n=(N-a)(N+b)$ with
$0\le a\le b\le\exp((\log n)^{2/7})$ has at most eighteen divisors within
$n^{1/4}(\log n)^{1/14}$ of $\sqrt n$.
