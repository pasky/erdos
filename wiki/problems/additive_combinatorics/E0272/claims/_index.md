# problems/additive_combinatorics/E0272/claims

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0272/_index|..]]

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0272/claims/1981_12_01_simonovits_sos|1981_12_01_simonovits_sos]]: Simonovits and Sós's 1981 European J. Combin. paper bounds the size of the
extremal family by $(\pi^2/24+1/2+o(1))N^2$. It also refutes the Erdős–Graham
guess that the progressions through a fixed element are extremal.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0272/claims/1999_07_01_szabo|1999_07_01_szabo]]: Szabó's 1999 European J. Combin. theorem shows that the extremal family has
$N^2/2+O(N^{5/3}(\log N)^3)$ members. The paper's construction of
$\binom N2+\lfloor(N-1)/4\rfloor+1$ sets refutes the Simonovits–Sós
conjecture.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0272/claims/2026_07_25_yang|2026_07_25_yang]]: Zhanfu Yang's unrefereed 2026 preprint reports $t(N)$ for $3\le N\le12$ by
computation and proves Szabó's lower bound exact for every family with a
common element. This corpus has not rerun its computations.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0272/claims/2026_09_09_jenw1n|2026_09_09_jenw1n]]: A Lean proof, accepted by the bounty site Conjectures.io in September 2026,
shows that the largest family with pairwise nonempty progression intersections
has $N^2/2+O(N)$ members. This answers Szabó's linear-error question but does
not give the exact value.
