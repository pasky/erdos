# problems/diophantine_problems/E0261/claims

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0261/_index|..]]

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0261/claims/1990_01_01_borwein_loring|1990_01_01_borwein_loring]]: Borwein and Loring's refereed 1990 identity writes $n/2^n$ as a sum of $m$
consecutive terms $k/2^k$ for $n=2^{m+1}-m-2$. So infinitely many $n$ have the
property, and the answer to the first question is yes.

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0261/claims/1990_01_01_borwein_loring_conditional|1990_01_01_borwein_loring_conditional]]: Borwein and Loring's Corollary 1 shows that if the iteration
$a\mapsto2(a\bmod n)$ always reaches zero (their Conjecture 1), then every $n$
has the property of the second question. The result is refereed and
conditional, so it settles no standing.

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0261/claims/2020_08_04_tengely_ulas_zygadlo|2020_08_04_tengely_ulas_zygadlo]]: Tengely, Ulas and Zygadło verify that $n/2^n$ is a sum of at least two
distinct terms $k/2^k$ for every $n\le10^4$. The paper is refereed and covers
the second question up to $10^4$.
