## Weakest steps and attack coverage

The report identifies three weak links and re-derives them rather than
merely endorsing the source. I checked those derivations against the frozen
proof and tried to make their interfaces fail. The checks below explain the
grade; they do not furnish a replacement theorem proof.

**Local cancellation and probability.** For the trial weights,
$\sum_h w_h=L\ell$, while each central index has weight $\ell$.
There are $L-\ell+1\geq7L/8$ central indices. The legal dyadic choice gives

$$
\frac{\lambda}{128B^2L}<2^{-d}\leq
\frac{\lambda}{64B^2L}.
$$

The independent central-hit probability is therefore at least
$7\lambda/(2048B^2)$. In the hostile configuration with only one central
hit and every other spike at its negative floor, the retained estimates
give a signal at least $(4\sqrt2-1)B\ell>2B\ell$.
The same normalization gives the block floor with $C_3=1$.
The count includes both central endpoints; there is no missing factor of
$L$ or $\ell$. This is a substantive attempted refutation of the signed
signal, with explicit slack, not a finite sample.

**Independence, convergence and null sets.** The digits for
$\phi_d(2^v x)$ are $v+1,\ldots,v+d$ off the dyadic null set.
The last bit of block $k$ is $V_k^{\max}+1$; the next block begins beyond
it under the stated $U$ choice. For a whole translated trial, a sufficient
containing interval ends at
$H=U+M+(L+\ell)D+d$; the next trial's first possible translated bit is
$H+2D+3$. The separate initial-stage condition places the new trial digits
beyond all previous event digits. These verify the two independence
interfaces actually needed. There is no need to assume independence of
every contribution to a partial sum.

The report does not infer almost-sure convergence merely from $L^2$
convergence. It applies the permitted independent centered summable-variance
criterion to the actual blocks, identifies the two limits, and pulls the
common convergence and floor set back under countably many selected
dilations. The resulting endpoint decomposition has the lower bound
$2B_k\ell-\mu N$. Thus the old terms and the omitted blocks are both
accounted for. The attempts to break the result by changing a representative
on a null set or introducing a shared boundary bit fail on the retained
argument.

**Stage growth, future floor and quantifiers.** With $\eta=1/20$ and integer
$P$, the source's ceiling is exactly $\ell=20(P+1)$. Hence

$$
N_k^*+1=(P_{k,1}+1)21^{T_k},\qquad
T_k=\left\lceil\Gamma B_k^2 a_k^{-1}\log(k+1)\right\rceil.
$$

For fixed $k$ and its already fixed past, this gives
$\log N_k^*\leq K_kB_k^2$, where $K_k$ is independent of the proposed
$B_k$. With the source's explicit $a_k=2^{-k-2}$, all past, current and
future negative contributions are bounded in advance by $C_3/400$.
Writing $\delta_k=1/(4(k+2))$, the lower bound needed in (7.5) grows at
least as $B_k^{2\delta_k}/(2K_k^{1/2-\delta_k})$ for sufficiently large
$B_k$. Its exponent is positive at each fixed stage. No uniform lower
bound on that exponent over all stages is claimed or required.

This defeats the strongest attack: increasing $B_k$ might otherwise have
made $T_k$, the count denominator and the unknown future floor defeat the
desired gain. The report supplies finite choices in the correct order.
Stage failure is at most $(k+1)^{-8}$; on the resulting fixed measure-one
set there is an endpoint in every sufficiently large stage. Those endpoints
increase, have $N\geq20$, and have positive numerators. Replacing
$\delta_k$ by any fixed larger $\varepsilon$ then reduces the denominator
and preserves the lower bound. This handles the uncountable quantifier
without taking an uncountable intersection of full-measure sets.

The catalog consequence has its own attack: large values under one
normalization need not refute a bound under another. Here the exact ratio
$(\log N)^{1/4}/\sqrt{\log\log N}$ tends to infinity. A positive infinite
limsup in the theorem's normalization therefore contradicts the stated
little-o assertion pointwise on the same set. Periodicity and countably
many integer translates transfer the conclusion from the circle to almost
every real $\alpha$. This also verifies the frozen Covers sentence.

Two source imprecisions are bounded rather than hidden. Summability of an
arbitrary positive $(a_k)$ does not imply $a_k\leq1$: one could take
$a_1=2$ and a summable positive tail. The report instead uses the source's
already supplied sequence, for which the bound holds. Likewise the digit
arguments require integer parameters; the actual recursive choices realize
those types. Neither observation adds a hypothesis to this existential
theorem or supplies a missing essential lemma. No universal assertion for
