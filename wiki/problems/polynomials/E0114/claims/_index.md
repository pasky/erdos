# problems/polynomials/E0114/claims

[[problems/polynomials/E0114/_index|..]]

[[problems/polynomials/E0114/claims/1998_01_01_wang|1998_01_01_wang]]: Wang Chunjie's 1998 paper on the arc length of the lemniscate of $w^2+c$
solves the degree-2 case of the conjecture, a year before Eremenko and Hayman.
It was refereed and published in Acta Mathematica Scientia (Chinese edition).

[[problems/polynomials/E0114/claims/1999_09_01_eremenko_hayman|1999_09_01_eremenko_hayman]]: Eremenko and Hayman prove that among monic quadratics the lemniscate of
$z^2+1$, the Bernoulli lemniscate, is the longest, which settles degree 2 of
the conjecture. It was refereed and published in the Michigan Mathematical
Journal.

[[problems/polynomials/E0114/claims/2025_12_13_tao|2025_12_13_tao]]: In an arXiv preprint, Tao proves that for every sufficiently large $n$ the
lemniscate of a monic polynomial of degree $n$ is at most as long as that of
$z^n-1$, with equality only up to rotation and translation. The claim is
pending.

[[problems/polynomials/E0114/claims/2026_03_23_mendoza|2026_03_23_mendoza]]: Mendoza's interval branch-and-bound search claimed the conjecture for degrees
3 to 10 and then 3 to 14. After an objection on the code repository, the
author conceded in October 2026 that it is a census, not a proof, for any
degree.

[[problems/polynomials/E0114/claims/2026_05_20_dahlke|2026_05_20_dahlke]]: Dahlke's 2026 Zenodo manuscript proves that among monic cubics the lemniscate
of $z^3-1$ is the longest, using the Eremenko–Hayman extremal reduction as Tao
states it. It is a partial claim, pending.

[[problems/polynomials/E0114/claims/2026_08_22_chatelet|2026_08_22_chatelet]]: Chatelet's 2026 write-up and Lean development assert that among monic cubics
the lemniscate of $z^3-1$ is the longest, under a domain reduction cited from
Eremenko–Hayman and Tao. It is a partial claim, pending review.
