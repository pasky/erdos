# problems/discrete_geometry/E0634/claims

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0634/_index|..]]

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0634/claims/1991_08_01_snover_waiveris_williams|1991_08_01_snover_waiveris_williams]]: In a refereed paper in Discrete Mathematics, Snover, Waiveris and Williams
classify the cuttings of a triangle into $N$ congruent copies similar to
itself. So the values $n^2+m^2$ and $3n^2$ occur.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0634/claims/2009_01_01_soifer|2009_01_01_soifer]]: In a book chapter, Soifer shows that some triangle can be cut into $2n^2$,
$3n^2$, $6n^2$ and $n^2+m^2$ congruent triangles. The erdosproblems.com
remarks credit these constructions, and the later manuscripts call them
classical.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0634/claims/2018_11_23_beeson|2018_11_23_beeson]]: Beeson proves that no triangle can be cut into seven congruent triangles, nor
into eleven. The erdosproblems.com remarks credit this result through Beeson's
talk slides, and the paper is a preprint without a journal record.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0634/claims/2025_12_27_zhang|2025_12_27_zhang]]: In a preprint, Zhang proves that for an integer-sided tile with sides $a$, $b$
and $c=\sqrt{a^2+ab+b^2}$, an equilateral triangle can be cut into $m^2ab$
congruent copies for every $m$ above an explicit threshold.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0634/claims/2026_06_27_bonfioli|2026_06_27_bonfioli]]: Bonfioli's manuscript claims to exclude every prime $p\equiv7\pmod{12}$, $19$
among them, and to determine every $n$ up to $80$ by exact search. It leaves
the primes $p\equiv11\pmod{12}$ open, excluding them only under an unproved
hypothesis, and disputes several published exclusions.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0634/claims/2026_07_17_george|2026_07_17_george]]: An unreviewed write-up by George, GPT-5.6 Pro and Inkling on the
erdosproblems.com proof-claims thread asserts that no triangle can be cut into
$19$ or $46$ congruent triangles, nor into $p$ of them for any prime $p>3$
with $p\equiv3\pmod4$.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0634/claims/2026_07_24_harries|2026_07_24_harries]]: Harries's manuscript on the erdosproblems.com proof-claims thread asserts that
no triangle is tiled by $p$ congruent triangles for a prime $p>3$ with
$p\equiv3\pmod4$, so the prime counts are $2$, $3$ and the primes
$p\equiv1\pmod4$. It is unreviewed.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0634/claims/2026_07_26_beeson|2026_07_26_beeson]]: Beeson's unreviewed preprint asserts that a triangle cut into a prime number
$N>3$ of congruent triangles is a reptiling, so that the prime counts are
exactly $2$, $3$ and the primes $p\equiv1\pmod4$. It is one of three
contemporaneous proofs of the prime case.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0634/claims/2026_07_27_harries|2026_07_27_harries]]: Harries's unreviewed manuscript gives exact certificates that $88$ and $189$
occur, so that $21n^2$ occurs exactly for $n\ge2$. It also gives a
computer-assisted, independently certified exclusion of $33$.
