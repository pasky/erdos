# Additive properties of dense subsets of sifted sequences

[[primes/_index|..]]

[[primes/ramare_ruzsa_2001_dense_subsets_sifted_sequences/g_functions|g_functions]]: Exact residue-support, uniform-lifting and G-function conventions from
Section 4.1, including the comparison used in the improved large sieve.

[[primes/ramare_ruzsa_2001_dense_subsets_sifted_sequences/theorem_4|theorem_4]]: The exact nonnegative decomposition of reduced-fraction Fourier energy
for a sequence supported by a compact satisfying uniform lifting.

[[primes/ramare_ruzsa_2001_dense_subsets_sifted_sequences/theorem_5|theorem_5]]: The two-level Fourier bound with factor G_1(Q_0)/G_1(Q/Q_0) and
enlarged-level cost N+Q squared, with its exact support hypotheses.

***

Olivier Ramaré and Imre Z. Ruzsa, *Additive properties of dense subsets of
sifted sequences*, *Journal de Théorie des Nombres de Bordeaux* **13** (2)
(2001), 559–581, [DOI 10.5802/jtnb.338](https://doi.org/10.5802/jtnb.338).
The edition read is the journal scan at the
[Numdam record](https://www.numdam.org/item/JTNB_2001__13_2_559_0/),
obtained from its
[PDF endpoint](https://www.numdam.org/article/JTNB_2001__13_2_559_0.pdf).

Section 4.1 supplies a sieve interface that allows prime-power moduli. Its
supporting compact has compatible residue sets, and the Johnsen condition
requires uniform lifting between successive prime-power levels. The
[[primes/ramare_ruzsa_2001_dense_subsets_sifted_sequences/g_functions|G-functions]]
encode these sets. Under those hypotheses,
[[primes/ramare_ruzsa_2001_dense_subsets_sifted_sequences/theorem_4|Theorem 4]]
expresses Fourier energy as a nonnegative sum, and
[[primes/ramare_ruzsa_2001_dense_subsets_sifted_sequences/theorem_5|Theorem 5]]
bounds the energy through a ratio of G-functions at two different levels.

**Read status: claims checked.** The support definition (4.1.1), G-functions
(4.1.3)–(4.1.6), Lemma 1, Johnsen condition (4.1.12), and Theorems 4 and 5
were checked against images of printed pp. 567–571. Their proofs on pp.
570–571 were read for the stated interfaces, without an independent proof
audit. No reading or verification of the paper's other theorems is asserted.

**Bears on.** The two-level estimate is a possible input to the multiscale
large-sieve approach to
[[../wiki/problems/integer_sequences/E0012/_index|Problem 12(iii)]].
An application must specify its actual residue sets and support, check the
uniform-lifting condition, estimate both G-functions, and retain the enlarged
level's quadratic cost. This source supplies no property-P guard-budget bound.
