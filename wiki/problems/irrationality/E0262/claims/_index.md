# problems/irrationality/E0262/claims

[[problems/irrationality/E0262/_index|..]]

[[problems/irrationality/E0262/claims/1975_01_01_erdos|1975_01_01_erdos]]: Erdős's 1975 paper proves that $\sum_k1/n_k$ is irrational whenever each $n_k$
is a positive multiple of $2^{2^k}$. So $2^{2^n}$ is an irrationality sequence
of the problem's kind.

[[problems/irrationality/E0262/claims/1991_01_01_hancl|1991_01_01_hancl]]: Hančl proves that every irrationality sequence satisfies
$\limsup(\log_2\log_2a_n)/n\ge1$. With Erdős's example $2^{2^n}$, this pins
the slowest growth at doubly exponential.
