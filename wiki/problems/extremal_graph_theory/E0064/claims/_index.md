# problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/claims

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/_index|..]]

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/claims/1998_01_01_shauger|1998_01_01_shauger]]: Shauger (Congressus Numerantium 1998) proves the conjecture for $K_{1,m}$-free
graphs with minimum degree at least $m+1$ or maximum degree at least $2m-1$,
and bounds the order of a claw-free counterexample. It is a proceedings paper.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/claims/2001_01_01_daniel_shauger|2001_01_01_daniel_shauger]]: Daniel and Shauger (Congressus Numerantium 2001) prove the conjecture for
planar claw-free graphs, using results of Dean and of Dean, Lesniak and Saito.
It is a proceedings paper.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/claims/2004_01_01_markstrom|2004_01_01_markstrom]]: Markström (Congressus Numerantium 2004) reports an exhaustive computer search
showing that every cubic graph on fewer than 30 vertices has a cycle of length
4 or 8. It is a proceedings paper, and the computation is reported by the
author.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/claims/2011_09_18_nowbandegani_esfandiari|2011_09_18_nowbandegani_esfandiari]]: A 2011 workshop result of Salehi Nowbandegani and Esfandiari, obtained by
computation, says that a bipartite counterexample has at least 32 vertices, so
the conjecture holds for bipartite graphs on at most 31 vertices. The same
authors' claw-free paper cites it.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/claims/2011_09_25_nowbandegani_esfandiari_haghighi_bibak|2011_09_25_nowbandegani_esfandiari_haghighi_bibak]]: Salehi Nowbandegani and three coauthors prove that a cubic claw-free
counterexample has at least 114 vertices, and that a claw-free graph of
minimum degree at least 3 has a cycle of length $2^k$ or $3\cdot2^k$. The
claim is accepted on the refereed paper in Discuss. Math. Graph Theory.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/claims/2013_04_09_heckman_krakovski|2013_04_09_heckman_krakovski]]: Heckman and Krakovski prove the conjecture for 3-connected cubic planar graphs
by a discharging argument that is partly computer-based. The claim is accepted
on the refereed paper in the Electronic Journal of Combinatorics.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/claims/2017_11_21_ghaffari_mostaghim|2017_11_21_ghaffari_mostaghim]]: Ghaffari and Mostaghim prove the conjecture for Cayley graphs on generalized
quaternion, dihedral and semidihedral groups and on groups of order $p^3$. The
claim is accepted on the refereed paper in Aequationes Mathematicae.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/claims/2020_10_29_liu_montgomery|2020_10_29_liu_montgomery]]: Liu and Montgomery's unavoidable-sequence corollary shows that every graph
with average degree above an absolute constant, and so every graph with
minimum degree above it, has a cycle whose length is a power of two. The claim
is accepted on the refereed JAMS paper.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/claims/2021_01_01_ghasemi_varmazyar|2021_01_01_ghasemi_varmazyar]]: Ghasemi and Varmazyar prove the conjecture for Cayley graphs of order $2p^2$
and of order $4p$, where $p$ is a prime. The claim is accepted on the refereed
paper in Matematički Vesnik.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/claims/2021_09_03_gao_shan|2021_09_03_gao_shan]]: Gao and Shan prove that every $P_8$-free graph of minimum degree at least
three contains a cycle of length 4 or 8, which confirms the conjecture for
$P_8$-free graphs. The claim is accepted on the refereed paper in Graphs and
Combinatorics.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/claims/2023_08_10_hu_shen|2023_08_10_hu_shen]]: Hu and Shen prove that every $P_{10}$-free graph of minimum degree at least
three contains a cycle of length 4 or 8, which confirms the conjecture for
$P_{10}$-free graphs. The claim is accepted on the refereed paper in Discrete
Mathematics.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/claims/2024_10_30_hegde_sandeep_shashank|2024_10_30_hegde_sandeep_shashank]]: Hegde, Sandeep and Shashank prove, with the aid of a computer search, that
every $P_{13}$-free graph of minimum degree at least three has a cycle whose
length is a power of two. This extends Hu and Shen's $P_{10}$-free case and
appears in an arXiv preprint.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/claims/2025_08_25_carr|2025_08_25_carr]]: Carr asserts that every graph of diameter 2 and minimum degree at least 3
contains a cycle of length 4 or 8, confirming the conjecture for graphs of
diameter 2. The arXiv note is reported accepted by the Bulletin of the ICA.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/claims/2026_08_02_tranquilli|2026_08_02_tranquilli]]: Tranquilli reports a certified exhaustive computation, posted on arXiv,
showing that every cubic bipartite graph on at most 58 vertices has a cycle of
length 4, 8 or 16. So a cubic bipartite counterexample has at least 60
vertices.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/claims/2026_08_19_duran_ballester|2026_08_19_duran_ballester]]: Duran Ballester deposited on Zenodo a case analysis of a minimal
counterexample as a proof of the Erdős–Gyárfás conjecture. In a manuscript
of 1 October 2026 the claimant retitled it as a reduction that leaves six
outcomes open.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/claims/2026_09_04_garcia|2026_09_04_garcia]]: Garcia reports a SAT search with DRAT certificates showing that every graph of
minimum degree at least three on at most 23 vertices has a cycle of length 4
or 8, so a counterexample has at least 24 vertices. The result appears on
arXiv only.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0064/claims/2026_10_02_temeller|2026_10_02_temeller]]: Temeller's proof, posted as a GitHub issue, asserts that every finite graph
with minimum degree at least four and diameter at most three has a cycle of
length four or eight. It extends Carr's diameter-two theorem and is
unverified.
