# problems/polynomials/E0119/claims

[[problems/polynomials/E0119/_index|..]]

[[problems/polynomials/E0119/claims/1980_03_01_wagner|1980_03_01_wagner]]: Wagner proves that for every sequence on the unit circle, the maximum modulus
$M_n$ of the partial products exceeds $(\log n)^c$ for some $c>0$ and
infinitely many $n$, so $M_n$ is unbounded. This answers the first question,
is refereed and is credited by erdosproblems.com.

[[problems/polynomials/E0119/claims/1991_11_01_beck|1991_11_01_beck]]: Beck proves that for every sequence on the unit circle, the largest of the
maximum moduli $M_1,\dots,M_N$ exceeds $N^c$ for all large $N$, with an
absolute constant $c>0$. This answers the second question, is refereed in the
Annals and is credited by erdosproblems.com.

[[problems/polynomials/E0119/claims/2026_07_14_korsky|2026_07_14_korsky]]: Korsky, working with GPT 5.6-Pro, proves that for every sequence on the unit
circle the maximum moduli satisfy $\sum_{k\le N}M_k\gg N^{5/4}/\sqrt{\log N}$.
This answers the third question and is accepted by erdosproblems.com.

[[problems/polynomials/E0119/claims/2026_08_29_korsky|2026_08_29_korsky]]: Korsky, working with GPT-5.6 Pro, claims that for every sequence on the unit
circle the maximum moduli satisfy $\sum_{k\le N}M_k\ge(e^{-1/2}+o(1))N^{3/2}$,
which the claimant believes is sharp. The write-up is shared on the
erdosproblems.com thread and credited by erdosproblems.com.
