# problems/polynomials/E0525/claims

[[problems/polynomials/E0525/_index|..]]

[[problems/polynomials/E0525/claims/1987_01_01_kashin|1987_01_01_kashin]]: A refereed 1987 paper proves that a random polynomial of degree $n$ with
coefficients $\pm1$ has minimum modulus on the unit circle at most
$\sqrt n/(\log n)^{1/3}$ with probability tending to one.

[[problems/polynomials/E0525/claims/1994_06_20_konyagin|1994_06_20_konyagin]]: Konyagin proves that a random polynomial of degree $n$ with coefficients
$\pm1$ has minimum modulus below $n^{-1/2+\varepsilon}$ with probability
tending to one. This refereed 1994 paper gives the first answer to the first
question.

[[problems/polynomials/E0525/claims/1999_08_27_konyagin_schlag|1999_08_27_konyagin_schlag]]: Konyagin and Schlag prove that a random polynomial of degree $n$ with
coefficients $\pm1$ has minimum modulus below $\varepsilon/\sqrt n$ with
limiting probability at most $C\varepsilon$, so the exponent $-1/2$ is
optimal.

[[problems/polynomials/E0525/claims/2021_01_18_cook_nguyen|2021_01_18_cook_nguyen]]: Cook and Nguyen prove that the minimum modulus of a random polynomial with
coefficients $\pm1$, scaled by the square root of the degree, has the same
exponential limit law as in the Gaussian case. This contains the answer to the
first question.
