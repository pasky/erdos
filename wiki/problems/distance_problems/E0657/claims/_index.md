# problems/distance_problems/E0657/claims

[[problems/distance_problems/E0657/_index|..]]

[[problems/distance_problems/E0657/claims/2008_12_01_dumitrescu|2008_12_01_dumitrescu]]: Dumitrescu's refereed 2008 note proves the problem's assertion for collinear
sets: $n$ points on a line with no isosceles triple determine at least
$(\log n)^c\,n$ distinct distances. Some such sets determine at most
$n\,2^{O(\sqrt{\log n})}$.

[[problems/distance_problems/E0657/claims/2025_08_19_hunter|2025_08_19_hunter]]: The site's commentary credits Zach Hunter with the observation that collinear
sets with no isosceles triple determine at least $2^{c(\log n)^{1/9}}\,n$
distances, and Alfaiz and Tang give details in the thread. The claim is
pending.
