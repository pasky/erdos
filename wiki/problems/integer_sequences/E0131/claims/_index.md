# problems/integer_sequences/E0131/claims

[[problems/integer_sequences/E0131/_index|..]]

[[problems/integer_sequences/E0131/claims/1999_04_01_erdos_lev_rauzy_sandor_sarkozy|1999_04_01_erdos_lev_rauzy_sandor_sarkozy]]: Erdős, Lev, Rauzy, Sándor and Sárközy (Discrete Math. 1999, refereed) bound
the size $F(N)$ of the largest non-dividing subset of the first $N$ integers.
They prove $F(N)<3N^{1/2}+1$ for all $N$ and deduce $F(N)\gg N^{1/5}$ from
Straus and Bosznay.

[[problems/integer_sequences/E0131/claims/2024_10_18_pham_zakharov|2024_10_18_pham_zakharov]]: Pham and Zakharov (Geom. Funct. Anal. 2025, refereed) prove the bound
$N^{1/4+o(1)}$ for non-averaging sets. Non-dividing sets are non-averaging, so
the displayed question, whether $F(N)>N^{1/2-o(1)}$, is answered no.

[[problems/integer_sequences/E0131/claims/2026_07_24_xeff|2026_07_24_xeff]]: A full proof claim of 24 July 2026 by Theofil Xeff, made with GPT 5.6 Sol and
Fable 5, asserts that $F(N)=N^{1/5+o(1)}$, by a normalization of the
Pham-Zakharov density-increment argument. erdosproblems.com has not examined
it.
