**Claim.** For a real number $u$ write $\{u\}$ for its fractional part,
read in $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$. An arc is a nonempty connected subset $I$
of $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$, open, closed or half-open, and it is sum-free if
$t+t'\notin I$ for all $t,t'\in I$. Let $N$ and $k$ be positive integers
and $\{1,\ldots,N\}=A_1\cup\cdots\cup A_k$, for instance the color classes
of a $k$-coloring. Suppose that for each $i$ there are a real number
$\theta_i$ and a sum-free arc $I_i$ such that $\{\theta_ix\}\in I_i$ for
every $x\in A_i$. Then

$$
N+1\le8^k.
$$

Each set $\{x:\{\theta x\}\in I\}$ with $I$ a sum-free arc is sum-free,
so a coloring of this kind has no monochromatic solution to $a+b=c$,
equal summands allowed. Thus the least $N$ such that $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ has
no $k$-coloring of this kind is at most $8^k$.

**Covers.** The exponential question of
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0483/_index|Problem 483]] for the
$k$-colorings of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ in which each color class lies in a
single sum-free Bohr arc $\{x:\{\theta_ix\}\in I_i\}$: for these colorings
the exponential bound holds, since none exists once $N\ge8^k$. The class
contains Schur's tripling coloring by $k$ colors, of
$\{1,\ldots,(3^k-1)/2\}$. A $k$-coloring of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ with
$N\ge8^k$ and no monochromatic solution therefore has a class lying in no
single sum-free Bohr arc. The bound does not apply to general colorings:
$f(k)$ itself is not bounded by it, and the question whether
$f(k)<c^k$, with the estimate of $f(k)$ and whether $f(k)^{1/k}$ tends to
a finite limit, remains open.
