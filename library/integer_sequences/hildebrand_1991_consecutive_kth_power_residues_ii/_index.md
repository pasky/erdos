For positive integers $k$ and $l$ and a large prime $p$, let $r(k,l,p)$ be the
least positive $r$ such that $r, r+1, \dots, r+l-1$ are all $k$th power residues
modulo $p$, and let $\Lambda(k,l) = \limsup_{p \to \infty} r(k,l,p)$. The
introduction recalls that $\Lambda(k,l) = \infty$ for $l \ge 4$ and every
$k \ge 2$, and for $l = 3$ and every even $k$, and that an earlier paper of the
author proved $\Lambda(k,2) < \infty$ for prime $k$. Theorem 1 proves
$\Lambda(k,2) < \infty$ for every positive integer $k$. It is deduced from
Theorem 2: for each $k$ there is a constant $c_0(k)$ such that every completely
multiplicative function $f$ whose values are $k$th roots of unity takes the
value $f(n) = f(n+1) = 1$ at some $n \le c_0(k)$.
