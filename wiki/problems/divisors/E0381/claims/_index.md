# problems/divisors/E0381/claims

[[problems/divisors/E0381/_index|..]]

[[problems/divisors/E0381/claims/1944_07_01_erdos|1944_07_01_erdos]]: Erdős's 1944 theorem shows that consecutive highly composite numbers lie
within a factor $1+(\log n)^{-c}$ of each other, so more than $(\log x)^{1+c}$
of them lie below $x$. It answers the question yes for the exponents
$k\le1+c$.

[[problems/divisors/E0381/claims/1971_02_01_nicolas|1971_02_01_nicolas]]: Nicolas proved in 1971 that the number $Q(X)$ of highly composite numbers up
to $X$ is $O\bigl((\log X)^{1+c}\bigr)$. The count stays below a fixed power
of the logarithm, so the answer to the question is no.
