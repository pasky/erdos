***

**Claim.** Kenta Kitamura's Lean development, announced on 11 September 2026
in the discussion thread of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0873/_index|Problem 873]], claims a proof that
every increasing sequence $A$ has $\liminf_{X\to\infty}F(A,X,3)/X^{1/3}\le6$.
So no sequence has $F(A,X,3)\gg X^{1/3}\log X$ for every large $X$, against
the suggestion of Erdős in
[[../library/integer_sequences/erdos_1992_my_forgotten_problems_number_theory/_index|Some of my forgotten problems in number theory]]
(p. 48), repeated in the erdosproblems.com commentary, that some sequence may
meet the Erdős–Szemerédi bound $F(A,X,3)\ll X^{1/3}\log X$ for every $X$ and not
only for infinitely many. The post names formal-conjectures pull request 5544 as
the submission and the repository linked above, at the commit its web
type-checker link names, as the development. formal-conjectures registers the
development as the formal proof of its variant
`erdos_873.variants.supplement_all_scale`, which states that no increasing
sequence and constant $c>0$ have $F(A,X,3)\ge cX^{1/3}\log X$ for all large $X$.

**Why it is rejected.** It answers a variant, not the Statement.
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0873/_index|Problem 873]] asks whether, for
every $\epsilon>0$, some $k$ gives $F(A,X,k)<X^\epsilon$. The development
answers instead the question the erdosproblems.com commentary raises after the
Erdős–Szemerédi bound, whether some sequence has $F(A,X,3)\gg X^{1/3}\log X$
for every large $X$, formal-conjectures' variant
`erdos_873.variants.supplement_all_scale`. Its bound,
$F(A,X,3)\le(6+o(1))X^{1/3}$ for arbitrarily large $X$, decides no
exponent $\epsilon\le1/3$ that the Erdős–Szemerédi bound leaves open, so the
development's mathematics answers the variant and does not settle this
problem. The value `disproved` is its answer to that variant.

**Standing.** Rejected, as answering a variant. This corpus has neither read
the repository nor built the development, so it gives a formalization link and
no `formalized` evidence, and the axiom report above is the poster's own
statement.
