**Claim.** Write $D_k(X)=\lvert D_k\cap[1,X]\rvert$, $s(v)$ for the squarefree
part of $v$, $S=\{s(c!):c\ge1\}$ and
$\kappa_3=\sum_{a\in S\setminus\{1\}}a^{-1/2}=2.709751\ldots$. Theorem 1.1:
for every $\varepsilon>0$,
$D_3(X)=\kappa_3\sqrt X+O_\varepsilon(X^{2/5+\varepsilon})$. Theorem 1.2:
there is $c^*>0$ such that for $k\in\{5,6\}$ and all large $X$ at least $c^*X$
squarefree $n\in[X/2,X]$ have $F(n)=k$, for $k=5$ separately among even and
among odd $n$. Corollary 1.3 collects the orders of growth:
$D_2(X)=\lfloor\sqrt X\rfloor-1$, $D_3(X)\sim\kappa_3\sqrt X$,
$D_4(X)\asymp D_5(X)\asymp D_6(X)\asymp X$ and $D_k(X)=0$ for $k\ge7$, the
lower bound for $D_4$ coming from the identity
$n!(n-1)!u!(u-1)!=(vu(n-1)!(u-1)!)^2$ for $n=v^2u$. The corollary answers the
question of [[problems/diophantine_problems/E0374/_index|Problem 374]] for
every $k$ from $3$ to $6$ and confirms the conjecture of Erdős and Graham that
$D_6(X)\gg X$; whether $D_k(X)/X$ converges for $k=4,5,6$ is left open. The
source is F. Yudin, *Square products of factorials and a conjecture of Erdős
and Graham*, arXiv:2610.01899, version 1 of 2026-10-01 (Creative Commons
Attribution 4.0, by the arXiv record), submitted to the erdosproblems.com
proof-claims tab on 2026-10-02. The three-factorial estimate counts
representations $n!(n-\ell)!c!=\square$ with $\ell\ge2$ by combining Tao's
uniform bound for Pell equations
([[../library/diophantine_problems/tao_2026_products_consecutive_integers_unusual_anatomy/_index|card]])
with the larger sieve of Gallagher and Weil's character-sum bound, the
exponent $2/5$ coming from balancing the two cases; the consecutive
representations $n!(n-1)!c!=\square$ give the main term. For five and six
factorials the argument takes $n=ap$ with $p>X^{1-\alpha}$ prime, uses the
six-factor identity of Erdős and Graham, and excludes shorter representations
by bounding the two factorial blocks through the prime equidistribution
estimate of Matomäki, Radziwiłł, Shao, Tao and Teräväinen with the large
sieve, after Huxley's theorem on primes in short intervals has given
preliminary bounds on the two block lengths (Lemma 4.2). The positive lower
density of the surviving $n$ comes from the prime number theorem, summed over
cofactors $a$ from a set of positive lower density (Proposition 7.3). An
appendix constructs, for every $m\ge2$, perfect $m$th powers that are products
of a bounded number of distinct factorials with affine arguments, through a
zero-sum theorem for finite abelian groups.

**Acceptance.** Reviewed and formalized. Reviewed: Jacob Parish at Plasma AI
checked the claim and accepted it; the acceptance was recorded on 2026-10-12.

Formalized: the Lean development of
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0374/claims/2026_09_29_hartley_olson|Hartley and Olson]],
which this corpus built and audited (their claim page records how), proves the
statements with which this claim answers the problem:
$D_3(X)=\kappa_3\sqrt X+O_\varepsilon(X^{2/5+\varepsilon})$ for every
$\varepsilon>0$, with $\kappa_3$ the same sum of $a^{-1/2}$ over the squarefree
parts $a\ne1$ of factorials, and positive lower density for $D_5$ and $D_6$ with
each count at most $X$, so that $D_5(X)$ and $D_6(X)$ are of order $X$. The
formalized proof is theirs, not this preprint's. It does not formalize Theorem
1.2's refinement to squarefree $n$ in $[X/2,X]$, or its separate counts among
even and among odd $n$ for $k=5$.

Not refereed: the preprint is on arXiv only. The erdosproblems.com proof-claims
tab lists it as a full proof claim, the erdosproblems.com label was unchanged,
and the curator has not credited the result. The claimant's submission invites
corrections, especially to the fifth section, in which the equidistribution
estimate controls how long the two blocks of consecutive integers in a
representation can be. The claim's tools field names the AI systems GPT 6 Astra
and Claude 5.5 Opus; in a comment on the claim of 2026-10-02 the claimant says
most of the proof ideas came from GPT, that their own part was to choose the
questions, make occasional suggestions, work through the arguments and revise
the exposition, and that the appendix came first. A reader's comment of
2026-10-03 observes that the $k=3$ estimate is a small modification of Tao's
argument, which split its cases suboptimally.

**Priority.**
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0374/claims/2026_09_29_hartley_olson|Hartley and coauthors]]
were first with $D_6$: their paper was posted on SSRN on 2026-09-29, and their
forum claim of 2026-10-02 covers $D_6$ only. This preprint, with the asymptotic
for $D_3$ and the orders of $D_5$ and $D_6$, appeared on arXiv on 2026-10-01.
Their repository's first commit, of 2026-10-04, already holds the expanded paper
and the Lean development for $D_3$ through $D_6$, and the repository has no
earlier history. Until a commit of 2026-10-05 their paper said, after its growth
theorem, "For (i) and (iii), see also [8]", citing this preprint for $D_3$ and
$D_5$; that commit replaced the sentence with a footnote saying that Yudin
proved these results independently and listing the differences between the
arguments. The record does not settle whether their $D_3$ and $D_5$ arguments
were found independently of this preprint. The two $D_3$ arguments share their
structure: the consecutive representations give the main term, and the rest,
once the block length is shown small, split at a threshold on the squarefree
part of the smallest factorial into a case counted through Pell equations and a
case bounded by Gallagher's larger sieve with the Weil bound, the two balanced
to give the exponent $2/5$. They differ in the Pell count, Tao's uniform bound
here and an elementary count there, and in how the block length is bounded.
