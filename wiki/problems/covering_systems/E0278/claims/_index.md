# problems/covering_systems/E0278/claims

[[problems/covering_systems/E0278/_index|..]]

[[problems/covering_systems/E0278/claims/1966_01_01_rogers|1966_01_01_rogers]]: Rogers' theorem, first published in Halberstam and Roth's Sequences (1966),
states that for fixed moduli the density of integers in none of the classes is
largest when every residue is zero, so equal residues cover least. It is a
book result, accepted on the refereed work that proves and cites it.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0278/claims/1986_04_01_simpson|1986_04_01_simpson]]: Simpson's 1986 bound states that the density covered by one class per modulus
is at least the alternating sum of reciprocal least common multiples, with
equality when all residues agree. It is accepted on the refereed paper.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0278/claims/2025_08_25_cambie|2025_08_25_cambie]]: Stijn Cambie's arXiv note gives exact balanced-partition formulas for
structured moduli, a Chvátal-hard knapsack order, and NP-hardness of zero
density for lists of moduli. Its second version credits GPT Pro Sol 5.6 with
one observation.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0278/claims/2026_09_10_onishi|2026_09_10_onishi]]: Yoshiharu Onishi's 2026 manuscript, prepared with GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-6 Astra,
claims that the maximum covered density is the largest clique
inclusion-exclusion value over the realizable graphs that a gcd dynamic
program enumerates. No review is recorded.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0278/claims/2026_09_22_schroeder|2026_09_22_schroeder]]: Michael Schroeder's 2026 Zenodo manuscript on the first question gives an
exact formula when noncoprime classes can be made disjoint, #P- and
NP-hardness results, and fixed-parameter tractability in treewidth. No review
is recorded.
