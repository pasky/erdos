# research/erdos_25/weighted_divisibility_moments

***

These are proved estimates, not a resolution of Problem 25. They remove
the overlap hypothesis from the [parent-refinement obstruction](parent_refinement_overlap.md)
on a substantial, but bounded, range of sampling scales.

## A weighted version of harmonic domination

Let $W:\mathbb N\to[0,\infty)$ depend only on divisibility by a
fixed finite set of primes, and suppose that $W$ is nondecreasing
under divisibility. Regard the same function as a function on
$\widehat{\mathbb Z}$, with Haar measure $\mu$. Then
$$
 \sum_{n\le X}\frac{W(n)}n
 \le H_{\lfloor X\rfloor}\int W\,d\mu.
 \tag{1}
$$
Here the stated prime-divisibility dependence means that only the
indicators $\mathbf1_{p\mid n}$, not unbounded valuations, are used.
Thus $W$ is bounded. Each superlevel set $\{W>t\}$ is a
multiples set generated by finitely many squarefree divisors of the
product of the chosen primes. Apply the exact multiples harmonic
inequality from [basic reductions](basic_reductions.md#an-exact-finite-harmonic-inequality-for-multiples-proved)
to every superlevel, and integrate in $t$. This proves (1).

## The parent weights

Use the notation of the parent-refinement note:
$$
 \mathcal P=\{p:3\le p\le z\},\quad
 \lambda=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\frac1p,\quad
 r=\lfloor2\lambda-6\sqrt\lambda\rfloor,\quad
 b=\lceil2\lambda+2\sqrt\lambda\rceil,\quad s=b-r.
$$
All assertions below concern sufficiently large $\lambda$. Put
$$
 P_n=\prod_{\substack{p\in\mathcal P\\p\mid n}}(1-2/p)^{-1},
 \qquad c_0=2\log2-1,
 \qquad \kappa=2+c_0=1+2\log2.
$$
For either $a=1/2$ or $a=1$, (1) and a weighted Chernoff
bound give
$$
 \frac1{H_{\lfloor X\rfloor}}
 \sum_{\substack{n\le X\\\omega_{\mathcal P}(n)\ge r}}
       \frac{P_n^a}{n}
 \le \exp\{-c_0\lambda+O(\sqrt\lambda)\}.
 \tag{2}
$$
Indeed $W(n)=P_n^a\mathbf1_{\omega_{\mathcal P}(n)\ge r}$
is divisibility-monotone. With independent Bernoulli variables
$I_p$ of parameters $1/p$, its Haar expectation is at most
$$
 2^{-r}\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}
 \left(1-\frac1p+\frac2p(1-2/p)^{-a}\right).
$$
For each of the two fixed values of $a$, the logarithm of the
local factor is $1/p+O(p^{-2})$, with an absolute summable
error, including the prime 3. The product is consequently
$\exp\{\lambda+O(1)\}$. Substituting the definition of $r$
proves (2), uniformly in $X$.

## An overlap-free protected-trace bound

Consider arbitrary distinct singleton moduli $q$, each prescribing
0 or 1 at at least $r$ primes of $\mathcal P$. Write
$$
 T=\{n:\#\{p\in\mathcal P:n\bmod p\in\{0,1\}\}\le b\},
 \qquad
 B=\bigcup_q\{a_q+tq:t\ge1\},\quad 0\le a_q<q.
$$
No preparatory sieve, incremental-footprint hypothesis, or overlap
restriction is imposed. Uniformly in this family and $X\ge2$,
$$
 \frac{H_{T\cap B}(X)}{H_{\lfloor X\rfloor}}
 \le H_{\lfloor X\rfloor}
       \exp\{-\kappa\lambda+O(\sqrt\lambda\log(2\lambda))\}
    +\exp\{-c_0\lambda+O(\sqrt\lambda\log(2\lambda))\}.
 \tag{3}
$$

To prove this, discard the classes prescribing successes at more than
$b$ primes, since they miss $T$. Let
$\theta_q=\mu(T\mid[a_q]_q)$,
$\sigma_X=\sum_{q\le X}\theta_q/q$, and
$H=H_{\lfloor X\rfloor}$. The already-proved dimension-two
progression estimate, namely (9) of the parent-refinement note, states
$$
 \frac{H_{T\cap B}(X)}H
 \le C\sigma_X+
 \frac{\exp\{O(\sqrt\lambda\log(2\lambda))\}}H
              \sum_{q\le X}\frac{\sqrt{P_q}}q.
 \tag{4}
$$
Its hypotheses concern only these classes and $T$, and do not
include a multiplicity bound. Formula (2) with $a=1/2$ bounds
the second term by the second term in (3).

The same note gives
$$
 \theta_q\le V_zF_sP_q,\qquad
 V_z=\exp\{-2\lambda+O(1)\},\qquad
 F_s=\exp\{O(\sqrt\lambda\log(2\lambda))\}.
$$
Every retained modulus has at least $r$ prime factors from
$\mathcal P$. Distinctness permits its reciprocal sum to be
bounded by the sum over all integers in (2). Using $a=1$,
$$
 \sigma_X\le V_zF_s H
          \exp\{-c_0\lambda+O(\sqrt\lambda)\}.
$$
Substitution in (4) proves (3). In an infinite family only finitely
many moduli $q\le X$ contribute. The representation $t\ge1$
is exactly the original activation $n\ge q$; no inactive root
has been counted.

For each fixed $\varepsilon>0$, (3) tends to zero uniformly when
$$
 H_{\lfloor X\rfloor}\le
                 \exp\{(\kappa-\varepsilon)\lambda\}.
 \tag{5}
$$
Since $\lambda=\log\log z+O(1)$, this includes
$X\le\exp((\log z)^{\kappa-\varepsilon})$, after reducing the
fixed margin if necessary, and in particular $X\le z^A$ for
every fixed $A$. The elementary two-sided prime reciprocal estimate
used here is already established in the earlier notes; the formulation
(5) needs no translation to $z$.

Thus the preparations with moduli at most $z$ cannot produce
a fixed positive protected harmonic deletion in any fixed
polynomial-in-$z$ epoch, even with unrestricted overlap and
without assuming a small incremental footprint. Epochs starting
at much larger scales are not excluded.

## Improvement of the all-scale bounded-overlap error

The $a=1/2$ case also improves (11) of the parent-refinement note to
$$
 \frac{H_{T\cap B}(X)}H
 \le C\sigma_X+
       \exp\{-c_0\lambda+O(\sqrt\lambda\log(2\lambda))\}
 \qquad(X\ge2).
 \tag{6}
$$
This follows directly from (4); its old cutoff split and
Cauchy--Schwarz loss are unnecessary. Combining (6) with that note's
unchanged survivor comparison and in-parent multiplicity bound gives
$$
 \frac{H_{S\cap T\cap B}(X)}H
 \le L\exp\{O(\sqrt\lambda\log(2\lambda))\}\tau
     +\exp\{-c_0\lambda+O(\sqrt\lambda\log(2\lambda))\}.
 \tag{7}
$$
The stronger error does not remove the multiplicity hypothesis from
this **all-scale**, footprint-sensitive conclusion. Formula (3) does
remove it, but only yields a vanishing bound in the range (5).
