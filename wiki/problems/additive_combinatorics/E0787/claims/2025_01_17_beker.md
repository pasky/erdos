***

**Claim.** Theorem 1.2 of A. Beker, *The Erdős--Moser sum-free set problem
via improved bounds for $k$-configurations*, arXiv:2501.10203v1 and v2,
p. 3: "Let $c\in(0,\frac1{68})$ be arbitrary. Then for any sufficiently large
finite set $A\subseteq\mathbb Z$, there exists a subset $B\subseteq A$ of size
at least $(\log|A|)^{1+c}$ such that $b_1+b_2\notin A$ for any distinct
$b_1,b_2\in B$." In the notation of
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0787/_index|Problem 787]],

$$
g(n)\ge(\log n)^{1+c}\qquad\text{for every }c<\tfrac1{68}\text{ and all large }n,
$$

which the paper states as $\phi(n)=\Omega((\log n)^{1+c})$ with $c=1/69$.
It is deduced from Theorem 1.1 (for $\alpha\in(0,1]$ and $k\ge2$, a set of
density $\alpha$ in $[N]$ with $N\ge\exp(Ck^{68}\log(2/\alpha)^{16})$, $C$
an absolute constant, contains a non-degenerate $k$-configuration), with
Sanders's Proposition 2.7 in explicit form. The paper claims no improvement
in the value of $c$ over Sanders's theorem and notes that the
$k$-configuration route is limited to $c<1$; its gain is an explicit
exponent, which the site renders as $(\log n)^{1+1/68+o(1)}$. Cited as
[Be25] on the problem page. Library home
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/beker_2025_erdos_moser_sum_free_set_problem/_index|beker_2025_erdos_moser_sum_free_set_problem]];
result page
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/beker_2025_erdos_moser_sum_free_set_problem/theorem_1_2|Theorem 1.2]].

**Covers.** The lower bound $g(n)\ge(\log n)^{1+c}$ for every $c<1/68$, an
explicit form of
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0787/claims/2018_04_10_sanders|Sanders's]]
bound, which
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0787/claims/2026_09_25_zhi|Zhi's pending claim]]
extends to every $c<1/36$ by refining this argument. Not covered: the order
of growth of $g(n)$.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0787/claims/2018_04_10_sanders|Sanders's theorem]],
whose deduction of the bound from Proposition 2.7 the paper follows, and
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0787/claims/1971_12_01_choi|Choi's 1971 paper]]
for the reduction from real sets to integer sets (Beker's footnote 1).

**Standing.** Claimed. The arXiv v2 of 2 October 2026 (24 pages) is the
final version, which its arXiv comment says incorporates the referee's
comments, corrects an error in the proof of Lemma 2.2 of v1, and is to
appear in International Mathematics Research Notices; its acknowledgments
thank the anonymous referee for pointing out the error. In v2 the lemma is
Lemma 2.3, after a new Remark 2.2. The error is in the last case of the proof,
where the centered part of an edge function has a large grid norm. That case
ends in one of two outcomes: a large grid norm of the edge function itself,
which v1 handles correctly, or a bound on the row degrees, where the error
lies. The v1 proof read that bound as an $L^2$ bound and so stated the degree
alternative (ii) at the moment $p\le8mL(\alpha)$, where $m$ is the number of
edges and $L(\alpha)=\log(2/\alpha)$; the bound holds only at the larger moment
$p'=2\lceil p/\eta^2\rceil$ of that last step, and v2 states (ii) with
$p\le8\tilde\delta^{-1}mL(\alpha)$, $\tilde\delta$ being the lemma's increment
parameter. In Theorem 2.1 of v2, with $\varepsilon$ the counting discrepancy,
the low-degree alternative (ii) therefore has the weaker density bound
$\exp(-O(\varepsilon^{-2}m^3L(\varepsilon m^{-1})L(\alpha)))$ in place of
$\exp(-O(mL(\varepsilon m^{-1})L(\alpha)))$, and a new alternative (iii), a
set of rows of high degree, takes the case that v1 folded into the rectangle
alternative (i). The proof of Proposition 3.2 handles (iii) with Corollary
3.5 at an added rank of $O(k^{28}(\log k)^4L(\alpha)^8)$, below the
$O(k^{46}L(\alpha)^{11})$ of the rectangle case. Proposition 3.2, Theorem 3.1
(the bound $\exp(-O(k^{68}L(\alpha)^{16}))$), Theorem 1.1, Proposition 4.1 and
Theorem 1.2 keep their v1 statements and bounds, so the correction changes
nothing this page records. No journal record existed on 2026-10-07, a Crossref
title query on 2026-10-13 found none, and the arXiv page of v2 carries no
journal reference, so `refereed` is not listed until the publication appears.
The site's curator, Thomas F. Bloom, credits the bound to Beker in the problem
page's commentary (page last edited 23 January 2026, read 2026-10-13); the
site's label was OPEN, so the problem was not marked settled there, and the
credit is not `reviewed`. The statements are quoted from v1 and read the same on
p. 3 of v2. Neither proof is checked in this corpus, and the comparison of the
two versions' Section 2 and proof of Proposition 3.2 is a reading, not a check.
