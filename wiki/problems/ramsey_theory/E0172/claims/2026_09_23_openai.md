***

**Claim.** The preprint *Monochromatic finite sums and products in the
positive integers* of the OpenAI mathematics release (dated 23 September
2026, authored by OpenAI) states as its Theorem 1.1 that for all integers
$r,m\ge1$ and reals $R\ge2$, $D\ge1$, every coloring
$\chi:\mathbb N\to[r]$ admits distinct positive integers $a_1<\cdots<a_m$
and a color $c$ with

$$
\chi\Bigl(\sum_{j\in J}a_j\Bigr)=\chi\Bigl(\prod_{j\in J}a_j\Bigr)=c
\qquad\text{for every nonempty }J\subseteq[m],
$$

and moreover $a_1>R$ and $a_d>R\bigl(\sum_{k<d}a_k+\prod_{k<d}a_k\bigr)^D$
for $2\le d\le m$. Its Corollary 1.2 draws from the separation that the
$2^m-1$ subset sums and the $2^m-1$ subset products are each distinct and
meet exactly in the $m$ singletons. The theorem is the statement of
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0172/_index|Problem 172]] for every size $m$ and
every finite coloring, in the reading the problem page fixes (Hindman's
conjecture; Alweiss's Conjecture 1.1): all sums and products of distinct
elements of an $m$-element set in one color. The manuscript is carded at
[[../library/ramsey_theory/openai_2026_monochromatic_finite_sums_products_positive_integers/_index|openai_2026_monochromatic_finite_sums_products_positive_integers]],
with its main statements paged there. It names Hindman's finite sums and
products conjecture as what it proves, notes that the statement for colorings of
$\mathbb N_0$ follows by restriction, and distinguishes it from the false
infinite version, Hindman's 1980 coloring recorded on the problem page. Its
history section cites the partial results the problem page records (Graham and
Hindman's computer-assisted $\{x,y,x+y,xy\}$ for two colors, Bowen, Moreira,
Alweiss, Bowen and Sabok, and Alweiss's theorem over $\mathbb Q$) and says that
the passage from $\mathbb Q$ to $\mathbb N$ needs more than clearing
denominators, since a dilation scales sums and $j$-fold products by different
powers. The release's README says its manuscripts were produced by an unreleased
internal OpenAI model and come at different stages of verification, not all with
Lean formalizations.

**Method and read depth.** By the introduction, the proof selects ordered
blocks of widely separated variables whose block products already have one
color by a Ramsey argument and the finite sums theorem, and then arranges
that their sums take the same color through two principles: a Prediction
Principle, which replaces the color indicators in the relevant counts by
piecewise nilsequences while keeping the multiplicative color masks, and an
Alignment Principle, which chooses rational scale vectors from a finite
list so that positive predictions survive the required additive shifts,
with a success probability independent of the model complexity through
polynomial recurrence in a nilpotent group. The inputs named are the
inverse theorem of Green, Tao and Ziegler, the concatenation theorem of Tao
and Ziegler, quantitative polynomial equidistribution and nilpotent
polynomial recurrence; the manuscript says all parameters are qualitative
and claims no numerical bound for the smallest configuration. This page
rests on the abstract, the introduction and the statements of Theorem 1.1
and Corollary 1.2; no proof is checked, and nothing here is review.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the manuscript's own argument carries
the claim.

**Formalization.** None of the theorem in the release; a third-party Lean
proof, described below, has not been built here. The release's Lean tree at
the pinned revision has no comparator challenge and no catalog entry for this
manuscript. It holds a development
[`OAI/Combinatorics/SumProduct/Alignment/`](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/OAI/Combinatorics/SumProduct/Alignment)
of 259 files without `sorry`, imported by the project's root module
through its `Main.lean`, which only aggregates imports. Its top
declaration, `SourceRawMenu.alignment` in `RawMenu.lean` (re-exported as
`raw_alignment_reference`), states and proves the manuscript's Principle
2.4 (Alignment): for all parameters there are a finite list of scales, a
finite list of positive rationals and a $\delta>0$ such that, for every
admissible family and every system of models, the lower limit of the
probability of the alignment event is at least $\delta$, independent of
the tolerance and of the model complexity. On the way, `WordPlan01.lean`
proves `coloring_polynomial_recurrence` and `coloring_recurrence`,
polynomial recurrence for colorings of nilpotent groups, with supporting
lemmas on rational lattices, Malcev coordinates, polynomial Weyl sums and
cube faces. The development does not state Theorem 1.1, the Prediction
Principle (2.3) or the deduction of the theorem from the two principles
(Section 2.5), so it is a formalization of one ingredient and not of the
claim; nothing was built or audited here.

The statement is proved in Lean outside the release, in Yao Xu's repository
[`hindman-sums-products-lean4`](https://github.com/cadamcat/hindman-sums-products-lean4/tree/3064cac5f9133a968482886ab1c7a1d69c16207f)
(account cadamcat; tag v1.0.0 of 7 October 2026, linked here at its commit of
10 October 2026). Its README names Yao Xu as author and credits the theorem to
this preprint, and says the work was developed with AI assistance (Claude Code
and Codex). Its theorem `HindmanSumsProducts.hindman_finite_sums_products`, in
[`HindmanSumsProducts/Main.lean`](https://github.com/cadamcat/hindman-sums-products-lean4/blob/3064cac5f9133a968482886ab1c7a1d69c16207f/HindmanSumsProducts/Main.lean),
states that for every $r$, every $r$-coloring $\chi$ of $\mathbb N$ and every
$m$ there are a set of exactly $m$ positive integers and a color $c$ such that
every nonempty subset has its sum and its product colored $c$: the problem's
statement in the reading the problem page fixes, without the separation clause
of Theorem 1.1 or Corollary 1.2. Its `audit/Erdos172.lean`, the second
`formalization` link, derives from it the right-hand side of the
formal-conjectures statement `Erdos172.erdos_172`, copied from that catalog.
The proof follows the manuscript's deduction from its two principles, takes the
Alignment Principle and a cyclic inverse theorem from the release's Lean
library at the pinned revision, and replaces the Tao–Ziegler concatenation
theorem by the box-norm concatenation of Kravitz, Kuca and Leng
(arXiv:2407.08636, Section 6); it builds on Lean v4.34.1, with the release's
compatibility patches applied to the library's pinned packages. The
repository's
[verification record](https://github.com/cadamcat/hindman-sums-products-lean4/blob/3064cac5f9133a968482886ab1c7a1d69c16207f/docs/verification.md)
reports, for the tag v1.0.0, a fresh build on 7–8 October 2026 with no `sorry`,
the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` only, a replay in a
fresh Lean kernel, and a Comparator check of the theorem against the
repository's challenge statement; and, on 10 October 2026, a second Comparator
run in which the independent checker nanoda also accepted the proof. Both
Comparator runs used the tool's development stand-in for its sandbox. Between
the tag and the linked commit the proof modules changed only in comments, a
comparison made here from the repository's history; the audit file was added
after those checks. On 11 October 2026 formal-conjectures (the `record` link)
moved `erdos_172` from `research open` to `research solved`, set
`answer(True)` and linked the audit file as its formal proof, keeping its own
proof `sorry`; the catalog links a formal proof and does not referee it. This
corpus has not built the development, printed its axioms or audited its
definitions, so the formalization is a link, not a warrant.
