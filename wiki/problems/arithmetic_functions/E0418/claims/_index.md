# problems/arithmetic_functions/E0418/claims

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0418/_index|..]]

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0418/claims/1994_04_11_browkin_schinzel|1994_04_11_browkin_schinzel]]: Browkin and Schinzel prove that no number $2^k\cdot509203$ with $k\ge1$ is of
the form $n-\phi(n)$, so infinitely many positive integers are not of that
form. The refereed paper appeared in Colloquium Mathematicum, and Guy records
the result.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0418/claims/1999_07_02_flammenkamp_luca|1999_07_02_flammenkamp_luca]]: In a refereed paper, Flammenkamp and Luca give a sufficient condition on $k$
for every $2^mk$ with $m\ge1$ to be a noncototient, and find seven such $k$ by
computation. So infinitely many integers are not of the form $n-\phi(n)$.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0418/claims/2004_09_14_banks_luca|2004_09_14_banks_luca]]: Banks and Luca prove that $2p$ is a noncototient for almost all primes $p$, so
at least $(1+o(1))x/(2\log x)$ integers up to $x$ are not of the form
$n-\phi(n)$. The theorem is in the arXiv preprint only, since the journal
version omits it.
