# problems/unit_fractions/E0291/claims

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0291/_index|..]]

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0291/claims/2016_07_11_shiu|2016_07_11_shiu]]: Shiu's 2016 preprint proves that the reduced denominator of the $n$-th
harmonic number is smaller than $\mathrm{lcm}(1,\ldots,n)$ for infinitely many
$n$, with the exact prime-by-prime criterion. This answers the second question
yes.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0291/claims/2022_01_26_wu_yan|2022_01_26_wu_yan]]: Wu and Yan's 2022 paper proves that $(a_n,L_n)>1$ on a set of upper density
$1$, assuming that the numbers $1/\log q$ over distinct primes $q$ are
linearly independent over $\mathbb Q$. The hypothesis is open, so the page
derives nothing.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0291/claims/2024_11_07_steinerberger|2024_11_07_steinerberger]]: Steinerberger's observation, recorded in the site's commentary, that $3$
divides $(a_n,L_n)$ whenever the leading digit of $n$ in base $3$ is $2$. It
answers yes to the second question of Problem 291, whether $(a_n,L_n)>1$
infinitely often.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0291/claims/2026_02_05_van_doorn|2026_02_05_van_doorn]]: Van Doorn's note of February 2026 proves that $\gcd(X_n,L_n)>1$ infinitely
often, where $X_n/L_n=\sum_{i\le n}r_i/i$, $L_n=\mathrm{lcm}(1,\ldots,n)$ and
$(r_i)$ is any periodic sequence of non-zero numerators. It has a Lean proof
by Aristotle and covers the second question.
