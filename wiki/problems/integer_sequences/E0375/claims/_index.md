# problems/integer_sequences/E0375/claims

[[problems/integer_sequences/E0375/_index|..]]

[[problems/integer_sequences/E0375/claims/1969_12_01_grimm|1969_12_01_grimm]]: In a refereed 1969 paper, Grimm proves that $n+1,\ldots,n+g$ have distinct
prime divisors $p_i\mid n+i$ for $g=[\log n/(2\log\log n)]$ and all large $n$.
This settles the question for every run of composites of that length.

[[problems/integer_sequences/E0375/claims/1975_03_01_ramachandra_shorey_tijdeman|1975_03_01_ramachandra_shorey_tijdeman]]: In a refereed 1975 paper, Ramachandra, Shorey and Tijdeman prove that
$n+1,\ldots,n+g$ have distinct prime divisors $p_i\mid n+i$ for
$g=[\alpha_3(\log n/\log\log n)^3]$ and all $n\ge3$. This settles every run of
composites of that length.

[[problems/integer_sequences/E0375/claims/2006_06_01_laishram_shorey|2006_06_01_laishram_shorey]]: In a refereed 2006 paper, Laishram and Shorey prove that the distinct primes
exist for every run of composites $n+1,\ldots,n+k$ with $n\le19236701629$ and
any $k$. The proof reduces the question to the prime gaps and then uses a
computation.
