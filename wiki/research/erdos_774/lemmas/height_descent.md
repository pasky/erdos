# Short-relation rank and a genuine height-descent template

***

## Standing

These elementary lemmas refine the remaining
[bounded-height focusing problem](../routes/bounded_height_flattening.md).
They distinguish algebraic descent from the missing color matching. The
six-column template below is not asserted to embed in the prime grids or
occur monochromatically in an arbitrary coloring.

The [actual prime-grid obstruction](prime_pivot_obstruction.md) shows that
arbitrarily large connected full short-relation rank, with saturated image,
can still leave only full fibers as minimal ternary supports in the next
simplex extension. Its explicit nullity density is $1/20$; the common-pivot
mechanism has an absolute half-density ceiling. A quantitative high-density
descent hypothesis was left open there. The
[saturated staircase](prime_staircase_obstruction.md) excludes that
repair as well: its full integral height-two circuit rank has density
tending to one, with mandatory coordinates still preventing fiber-free
descent. The exact algebraic template below remains valid, but requires
the stated arrangement and actual-color matching.

## 1. Counting the span of short relations

Let $C\subset\mathbb Z^d$ have $s$ columns, each of norm at most
$P$. Let $W_h(C)$ be the rational span of its integer relation
vectors with coefficient magnitudes at most $h$, and let $r=\dim W_h(C)$.
Then

$$
r\geq s-\frac{d\log(1+hPs/d)}{\log(h+1)}. \tag{1}
$$

Choose $r$ coordinate labels whose coordinate projection is injective
on $W_h(C)$, and delete those labels. The remaining set $C'$ is
$h$-degree independent: any short relation supported there belongs to
$W_h(C)$ and has zero chosen coordinates. Its $(h+1)^{s-r}$
coefficient sums in $\{0,\ldots,h\}$ are distinct. Coordinate-box
counting gives
$(h+1)^{s-r}\leq(1+hP(s-r)/d)^d\leq(1+hPs/d)^d$, proving (1).
This concerns the span of **short integer relations**, not all rational
dependencies.

## 2. Large connected short-relation rank does not itself descend

For $H=h+1$ and $n\geq2$, the scalar set
$C_n=\{1,H,\ldots,H^{n-1}\}$ is $h$-degree independent, by
successive reduction modulo $H$. Its height-$H$ relations
$He_i-e_{i+1}$ form a connected system and span its full
$(n-1)$-dimensional rational kernel.

Nevertheless, any nonzero $u\in\Sigma_h(C_n)$ has a mandatory
coordinate in every such bounded representation. If $H^j$ is the largest
power of $H$ dividing $u$, reduction modulo $H$ forces all
coefficients below $j$ to vanish and the coefficient at $j$ to be
nonzero. The argument uses divisibility by powers of $H$, whether or not
$H$ is prime.

In a height-at-most-$h$ relation on $C_n\otimes S_p$, all layer
sums equal one $u$. If $u=0$, base independence makes the relation
trivial; otherwise every layer uses that mandatory coordinate. Thus every
such relation contains a full $p$-fiber. This rules out a height-descent
argument from connected short-relation rank alone. These scalar examples are
not prime-grid subconfigurations.

## 3. Two arranged directions do descend one coefficient-height step

Fix $h\geq1$, $H=h+1$. In $\mathbb Z^4$, take

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_1=e_1,\quad c_2=e_2,\quad c_3=e_3,\quad c_6=e_4,\\
c_4=-c_1-Hc_2-hc_3+c_6,\qquad
c_5=-Hc_1-c_2+hc_3+c_6.
\end{aligned}
$$

Their integral kernel is generated by

$$
d_1=(H,1,-h,0,1,-1),\qquad
d_2=(1,H,h,1,0,-1).
$$

Indeed $c_1,c_2,c_3,c_6$ are a basis; the coefficients on $c_5,c_4$
read off the integer coefficients $\lambda,\mu$ of any kernel vector
$\lambda d_1+\mu d_2$. This proves saturation as well.
There is no nonzero kernel vector of height at most $h$. If just one
parameter is nonzero, a first or second coordinate has magnitude at least
$H$; the same is true if both have the same sign. If they have opposite
signs, the third coordinate has magnitude
$h(|\lambda|+|\mu|)\geq2h\geq H$.

On the other hand, the three coefficient vectors

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_1&=(1,1,0,1,1,0),\\
v_2&=(-h,0,h,1,0,1),\\
v_3&=(0,-h,-h,0,1,1)
\end{aligned}
$$

have height at most $h$, have empty total support intersection, and
all represent
$u=-Hc_1-Hc_2+2c_6\ne0$. Use each type at least once as the layers
of $C_h\otimes S_p$, repeating types for $p>3$. The layer sums
all equal $u$, so they form a nonzero height-at-most-$h$ relation,
but no full $p$-fiber lies in its support. At $h=1$ this is an
actual ternary relation over a dissociated base.

This is a positive algebraic template for $H\to H-1$ descent. Its
required arrangement is stronger than having two independent short relations.
The [one-line obstruction](../routes/bounded_height_flattening.md) explains
why a single short-relation direction cannot supply such a fiber-free step.

## 4. Color matching cannot be omitted

For any $h$-degree-independent base $C$, color one whole layer of
$C\otimes S_p$ blue and all other layers red. Each color omits a
simplex direction, and each constituent base layer is $h$-degree
independent, so both colors are $h$-degree independent. A selected old
layer can retain its original color.

Thus an arbitrary-color descent cannot be forced by working solely inside
one previously monochromatic independent support and leaving its new layer
colors unrestricted. It must couple actual old color classes, force the
specific matching of representation supports, or supply a monochromatic
determinant support as in the main bounded-height route. None of those
forcing statements is proved here.
