Theorem 1.1 includes every fixed integer k>=2, t>=1 and n>=1, with
M=t^2*k^(2^t) and c_{k,t}^(-1)=k^(2^t+1)*t^t*(4M^2)^(t+1).
Its two-color threshold is n>=2^((t+2)^2*2^t). Theorem 1.2 includes
the strict hypothesis 2^d>n*d+2 for integer d>=1, its binomial upper
bound and the stated explicit choice of d for n>=2. Corollary 1.3
includes its explicit lower exponent, strict all-positive-n upper
inequality, exponent comparison, and fixed-color extension. The examination covers every
small case and quantifier needed by those exact conclusions.

Sections 2--5 supply the complete internal permutation/cube, box,
supersaturation, slice, averaging, gluing, union-bound and consequence
arguments. Their proofs are internal, not accepted external lemmas.
The original figures named by the excerpts are supplied as text; visual
rendering or TeX execution is not required. This is not an original-PDF
fidelity claim. Bibliography and source references do not expand the read set.

The one named external theorem interface is Sauer--Shelah:
for a family A of subsets of an n-element set with VC dimension at most
an integer q>=0, |A|<=sum_{j=0}^q binom(n,j), with binom(n,j)=0 when
j>n. VC dimension uses shattering by intersections with the test set.
The application takes q=d-1. The exact statement appears as Lemma
5.1(c) in upper_proof.tex.txt and the original Sauer and Shelah citations
are in source_references.tex.txt. Its classical proof is an explicit
external premise; no original Sauer or Shelah paper is supplied and no
independent proof of that theorem is claimed. This reliance and
reading limit are stated in the report. Gowers is credited by the source for the
box method; the needed box inequality is proved in the supplied text.
Routine finite counting, Cauchy--Schwarz, convexity/Jensen and elementary
logarithmic limits retain their stated roles.
