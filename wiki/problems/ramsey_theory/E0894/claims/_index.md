# problems/ramsey_theory/E0894/claims

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0894/_index|..]]

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0894/claims/2001_04_01_katznelson|2001_04_01_katznelson]]: Katznelson's refereed 2001 theorem in Combinatorica states that the Cayley
graph on the integers whose edges are the differences in a lacunary sequence
has finite chromatic number. It is the original answer to Erdős's 1987
question.

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0894/claims/2007_06_01_peres_schlag|2007_06_01_peres_schlag]]: In a refereed 2010 paper in Bull. LMS, Peres and Schlag show that a lacunary
sequence of ratio at least $1+\epsilon$ admits a coloring of the integers with
order $(1/\epsilon)\log(1/\epsilon)$ colors. This is sharp up to the
logarithm.
