## Mathematical checks of the examination

### Construction, intersection, and the endpoint

Put $n=r-1\geq1$. The frozen definition takes an old set $S$ of size $2n$,
one new vertex $x_P$ for each unordered partition into two $n$-sets, all
$(n+1)$-subsets of $S$ as edges, and both halves of each partition with their
common new vertex. This directly gives finite vertex and edge sets. A disjoint
tagged copy of the finite partition set supplies the new labels without a
separate existence premise.

I checked the report's attempt to separate two edges against this exact
definition. Two old edges intersect in at least $2(n+1)-2n=2$ points. An old
edge meets any old half in at least $(n+1)+n-2n=1$ point. The two edges from
one partition share their new vertex. For distinct partitions, the only
possible intersection is between their old halves $A,B$. Disjointness would
give $B\subseteq S\setminus A$ with both sets of size $n$, hence equality;
then their unordered partition indices coincide. This contradiction rules out
the last possible disjoint pair. The report has not substituted a common
vertex for pairwise intersection or strengthened the required intersection
size.

For an old point, the $2n-1$ other old points contain the $n$ needed to form
an old edge through it. Every new vertex belongs to its two edges. Thus the
counted vertex set is exactly the union of the edges. No repeated-edge
convention is required: old edges contain no new label, different partition
indices carry different labels, and a partition's two nonempty halves differ.

At $r=2$, the three edges are $\{a,b\}$, $\{a,x\}$, and $\{b,x\}$.
This is exactly the report's triangle, with count three. The assertion about
two distinct old edges is vacuous at this order; it does not incorrectly
assert that every pair in the triangle meets in two vertices. The report
explicitly keeps this endpoint diagnostic separate from its universal proof.

### The strongest colouring attack

The report's strongest attack gives a putative two-colouring all possible
freedom on the new vertices after colouring $S$. An unbalanced old colouring
has at least $n+1$ points of one colour, producing a monochromatic old edge.
The only remaining case has colour classes $R,B$ of size $n$ each. Because
every unordered partition is indexed, $P_0=\{R,B\}$ is present. Its one new
vertex has no legal colour: red violates $R\cup\{x_{P_0}\}$ and blue
violates $B\cup\{x_{P_0}\}$.

I also checked the report's stronger diagnostic about the other new vertices.
For $Q\ne P_0$, a monochromatic old half of size $n$ would equal $R$ or
$B$ and force $Q=P_0$. Thus both halves are mixed, and either colour at
$x_Q$ is harmless. At $n=1$ there is no such $Q$. This diagnostic is true
and shows precisely why independently choosing all other new colours cannot
remove the obstruction. The attack could have found a defect if one partition
were absent, the two halves used different labels, or an edge were omitted;
the frozen definition excludes each of these possibilities.

The upper colouring bound is checked separately. Give two old $n$-sets
colours one and two, and every new vertex colour three. No old edge fits inside
one old colour class. Every new edge contains colour three and at least one
old point of another colour. This works for every partition edge and uses
$n\geq1$, so no exception at $r=2$ is hidden. Excluding two colours and
exhibiting three proves the exact value; mere non-two-colourability alone
would not have sufficed.

### Count, growth, and target consequences

I checked the counting map in the report, rather than counting ordered
descriptions as vertices. Each $n$-subset maps to its unordered complementary
pair, and each pair has precisely its two distinct halves as preimages. Its
fibres have size two because the halves are disjoint and nonempty. The new
labels are disjoint from $S$ and all incident, so their number adds directly
to $2n$. This yields the asserted $N_r$.

For growth, the consecutive binomial coefficient ratio
$(2n-k)/(k+1)$ proves the central coefficient is largest. Counting subsets by
size gives the row sum $4^n$. These justify the report's bound in the claimed
ratio units:

$$
\frac{N_r}{r^2}\geq b_r
 :=\frac{4^{r-1}}{2(2r-1)r^2}>0,
\qquad
\frac{b_{r+1}}{b_r}
 =4\frac{2r-1}{2r+1}\left(\frac r{r+1}\right)^2.
$$

The arithmetic in the report is exact. For $r\geq5$, the unsquared fractions
are at least $9/11$ and $5/6$, so the quotient is at least
$4(9/11)(5/6)^2=25/11>2$. Also
$b_5=256/450=128/225$, $b_4=64/224=2/7$, and
$b_5/b_4=448/225<2$. Thus the report correctly rejects an all-order doubling
reading while proving the eventual assertion actually present in the frozen
argument. Iterating the bound gives
$b_{5+k}\geq(128/225)2^k$ for every integer $k\geq0$. Its divergence uses
only the elementary growth of $2^k$, for instance $2^k\geq k+1$ by finite
induction. No asymptotic formula for binomial coefficients is assumed.

The consequence is checked with its own quantifiers. For arbitrary $C>0$ and
$r_0$, choose $k$ so that $5+k\geq r_0$ and $(128/225)2^k>C$. Then the
same admissible graph $G_{5+k}$ has more than $C(5+k)^2$ vertices. The
constant being contradicted must be uniform in both $r$ and the hypergraph.
The report states that requirement explicitly. This is stronger than merely
exceeding one proposed constant and does not infer an extremal formula.

The first formulation's leading phrase specifies exact chromatic number three,
while its parenthetical gloss states only three-colourability. The report
tests both readings and also the modified formulation's incidence requirement.
The witnesses satisfy the stricter combination of conditions. Allowing isolated
vertices or further hypergraphs in the original formulation does not remove
these witnesses. The implication therefore survives every supplied reading,
without choosing the weaker gloss to avoid an unproved hypothesis.
