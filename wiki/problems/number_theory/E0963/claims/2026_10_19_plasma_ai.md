***

**Claim.** A set $B$ of real numbers is dissociated if distinct finite
subsets of $B$ have distinct sums, the empty subset with sum $0$ included;
for a finite set $X\subset\mathbb R$ let $d(X)$ be the largest size of a
dissociated subset of $X$, and let $f(n)$ be as in the problem.

First, every nonempty finite set $A\subset\mathbb R$ with $n$ elements
contains a dissociated subset of size at least
$\log_2n-\log_2(\log_2n+1)$, so

$$
f(n)\ge\log_2n-\log_2(\log_2n+1)\qquad(n\ge1).
$$

Second, every finite set $A\subset\mathbb R$ with $n=|A|\ge5$ satisfies

$$
d(A)\ge\log_2(n-1)-\log_2\bigl(\lceil\log_2\log_2n\rceil+2+\sqrt2\bigr)-\log_26,
$$

and consequently $f(n)\ge\log_2n-\log_2\log_2\log_2n-4$ for every
$n\ge2^{16}$.

Third, a nonempty finite set $P$ of $n$ positive reals with
$\max P\le\frac43\min P$ has $n\le2^{d(P)}-1$; in particular
$d(P)\ge\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor$ for such sets.

**Covers.** The open-ended estimate of $f(n)$ asked by
[[problems/number_theory/E0963/_index|Problem 963]]: the lower bound rises
from Erdős's greedy bound $\lfloor\log_3n\rfloor$ to
$\log_2n-O(\log\log n)$ for every $n$, and to
$\log_2n-\log_2\log_2\log_2n-4$ for $n\ge2^{16}$. The definite question,
whether $f(n)\ge\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor$ for every $n$, stays open: the first
bound lies strictly below $\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor$ for every $n\ge2$, the
second does not decide it, and the third gives the asked bound only for
positive sets whose largest element is at most $4/3$ times the smallest.
No upper bound on $f(n)$ and no sharpness of these bounds is claimed.
