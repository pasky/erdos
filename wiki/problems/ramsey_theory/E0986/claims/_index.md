# problems/ramsey_theory/E0986/claims

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0986/_index|..]]

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0986/claims/1977_01_01_spencer|1977_01_01_spencer]]: Spencer's Theorem 2.1, in a refereed 1977 paper in Discrete Mathematics,
proves $R(3,t)\ge(1/27-o(1))(t/\ln t)^2$ by the local lemma. This is the case
$s=3$ of the problem, with $c(3)=2$.

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0986/claims/2023_06_06_mattheus_verstraete|2023_06_06_mattheus_verstraete]]: Theorem 1 of Mattheus and Verstraete, in their refereed 2024 paper in the
Annals, shows that $r(4,t)$ is at least a constant times $t^3/(\log t)^4$.
This is the case $s=4$ of the problem, with $c(4)=4$.

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0986/claims/2026_06_16_bradac|2026_06_16_bradac]]: Bradač's Theorem 1.1 gives $r(s,k)\ge c_s k^{s-1}/(\log k)^{2s-4}$ for every
fixed $s\ge3$, which answers the problem with $c(s)=2s-4$. It is an arXiv
preprint, accepted by the erdosproblems.com curator and not refereed.

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0986/claims/2026_09_24_openai|2026_09_24_openai]]: Two unreviewed manuscripts of the OpenAI mathematics release prove
$r(s,t)=t^{s-1}/(\log t)^{s-2+o(1)}$ for $s=5$ and for every fixed $s\ge6$.
The claim is accepted as a partial answer on the two Lean declarations that
the corpus's verification built and audited.
