# Karagiannis: a combinatorial infinite Hales–Jewett proof

***

[Held copy and library card](../../../../library/ramsey_theory/karagiannis_2013_combinatorial_proof_infinite_version_hales_jewett_theorem/_index.md).

Nikolaos Karagiannis, "A combinatorial proof of an infinite version of the
Hales--Jewett theorem," *Journal of Combinatorics* 4 (2013), no. 2,
273--291.

Karagiannis gives a combinatorial proof, resting on the classical
Hales–Jewett theorem, of the infinite extension due to Carlson and,
independently, Furstenberg and Katznelson (Theorem 2, p. 274), and of its
version for an increasing sequence of finite alphabets (Theorem 3).  The
source is useful when an attempted E0774 construction organizes elements as
words and wants to force a uniform combinatorial line or a nested sequence of
variable patterns.

As with the other general Ramsey sources, the theorem controls colorings but
not dissociated density.  A proposed application should explicitly translate a
combinatorial line into a signed additive relation and separately prove that
every finite vertex set still contains a fixed positive fraction avoiding all
such relations.
