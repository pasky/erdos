# problems/diophantine_problems/E0365/claims

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0365/_index|..]]

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0365/claims/1970_10_01_golomb|1970_10_01_golomb]]: Golomb's observation, refereed in the American Mathematical Monthly, that
$12167=23^3$ and $12168=2^3\cdot3^2\cdot13^2$ are consecutive powerful
numbers, neither of them a square. This answers the first question no.

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0365/claims/1976_04_01_walker|1976_04_01_walker]]: Walker's 1976 theorem, refereed in the Fibonacci Quarterly, that the equation
$7^3x^2=3^3y^2+1$ has infinitely many solutions, each giving two consecutive
powerful numbers, neither of them a square.
