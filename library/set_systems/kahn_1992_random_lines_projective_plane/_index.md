# On a problem of Erdős and Lovász: random lines in a projective plane

[[set_systems/_index|..]]

***

Jeff Kahn, *On a problem of Erdős and Lovász: random lines in a projective
plane*, Combinatorica **12** (1992), no. 4, 417–423,
[DOI 10.1007/BF01305234](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01305234).

The edition read is the publisher's PDF of the journal article, seven pages
with the journal pagination 417–423, obtained from the publisher's site for
the DOI on 2026-10-16.

Let $n(k)$ be the least size of an intersecting family of $k$-sets whose cover
number is $k$. Erdős and Lovász showed $n(k) \ge 8k/3 - 3$ and, when a
projective plane of order $k-1$ exists, $n(k) \le 4k^{3/2} \log k$, and they
conjectured $n(k) = O(k \log k)$. The paper proves the conjecture: there is a
constant $A$ such that a uniformly random set of at least $A k \log k$ lines of
a projective plane of order $k-1$ has cover number $k$ with probability tending
to $1$ as $k \to \infty$, and Theorem 1 states this with the constant $22$.
Hence $n(k) = O(k \log k)$ whenever such a plane exists.

**Bears on.** The theorem concerns random sets of lines in a finite projective
plane and the point sets that meet all of them, the setting of
[[../wiki/problems/set_systems/E1159/_index|Problem 1159's question on point sets meeting every line in between $1$ and $C$ points]].
