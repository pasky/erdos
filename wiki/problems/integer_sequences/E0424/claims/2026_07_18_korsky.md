***

**Claim.** Samuel Korsky's proof claim of 18 July 2026, which the site lists as
a partial proof claim of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0424/_index|Problem 424]], claims a proof that
the set of integers generated from $2$ and $3$ by appending $a_ia_j-1$ for all
$i$ and $j$, so that $a_i^2-1$ is appended too, has positive lower density.
The argument composes the affine maps $x\mapsto mx-1$ for the multipliers
$2,3,5,8,9$, early members of that sequence, and uses a finite interval
partition with transition probabilities to show that distinct compositions with
a common slope land on distinct integers often enough. The claimant notes that
this variant is the wording of Green's open problem list, while Erdős's own
statement and the site require distinct factors. The proof is an external
document linked from the tab.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Samuel
Korsky (account SamKorsky) on 18 July 2026, giving "GPT 5.6-Pro" as the AI used:

> I believe I am able to prove (with help from GPT-5.6 Pro) the positive lower
> density claim for a more favorable sequence (namely, removing the $i \ne j$
> restriction). The general idea is to produce many distinct affine maps with a
> common slope (using initial elements of the sequence $2, 3, 5, 8, 9$) and use
> a finite interval partition with transition probabilities to distinguish the
> resulting maps. Comments are welcome! Notes: Some remarks with regards to the
> literature: [Er77c] states the problem exactly as written here, while the
> wording in Green's open problem list allows for a1 = a2 (the case studied
> here). However, given that Green cites this page and Erdos directly, I assume
> this difference was unintentional. Additionally, I spent some time prompting
> GPT 5.6-Pro about developing a similar argument for the exact problem here,
> but was only able to GPT to claim (not human-verified) a proof that if $A$ is
> the set corresponding to the sequence in the problem, then $|A \cap [1,x]| \gg
> \frac{x}{\sqrt{\log x}}$. If there's interest I can try to verify its
> correctness and write it up.

**Why it is rejected.** It answers a variant, not the Statement.
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0424/_index|Problem 424]] appends $a_ia_j-1$ only
for $i\neq j$, as Erdős's 1977 report and the site state it. The claim treats
instead the sequence in which the two factors may coincide, whose set contains
the problem's set and is strictly larger: it contains $8=3\cdot3-1$, which the
problem's set lacks. Positive lower density of the larger set gives no lower
bound for the smaller one, so its mathematics answers the variant and does not
settle this problem; the value `proved` is its answer to that variant. The
claimant's full claim of 20 July 2026, which extends the argument to distinct
factors, is recorded on
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0424/claims/2026_07_20_korsky|its own claim page]].

**Standing.** Rejected, as answering a variant. The site lists the entry with
two comments and no review, and this corpus has not read the external
document.
