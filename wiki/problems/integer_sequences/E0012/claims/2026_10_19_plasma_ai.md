**Claim.** Let $A$ be an infinite set of positive integers such that no
$a\in A$ divides $b+c$ for distinct $b,c\in A$ with $b,c>a$. Such a set
contains neither $1$ nor $2$.

1. Let $P_2$ be the set of primes dividing at least two elements of $A$.
   Then

   $$
   \sum_{a\in A}\frac1a\le9.33\prod_{p\in P_2}\frac p{p-1},
   $$

   so $\sum_{a\in A}1/a<\infty$ whenever $\sum_{p\in P_2}1/p<\infty$. In
   particular $\sum_{a\in A}1/a\le9.33$ when the elements of $A$ are pairwise
   coprime, and $\sum_{a\in A}1/a\le9.33\prod_{p\le G}p/(p-1)$ when no prime
   above $G$ divides two elements, for instance when every two elements have
   greatest common divisor at most $G$.
2. Let $c_1=\min A$, and let $c_{k+1}$ be the least element of $A$ above
   $c_k$ that is coprime to $c_1\cdots c_k$. If this sequence is infinite,
   then

   $$
   \sum_{a\in A}\frac1a\le6+\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\log c_{k+1}}{2^k}.
   $$

3. For every $N\ge3$, every subset of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ with the same
   property has reciprocal sum at most $1440\log\Delta_N$, where
   $\Delta_N=\lceil8(\log_2N+1)\rceil$. Consequently
   $\sum_{a\in A,\,a\le N}1/a=O(\log\log N)$, and
   $\sum_{a\in A}1/(a(\log\log a)^{1+\varepsilon})<\infty$ for every
   $\varepsilon>0$.

**Covers.** The third question of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0012/_index|Problem 12]], answered yes for the
sets of items 1 and 2: pairwise coprime sets, sets whose pairwise greatest
common divisors are bounded, and more generally sets whose shared primes
have convergent reciprocal sum, together with sets whose greedy coprime
elements recur as item 2 requires. For pairwise coprime sets,
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0012/claims/2001_04_01_schoen|Schoen]] and
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0012/claims/2004_10_08_baier|Baier]] answer
the second question; item 1 answers the third in that class. Item 3 bounds
the partial reciprocal sums of every such set by $O(\log\log N)$, against
the $o(\log N)$ that density zero gives, but does not give convergence. The
third question for general sets remains open, so no part is settled; the
first two questions are not addressed.

**Credit.** The analytic large sieve is used in the form of Lemma 1 of
Schoen's paper, whose
[[../library/integer_sequences/schoen_2001_problem_erdos_sarkozy/_index|source card]]
holds the bibliography, and the Brun–Titchmarsh inequality in the form of
[[../library/primes/montgomery_vaughan_2006_multiplicative_number_theory_i/theorem_3_9|Montgomery and Vaughan's Theorem 3.9]]
with the error term removed as their notes state; both are used at their
published statements. The residue-occupancy bound, the propositions behind
items 1 and 2, and the sieve argument behind item 3 are Plasma AI's. The
pairwise coprime bound was not compared with literature beyond the sources
the lead cites.

**Standing.** Author-recorded at tier 0, with no independent review. The
lead's evidence program checks the explicit constant in the pairwise coprime
bound and finite instances of the bounds of item 1; items 2 and 3 and the
proofs of item 1 are written arguments. Catalog acceptance is pending.
