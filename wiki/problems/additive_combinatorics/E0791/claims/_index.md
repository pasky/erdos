# problems/additive_combinatorics/E0791/claims

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0791/_index|..]]

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0791/claims/1937_12_01_rohrbach|1937_12_01_rohrbach]]: Rohrbach (Math. Z. 42 (1937)) proves $g(n)<2\sqrt n$ for $n>1$ by an explicit
basis, and $n<0.4992\,k^2$ for large $k$, so that $g(n)^2>2.0032\,n$ for large
$n$ in Problem 791. The paper is refereed.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0791/claims/1976_11_01_hammerer_hofmeister|1976_11_01_hammerer_hofmeister]]: Hämmerer and Hofmeister (J. Reine Angew. Math. 1976) build $2$-bases of $k$
positive elements with range above $(10/9)(k^2/4)$, so $g(n)^2\le(18/5+o(1))n$
and the guess $g(n)\sim2n^{1/2}$ of Problem 791 is false. The paper is
refereed.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0791/claims/1979_04_01_mrose|1979_04_01_mrose]]: Mrose's 1979 construction of finite additive $2$-bases of $k$ elements with
range at least $(8/7)(k/2)^2+O(k)$, which gives $g(n)^2\le(7/2+o(1))n$ and
shows that the guess $g(n)\sim2n^{1/2}$ of Problem 791 is false. The paper is
refereed and erdosproblems.com credits it.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0791/claims/2015_11_01_yu|2015_11_01_yu]]: Yu (J. Number Theory 156 (2015)) proves $\limsup n(k)/k^2\le0.4585$ for the
maximal range $n(k)$ of a $2$-basis of size $k$, so
$g(n)^2\ge(1/0.4585-o(1))n$ in Problem 791. The paper is refereed.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0791/claims/2016_06_15_kohonen|2016_06_15_kohonen]]: Kohonen (J. Number Theory 174 (2017)) proves $\liminf n(k)/k^2\ge85/294$ for
the maximal range $n(k)$ of a $2$-basis of size $k$, so
$g(n)^2\le(294/85+o(1))n$ in Problem 791. The paper is refereed.
