**Claim.** [[problems/ramsey_theory/E0545/_index|Problem 545]] asks, for a
graph $G$ with $m=\binom n2+t$ edges ($0\le t<n$) and no isolated vertices,
whether $R(G)\le R(H)$, where $H$ is $K_n$ with a new vertex joined to $t$
of its vertices and $R$ is the diagonal two-color Ramsey number. The
comments posted to the site's thread on 28 October 2025 under the account
LouisD give the matching $mK_2$ as a counterexample for
$m\in\{3,4,5,7,8,9\}$. The matching has $R(mK_2)=3m-1$ (Cockayne and
Lorimer, 1975, as the comments cite it; the upper bound by removing a red
edge meeting a blue one and inducting, the lower bound from a set of $m-1$
vertices whose edges are all red against $2m-1$ others). Against it: for
$m=3$, $H=K_3$ and $R(3K_2)=8>6=R(K_3)$; for $m=4$ and $m=5$, $H$ is a
subgraph of $K_4-e$ and equal to it, with $R(K_4-e)=10$ from
Radziszowski's survey, below $R(4K_2)=11$ and $R(5K_2)=14$; for $m=7$,
$H=K_4^{+1}$ is $K_4$ with one pendant edge, and a written argument in the
thread gives $R(K_4^{+1})=18=R(K_4)$, below $R(7K_2)=20$; for $m=8$ and
$m=9$, $H$ is a subgraph of $K_5-e$ and equal to it, with $R(K_5-e)=22$
from the same survey, below $R(8K_2)=23$ and $R(9K_2)=26$. The same
matching fails the statement at $m=2$, the smallest case the site's
commentary lists: $m=2=\binom22+1$ gives $n=2$, $t=1$ and $H=P_3$, the
path with two edges, with $R(P_3)=3$ (two of the three edges of $K_3$
share a color and share a vertex), while $R(2K_2)=5$, the case $m=2$ of
$R(mK_2)=3m-1$ (a red triangle in $K_4$ with the three edges at the fourth
vertex blue has no two disjoint edges of one color; in any $2$-coloring of
$K_5$ one color has at least five edges, more than a star or a triangle on
five vertices can hold, so that color contains two disjoint edges). A
universal statement with a false instance is false, so the question as
the site states it, which quantifies over every $m$, has the answer no.
The comments note that the comparison holds at $m=6$
($R(6K_2)=17<18=R(K_4)$), which the site's curator confirms from Burr's
1989 table of the Ramsey numbers of graphs with at most six edges, and
that the matching gives no counterexample at $m=10$
($R(10K_2)=29<43\le R(K_5)$); the commenter asks whether the statement
holds from ten edges on.

The instance $m=2$ was posted to the same thread later the same day under
the account Adenwalla, in a comment the site marks as addressed, and a
comment of the next day under a third account adds that $m=1$ allows no
comparison; the site's acknowledgment line thanks all three accounts. The
curator's commentary credits the small-$m$ failures, $m=2$ among them, to
the account named by this page, which posted first, so the later comment
is disclosed here rather than given a page of its own. The problem page
verifies the two Ramsey numbers of the $m=2$ case without a source, an
authored check that warrants nothing here.

**Submission note.** Posted to the site's forum by LouisD on 28 October 2025:

> In my previous comment, I indicated that I couldn't find the statement of this
> problem in [Er84b]. I still don't see the exact statement, but I now see the
> following sentence (at the top of page 11) "Probably r(G) is maximal if G is
> as complete as possible." I guess this must be the intended reference. On the
> other hand, he makes no reference to Graham, so I continued my search.
>
> In
> https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~erdosproblems/erdos/newproblems/ExtremeCliques.html,
> the reference "Some partition theorems in finite graphs" by Erdős and Graham
> (1973) is given; however, in that paper I was only able to find the statement
> of the first part (on page 526), not the second part about G having
> $\binom{n}{2}+t$ edges.
>
> I noticed that in
> https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~erdosproblems/erdos/newproblems/ExtremeCliques.html,
> there is an extra condition which says $n\ge 4$. Presumably, this is because
> $R(3K_2)=8>6=R(K_3)$ (where $mK_2$ is a matching with $m$ edges).* On the
> other hand, note that $R(6K_2)=17<18=R(K_4)$.
>
> Finally I get to my point. I claim that the conjecture is also false for $n=4$
> and $t=1$. Let $K_4^{+1}$ be the graph on $5$ vertices and $7$ edges obtained
> from $K_4$ by adding a new vertex adjacent to one vertex in the $K_4$. I claim
> that $R(7K_2)=20>18=R(K_4^{+1})$. We already have $18=R(K_4)\leq R(K_4^{+1})$
> and to see that $R(K_4^{+1})\leq 18$, the argument is essentially the same as
> proving that $R(K_4)\leq 18$. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
> there exists a vertex $x$ and a set $A$ with $|A|=9$ such that every edge from
> $x$ to $A$ is red. If there is a red triangle in $A$, we are done, so suppose
> not. If there is a vertex $u\in A$ with 6 blue neighbors in $A$, we are also
> done (since we will get a blue triangle in the blue neighborhood of $u$).
> Since it isn't possible for every vertex in $A$ to have blue degree $5$ to
> $A$, we have a vertex $v\in A$ with red degree at least 4 in $A$ and we can
> again see that it must be exactly 4 (otherwise we would get a blue $K_5$). Let
> $B$ be the set of 4 blue neighbors of $v$ in $A$. Every edge in $B$ is blue,
> and thus every edge from $B$ to $A\setminus B$ is red. So finally, we must
> have that every edge in $A\setminus B$ is blue in which case we have a blue
> $K_5$ and we are done.
>
> So the conjecture certainly needs to be updated to add the condition that it
> holds provided the number of edges is large enough. How large is large enough?
> Can anyone find a counterexample with 8 edges? We have $R(8K_2)=23$ and I
> wouldn't be surprised if $R(K_4^{+2})\le 22$. However, I am out of time at the
> moment to attempt to prove this by hand as I did above for $K_4^{+1}$.
> Conceivably, this could be checked computationally?
>
> *In general, Cockyane and Lorimer "The Ramsey number for stripes" (1975)
> proved that for all positive integers $m$ and $r$, the $r$-color Ramsey number
> of $mK_2$ is $(r+1)m+(r-1)$. For $r=2$, this says $R(mK_2)=3m-1$ and in this
> case, the upper bound can be seen by a simple inductive argument (either we
> have a red edge incident to a blue edge and we can remove those three vertices
> and apply induction, or else every edge has the same color). The lower bound
> comes from taking disjoint sets $A$ and $B$ with $m-1$ and $2m-1$ vertices
> respectively, coloring all edges incident with $A$ red and all other edges
> blue.
>

**Standing.** Rejected: answers the site's wording, not the modified
Statement. The counterexamples lie at $m\le9$ and settle no instance of the
question for all sufficiently large $m$ that the
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0545/_index|problem page]] shows; the comments
themselves ask how large is large enough. The site's curator, T. F. Bloom,
wrote the failures into the problem's commentary, crediting the account by
name and stating that the statement fails for $2\le m\le5$ and
$7\le m\le9$ (page last edited 2 December 2025), added the contributors to the
page's acknowledgment line, marked the comments as addressed, and kept the
label OPEN for the large-$m$ question. The tabulated values the argument uses,
$R(mK_2)=3m-1$, $R(K_4-e)=10$ and $R(K_5-e)=22$, are classical and cited in
the thread from refereed sources; the bound $R(K_4^{+1})\le18$ rests on the
thread's written argument. There is no entry on the site's proof-claim tab.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the argument is the comments' own
comparison of tabulated Ramsey numbers.
