# problems/integer_sequences/E1062/claims

[[problems/integer_sequences/E1062/_index|..]]

[[problems/integer_sequences/E1062/claims/1977_06_01_lebensold|1977_06_01_lebensold]]: Lebensold proved in 1977 that $0.6725n\le f(n)\le0.6736n$ for all large $n$,
by decomposing $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ into divisibility chains. The result is
refereed in Studies in Applied Mathematics.

[[problems/integer_sequences/E1062/claims/2026_04_19_davis|2026_04_19_davis]]: Davis's arXiv paper of 19 April 2026, an unrefereed preprint, proves
$f(n)=cn+o(n)$ for an effectively computable constant $c$. This answers how
large $f(n)$ can be in asymptotic form and leaves irrationality open.

[[problems/integer_sequences/E1062/claims/2026_04_20_chojecki|2026_04_20_chojecki]]: A note dated 15 April 2026, generated with GPT-5.4 Pro and posted by Przemek
Chojecki on 20 April 2026, proves $f(n)=\lambda n+o(n)$ with $\lambda$
effectively computable. It is unreviewed.

[[problems/integer_sequences/E1062/claims/2026_09_21_jenw1n|2026_09_21_jenw1n]]: A Lean proof submitted to the bounty site Conjectures.io under the username
JenW1N proves that $f(n)/n$ converges to an irrational limit. It was
kernel-verified there on 21 September 2026 and certified on 23 September 2026.

[[problems/integer_sequences/E1062/claims/2026_09_23_turturean|2026_09_23_turturean]]: A thread post of 23 September 2026 by David Turturean announces an independent
manuscript that gives an extremal formula for $f(n)$ and the transcendence,
hence irrationality, of $\lim f(n)/n$. The post credits the argument to
GPT-6-Astra Pro, and the claim is unreviewed.
