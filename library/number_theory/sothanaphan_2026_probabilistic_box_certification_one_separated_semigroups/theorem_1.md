## Statement

For reals $1<x_1<\cdots<x_n$ the note considers the multiplicative semigroup
they generate and calls it one-separated when it is free and any two of its
elements differ by at least 1; $m(n)$ is the infimum of
$\max\{x_1,\dots,x_n\}$ over such generators, and the first $n$ primes give
$m(n)\le p_n$ (p. 1, following the definitions of the 28 January note).

**Theorem 1** (p. 1). For each $3\le n\le7$, $m(n)<p_n$, where $p_n$ is the
$n$-th prime.

The proof (Section 2, pp. 2--3) asserts for each $n$ generators
$1<x_1<\cdots<x_n<p_n$ of a one-separated semigroup in the box
$|\log x_i-\log a_i|\le r$ ($1\le i\le n$) with the center $a$ and radius $r$
below, by
[[number_theory/sothanaphan_2026_probabilistic_box_certification_one_separated_semigroups/theorem_2|Theorem 2]]
with the cutoff $T_0$ shown; the generators themselves are not given.

| $n$ | center $(a_1,\dots,a_n)$ | $r$, about | $T_0$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | $101/42$, $367/103$, $113/24$, as long decimals | $10^{-6}$ | 50 |
| 4 | $2.6728591026637005$, $3.733826126571687$, $4.841402115246144$, $5.999121968603969$ | $2\cdot10^{-9}$ | 50 |
| 5 | $6.919$, $7.939$, $8.959$, $9.979$, $10.999$ | $3\cdot10^{-8}$ | 50 |
| 6 | $7.899$, $8.919$, $9.939$, $10.959$, $11.979$, $12.999$ | $10^{-10}$ | 65 |
| 7 | $10.879$, $11.899$, $12.919$, $13.939$, $14.959$, $15.979$, $16.999$ | $5\cdot10^{-11}$ | 72 |

The note prints each radius as a long decimal expansion, rounded here. The
$n=3$ center is the triple of the 28 January note, the $n=4$ center comes from
Tao's comment in the erdosproblems.com thread, and the $n=5$, $6$ and $7$
centers come from searches and form arithmetic progressions, which the note
says need not be available for larger $n$ (p. 3). The note calls its $n=3$ and
$n=5$ cases a different approach from the earlier notes (p. 1).

**Source.** N. Sothanaphan, Probabilistic box-certification for one-separated
multiplicative semigroup problem, note dated 2 February 2026, 8 pp.; Theorem 1
on p. 1, the boxes and cutoffs in Section 2 (pp. 2--3). The edition read is

## Proof pointer

Section 2, pp. 2--3, with
[[number_theory/sothanaphan_2026_probabilistic_box_certification_one_separated_semigroups/theorem_2|Theorem 2]]
(p. 6). For each $n$ the supplementary program `interval_certify.py` checks
the two hypotheses of Theorem 2 on the listed box and cutoff: the
small-regime interval separation and a tail bound below 1. Theorem 2 then
gives a point $b$ of the box in $\log$ coordinates at which the semigroup
generated by $e^{b_1},\dots,e^{b_n}$ is 1-separated. The note states the
boxes and cutoffs of its runs but prints none of their output, so the
certificate is the program and its runs.
