## Exact implication

For real $x$, define

$$
h(x)=\#\{(a,b)\in\mathbb N^2:
1\le a<b<x,\ (a,b)=1,\ \sigma(a)=\sigma(b)\},
$$

where $\sigma(n)$ is the sum of the positive divisors of $n$. Let
$h_{\rm sf}(x)$ count the same pairs with both entries squarefree.

Hypothesis $\mathrm A^+$ says that for every $0<\delta<1/4$ and every
$\eta>0$, every sufficiently large real $y$ has at least $y^{1-\eta}$ primes
$p\le5y$ satisfying $P^+(p+1)\le y^\delta$. Under this hypothesis, for every
$\varepsilon>0$ and every sufficiently large real $x$,

$$
h(x)\ge h_{\rm sf}(x)>x^{2-\varepsilon}.
$$

This implication uses no weighted-dilation estimate. Such an estimate is needed
only to supply its antecedent.

## Many products and few divisor sums

It suffices to fix $0<\varepsilon<1$: a conclusion with any smaller positive
exponent loss also gives the conclusion when $\varepsilon\ge1$. Set

$$
B\ge8/\varepsilon,\qquad \eta=\varepsilon/16,\qquad
\delta=\frac1{2B},\qquad L=\log x,\qquad y=L^B.
$$

The first three parameters are fixed before $x$ grows. In particular,
$0<\delta<1/4$, $B(1-\eta)>2$ and $y^\delta=\sqrt L$. Let

$$
\mathcal P=\{p\text{ prime}:p\le5y,\ P^+(p+1)\le\sqrt L\},\qquad
M=|\mathcal P|,\qquad k=\left\lfloor\frac L{\log(5y+1)}\right\rfloor.
$$

Hypothesis $\mathrm A^+$ gives $M\ge L^{B(1-\eta)}$ for all sufficiently large
real $x$. Also

$$
k=\frac{L}{B\log L}(1+o(1)),\qquad
(5y+1)^k\le x,\qquad M\ge2k
$$

eventually. Form $\mathcal A$ from all products of $k$ distinct members of
$\mathcal P$. Unique factorization gives $N:=|\mathcal A|=\binom Mk$.
Every member is squarefree, and the strict bound

$$
a\le(5y)^k<(5y+1)^k\le x
$$

holds once $k\ge1$, including when $x$ is an integer or $(5y+1)^k=x$.
Using $\binom Mk\ge(M/k)^k$ and $\log k\le\log L$,

$$
\log N\ge k\log(M/k)
\ge \left(1-\eta-\frac1B\right)L-o(L).
$$

For $a=\prod_{p\in S}p\in\mathcal A$, squarefreeness gives
$\sigma(a)=\prod_{p\in S}(p+1)\le(5y+1)^k\le x$. Its prime divisors are all
at most $\sqrt L$. Each exponent in a $\sqrt L$-smooth integer at most $x$
is at most $L/\log2$, and there are at most $\sqrt L$ primes available.
Thus the number of possible values of $\sigma(a)$ is bounded by

$$
U=\left(1+\frac L{\log2}\right)^{\sqrt L},\qquad \log U=o(L).
$$

If $r(s)=\#\{a\in\mathcal A:\sigma(a)=s\}$, Cauchy's inequality yields
$\sum_s r(s)^2\ge N^2/U$ ordered equal-value pairs, including the diagonal.

## Removing pairs with a large common support

Put $t=\lceil\eta k\rceil$. For large $x$, $1\le t<k$. For a fixed member
$a$, choose $t$ of its primes and then the remaining $k-t$ primes of a member
$b$. This overcounts every pair sharing at least $t$ primes, so their number
is at most

$$
N\binom kt\binom M{k-t}.
$$

After division by $N^2$, the fraction is at most

$$
\binom kt
\prod_{i=0}^{t-1}\frac{k-i}{M-k+t-i}
\le 2^k\left(\frac{2k}{M}\right)^t.
$$

Since $2k/M<1$ and $t\ge\eta k$, the logarithm of $2U$ times this last bound
is at most

$$
(k+1)\log2+\log U
-\eta k\bigl(B(1-\eta)\log L-\log(2L)\bigr)
=-\eta\left(1-\eta-\frac1B\right)L+o(L).
$$

Its leading coefficient is negative. Consequently fewer than $N^2/(2U)$
ordered pairs share at least $t$ primes. At least $N^2/(2U)$ ordered
equal-value pairs share fewer than $t$ primes. In particular, these are
off-diagonal: identical members share all $k$ primes.

## Cancelling the gcd and controlling multiplicity

For a retained pair let $g=(a,b)$ and write $a=gu$, $b=gv$.
Squarefreeness of $a,b$ implies that $g,u,v$ are pairwise coprime and
squarefree. Multiplicativity therefore gives

$$
\sigma(g)\sigma(u)=\sigma(a)=\sigma(b)=\sigma(g)\sigma(v),
$$

so $\sigma(u)=\sigma(v)$. Also $(u,v)=1$, $u\ne v$, and $u,v<x$.
The common factor uses at most $t-1<\eta k$ primes, whence

$$
g<(5y+1)^{\eta k}\le x^\eta.
$$

For a fixed unordered reduced pair $\{u,v\}$ and a fixed positive integer
$g\le x^\eta$, there are at most two ordered original pairs, $(gu,gv)$ and
$(gv,gu)$. Hence the map to unordered reduced pairs has fibers of size at
most $2\lfloor x^\eta\rfloor\le2x^\eta$. Each unordered reduced pair gives
exactly one pair ordered by $u<v$ in the definition of $h_{\rm sf}$. Thus

$$
h_{\rm sf}(x)\ge\frac{N^2}{4Ux^\eta},\qquad
\log h_{\rm sf}(x)
\ge\left(2-3\eta-\frac2B\right)L-o(L).
$$

Finally,
$3\eta+2/B\le3\varepsilon/16+\varepsilon/4=7\varepsilon/16<\varepsilon$,
so the last inequality is strictly greater than $(2-\varepsilon)L$ for
all sufficiently large real $x$. This proves the stated implication.

## Quantifier interface

For each fixed $\varepsilon$, the proof calls $\mathrm A^+$ only with
$\delta=1/(2B)$ and $\eta=\varepsilon/16$, at the real argument
$y=(\log x)^B$. These constants need not be uniform as $\varepsilon$ tends
to zero. A result for infinitely many $y$ would not supply the eventual
conclusion proved here.

The argument handles real $x$ directly. Equivalently, the strict integer
range in the definition gives $h(x)=h(\lceil x\rceil)$ and the same identity
for $h_{\rm sf}$, so a natural-number version with $0<\varepsilon<1$ also
extends to real $x$.
