# Problem 114: Low-degree lemniscate extremizers

[[research/_index|..]]

[[research/erdos_114/cubic_extremizer_reduction|cubic_extremizer_reduction]]: An existential cubic reduction, a uniform ball-arithmetic family inequality,
and a deformation argument give the cubic bound and uniqueness at tier 1; the all-degree problem remains open.

[[research/erdos_114/quartic_critical_values|quartic_critical_values]]: A compact reduction for monic quartics with unit critical values, an exact
obstruction to comparison at the same constant term, a rotated comparison
and uniform estimates through collisions and flattening. Bounds on both
collision strata and a variance criterion isolate the remaining quartic
inequality, which is unproved on the whole family.

[[research/erdos_114/quartic_evidence/_index|quartic_evidence/]]: Ball-arithmetic proof that the rotated deficit integral is below -1 on every
circle and line of both quartic collision pencils, so every polynomial of the
two collision strata is shorter than z^4-1 by at least its scale.

***

The [[research/erdos_114/cubic_extremizer_reduction|argument on cubic
extremizers with critical values on the unit circle]] records its exact scope,
source premises, and remaining gap toward
[[problems/polynomials/E0114/_index|Problem 114]]. With the
[[theory/polynomials/L144_cubic_lemniscate_length/_index|family inequality of L144]]
and the deformation in Eremenko and Hayman's proof of Lemma 5, it gives a
tier-1, computer-assisted proof that $z^3-1$ is the unique cubic
maximizer up to rotation and translation; the other open degrees remain.

The [[research/erdos_114/quartic_critical_values|quartic research sketch]]
combines the compact coordinates of
[[theory/polynomials/L186_quartic_critical_value_parameters/_index|L186]]
with the explicit harmonic-line deficit of
[[theory/polynomials/L179_quartic_harmonic_deficit/_index|L179]], its
uniform small-curvature extension
[[theory/polynomials/L180_quartic_small_curvature_deficit/_index|L180]],
and the full collision-pencil bounds of
[[theory/polynomials/L181_quartic_collision_pencil_deficits/_index|L181]].
These are author-recorded results at tier 0; the collision bounds use
ball-arithmetic certificates. They cover the two collision strata and an
explicit, very small curvature range near the unicritical limit. Practical
two-dimensional neighborhoods of the collision pencils and the remaining
regular compact family still need a uniform estimate. No full quartic
inequality or quartic uniqueness theorem follows yet.
