**Claim.** There is an infinite set $A$ of positive integers in which no
element divides the sum of two distinct larger elements (property P in the
reading of [[problems/integer_sequences/E0012/_index|Problem 12]]) with
$\liminf|A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}|/N^{1/2}>0$, and for every $\varepsilon>0$
there is one with $|A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}|\ge N^{1-\varepsilon}$ for all
large $N$. The first set answers the first question yes; the second shows
that no absolute $c>0$ makes every such set thinner than $N^{1-c}$
infinitely often, so the second question is answered no. The thread's
sharpening, recorded below, gives one set with
$|A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}|\ge N/(\log N)^{O(\log\log\log N)}$ for all large
$N$.

**The construction.** The Lean proof of part (i) of the formal-conjectures
statement was first made public on 3 April 2026, as a pull request to that
collection from a fork (the pull request linked above, opened at 13:52 UTC and
merged on 7 April); the proof of part (ii) followed in a second pull request
opened on 7 April 2026. A comment of 7 April 2026 in the site's thread, posted
by George Tsoukalas, the first author of the paper linked above, reports that
DeepMind's automated prover produced the two Lean proofs, links them in the
fork, and derives informal arguments from them. Both build $A$ as a union of
blocks $B_i$, each inside a short interval $[P_i,1.1P_i]$ and each free of
three-term arithmetic progressions (a base-three digit set in the first proof,
a Behrend sphere in the second), with every element of $B_i$ divisible by the
$i$-th odd prime and congruent to $1$ modulo the earlier ones; a relation
$a\mid b+c$ across blocks then fails modulo that prime, and within a block it
forces $b+c=2a$, which the progression-free design excludes. The first proof
takes $|B_i|\gg\sqrt{P_{i+1}}$, which gives the liminf; the second fixes an
exponent $c\in(0,1)$ and takes $|B_k|\ge(\max B_{k+1})^{1-c}$, which gives
$|A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}|\ge N^{1-c}$ for all large $N$. The thread then
simplified and sharpened the construction: Terence Tao observed the same day
that distinct $b,c>a$ already have $b+c>2a$, so the progression-free
ingredient is unnecessary and a small change to the 1970 construction of Erdős
and Sárközy suffices; the site's curator gave the blocks
$B_k=\{C^{k\log k}<n<\tfrac32C^{k\log k}:n\equiv1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p_i)\text{ for }i<k,\ n\equiv0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p_k)\}$,
whose union, for a fixed $C>e$, has
$|A\cap[1,N]|\gg(\log N)^{-O(1/\varepsilon)}N^{1-\varepsilon}$ for all large
$N$, where $\varepsilon=1/\log C$; the same comment suggests, with a caveat,
that the construction gives
$|A\cap[1,N]|\gg N/\exp(O(\sqrt{\log N\log\log N}))$, which needs $C$ to grow
with $k$, since with $C$ fixed the count is $N^{1-1/\log C+o(1)}$; on 8 April
2026 Tao encoded the block index in binary, so that each block carries
congruence conditions at about $\log k$ primes instead of $k$, which gives the
bound stated above, and the curator's equivalent form reads the conditions off
the binary digits of $k$, with density $\gg1/((\log N)(\log\log N)^{O(1)})$ for
infinitely many $N$ when $n\equiv1$ is relaxed to a half-interval of residues.
The informal arguments are those given in the thread; they have not been checked
step by step.

**Covers.** The first two questions, for sets in the site's reading (two
distinct larger elements): yes to the liminf question and no to the
$N^{1-c}$ question. Not covered: the third question, whether
$\sum_{n\in A}1/n$ converges for every such set. Every set constructed here
has a convergent reciprocal sum, and the thread's barrier remark, recorded
on the problem page, says why block constructions with congruence
conditions cannot reach divergence.

**Acceptance.** Refereed. The paper of Tsoukalas et al., *Advancing
mathematics research with AI-driven formal proof search*, was published in
Science 394 (2026), 234--239, on 8 October 2026, the refereed version of
arXiv:2605.22763 (21 May 2026, revised 8 June 2026); that publication is the
`refereed` evidence. Table 1 of its Section 3 (v2) lists parts (i) and (ii) of
this problem among the nine problems its agent resolved, Section 3 discusses
the first question, and Appendix B.4 proves both from the Lean proofs: the
library pages
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/tsoukalas_2026_advancing_mathematics_research_ai_driven_formal_proof_search/erdos_12_i|Erdős #12-(i)]]
and
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/tsoukalas_2026_advancing_mathematics_research_ai_driven_formal_proof_search/erdos_12_ii|Erdős #12-(ii)]]
record them. The Science text and its supplement could not be read here (the publisher's site refused access); the
account follows arXiv v2, as the
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/tsoukalas_2026_advancing_mathematics_research_ai_driven_formal_proof_search/_index|library card]]
records. The paper's own statements carry two slips that do not touch this
claim: Table 1 summarizes part (i) as a bound "infinitely often", weaker than
the liminf its theorem proves, and its Theorem (ii) is printed with one set for
every $\varepsilon$, while its proof gives one set for each $\varepsilon$, the
form stated above.

The site's credit is not acceptance. The site labeled the problem OPEN, a label
that settles neither question this claim answers, and commentary on a problem
so labeled is not acceptance. On 8 April 2026 the site's curator, Thomas Bloom,
rewrote the commentary. The rewrite credits DeepMind with a construction that
answers the second question no, and hence the first yes. It also records the
improved bound reached in the thread. That commentary, with the re-derivation
of the construction in the thread by Tao and the curator, is credit and not
acceptance. The curator's own contribution, the simpler blocks above, came
after the claim and is disclosed here.

Not formalized in this corpus's sense. The fork holds two Lean proofs:

pointing at the fork's proofs and `sorry` bodies.
