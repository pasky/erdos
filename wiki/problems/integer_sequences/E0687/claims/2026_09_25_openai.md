**Claim.** The manuscript *A quadratic bound for Jacobsthal's function* of
the OpenAI mathematics release, dated 25 September 2026 and authored by
OpenAI (its intake card is
[[../library/integer_sequences/openai_2026_quadratic_bound_jacobsthal_function/_index|openai_2026_quadratic_bound_jacobsthal_function]]),
proves as its Theorem 1.1 that Jacobsthal's function $h(k)$, the least $m$
such that every $m$ consecutive integers contain one coprime to any given
positive integer with at most $k$ distinct prime factors, satisfies

$$
h(k)\ll\frac{k^2}{(\log\log 3k)^2},
$$

uniformly in the modulus and in the position of the interval. The release
states that its manuscripts were produced by an internal OpenAI model and
come at different stages of verification, not all with Lean formalizations.
Its abstract presents this as a yes to Jacobsthal's quadratic-bound
question, the displayed question of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0970/_index|Problem 970]].
The manuscript states the covering form of this problem. Its introduction
observes that $h(k)-1$ is the greatest length of an interval that the
divisibility classes of at most $k$ primes can cover, and calls one
prescribed residue class at each prime below a cutoff another form of the
same covering problem, through the Chinese remainder theorem. Its Theorem
1.2 (the library's
[[../library/integer_sequences/openai_2026_quadratic_bound_jacobsthal_function/theorem_1_2|Theorem 1.2 page]]) is that covering
statement at one interval length: for every large $z$ and every choice of
one class $a_p\pmod p$ for each prime $p\le z$, at least $cYV_0/B^2>0$
integers of $[1,Y]$, $Y=\lfloor z^2/(\log z)^2\rfloor$, avoid every class.
So no choice of classes covers $[1,Y]$, and

$$
Y(z)<\Bigl\lfloor\frac{z^2}{(\log z)^2}\Bigr\rfloor
$$

for every large $z$: a saving of $(\log x)^2$ over Iwaniec's $Y(x)\ll x^2$
and a yes to the first displayed question, $Y(x)=o(x^2)$, directly from the
manuscript. The manuscript does not name $Y(x)$ or Problem 687, and its
catalog entry speaks only of Jacobsthal's question and the displayed bound of
Problem 970: the reading of Theorem 1.2 as a bound on $Y(z)$ is made here and
in the Status paragraph of the
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0687/_index|problem page]].

The answer this page accepts comes by the release's Lean route and has an
unspecified constant. The release proves in Lean the declaration
`OAI.Erdos970.Erdos970Final.erdos_970_quadratic`: one absolute $C>0$ gives
$h(k)\le Ck^2$ for every $k\ge1$, accepted on
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0970/claims/2026_09_25_openai|Problem 970's claim page]]
on this corpus's build and audit. For real $x\ge2$ the product $P(x)$ of
the primes up to $x$ has exactly $\pi(x)\ge1$ prime factors, all distinct,
so $j(P(x))\le h(\pi(x))$; display (1.3) of Ford, Green, Konyagin, Maynard
and Tao gives $Y(x)=j(P(x))-1$; and Chebyshev's bound $\pi(x)\ll x/\log x$
finishes the deduction:

$$
Y(x)=j(P(x))-1\le h(\pi(x))-1<C\,\pi(x)^2\ll\frac{x^2}{(\log x)^2}.
$$

So $Y(x)=o(x^2)$, with the same saving $(\log x)^2$ over Iwaniec's bound.
The constant $C$ is existential and, by the manuscript's conventions, need
not be effective. The accepted statement is therefore
$Y(x)\ll x^2/(\log x)^2$, not the constant-1 form displayed above, which
stays the manuscript's claim and is unreviewed. The two are one argument,
not two independent sources: the manuscript derives Theorem 1.1 from
Theorem 1.2 in its Section 11.2, and the release's tree proves
`erdos_970_quadratic` from `source_root_survivor_lower`, its form of
Theorem 1.2. The release's stronger declaration `erdos_970_iterated_log`
would give $Y(x)\ll x^2/((\log x)^2(\log\log x)^2)$ the same way, but this
corpus has not accepted it, so $x^2/(\log x)^2$ is the accepted bound and
not the best the release's statements would give for $Y$.

**Covers.** The first displayed question, $Y(x)=o(x^2)$, answered yes with
the saving $(\log x)^2$ over Iwaniec's $Y(x)\ll x^2$: accepted as
$Y(x)\ll x^2/(\log x)^2$ with an unspecified constant, and claimed by the
manuscript in the constant-1 form of Theorem 1.2. Not covered: the second
displayed question, $Y(x)\ll x^{1+o(1)}$, and the estimate of $Y(x)$, which
lies between the lower bounds on the problem page and $x^2/(\log x)^2$.

**Formalization.** The release's Lean tree at the pinned revision states the
theorem as the challenge `erdos_970_iterated_log`, of type
`NumberTheoryLean.Targets.JacobsthalIteratedLog`, in
`ComparatorChallenges/JacobsthalImproved.lean` (body `sorry`, the challenge
form) and proves a declaration of the same name and type in
`OAI/NumberTheory/Jacobsthal/Conclusions/IteratedLogBound.lean`. The target
asserts a constant $C>0$ such that every $k\ge1$ has some
$m\le Ck^2/(\log\log 3k)^2$ with `IsJacobsthalBound k m`, and that
predicate says that for every positive $n$ with at most $k$ distinct prime
factors and every integer $a$, some $a+i$ with $0\le i<m$ is coprime to $n$.
The release's catalog `lean/formalization.yaml` lists the manuscript and
records the plain quadratic declaration `erdos_970_quadratic`
(`Conclusions/QuadraticBound.lean`, comparator configuration
`ComparatorChallenges/Jacobsthal.json`) as its formalized main result. The
tree also holds the covering statement for one class per prime, as
interior declarations: `cutoffSurvivors Y z residue`
(`OAI/NumberTheory/Jacobsthal/Primes/LargePrimeDeletion.lean`) is the set
of $i<Y$ with $i\not\equiv$ `residue p` $\pmod p$ for every prime $p\le z$,
and `OAI.Erdos970.Erdos970Final.source_root_survivor_lower`
(`Conclusions/SourceRootCompletion.lean`, with `sourceY z` the floor of
$z^2/(\log z)^2$) bounds the survivor count below, for every residue
choice and all large $z$, in the form Theorem 1.2 takes in the tree;
`RootCutoffSurvivors.cutoff_lower_of_root_lower` carries that bound to
`cutoffSurvivors`, and `erdos_970_quadratic` is proved from it. None of
these is on the comparator surface or was audited for fidelity, and
nothing in the tree names $Y(x)$, the primorial $P(x)$ or this problem, so
the formalization reaches this problem only through the unformalized
readings above. The corpus's verification built `erdos_970_quadratic` at
the pinned revision, found its axioms to be `propext`, `Classical.choice`
and `Quot.sound` only, and audited its statement against the formulation
of Problem 970, as
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0970/claims/2026_09_25_openai|that problem's claim page]]
records. That declaration concerns $h$, not $Y$, so `formalized` is listed
here for the bound $h(k)\le Ck^2$ alone: the step from $h$ to $Y$, through
(1.3), the count $\omega(P(x))=\pi(x)$ and Chebyshev's bound, is authored
and not formalized. The survivor declarations are recorded from the source
text and were not built or audited.

**Acceptance.** Reviewed and formalized, for the answer $Y(x)=o(x^2)$ in the
