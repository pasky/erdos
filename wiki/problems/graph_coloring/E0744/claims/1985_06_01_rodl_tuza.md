***

**Claim.** For each large fixed $k$ and all sufficiently large $n$,

$$
f_k(n)=\binom{k-1}{2}:
$$

there is a graph on $n$ vertices with chromatic number $k$, every proper
subgraph of which has chromatic number below $k$, that becomes bipartite after
deleting $\binom{k-1}{2}$ edges, and no such graph on $n$ vertices does so
with fewer. So $f_k(n)$ is eventually constant and does not tend to infinity;
the erdosproblems.com record applies the value to $k=4$, giving $f_4(n)=3$ for
all large $n$, which refutes $f_4(n)\gg\log n$. The range of $k$ is the
problem's: erdosproblems.com states it for $k$ a large fixed constant, Erdős
[Er81] states the conjecture for $k>k_0$ and notes that odd circuits make it
false for $k=3$, and the erdosproblems.com record of the theorem gives no
explicit threshold. Both questions of
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0744/_index|Problem 744]] are answered no. The page
is dated by the paper's journal issue, June 1985; the day is not recorded.

**Context.** The question is printed in Erdős's 1981 survey [Er81]
([[../library/set_systems/erdos_1981_combinatorial_problems_which_i_would_most/_index|card]]),
Part VII, as a conjecture stated in the then-forthcoming paper of Erdős,
Hajnal and Szemerédi, for $k>k_0$; Erdős adds that it no doubt holds already
for $k=4$, that odd circuits make it false for $k=3$, and that the authors had
made no progress with it. erdosproblems.com attributes the problem to that paper
[EHS82]
([[../library/graph_coloring/erdos_1982_almost_bipartite_large_chromatic_graphs/_index|card]]),
whose text does not state the critical-graph question. Odd cycles give
$f_3(n)=1$, and before the refutation the earlier upper bounds were Gallai's
construction, $f_4(n)\ll n^{1/2}$, and Lovász's extension, $f_k(n)\ll
n^{1-1/(k-2)}$, as erdosproblems.com records.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Rödl, V. and Tuza, Zs., On color critical graphs, J.
Combin. Theory Ser. B 38 (1985), no. 3, 204–213. Reviewed: the erdosproblems.com
curator, Thomas Bloom, labeled the problem disproved and credits the result to
Rödl and Tuza, independently of its authors. The paper is not held in the
library. The exact value and the range of $n$ stated above follow the
erdosproblems.com record (accessed 2026-09-04).
