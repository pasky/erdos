***

**Claim.** Call a triple $1\le i<j\le n/2$ bad when no prime $p\ge i$
divides both $\binom ni$ and $\binom nj$, so that
[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0699/_index|Problem 699]] asks whether bad
triples exist.

1. No bad triple has $i=7$ or $i\ge9$: for every $1\le i<j\le n/2$ with
   $i=7$ or $i\ge9$, some prime $p\ge i$ divides both $\binom ni$ and
   $\binom nj$.
2. No bad triple with $i=3$ has $n\le10^{19}$ or $v_2(n)\le48$, and none has
   $n$ even with $(n-2)/2$ divisible by at most two distinct primes. Thus a
   bad triple with $i=3$ would have $n>10^{19}$, $v_2(n)\ge49$ and at least
   three distinct prime factors of $(n-2)/2$.

Both items are computer-assisted. Item 1 joins, at each index, analytic
bounds that exclude every row above an explicit height to certified
exclusions of the rows below it, using exact integer and rational
calculations and directed bounds for analytic quantities. In item 2 the
bounds on $n$ and $v_2(n)$ come from an exact search with certified
primality, and the three prime factors from a uniform proof.

**Covers.** Every triple of
[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0699/_index|Problem 699]] with $i=7$ or
$i\ge9$, and the triples with $i=3$ in the rows of item 2. The indices
$4,5,6,8$ and the other rows at $i=3$ are not covered, so the problem remains
open; the indices $1$ and $2$ are van Doorn and Rocca's and are not claimed
here. Of the covered triples, those with $i\ge1476$ are also claimed in
[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0699/claims/2026_07_25_van_doorn_rocca|van Doorn and Rocca's pending claim]]
of 2026-07-25, on which this claim builds, and those with $j\le3i/2$ or
$n=2j$ are settled by
[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0699/claims/2026_07_18_price|Price's accepted claim]]
of 2026-07-18. The lead also proves that, assuming the abc conjecture over
$\mathbb Q$, only finitely many bad triples have $i=3$, and so, with van
Doorn and Rocca's fixed-index finiteness at $4,5,6,8$, only finitely many
exist in all. Finiteness settles no triple, so that conditional result is
not part of this claim.

**Credit.** The argument builds on Wouter van Doorn and Stefano Rocca's two
unpublished 2026 manuscripts. The first,
[[../library/factorials_binomials/van_doorn_rocca_2026_partial_progress_erdos_problem_699/_index|Partial Progress on Erdős Problem #699]],
supplies the Kummer localization of the part of $\binom ni$ on primes at
least $i$ to the triangle $\{(r,s):r,s\ge0,\ r+s<i\}$, the divisibility it
gives for polynomials vanishing to high order there, the fat-point divisor,
and the orbit polynomial with its Jacobi discriminant; it says that its
specific arguments follow L. Price's Overleaf project *Common Prime Divisor
of Binomial Coefficients* (2026). For $i\ge906$ the claim rests on Lemma 1 of
the second,
[[../library/factorials_binomials/van_doorn_rocca_2026_binomial_coefficients_sharing_large_prime_divisor/_index|Binomial coefficients sharing a large prime divisor]],
through the calculation retained beside its card, a repository deduction
that supplies the cutoff $i\ge1000$ the manuscript states without its
calculation and lowers it to $906$. The cofactor estimates build on the Padé
construction of Bennett, Filaseta and Trifonov, and the proofs use explicit
prime estimates and the exhaustive prime-gap computation of Oliveira e Silva,
Herzog and Pardi, as cited on the lead's pages. The proofs at $i=7$ and
$9\le i\le905$ and the results at $i=3$ are Plasma AI's. No novelty is
asserted.

**Standing.** Author-recorded at tier 0; no independent review of the
repository deductions is retained. The proofs are computer-assisted; the
lead's retained checkers and certificates are author evidence and confer no
tier, and no Lean formalization is asserted. The manuscripts' proofs have
not been reviewed here: both cards stand at author-recorded reading, and
their proofs count as not verified, so item 1 rests on unverified premises.
Catalog acceptance is pending.
