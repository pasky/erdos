**Claim.** For all $\alpha,\beta>0$ with $\alpha/\beta$ irrational, every
sufficiently large integer is

$$
\sum_{s\in S}\lfloor2^s\alpha\rfloor+\sum_{t\in T}\lfloor2^t\beta\rfloor
$$

for some finite $S,T\subset\mathbb N$: the first question of
[[problems/additive_bases/E0354/_index|Problem 354]], answered yes in
erdosproblems.com's own reading, where indices rather than values are used at
most once, so equal values at different indices count separately and zero terms
are harmless. The theorem proved is the formal-conjectures statement
`Erdos354.erdos_354.parts.i` with its answer instantiated to true,

```lean
True ↔ ∀ α > 0, ∀ β > 0, Irrational (α / β) →
  IsAddCompleteNatSeq' (Erdos354.FloorMultiples.interleave α β 2)
```

whose `interleave α β 2` is the sequence
$\lfloor\alpha\rfloor,\lfloor\beta\rfloor,\lfloor2\alpha\rfloor,\lfloor2\beta\rfloor,\ldots$,
whose `subseqSums'` sums over finite index sets, and whose
`IsAddCompleteNatSeq'` asks that every sufficiently large integer be such a
sum; a finite index set is exactly a pair $(S,T)$. The proof's reduction chain,
as the file (10,152 lines) presents it: a scaling to $\alpha,\beta\ge1$, then
two criteria on the binary digits of $\alpha$ and $\beta$, whose joining and
disjointness argument on the digit sequences (about 9,000 lines) rests on the
kernel replay under **Acceptance.** The result page
[[../library/additive_bases/jenw1n_2026_erdos_354_part_i_lean_proof/target|target]]
unfolds the accepted theorem to Conjectures.io's wording, and the source card
[[../library/additive_bases/jenw1n_2026_erdos_354_part_i_lean_proof/_index|jenw1n_2026_erdos_354_part_i_lean_proof]]
records Conjectures.io's solution file.

**Covers.** The first question only: base $2$, the irrational-ratio
hypothesis, indexed (multiset) sums, every exponent included. It makes no
strong-completeness or set-union claim, says nothing about the second question
(a base $\gamma\in(1,2)$ in place of $2$), and leaves the rational-ratio cases
of Hegyvári's conjecture untouched.

**Claimant and posting.** The bounty site credits the proof to the solver
handle JenW1N; the file's header names no author and declares no AI system,
and no paper, preprint or write-up accompanies it. The handle is Liam Kruer and
Jensen Kohlmeyer's: the bounty site's exposition of their JenW1N record on
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0196/claims/2026_09_09_kruer_kohlmeyer|Problem 196]]
names them as its authors, and Kruer's
[post of 5 October 2026](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/proof-claim:81681165220c4b07961cfd357bf6b996#post-9293)
on erdosproblems.com, announcing their Lean formalization of a Problem 790 claim
in the GitHub account JENW1N, is signed "(Jensen Kohlmeyer and Liam Kruer)".
Conjectures.io shows the proof as verified on 11 September 2026, the earliest
date the record carries, which names this page. The same result in a stronger
form was later claimed by Yu and Chen (manuscript of 13 September 2026,
repository created 12 September), whose own page is
[[problems/additive_bases/E0354/claims/2026_09_13_yu_chen|the Yu–Chen claim page]];
Conjectures.io's review notes that claim as later than its acceptance.

**Acceptance.** Formalized. This corpus's verification rebuilt the record's
solution file on the repository's pinned toolchain, `leanprover/lean4:v4.34.1`
with Mathlib at the revision `lean/lake-manifest.json` pins, after changes for
Mathlib's `ProbabilityMeasure.map`, which now takes the function rather than its
measurability proof, and for two renamed lemmas; the target's statement is
unchanged. The kernel replayed, from an empty environment, every declaration the
target `Bounty.target` depends on, Lean core, Mathlib and formal-conjectures
included, and accepted them all. The target's axioms are exactly `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. Its type, and every definition its
statement reaches, are identical to the formal-conjectures statement of
Conjectures.io's task, which **Claim.** above reads as the first question:
`interleave α β 2` lists the floors of $2^k\alpha$ and $2^k\beta$, and sums run
over finite index sets. Acceptance rests on this replay and that check.

The bounty site Conjectures.io documented its acceptance of the exact statement;
that certification is reported, not counted. Its Lean kernel accepted the proof
on 11 September 2026, with `propext`, `Quot.sound` and `Classical.choice` as the
only permitted axioms. Its static scan found no imports, axiom declarations,
`sorry`, `native_decide` or unsafe options. Its report records that the theorem
proved has exactly the task's canonical type. Its manual review approved the
record on 15 September 2026 under its review policy v3, after a prior-art check
that found no earlier public solution. The record was certified on 16 September
2026, and the bounty was paid.

Conjectures.io's second, independent kernel was not run, so the kernel verdict
rests on one implementation. The review's note says its novelty findings are
bounded by the sources it inspected.
