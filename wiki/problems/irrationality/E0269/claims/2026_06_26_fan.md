***

**Claim.** Let $P=\{p,q\}$ with primes $p<q$, and let $S$ be the sum of
[[problems/irrationality/E0269/_index|Problem 269]] for this $P$, with every
term retained, including those at which the running lcm repeats. Then $S$ is
transcendental, and in particular irrational. Steve Fan posted the proof in the
problem's thread on 26 June 2026. With $\beta=\log q/\log p$, which is
irrational, the running lcm at $a_n=p^iq^j$ is
$p^{i+\lfloor j\beta\rfloor}q^{j+\lfloor i/\beta\rfloor}$, so $S=S_1S_2$ with

$$
S_1=\sum_{i\ge0}p^{-i}q^{-\lfloor i/\beta\rfloor},\qquad
S_2=\sum_{j\ge0}p^{-\lfloor j\beta\rfloor}q^{-j}.
$$

For the Hecke–Mahler series
$F_\theta(z_1,z_2)=\sum_{i\ge1}z_1^i\sum_{1\le j\le i\theta}z_2^j$, the post
shows that $S_2=1+1/(q-1)-(p-1)F_\beta(1/q,1/p)$ and, from the reflection
identity $F_\theta(z_1,z_2)+F_{1/\theta}(z_2,z_1)=z_1z_2/((1-z_1)(1-z_2))$,
that $(p-1)S_1+(q-1)S_2=p+q-1$. So $S$ is a quadratic polynomial with rational
coefficients in $S_2$. The post takes the transcendence of $F_\beta(1/q,1/p)$
from the classical theory of Hecke–Mahler series, naming no theorem, and
concludes that $S_2$ and then $S$ are transcendental.

**Submission note.** Posted to the erdosproblems.com forum by Steve Fan
(account Steve_Fan) on 26 June 2026:

> Here is a short proof of the case $|P|=2$, which actually shows that $S$ is
> transcendental. Let $P =\{p_1<p_2<...<p_k\}$. Then each $a_n$ is of the form
> $p_1^{i_1}\cdots p_k^{i_k}$, where $i_1,...,i_k\ge0$, and
> $$
> \text{lcm}[a_1,...,a_n]=\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_j^{\big\lfloor \frac{\log a_n}{\log p_j}\big\rfloor}.
> $$
> Hence
> $$
> S=\sum_{i_1,...,i_k\ge0}\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_j^{-\big\lfloor \frac{\log p_1^{i_1}\cdots p_k^{i_k}}{\log p_j}\big\rfloor}.
> $$
> Specializing to the case $P=\{p<q\}$ and setting $\beta=\log q/\log p>1$
> which is irrational, we have
> $$
> S=\sum_{i,j\ge0}p^{-i-\lfloor j\beta\rfloor}q^{-j-\lfloor i\beta^{-1}\rfloor}=S_1S_2,
> $$
> where
> \begin{align*}
> S_1&:=\sum_{i\ge0}p^{-i}q^{-\lfloor i\beta^{-1}\rfloor},\\
> S_2&:=\sum_{j\ge0}p^{-\lfloor j\beta\rfloor}q^{-j}.
> \end{align*}
> We can relate $S_1,S_2$ to the Hecke--Mahler series
> $$
> F_{\theta}(z_1,z_2):=\sum_{i_1\ge1}z_1^{i_1}\sum_{1\le i_2\le i_1\theta}z_2^{i_2},
> $$
> where $|z_1|,|z_2|<1$ and $\theta>0$. When $\theta\ge1$, we have
> $$
> F_{\theta}(z_1,z_2)=\frac{z_2}{1-z_2}\left(\frac{z_1}{1-z_1}-\sum_{i_1\ge1}z_1^{i_1}z_2^{\lfloor i_1\theta\rfloor}\right),
> $$
> and so
> $$
> S_2=\frac{1}{q-1}-(p-1)F_{\beta}(1/q,1/p)+1.
> $$
> Moreover, for any $\theta>0$,
> $$
> F_{\theta}(z_1,z_2)+F_{\theta^{-1}}(z_2,z_1)=\sum_{i_1,i_2\ge1}z_1^{i_1}z_2^{i_2}=\frac{z_1z_2}{(1-z_1)(1-z_2)}.
> $$
> This yields the nice identity
> $$
> (p-1)S_1+(q-1)S_2=p+q-1,
> $$
> which allows one to write $S$ as a quadratic polynomial with rational
> coefficients in $S_2$. We know by the classical theory of Hecke--Mahler series
> that $F_{\beta}(1/q,1/p)$ is transcendental. Therefore, $S_2$ is also
> transcendental, and so is $S$.
>
> This argument does not seem to generalize immediately to $|P|\ge3$, since $S$
> does not factor nicely in the first place.

**Covers.** Every $P$ of exactly two primes: the sum is transcendental, so the
answer is yes for those sets. Sets of three or more primes are not covered; the
post itself says that the argument does not seem to generalize immediately to
$|P|\ge3$, since the sum does not factor there.

**Claimant and attribution.** The post appears under the account Steve_Fan,
whose display name is Steve Fan, and mentions no AI assistance. The same day
the account old-bielefelder replied that the argument works for any two coprime
integers in place of the two primes, and Fan agreed in a further post,
suggesting more generally sets of nonzero algebraic numbers whose logarithms
are linearly independent over $\mathbb Q$, ordered by their norms; that
suggestion carries no proof and lies outside this problem. Will Cook's post of
11 September 2026 in the same thread (display name williamwkcook) states that
the two-prime factorization, the Hecke–Mahler reduction and the transcendence
conclusion are Fan's. Cook's write-up,
[[../library/irrationality/cook_2026_running_least_common_multiples/_index|Running least common multiples]],
reproduces the calculation with that credit and claims no priority for it.

**The transcendence input.** The post's slope $\beta=\log q/\log p$ exceeds
$1$, while Bugeaud and Laurent's Theorem 1.1
([[../library/irrationality/bugeaud_2022_transcendence_continued_fraction_hecke_mahler/_index|its card]])
treats slopes in $(0,1)$. By the post's reflection identity,
$F_\beta(1/q,1/p)$ is a rational number minus $F_{1/\beta}(1/p,1/q)$, the
value of slope $\log p/\log q\in(0,1)$ whose fit to that theorem's hypotheses
the [[research/smooth_lcm_series/_index|two-prime research page]] checks. Cook's
write-up attributes the same input to Loxton and van der Poorten, in Bugeaud
and Laurent's formulation.
