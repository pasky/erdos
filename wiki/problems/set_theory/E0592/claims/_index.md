# problems/set_theory/E0592/claims

[[problems/set_theory/E0592/_index|..]]

[[problems/set_theory/E0592/claims/1956_12_01_specker|1956_12_01_specker]]: Specker (Comment. Math. Helv. 31, 1956/57, refereed) proves
$\omega^2\to(\omega^2,n)^2$ for every finite $n$ and
$\omega^m\not\to(\omega^m,3)^2$ for finite $m\ge3$. So the answer is yes at
$\beta=2$ and no at every finite $\beta\ge3$.

[[problems/set_theory/E0592/claims/1972_05_01_chang|1972_05_01_chang]]: Chang (J. Combin. Theory Ser. A 12, 1972, refereed) proves
$\omega^\omega\to(\omega^\omega,3)^2$, so the answer is yes in the case
$\beta=\omega$ of the question.

[[problems/set_theory/E0592/claims/1975_01_01_galvin_larson|1975_01_01_galvin_larson]]: Galvin and Larson (Fund. Math. 82, 1974/75, refereed) show that a countable
partition ordinal is $0$, $1$, $\omega^2$ or $\omega^{\omega^\gamma}$. So
$\omega^\beta$ fails the relation for every decomposable $\beta\ge3$.

[[problems/set_theory/E0592/claims/2010_05_13_schipperus|2010_05_13_schipperus]]: Schipperus (Ann. Pure Appl. Logic 161, 2010, refereed) proves the relation for
$\beta=\omega^\gamma$ with $\gamma$ the sum of one or two indecomposables, and
refutes it for four or more. The case of three summands stays open.
