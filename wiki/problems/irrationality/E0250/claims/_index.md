# problems/irrationality/E0250/claims

[[problems/irrationality/E0250/_index|..]]

[[problems/irrationality/E0250/claims/1995_09_18_duverney|1995_09_18_duverney]]: Duverney's 1995 note (refereed and reviewed) proves that
$(q-1)^2\sum_n\sigma(n)/q^n$ is irrational for every integer $q$ other than
$-1$, $0$ and $1$. The case $q=2$ is the series of Problem 250, so the answer
is yes.

[[problems/irrationality/E0250/claims/1996_03_07_nesterenko|1996_03_07_nesterenko]]: Nesterenko's 1996 theorem says that at least three of $q$, $P(q)$, $Q(q)$,
$R(q)$ are algebraically independent for $0<|q|<1$. It makes
$P(1/2)=1-24\sum_n\sigma(n)/2^n$ transcendental, so the sum is irrational.

[[problems/irrationality/E0250/claims/2001_11_08_zudilin|2001_11_08_zudilin]]: Zudilin (Mat. Sb. 193 (2002), refereed) proves that the $q$-analog of
$\zeta(2)$ is irrational at $q=1/p$, with irrationality measure at most
$4.0787$. At $q=1/2$ this number is $\sum_n\sigma(n)/2^n$.

[[problems/irrationality/E0250/claims/2006_04_13_postelmans_van_assche|2006_04_13_postelmans_van_assche]]: Postelmans and Van Assche (J. Number Theory 126 (2007), refereed) prove that
$1$, $\zeta_q(1)$ and $\zeta_q(2)$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb Q$
for $q=1/p$ with $p\ge2$. At $q=1/2$ this makes $\sum_n\sigma(n)/2^n$
irrational.

[[problems/irrationality/E0250/claims/2008_09_15_smet_van_assche|2008_09_15_smet_van_assche]]: Smet and Van Assche (Acta Arith. 138 (2009), refereed) prove that $\zeta_q(2)$
is irrational at $q=1/p$, with irrationality measure at most
$10\pi^2/(5\pi^2-24)$. At $q=1/2$ this number is $\sum_n\sigma(n)/2^n$.

[[problems/irrationality/E0250/claims/2025_10_21_romanlelan|2025_10_21_romanlelan]]: A note linked in the site's thread claims that $\sum_n\sigma(n)q^n$ is
transcendental for every algebraic $q$ with $0<|q|<1$. The curator showed the
same day that the argument does not reach the series itself.
