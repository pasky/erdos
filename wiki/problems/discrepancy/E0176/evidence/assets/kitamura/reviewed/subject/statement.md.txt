# Arithmetic progression discrepancy: exact subject

For positive integers k and real l>0, let N(k,l), when it exists, be the
least positive integer N such that every map f:{0,...,N-1}->{-1,1}
has integers a>=0 and d>=1 with a+(k-1)d<N and
|sum_{j=0}^{k-1} f(a+jd)|>=l. The target inequalities are non-strict.
Translation by one gives the identical minimum for the interval {1,...,N}.

The complete proposed conclusions are:

1. For every integer k>=5,
   N(k,2)<=floor(2(k^2-1)(k-1)(2k+1)/(k-3))+1.
2. For every integer k>=2,
   N(k,sqrt(k))<=floor((4/3)(k^2-k+1)(k-1)(k(k-1)+1))+1.
3. For every integer k>=2, N(k,2)<=16^k, including the finite claims
   N(2,2)=3, N(3,2)=9 and N(4,2)=13. N(1,2) does not exist.
4. For every integer k>=1, N(k,sqrt(k))<=64^k, including N(1,1)=1.

Floors and divisions in the displayed bounds are real operations before
taking the integer floor. Cyclic progressions are intermediate objects;
the final ordinary progression has positive difference and no wrapping.
Source abbreviations N176 and N176Sqrt have default values outside their
guarded domains. In particular the source's N176Sqrt 1=0 is not the
mathematical minimum N(1,1)=1.
