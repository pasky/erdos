**Claim.** Put $T(n)=\phi(n)+1$. Let $F(n)$ be the number of iterations of
$T$ needed to reach a prime from $n$, with $F(p)=0$ for a prime $p$, and for a
prime $p$ let $B_p$ be the set of positive integers that reach $p$ after zero
or more iterations of $T$. The five-page notes *Notes on Erdős Problem #409:
the forest structure of $n\mapsto\varphi(n)+1$*, dated 22 July 2026 (the
`preprint` link, pinned to the commit of 2026-07-22 that uploaded it), state
three results on the problem's questions.

First, $F(n)\le5\sqrt n+1$ for every $n\ge1$ (Theorem 1(vii)). The notes
derive it from $T(m)\le m-\sqrt m+1$ for composite $m\ge4$, whose least prime
factor is at most $\sqrt m$: then $m-T(m)\ge\frac12\sqrt m$, at most
$\sqrt{2x}$ composite iterates lie in $(x/2,x]$, and summing over dyadic
ranges gives $F(n)\le\sqrt{2n}\,(1-2^{-1/2})^{-1}+O(1)\le5\sqrt n+1$.

Second, every basin $B_p$ of a prime $p<30000$ is finite and was computed
exactly (Section 4): $3245$ trees, none truncated at caps of $4\cdot10^4$
nodes and $10^{12}$ values. The search lists, from $p$ upward, the solutions
$x$ of $\phi(x)=m-1$ at the root and at every odd node $m$; an even number at
least $4$ is not a value of $T$, so even nodes are leaves. The
inverse-totient solver is reported validated against brute force for every
value up to $2000$, using $n\le2\phi(n)^2$, and the search is the
repository's `basins.py`. Among the results, $B_2=\{1,2\}$, $B_3=\{3,4,6\}$,
$B_5=\{5,8,10,12\}$,
$B_7=\{7,9,14,15,16,18,20,24,30\}$ and $B_{11}=\{11,22\}$; $B_{13}$ has $31$
elements, height $4$ and largest element $138$, and $B_{61}$ has $57$
elements, height $8$ and largest element $4314$. The largest basin found is
$B_{21089}$, with $5557$ elements, and the tallest is $B_{23509}$, of height
$20$.

Third, let $p>3$ be a prime with $p\equiv3\pmod4$ (Theorem 2). Then the
solutions $n\ne p$ of $T(n)=p$ are $2p$ and the numbers $q^a$ and $2q^a$ with
$q$ a prime, $q\equiv3\pmod4$, $a\ge2$ and $q^{a-1}(q-1)=p-1$. If $p-1$ has no
such form, then $B_p=\{p,2p\}$, since $2p$ is even and at least $4$ and so is
not a value of $T$. The primes $p\le x$ for which $p-1$ has that form number
at most $\sqrt{2x}+O(x^{1/3}\log x)$, so for all but a proportion tending to
zero of the primes $p\equiv3\pmod4$, exactly two positive integers reach $p$
and the set of those that do has density zero. The proof writes $n=2^bm$ with
$m$ odd and uses $v_2(\phi(n))=\max(b-1,0)+\sum_{q\mid m}v_2(q-1)$, which must
equal $1$ since $p\equiv3\pmod4$. The notes check the characterization on the
$1632$ primes $3<p<30000$ with $p\equiv3\pmod4$: $1620$ have the basin
$\{p,2p\}$, and the $12$ exceptions are the primes the characterization
predicts.

The forum comment (the `discussion` link) states the third result, with the
count $O(\sqrt x)$, and the second; the first is in the notes only.

**Submission note.** Posted to the erdosproblems.com forum by the account
ankit on 22 July 2026:

> Two observations bearing on the finiteness conjecture recorded above (that
> only finitely many integers reach any fixed prime), plus exhaustive data.
>
> 1. For 100% of primes $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, exactly two integers ever reach
>    $p$, namely $p$ and $2p$. Sketch: if $\varphi(n) = p-1 \equiv 2 \pmod 4$,
>    write $n = 2^b m$ with $m$ odd; then $v_2(\varphi(n)) = \max(b-1,0) +
>    \sum_{q \mid m} v_2(q-1) = 1$ forces (for $p>3$) either $n \in \{p, 2p\}$
>    or $n \in \{q^a, 2q^a\}$ with $q \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ prime, $a \geq 2$,
>    $q^{a-1}(q-1) = p-1$. Since $\varphi(n)+1$ is odd for $n \geq 3$, no even
>    number $\geq 4$ has a preimage, so $2p$ is terminal. Hence unless
>    $p - 1 = q^{a-1}(q-1)$ is solvable, the full basin of $p$ is
>    $\{p, 2p\}$; and the number of exceptional $p \leq x$ is $O(\sqrt{x})$,
>    which is $o(\pi(x))$. This proves the conjectured finiteness (and density
>    zero) for a full-density subfamily of primes $\equiv 3 \pmod 4$.
>    Computationally: of the 1632 primes $3 < p < 30000$ with
>    $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, exactly 1620 have basin $\{p, 2p\}$, and the 12
>    exceptions are precisely $\{7, 19, 43, 163, 487, 1459, 4423, 6163, 14407,
>    19183, 22651, 26407\}$, matching the characterization.
>
> 2. Exhaustive data: for every prime $p < 30000$ the full basin is finite and
>    computed exactly (largest: 5557 elements at $p = 21089$; tallest tree:
>    height 20 at $p = 23509$). Over all $n \leq 10^7$, $\max F(n) = 38$,
>    attained at $n = 7754613$, with decade records $4, 7, 11, 18, 28, 38$ for
>    $n \leq 10^2, \ldots, 10^7$, consistent with
>    $\max_{n \leq x} F(n) \asymp \log x$, and complementing the constructed
>    $F = 67$ examples posted elsewhere in this thread.
>
> 3. Two cautions on the conjecture. (a) Dickson's conjecture implies $F$ is
>    unbounded: for each $L$, the forms $q_i = 3^{i-1} 2^{L-i+1} t - 1$
>    ($1 \leq i \leq L$) and $p = 2^{L+1} t - 3$ form an admissible system,
>    and their simultaneous primality gives $F(3 q_L) = L$ exactly, via
>    $q_{i+1} = (3 q_i + 1)/2$ (verified witnesses through $L = 6$). (b)
>    Empirically, tree growth is a multitype branching process in the type
>    $v_2(m-1)$ (type-1 nodes are sterile by observation 1), and the measured
>    mean-offspring matrix has Perron root $\approx 1.07$ to $1.17$ across
>    scales $10^4$ to $10^7$: marginally supercritical. If the finiteness
>    conjecture holds, it holds near criticality; the alternative reading is
>    that infinite basins exist but are rare.
>
> Full notes with proofs, the exact basin certificates, and verification code:
> https://github.com/ak56ankit/erdos-409-notes/blob/main/erdos409.pdf .
> Disclosure per forum rules: this work was done with substantial AI
> assistance (Claude, Anthropic); I have independently verified the proofs by
> hand and reproduced all computations myself.

**Covers.** The three questions of
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0409/_index|Problem 409]], each in part. For
the first, how many iterations are needed, the explicit bound
$F(n)\le5\sqrt n+1$, against the trivial $o(n)$ that the problem page
records; it gives no lower bound and does not determine the order of growth.
For the second and third, every prime below $30000$, and every prime $p>3$
with $p\equiv3\pmod4$ for which $p-1$ is not $q^{a-1}(q-1)$ with $q$ a prime,
$q\equiv3\pmod4$ and $a\ge2$, which are all but $O(\sqrt x)$ of the primes up
to $x$: only finitely many $n$ reach each such prime, and the density of
those that do is zero. The primes above $30000$ that are $1\pmod4$ or
exceptional are not addressed, and the notes list the second and third
questions in full, whether any basin is infinite, as still open.

are outside this claim.

**Relation to other claims.** Wouter van Doorn's comment of the same day in

([[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0409/claims/2026_07_22_van_doorn|its claim page]])
gives $F(n)=O(\sqrt n)$ without a constant, from the same inequality for

[[research/prime_basins_phi_plus_one|research page on the small prime basins]],

$F(n)\le4(\sqrt n-\sqrt2)$ for $n\ge2$ and the same five basins of
$2,3,5,7,11$, by its own argument; they are pending on
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0409/claims/2026_10_19_plasma_ai_ii|its claim page]].
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0409/claims/2026_10_19_plasma_ai_i|Plasma AI's claim page on two-element basins]]
records the basin $\{p,2p\}$ for almost every prime, in every residue class,
from Theorem 4.1 of Banks, Ford, Luca, Pappalardi and Shparlinski (2005),
with at most $C_\delta x/(\log x)^{2-\delta}$ exceptional primes up to $x$.
That theorem covers all residue classes with a weaker exception count; the
third result here treats only the primes $p\equiv3\pmod4$, with $O(\sqrt x)$
exceptions and an explicit description of them. Both end with the same step:
$2p$ is even and at least $4$, so it is not a value of $T$.
