***

**Claim.** Let $R(N)$ count the ordered pairs $(u,v)$ of positive powerful
integers with $u+v=N$, where an integer is powerful if each of its prime
divisors occurs to exponent at least two, and $1$ is powerful. For every
$\varepsilon>0$ there is $C_\varepsilon$ such that

$$
R(N)\le C_\varepsilon N^{2/5+\varepsilon}
$$

for all integers $N\ge2$. In the notation of the problem,
$1_A\ast1_A(N)\le N^{2/5+o(1)}$.

**Covers.** A partial bound toward a positive answer on
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0943/_index|Problem 943]], which asks
whether $1_A\ast1_A(n)=n^{o(1)}$. The bound leaves the exponent $2/5$ and
does not answer that question.

**Credit.** The proof's three-way dyadic interpolation is the one used in
Section 2.5 of
[[../library/diophantine_problems/tao_2026_products_consecutive_integers_unusual_anatomy/_index|Tao's Products of consecutive integers with unusual anatomy]]
for a real-quadratic difference equation, which the proof cites as
methodological context. The uniform imaginary-quadratic norm count that the
sum of two powerful numbers needs is supplied in the proof. No novelty is
claimed. The same bound, $1_A*1_A(n)\ll_\varepsilon n^{2/5+\varepsilon}$, is
Theorem 1.2 of Przemek Chojecki's unrefereed write-up, posted on the
[erdosproblems.com forum thread](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/943)
on 2026-03-18, a pending claim recorded on
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0943/claims/2026_03_18_chojecki|its claim page]].
