# problems/additive_combinatorics/E0877/claims

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0877/_index|..]]

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0877/claims/2000_12_28_luczak_schoen|2000_12_28_luczak_schoen]]: Łuczak and Schoen's bound, refereed in the Proc. AMS, that for all large $n$
the first $n$ integers have at most $2^{n/2-n/2^{28}}$ maximal sum-free
subsets, which is $o(2^{n/2})$ and the first answer to the displayed question.
The paper is not held.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0877/claims/2009_04_09_wolfovitz|2009_04_09_wolfovitz]]: Wolfovitz's bound, refereed in the European Journal of Combinatorics, that the
first $n$ integers have at most $2^{3n/8+o(n)}$ maximal sum-free subsets,
which is $o(2^{n/2})$. The paper is not held, and the bound is quoted from
Balogh, Liu, Sharifzadeh and Treglown.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0877/claims/2014_09_19_balogh_liu_sharifzadeh_treglown|2014_09_19_balogh_liu_sharifzadeh_treglown]]: Theorem 1.1 of Balogh, Liu, Sharifzadeh and Treglown, refereed (Proc. AMS):
the number of maximal sum-free subsets of the first $n$ integers is
$2^{(1/4+o(1))n}$, which matches the Cameron--Erdős lower bound in the
exponent.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0877/claims/2015_02_26_balogh_liu_sharifzadeh_treglown|2015_02_26_balogh_liu_sharifzadeh_treglown]]: Theorem 1.1 of Balogh, Liu, Sharifzadeh and Treglown, in the JEMS: for each
residue $i$ of $n$ modulo $4$ there is a constant $C_i$ such that the number
of maximal sum-free subsets of the first $n$ integers is $(C_i+o(1))2^{n/4}$.
