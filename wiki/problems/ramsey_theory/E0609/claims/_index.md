# problems/ramsey_theory/E0609/claims

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0609/_index|..]]

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0609/claims/2016_02_24_day_johnson|2016_02_24_day_johnson]]: Day and Johnson (J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 124, 2017, refereed) give
$n$-colorings of $K_{2^n+1}$ whose shortest monochromatic odd cycles are long.
So $f(n)\to\infty$, and $f(n)\ge2^{\sqrt{2\log_2n}-O(1)}$.

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0609/claims/2024_12_10_girao_hunter|2024_12_10_girao_hunter]]: In an arXiv preprint (2024), Girão and Hunter prove that for fixed
$\varepsilon>0$ and large $n$, every $n$-coloring of $K_{2^n+1}$ has a
monochromatic odd cycle of length at most $(2^n+1)/n^{1-\varepsilon}$.

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0609/claims/2025_06_17_janzer_yip|2025_06_17_janzer_yip]]: Janzer and Yip (Math. Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc. 181, 2026, refereed) prove
that every $n$-coloring of $K_{2^n+1}$ has a monochromatic odd cycle of length
$O(n^{3/2}2^{n/2})$.

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0609/claims/2026_09_25_cai|2026_09_25_cai]]: Jeremy Cai's unreviewed partial proof claim, posted on the erdosproblems.com
tab on 25 September 2026, states that $f(3)=5$. The value $R(3,3,3)=17$ gives
a 3-coloring of $K_9$ with no monochromatic triangle, and a structural
argument with a finite enumeration and a SAT check gives the upper bound.
