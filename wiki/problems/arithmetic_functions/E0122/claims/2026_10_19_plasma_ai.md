**Claim.** For $\omega(n)$ the number of distinct prime factors, there is
$F:[1,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ tending to infinity and one set $G$ of
positive integers of natural density one such that
$F(n)/\omega(n)\to0$ through $G$, while

$$
\frac{\#\{n\ge1:x<n+\omega(n)<x+F(x)\}}{F(x)}\le7
$$

for every sufficiently large real $x$. Thus the normalized counts cannot
diverge along any unbounded sequence of real or integer locations.

**Covers.** The $f=\omega$ case of the modified Statement of
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0122/_index|Problem 122]], interpreting
"almost all" as smallness along one fixed set of natural density one.
No monotonicity or everywhere-small width condition is imposed. Other
arithmetic functions, including $\tau$, and the full classification are
not settled. The historical unpublished affirmative report's width
convention remains unexamined.

[[theory/number_theory/L34_exceptional_widths_for_omega/_index|L34]]
supplies the complete construction. It uses the $m=1$, $K=1$, $X\ge2$
instance of
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/erdos_1997_locally_repeated_values_arithmetic_functions_iv/lemma_1|EPS97 Lemma 1]]
and Euler's divergence of the prime reciprocal sum. The source statement
was checked at the consumed specialization; its proof has no new
independent verification here.
