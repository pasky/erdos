# problems/discrete_geometry/E0506/claims

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0506/_index|..]]

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0506/claims/1967_03_01_elliott|1967_03_01_elliott]]: Elliott's 1967 theorem asserts that $n>393$ points of the plane, not all on
one circle or one line, determine at least $\binom{n-1}{2}$ circles. The bound
is false, as a circle with $n-1$ points and one point off it shows.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0506/claims/1994_09_01_balintova_balint|1994_09_01_balintova_balint]]: Bálintová and Bálint prove that $n$ points, not all on one circle or one line,
determine at least $5n(n-1)/133$ circles through exactly three of them, and
print the corrected Elliott bound without proof. The paper is refereed.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0506/claims/2009_07_03_purdy_smith|2009_07_03_purdy_smith]]: Purdy and Smith correct Elliott's 1967 theorem. For $n>393$, $n$ points not
all on one circle or line determine at least
$1+\binom{n-1}{2}-\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$ circles, and the bound is attained,
so only finitely many $n$ remain.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0506/claims/2026_08_20_wrona|2026_08_20_wrona]]: Wrona claims a formula for the least number of circles determined by $n$
points that are neither all collinear nor all concyclic, for every $n\ge4$.
The claim comes with a Lean development by the claimant, is AI-assisted, and
is unreviewed.
