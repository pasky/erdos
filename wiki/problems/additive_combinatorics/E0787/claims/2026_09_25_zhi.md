***

**Claim.** Theorem 1.3 of Y. Zhi, *Branch-Sensitive Density Increments for
$k$-Configurations*, arXiv:2610.03750v1, p. 3: "Let $c<1/36$. For every
sufficiently large finite non-empty set $A\subseteq\mathbb Z$, there is a
subset $B\subseteq A$ of size at least $(\log|A|)^{1+c}$ such that
$b_1+b_2\notin A$ whenever $b_1,b_2\in B$ are distinct." In the notation of
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0787/_index|Problem 787]],

$$
g(n)\ge(\log n)^{1+c}\qquad\text{for every }c<\tfrac1{36}\text{ and all large
}n.
$$

It is deduced from Theorem 1.1 (p. 3): for a finite abelian group $G$ of odd
order, $k\ge2$ and $A\subseteq G$ of density $\alpha>0$, with
$L=\log(2/\alpha)$ and $\Lambda=L+\log(2k)$, the proportion of $k$-tuples
$(x_1,\dots,x_k)\in G^k$ whose means $(x_i+x_j)/2$, $1\le i\le j\le k$, all
lie in $A$ is at least
$\exp(-C\Phi)$ with $C$ absolute and
$\Phi=k^{36}L^6\Lambda^6+k^{34}L^8\Lambda^5+k^{32}L^{14}\Lambda+k^{30}L^{16}$,
where Beker's Theorem 3.1 has $\exp(-O(k^{68}L^{16}))$. The paper reruns
Beker's density-increment iteration and keeps apart the outcomes of the
graph-counting step (a row-degree anomaly, a non-centered grid anomaly, and a
centered grid anomaly treated through the positivity of Gram moments), each
charged its own density gain and rank cost. The proof of Theorem 1.3
(pp. 16--17) puts the new bound into Beker's Proposition 4.1, whose threshold
$\exp(k^{68+o(1)})$ becomes $\exp(k^{36+o(1)})$, and then follows Sanders's
iteration, in which a threshold $\exp(k^{\beta+o(1)})$ gives every
$c<1/\beta$. The paper's declaration on the use of artificial intelligence
(p. 19) says that ChatGPT (GPT-5.6 Sol) assisted with literature
organization, LaTeX drafting and editing, language polishing, and iterative
checking of proof details and quantitative bookkeeping, and that the author
is responsible for the mathematical content. Cited as [Zh26] on the problem
page. The preprint is not held in the library.

**Covers.** The lower bound $g(n)\ge(\log n)^{1+c}$ for every $c<1/36$, a
wider explicit range than
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0787/claims/2025_01_17_beker|Beker's]]
$c<1/68$ and an explicit form of
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0787/claims/2018_04_10_sanders|Sanders's]]
bound. Not covered: the order of growth of $g(n)$.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0787/claims/2025_01_17_beker|Beker's theorem]],
whose argument the paper refines: it cites Beker's preprint without a
version, uses its Proposition 3.2 and the proof of it, its local density
increments (Proposition 3.4 and Corollary 3.5), the Bohr-set lemmas of its
appendices and its Proposition 4.1 as stated there, and reproves only the
parts that change;
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0787/claims/2018_04_10_sanders|Sanders's theorem]],
whose iteration turns the threshold into the exponent; and
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0787/claims/1971_12_01_choi|Choi's 1971 paper]]
for the reduction from real sets to integer sets, which carries the theorem
to the site's formulation; the paper states the problem for sets of integers.
