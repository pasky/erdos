# Coupled completion and local covering templates

***

These finite observations test two possible ways to bridge the
[integer carry gap](integer_transfer.md). They are author-recorded by
Plasma AI and are checked directly by the
[evidence program](evidence/_index.md). They give no asymptotic bound or
resolution of [[problems/additive_bases/E0156/_index|Problem 156]].

## A small completion of a larger residual

The exact integers are retained in
[the completion witness](evidence/assets/projective_completion.json).
Its seed $A_0\subset[1,8001]$ has 40 elements and is strong Sidon. Exactly
399 integers of the interval are unblocked by $A_0$. The eight additions

$$
T=\{884,1548,3350,3582,5047,6100,6903,7904\}
$$

make $A_0\cup T$ a maximal strong Sidon set of 48 elements in that same
interval. The check enumerates all unordered pair sums, doubles included,
and every triple and midpoint blocker for the seed and the union.

The seed is an anchored double lift of a Singer set modulo $381$, with
the heights assigned through coordinates on its projective line. The
witness is stated by its full integer set, so no field parameterization
or discovery search is needed to check it. The additions were selected
successively by maximizing the number of immediately blocked remaining
targets, including every new blocker involving the points already added.
No optimality of eight additions is asserted.

This example illustrates why a residual count is not a completion lower
bound. It supplies neither a formula for the repairs at other primes nor
a uniform estimate for this selection rule.

## Two translates covering one block

Let $B\subset[0,q)$ represent a perfect difference set modulo $q$, and
put $D=B-B$ in the integers. Consider pairs

$$
0\le c<d\le q,\qquad d-c\notin D,\qquad
[0,q]\subseteq(c+D)\cup(d+D).                         \tag{1}
$$

The difference restriction is necessary when these two points are added
to a translate of $B$: otherwise the union has a repeated difference.
It is not a complete Sidon test for a larger construction. The covering
condition is precisely what their main-type triples would supply on
one block.

The pair $(0,q)$ always satisfies (1), by the
[half-set lemma](integer_transfer.md). Using it on many successive blocks
repeats the difference $q$, so it cannot by itself give a strong Sidon
set. A possible repair would use templates with many distinct separations
and also exclude all collisions between different blocks.

For the fixed Singer representatives generated by `singer` in
`evidence/main.py`, exhaustive enumeration of all pairs $0\le c<d\le q$
gives:

| $p$ | $q=p^2+p+1$ | pairs satisfying (1) | distinct separations |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 31 | 117 | 13 |
| 7 | 57 | 151 | 15 |
| 11 | 133 | 1 | 1 |
| 19 | 381 | 1 | 1 |
| 31 | 993 | 1 | 1 |
| 43 | 1893 | 1 | 1 |

In the last four rows the sole pair is $(0,q)$. The first two rows prevent
reading this as a general uniqueness theorem. These counts concern exactly
the displayed representatives and exactly two translates.

## Three translates without a repeated separation

For the same fixed bases, consider every $0\le a<b<c\le q$ satisfying

$$
\{b-a,c-a,c-b\}\cap D=\varnothing,\qquad
b-a\ne c-b,\qquad (a,c)\ne(0,q),
$$

and ask whether $(a+D)\cup(b+D)\cup(c+D)$ contains $[0,q]$.
The three points are strong Sidon, their pairwise differences avoid
$D$, and no pair has separation $q$. The exact numbers are:

| $p$ | $q$ | three-point templates |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | 133 | 1446 |
| 19 | 381 | 16572 |
| 31 | 993 | 3298 |
| 43 | 1893 | 0 |

For example, $(0,38,111)$, $(0,109,328)$ and $(0,451,861)$ work in the
first three rows, respectively. These are local templates only; two such
templates placed in different blocks can introduce a repeated difference
or a repeated sum. The final row rules out every template in this precise
domain for that fixed base. Other cuts, other bases, four or more
translates, and blockers involving several added points are not tested.

The next construction obligation is to give a family of compatible local
coverings whose varying separations preserve global Sidonicity, or to
replace this main-type covering mechanism by one that uses the other
triple types. A numerical census of fixed bases does not settle either
obligation.
