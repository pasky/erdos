**Claim.** Let $k(n)$ be the largest $k$ for which some integer $m$ with
$0\le m\le n$ has each of $m+1,\ldots,m+k$ divisible by a prime greater
than $k$; the conclusions below hold equally when $m$ is required to be
positive.

First, for all integers $K\ge64$ and $0\le m\le2^{-15}K^{28/11}$, the
interval $(m,m+K]$ contains an integer $c(q-t)(q+t)$ whose three factors
are positive integers at most $K$, hence an integer all of whose prime
factors are at most $K$. Consequently

$$
k(n)<\max\bigl(64,(2^{15}n)^{11/28}\bigr)\qquad(n\ge1),
$$

and in Erdős's notation $n_k>2^{-15}k^{28/11}$ for every integer $k\ge64$,
where $n_k$ is the least $n\ge0$ such that each of $n+1,\ldots,n+k$ has a
prime factor greater than $k$.

Second, for every real $\delta$ with $0<\delta<1/20$ there is an integer
$K_0(\delta)\ge64$ such that the same conclusion holds for all integers
$K\ge K_0(\delta)$ and $0\le m\le K^{97/38-\delta}$. Consequently, for
every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a constant $A_\varepsilon$ with

$$
k(n)\le A_\varepsilon n^{38/97+\varepsilon}\qquad(n\ge1).
$$

**Covers.** The estimate of $k(n)$ asked by
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0962/_index|Problem 962]], which is
open-ended: the upper exponent improves from the $2/5$ of Erdős and
Sárközy's published bound $k(n)\ll n^{2/5}$ to $11/28\approx0.3929$ with
explicit constants, and to every exponent above $38/97\approx0.3918$. No
bound with exponent exactly $38/97$ and a fixed constant is claimed, and no
lower bound. The displayed question, whether
$\log k(n)\le(\log n)^{1/2+o(1)}$, stays open.

**Credit.** The construction of a smooth member $c(q-t)(q+t)$ of the block
is Erdős and Sárközy's, from the proof of
[[../library/integer_sequences/erdos_sarkozy_1979_prime_factors_consecutive_integers/theorem_2|Theorem 2 of their 1979 paper]];
Plasma AI averages the multiplier $c$ over an interval. The first bound
consumes Vaaler's inequality and the explicit third-derivative test as
printed in Lemmas 7.1 and 8.6 of
[[../library/factorials_binomials/granville_1996_explicit_bounds_exponential_sums_scarcity_squarefree/_index|Granville and Ramaré (1996)]].
The second applies Bourgain's exponent pair $(13/84+\eta,55/84+\eta)$,
[[../library/number_theory/bourgain_2017_decoupling_exponential_sums_riemann_zeta/theorem_6|Theorem 6]]
of *Decoupling, exponential sums and the Riemann zeta function* (2017),
consumed as a published input.
