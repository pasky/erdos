**Claim.** [[problems/ramsey_theory/E0638/_index|Problem 638]] asks whether
every family $S$ of finite graphs that contains, for each $n$, a graph
forcing a monochromatic triangle under every coloring of its edges with $n$
colors must also contain, for every infinite cardinal $\aleph$, all the
finite subgraphs of some graph $G$ forcing a monochromatic triangle under
every coloring with $\aleph$ colors. Barreto's remark, written into the
erdosproblems.com commentary by its curator, is that this is false as worded
unless $S$ is also closed under taking subgraphs: let $S$ consist of one
complete graph for each $n$, large enough that every $n$-coloring of its edges
has a monochromatic triangle (the thread names the complete graphs of the
successive triangle Ramsey numbers). The hypothesis holds. But a graph $G$
with two or more vertices has a subgraph on two vertices with no edge,
which is not complete and so not in $S$, and a graph on one vertex contains
no triangle; so for no infinite cardinal does the graph asked for exist,
and the answer to the erdosproblems.com wording is no. The argument uses nothing
about triangles or colorings beyond the hypothesis: it rests on $S$ not being
closed under subgraphs. (The same family also misses complete graphs of most
orders, so the variant with induced subgraphs fails for it too; an authored
remark that warrants nothing here.)

**Submission note.** Posted to the erdosproblems.com forum by Kevin Barreto
(account KStar) on 5 January 2026:

> I believe Erdős likely intended to add an extra condition on $S$ that, e.g. it
> be closed under taking subgraphs, i.e. be hereditary. Otherwise, e.g.
> $S=\{K_{R_3(n)}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}$ gives a trivial counterexample.
>
> (The site has been updated to address this comment.)

**Why it is rejected.** It answers the erdosproblems.com wording, not the
modified Statement. [[problems/ramsey_theory/E0638/_index|Problem 638]] judges
the modified Statement, in which $S$ is closed under taking subgraphs; the
problem page's Notes give the evidence. The sparse family of complete graphs
is not closed under subgraphs, so the counterexample settles no instance of
the modified Statement. The problem page's Notes credit the result.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the argument is erdosproblems.com's own
observation.

**Acceptance.** None recorded. The erdosproblems.com curator, T. F. Bloom,
records the remark in the problem's commentary and credits Barreto by name. The
curator also tags the thread comment as addressed by an update of the page (page
last edited 10 April 2026). But the erdosproblems.com label stayed OPEN, which
fits the modified Statement. Under the corpus's rule, commentary on a problem
with that label is not an acceptance. No refereed publication or formalization
states the counterexample. The observation is elementary. The thread's later
comments (22 April to 27 April 2026) take the failure of the erdosproblems.com
wording as given and turn to the hereditary reading. None disputes the remark,
and the proof-claim tab is empty.

**Postings and dating.** The thread comment of 5 January 2026 is the first
posting and names this page; the commentary carries no date of its own.
erdosproblems.com keeps the problem labeled OPEN for the hereditary reading.
