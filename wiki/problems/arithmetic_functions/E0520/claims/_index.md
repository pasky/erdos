# problems/arithmetic_functions/E0520/claims

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0520/_index|..]]

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0520/claims/2026_07_29_korsky|2026_07_29_korsky]]: A pending forum claim submitted by Samuel Korsky asserts that Rademacher
random multiplicative sums are almost surely
$O(\sqrt N(\log\log N)^{1/4+\epsilon})$, so that the limit superior in Problem
520 is $0$ and the answer is no.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0520/claims/2026_07_31_durkan_pearce_crump|2026_07_31_durkan_pearce_crump]]: An arXiv preprint by Durkan and Pearce-Crump, a pending claim, proves that
Steinhaus and Rademacher random multiplicative sums are almost surely
$O(\sqrt x(\log\log x)^{1/4+\epsilon})$. In the Rademacher case the limit
superior in Problem 520 is then $0$, so the answer is no.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0520/claims/2026_08_04_hoystad|2026_08_04_hoystad]]: Høystad's self-contained Lean development, accepted after its Lean proof was
built here, shows that the Rademacher sums divided by $\sqrt{N\log\log N}$
tend to $0$ almost surely. So no positive constant is the limit superior, and
the answer to Problem 520 is no.
