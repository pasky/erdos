# Erdős and Sárközy on prime factors of consecutive integers

[[integer_sequences/_index|..]]

[[integer_sequences/erdos_sarkozy_1979_prime_factors_consecutive_integers/theorem_2|theorem_2]]: For all sufficiently large k, the least n with P(n+i) greater than k for every i from one through k exceeds k to the power 5/2 divided by sixteen.

***

Paul Erdős and András Sárközy, *On the prime factors of $\binom{n}{k}$ and
of consecutive integers*, Utilitas Mathematica **16** (1979), 197–215.
The edition read is the nineteen-page scan in the
[Rényi Institute's Erdős archive](https://static.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1979-15.pdf).
No license or usage notice was found in the scan on 2026-10-15, and no
separate rights record was established. No file of this source is held,
since no license on record permits redistribution.

For [[../wiki/problems/integer_sequences/E0962/_index|Problem 962]], the
relevant result is [[integer_sequences/erdos_sarkozy_1979_prime_factors_consecutive_integers/theorem_2|Theorem 2]],
p. 198. The paper defines $n_k$ as the least starting integer $n$ for which
every integer $n+1,\ldots,n+k$ has largest prime factor greater than $k$.
The theorem gives $n_k>k^{5/2}/16$ for all sufficiently large $k$.
This is a uniform statement about the first such block, not a statement
about almost every interval. It is a published lower bound relevant to the
problem's smooth-number formulation; it does not establish the conjectured
subpower scale.

**Read status: claims checked.** The definition on p. 197 and Theorem 2 on
p. 198 were checked against the printed page images. The proof is in
Section 3, beginning on p. 212; it has not been verified here. The separate
binomial-coefficient result, Theorem 1, is not consumed by this card.
