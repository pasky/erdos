# problems/unit_fractions/E0242/claims

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0242/_index|..]]

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0242/claims/1950_01_01_oblath|1950_01_01_oblath]]: Obláth (Mathesis 59 (1950)) proves that $4/n$ is a sum of three unit fractions
whenever $n+1$ has a prime factor congruent to $3$ modulo $4$, a set of
density one. The paper is refereed, and the result is known second-hand.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0242/claims/1970_09_23_terzi|1970_09_23_terzi]]: Terzi (BIT 11 (1971)) proves that $4/n$ is a sum of three unit fractions for
every prime $n$ coprime to $120120$ outside $198$ listed residue classes, and
so for every multiple of such a prime. The paper is refereed.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0242/claims/2023_02_06_alomari|2023_02_06_alomari]]: M. W. Alomari's preprint of February 2023, posted on Authorea, OSF and
Research Square, says in its abstract that it proves the conjecture. It is
unrefereed, and it is linked in the erdosproblems.com thread, where a reply
calls it mistaken.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0242/claims/2025_08_29_mihnea_dumitru|2025_08_29_mihnea_dumitru]]: An arXiv computation report of August 2025 by Mihnea and Dumitru states that
the conjecture holds for every prime up to $10^{18}$, hence for every $n$ up
to $10^{18}$. It is unrefereed, and the computation has not been rerun.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0242/claims/2025_11_07_dyachenko|2025_11_07_dyachenko]]: An unrefereed arXiv preprint of November 2025 by E. Dyachenko claims an
explicit representation $4/P=1/A+1/(bP)+1/(cP)$ for every prime
$P\equiv1\pmod4$. Together with the classical cases, this amounts to the whole
conjecture.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0242/claims/2026_02_12_bradford|2026_02_12_bradford]]: Kyle Bradford's arXiv preprint of February 2026 says in its abstract that it
outlines a solution of the conjecture, three unit fractions for $4/p$ at every
prime $p$. It was reported in the erdosproblems.com thread, is unrefereed, and
has been accepted by no one.
