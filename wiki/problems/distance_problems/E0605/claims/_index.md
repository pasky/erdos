# problems/distance_problems/E0605/claims

[[problems/distance_problems/E0605/_index|..]]

[[problems/distance_problems/E0605/claims/1989_08_01_erdos_hickerson_pach|1989_08_01_erdos_hickerson_pach]]: Erdős, Hickerson and Pach show that for every $n$ and every distance strictly
between 0 and 2, $n$ points on the unit sphere can have a constant times
$n\log^* n$ pairs at that distance, so the answer is yes. The paper is
refereed in the Monthly and credited by the site's curator.

[[problems/distance_problems/E0605/claims/2004_01_01_swanepoel_valtr|2004_01_01_swanepoel_valtr]]: Swanepoel and Valtr show that on every sphere in $\mathbb R^3$ of diameter
above 1, $n$ points can span more than a constant times $n\sqrt{\log n}$ unit
distances. This is a second proof of the answer yes, with a stronger bound,
and the site's curator credits it.
