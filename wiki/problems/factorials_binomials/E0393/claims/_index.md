# problems/factorials_binomials/E0393/claims

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0393/_index|..]]

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0393/claims/1992_10_01_berend_osgood|1992_10_01_berend_osgood]]: Berend and Osgood prove, in a refereed paper, that for every integer
polynomial $P$ of degree at least $2$ the $n$ for which $P(x)=n!$ is solvable
have density zero. Hence for each fixed $m$ there are $o(N)$ values $n\le N$
with $f(n)=m$, a partial result.

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0393/claims/2002_01_01_luca|2002_01_01_luca]]: Luca proves that the abc conjecture implies that $P(x)=n!$ has finitely many
solutions for every integer polynomial $P$ of degree at least $2$, a refereed
but conditional result. Through the problem's reduction, it gives
$f(n)\to\infty$ under abc.

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0393/claims/2022_04_18_bui_pratt_zaharescu|2022_04_18_bui_pratt_zaharescu]]: Bui, Pratt and Zaharescu prove that, for fixed $P$ of degree at least $2$, at
most $CN^{33/34}$ of the $n\in[N,2N)$ make $s\cdot n!=P(x)$ solvable. Hence
$F_m(N)=O_m(N^{33/34})$, a refereed partial result.

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0393/claims/2026_05_03_turturean|2026_05_03_turturean]]: David Turturean's write-up claims that, under the abc conjecture,
$n-O(\log n)\le f(n)\le n-2$ for all large $n$. It was built from Tao's thread
sketches by an audit-and-revise scaffold querying ChatGPT-5.5-Pro, and it is
conditional and unreviewed.
