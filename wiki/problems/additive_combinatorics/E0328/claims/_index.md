# problems/additive_combinatorics/E0328/claims

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0328/_index|..]]

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0328/claims/1980_03_01_erdos|1980_03_01_erdos]]: Erdős's refereed 1980 paper uses Ramsey's theorem to build sequences with at
most $C$ representations per sum that no finite partition splits below $C$. It
covers $C=2$, $C=3$, every power of two and every half central binomial
coefficient.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0328/claims/1985_01_01_nesetril_rodl|1985_01_01_nesetril_rodl]]: Nešetřil and Rödl prove that for every integer $C\ge2$ there is a set of
natural numbers whose pairwise sums each have at most $C$ representations, and
which no partition into finitely many parts reduces to fewer than $C$
representations in every part. This answers the question no.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0328/claims/2026_06_18_axiommath|2026_06_18_axiommath]]: A Lean 4 development by AxiomProver, published by AxiomMath, refutes the
site's wording (ordered pairs, under which $C=2$ fails trivially) by the
powers of two at $C=2$. It does not answer the corrected Statement (sums of
two distinct elements), so it does not count toward the problem's standing.
