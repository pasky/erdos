# problems/discrepancy/E0177/claims

[[problems/discrepancy/E0177/_index|..]]

[[problems/discrepancy/E0177/claims/1964_01_01_roth|1964_01_01_roth]]: Roth's 1964 theorem says that a set of integers up to $N$ with density
strictly between $0$ and $1$ has a progression of difference at most $N^{1/2}$
with discrepancy of order $N^{1/4}$. It is refereed, in Acta Arith. 9 (1964),
257-260.

[[problems/discrepancy/E0177/claims/1966_01_01_cantor_erdos_schreiber_straus|1966_01_01_cantor_erdos_schreiber_straus]]: In 1966 Erdős reported that Cantor, Schreiber, Straus and he built a sign
function with bounded partial sums along every single arithmetic progression,
with the bound $L(d)<c^d d!$. The report is a journal publication, in Mat.
Lapok 17.

[[problems/discrepancy/E0177/claims/2017_05_30_beck|2017_05_30_beck]]: Beck's 2017 chapter in an edited volume proves that for every $\epsilon>0$
there is a $\pm1$ coloring of the integers whose partial sums along every
arithmetic progression of common difference $d$ are at most $d^{8+\epsilon}$.

[[problems/discrepancy/E0177/claims/2026_09_19_korsky|2026_09_19_korsky]]: Samuel Korsky's 2026 manuscript claims a $\pm1$ coloring of the integers whose
discrepancy on every finite arithmetic progression of common difference $d$ is
at most $C\,d^{5/2+2\sqrt2}$ for a constant $C$.
