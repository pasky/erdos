***

**Claim.** E. Alkan, *A generalization of the Hardy-Littlewood conjecture*,
Integers 22 (2022), #A53, Theorem 1 (p. 4): if a set $P$ of primes has
counting function $\pi_P(x)=c\pi(x)+O(x/(\log x)^3)$ for a constant
$0<c\le1$, then there are effective constants $K,x_0>0$, depending on $P$,
such that

$$
\pi_P(x+y)<\pi_P(x)+\pi_P(y)
$$

for all $x,y\ge x_0$ with $Kx/\log x\le y\le x$. With $P$ the set of all
primes the hypothesis holds with $c=1$, and the theorem gives the problem's
inequality, strictly, on that region. Corollary 1 (pp. 4--5) deduces that at
most $O(X^2/\log X)$ pairs with entries at most $X$ fail it. The paper is
compiled at
[[../library/primes/alkan_2022_generalization_hardy_littlewood_conjecture/_index|its card]],
which holds the published article. Its conditional violations under the
prime $k$-tuples conjecture, display (4.5), rest on the estimate recorded on
[[problems/primes/E0855/claims/1973_01_01_hensley_richards|Hensley and Richards's claim page]].

**Covers.** Every pair with both entries at least $x_0$ whose smaller entry
$u$ and larger entry $v$ satisfy $u\ge Kv/\log v$. For large $v$ this region
lies inside the range of
[[problems/primes/E0855/claims/2002_01_01_dusart|Dusart's claim]], so the
claim adds the strict inequality there and no new pairs. Not covered: the
pairs with $u<Kv/\log v$, which include pairs with both entries arbitrarily
large, so the problem as posed is not settled.
