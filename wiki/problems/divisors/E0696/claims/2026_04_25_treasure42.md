***

**Claim.** For all but $o(x)$ integers $n\le x$,

$$
\tfrac{1}{20}\log_* x\le h(n)\le H(n)\le 4\log_* x,
$$

where $\log_*$ is the iterated logarithm. Hence $h(n)$ and $H(n)$ both have
order $\log_* n$ for almost all $n$, and the set of $n\le x$ with
$H(n)/h(n)>80$ has size $o(x)$, so the problem's second question, whether
$H(n)/h(n)\to\infty$ for almost all $n$, is answered in the negative.

> The proposed theorem is that, for all but o(x) integers n <= x,\[
> \frac{1}{20}\log_* x \le h(n)\le H(n)\le 4\log_* x. \]Consequently, the number
> of integers n <= x for which H(n)/h(n) > 80 is o(x), so H(n)/h(n) would not
> tend to infinity on a density-one set.

> The proof is a log-star barrier argument. The upper bound for H(n) uses a
> bad-pair union bound: if d < e and e ≡ 1 (mod d), then (d,e)=1, so asking for
> both d | n and e | n costs 1/(de). Excluding short steps forces large
> divisor-chain steps to grow at least like exp(sqrt d), which gives H(n) <<
> log_* x.
>
> The lower bound for h(n) constructs a prime chain through intervals\[
> Y_1=\exp(\exp(\sqrt{\log_* x})),\qquad Y_{j+1}=\exp(\exp(Y_j^2)). \]The
> transfer from this finite prime-divisibility model back to integers n <= x is
> done modulo the primorial M over the primes up to Y_k. Each event depending
> only on which primes up to Y_k divide n is a union of residue classes modulo
> M, and the Chinese remainder theorem identifies the uniform residue model
> exactly with independent divisibility events, with probability 1/p for each
> prime p. The tower estimate gives M=o(x), so this product-model calculation
> transfers to natural density up to o(1).
>
> The main analytic input is Lemma 2.3, a reciprocal prime mass lemma derived
> from the pi-form of Siegel--Walfisz plus partial summation. In particular,
> uniformly for primes p <= y,\[ \sum_{\substack{Z<q\le X\\ q\ {\rm prime}\\
> q\equiv 1\pmod p}}\frac{1}{q}\gg y, \]where Z=exp(exp y) and X=exp(exp(y^2)).
>
> I would especially appreciate checks of: the pi-form Siegel--Walfisz input;
> the partial summation error term, uniformly in p <= y; the CRT/product-model
> transfer; the bad-pair union bound for the upper bound.

**Argument.** The upper bound is a union bound over bad pairs: consecutive
chain members $d<e$ with $e\equiv1\pmod d$ are coprime, so both dividing $n$
costs a factor $1/(de)$, and excluding short steps forces a divisor chain to
grow like an exponential tower, giving $H(n)\ll\log_* x$. The lower bound
builds a prime chain through the windows $Y_1=\exp\exp\sqrt{\log_* x}$ and
$Y_{j+1}=\exp\exp(Y_j^2)$, using a reciprocal prime-mass lemma derived from
the Siegel--Walfisz theorem and partial summation, and transfers the
independent-prime model to the integers $n\le x$ through the Chinese
remainder theorem modulo a primorial that is $o(x)$. The write-up is the
Overleaf draft linked above.

**Acceptance.** Reviewed: the erdosproblems.com curator, Thomas Bloom,
summarized the lower-bound and upper-bound arguments from this write-up on 11
May 2026. On erdosproblems.com (page last edited 11 May 2026) the curator
records the two-sided bound $\log_* n\ll h(n)\le H(n)\ll\log_* n$ for almost all
$n$, attributes the proofs to GPT 5.5, and marked the problem solved. The lower
bound is a quantitative form of the argument Wouter van Doorn sketched on the
thread on 14 October 2025 for $h(n)\to\infty$ almost always. No refereed
publication and no Lean proof of this write-up exist. The Lean developments on
