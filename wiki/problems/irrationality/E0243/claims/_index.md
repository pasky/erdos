# problems/irrationality/E0243/claims

[[problems/irrationality/E0243/_index|..]]

[[problems/irrationality/E0243/claims/1964_01_01_erdos_straus|1964_01_01_erdos_straus]]: Erdős and Straus's 1964 Theorem 3 proves that if $\limsup n_k^2/n_{k+1}\le1$
and a limsup condition on the lcm of the terms holds, then $\sum1/n_k$ is
rational exactly when the Sylvester recurrence holds eventually.

[[problems/irrationality/E0243/claims/2001_01_01_duverney|2001_01_01_duverney]]: Duverney's 2001 Corollary 3.2 proves that if $\sum_n(u_{n+1}/u_n^2-1)$
converges, then $\sum1/u_n$ is rational exactly when the Sylvester recurrence
holds eventually. The corollary also allows signs on the terms.

[[problems/irrationality/E0243/claims/2025_04_08_koizumi|2025_04_08_koizumi]]: Koizumi's Corollary 1 proves that a sequence of positive integers with
$2/3\le a_n^2/a_{n+1}\le4/3$ and reciprocal sum $1$ is Sylvester's sequence.
Koizumi's Theorem 3 reduces the problem to a conjecture on pseudo-greedy
expansions.

[[problems/irrationality/E0243/claims/2026_09_11_cook|2026_09_11_cook]]: Cook, with a write-up by Astra and other AI agents, claims that the Sylvester
recurrence holds eventually whenever the product-weighted error
$(P_n/a_n)(a_n^2/a_{n+1}-1)$ is bounded above.
