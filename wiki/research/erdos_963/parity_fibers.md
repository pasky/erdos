# Parity fibers and the rank-one odd obstruction

***

The parity-fiber argument improves the proved rank-defect threshold from
two to three. It also isolates a necessary condition on a least-dimensional
counterexample to the **stronger odd-slice bound**. It does not settle the
unrestricted target.

## A parity-prescribed integer realization

Let $L$ be a free abelian group of finite rank, $X\subset L$ finite,
and $f:L\to\mathbb R$ a homomorphism positive on $X$. Suppose $f$
preserves exactly the zero ternary relations on $X$, and let
$\chi:L\to\mathbb F_2$ be a character with $\chi(x)=1$ for $x\in X$.
Then $X$ has a positive odd integer realization preserving all ternary
relations and distinctness.

Choose a basis $b_1,\ldots,b_r$ of $L$. For large $t>0$, choose
integers $m_i\equiv\chi(b_i)\pmod2$ with
$\lvert m_i-tf(b_i)\rvert\le1$, and let the integer functional
$\ell_t$ send $b_i$ to $m_i$. On the finite family of ternary
combinations of $X$, the error $\ell_t-tf$ is bounded independently
of $t$. Thus all nonzero combinations retain their signs when $t$
is sufficiently large; zero combinations remain zero. The prescribed
parity makes all $\ell_t(x)$ odd. Positivity and distinctness follow
from the one- and two-term combinations.

If the supplied positive functional kills some nonzero ternary
combinations, first perturb it within the positive cone to avoid the
finitely many corresponding proper hyperplanes. This realizes the
relations of the lattice configuration, rather than those additional
relations of the original functional.

## The fiber inequalities

Let $P\subset L$, $L=\langle P\rangle_{\mathbb Z}$, have rank $r$,
and suppose a positive real evaluation preserves all ternary relations
on $P$. Put $d=d(P)$. Let

$$
F(k)=\max\{|O|:O\subset\mathbb Z_{>0}\text{ odd},\ d(O)\le k\},
\quad
G(k)=\max\{|Q|:Q\subset\mathbb R_{>0},\ d(Q)\le k\},
$$

with $G(0)=0$. The elementary ternary-span bound makes these maxima
finite. Then

$$
|P|\le(2^r-1)F(d-r+1)+G(d-r). \tag{1}
$$

If a character $\lambda:L\to\mathbb F_2$ equals one on every
element of $P$, then

$$
|P|\le2^{r-1}F(d-r+1). \tag{2}
$$


To prove this, partition $P$ into fibers $P_v$ modulo $2L$.
The occurring residues span $L/2L\cong\mathbb F_2^r$. For $v\ne0$,
extend $v$ to a basis using $r-1$ occurring residues, represented
by $b_1,\ldots,b_{r-1}\in P$. Any dissociated subset $B\subseteq P_v$
remains dissociated after adjoining these representatives. Indeed, a
ternary relation reduced modulo $2L$ forces every coefficient on
the $b_i$ to vanish, and then dissociation of $B$ applies. Therefore

$$
d(P_v)\le d-r+1\quad(v\ne0).
$$

Adjoining a full residue basis to a dissociated subset of $P_0$
similarly gives $d(P_0)\le d-r$.

For each nonzero $v$, choose a character taking value one on $v$.
The realization lemma shows that $|P_v|\le F(d-r+1)$; also
$|P_0|\le G(d-r)$. There are $2^r-1$ nonzero residues, proving
(1). Under the hypothesis of (2), only the $2^{r-1}$ residues
with $\lambda(v)=1$ can occur.

## Application to intrinsic rank

Use the universal rational lift from [[research/erdos_963/corank_one|the
intrinsic-rank note]], and let $L$ be its generated integer lattice. Its rank
is the intrinsic ternary-relation rank $\rho$. The positive evaluation
preserves all ternary relations, so (1) applies. For an original positive odd
integer set, its evaluation modulo two gives the character required for (2).

The established small cases in [[research/erdos_963/proof_sketch|the proof
sketch]] give

$$
F(k)\le2^{k-1}\quad(1\le k\le4),\qquad
G(k)\le2^k-1\quad(0\le k\le3).
$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{\rho\ge d(P)-3\ \Longrightarrow\ |P|\le2^{d(P)}-1.} \tag{3}
$$

For positive odd sets under the same rank hypothesis,

$$
\boxed{|P|\le2^{d(P)-1}.} \tag{4}
$$

Indeed, put $t=d-\rho\le3$ in (1) to obtain
$(2^\rho-1)2^t+(2^t-1)=2^d-1$; (2) gives (4).

## The short-relation obstruction to odd induction

Suppose $d$ is the least dimension for which the stronger odd bound
fails, and let

$$
O\subset\mathbb Z_{>0}\text{ be odd},\qquad d(O)=d,\qquad
|O|>2^{d-1}.
$$

Let $R\subseteq\mathbb Z^O$ be generated by all genuine zero ternary
relations supported on at most $d+1$ coordinates, and put

$$
H=\mathbb Z^O/R,\qquad s=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}(H/2H).
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{s=1.} \tag{5}
$$


The original integer evaluation descends to $H$. Modulo two it gives
a character $\lambda$ taking value one on every coordinate column,
so $s\ge1$. If $s\ge2$, choose a character $\chi$ different from
both zero and $\lambda$. Its values on the generating columns are
not constant. Let $O_0,O_1$ be the two nonempty color classes.

Both classes have dimension at most $d-1$. If $O_0$ contained a
dissociated $d$-subset, adjoin an element of $O_1$. A dependence
on these $d+1$ original elements belongs to the defining relations
of $H$. Applying $\chi$ forces the coefficient of the adjoined
element to vanish, contradicting dissociation. For a $d$-subset
of $O_1$, use $\chi+\lambda$ instead.

By minimality of $d$, each color class has at most $2^{d-2}$
elements. Their union cannot be $O$, a contradiction proving (5).

Thus the supports of the short relations span the full even-weight
hyperplane in $\mathbb F_2^O$. Select $|O|-1$ independent rows
modulo two. Their integer relation matrix has rank $|O|-1$, with
an odd maximal minor, and annihilates the original weight vector.
Its rational row span is therefore the entire rational relation
space of $O$. In particular, the rational universal lift imposing
only these short relations already has rank one.

The short relations cannot all be balanced: otherwise both the
all-ones vector and the distinct original weights would lie in the
rational kernel, contradicting nullity one.

### A restriction on the common difference divisor

Normalize the odd weights to have greatest common divisor one, and put

$$
h=\gcd\{x-y:x,y\in O\}.
$$

Then $h$ is even, and

$$
h\le d-1.                                                   \tag{6}
$$

Indeed, every weight is congruent to a fixed unit modulo $h$, so the
coefficient sum of every integer relation is divisible by $h$. A
nonbalanced short relation exists by the preceding paragraph. Positivity
requires both signs; hence its nonzero coefficient sum has absolute value
at most $d-1$, proving (6).

More precisely, some nonbalanced short relation has coefficient sum of
(2)-adic valuation exactly $v_2(h)$. Let $K$ be the full integer
kernel of the primitive weight vector. The short-relation lattice $R$
has odd index in $K$: its rows span $K/2K$, since they span the
even-weight hyperplane and $K$ is a primitive corank-one lattice.
The coefficient-sum map $\sigma:K\to\mathbb Z$ has image $h\mathbb Z$.
For the reverse inclusion in this assertion, prescribe $\sum_i z_i=h$
and eliminate the last variable. The equation $\sum_i x_i z_i=0$
then becomes

$$
\sum_{i<n}(x_i-x_n)z_i=-x_nh,
$$

which is soluble in integers by the definition of $h$. The image
$\sigma(R)$ has odd index in $\sigma(K)$, as it is a quotient of
$K/R$. Its generator therefore has valuation $v_2(h)$. The image is
generated by the coefficient sums of the short relations, so at least
one of those generators has exactly this valuation.

This rules out an excessively fine common congruence class in a
least-dimensional odd counterexample, but does not exclude the typical
case $h=2$.

These restrictions concern a least-dimensional failure of the
**strong odd bound**. They do not, without further odd-bound hypotheses,
prove that a least-dimensional failure of the ordinary target has
intrinsic rank one.

## Remaining gap

No argument is known here for a rank-one short-relation presentation
whose supports span the entire even-weight binary hyperplane. In this
case every nonconstant binary coloring is crossed oddly by some short
relation; the two-color induction above cannot be applied. Positivity,
distinctness, and this binary condition have not been shown to force
a dissociated $(d+1)$-subset, nor has a violating odd set been found.

### Alternative-representation generation is false

The existing [[research/erdos_963/matching_obstruction|seven-point example]]

$$
O=\{3,15,31,33,35,41,55\}
$$

refutes the proposed generation reduction for **every** choice of
maximum dissociated $D$. Every five-subset is maximum, and every
representation of either omitted element on $D$ has full support.
It is unique: subtracting two full-support representations and
dividing by two would give a nonzero ternary relation on $D$.
Thus differences of alternative representations generate zero.

Nevertheless the seven displayed six-term relations in the cited
note have coefficient matrix congruent modulo two to $I+J$ of
order seven. Its kernel over $\mathbb F_2$ consists exactly of
the constant vectors, so it has rank six. The actual weight vector
annihilates the integer matrix; its rational rank is therefore
exactly six, and the intrinsic rank of $O$ is one.

For example, on $D=\{3,15,31,33,35\}$ the two unique outside
representations are

$$
41=-3+15+31+33-35,\qquad
55=3+15-31+33+35.
$$

Keeping these two equations while deforming $D$ does not preserve
the other short relations. This is a counterexample to the auxiliary
generation assertion, not to the logarithmic bound.

### A further restriction in dimension five

The [[research/erdos_963/congruence_extension|extended unit-congruence theorem]]
proves $|O|\le16$ whenever $d(O)\le5$ and $O$ lies in one odd
class modulo four. Consequently a primitive least-dimensional
failure of the strong odd bound with $d=5$ has

$$
\gcd\{x-y:x,y\in O\}=2.
$$

Indeed, (6) makes this positive even divisor at most four, and
the new theorem excludes four. The case of common difference
divisor two remains unresolved.
