## Weakest-step and strongest-attack assessment

**Valuations and the single failure.** The report rederives the window
count and Legendre carry identity, then keeps the contribution above
$A_p$ as a nonnegative count outside the selected coordinate. It never
equates an untruncated valuation with that of the offset in the potentially
cancelling case. Small primes with positive extra depth, exceptional large
primes, regular primes, and primes above $k$ each receive their own
coverage argument. The chosen failure prime cannot be regular: regularity
gives $A_p=\sigma_p=1$. At the chosen prime the retained deletion
calculation gives the strict deficit

$$
v_{p_*}(n-E)-v_{p_*}\binom nk
=v_{p_*}\!\left(k\binom{k-1}{E}\right)>0.
$$

This tests exactly the required failure, including arbitrarily increased
valuation of the multiplier. The alternative carry-tail derivation has the
same sign and does not assume the desired nondivisibility. The reported
diagnostic is arithmetically sound: $44102-6=44096=64\cdot689$, whereas
$2-6=-4$ has 2-adic valuation two. Its residues through 64 and at 128
support the stated six carry levels. This is a genuine challenge to the
tempting shortcut, not evidence extrapolated from one example.

**Common realization and admissibility.** The report checks finite
half-open pigeonhole boxes, a strictly positive index difference, and the
empty regular-prime case. Its strict residue margin keeps the actual
residue between 1 and $2E-1$, below $k\bmod p$, so signed approximation
cannot wrap through zero. The source's finite prime universe is explicit
in its anchor setup, $p\le k$ bound, and separate treatment of larger
primes; making that universe uniform across the notation is routine
reconstruction. Taking the least integer satisfying the anchors is a
legitimate choice for the construction's upper-bound calculation.

The supplementary admissibility check also uses only those anchors. Each
prime power dividing $k$ is covered by a small anchor or a large
exceptional square, hence $k\mid S$; the forced anchor gives $S>k$.
Thus $S\ge2k$, and the same permitted construction with $E=1$ supplies
all-$k$ nonemptiness. This does not depend on the unread small-$k$
attribution in the source. These short deductions introduce no missing
essential external lemma or replacement construction.

**Quantitative strength and consequences.** This was the strongest
grading challenge to the report's completeness. A conclusion merely
$\log S=o(k)$ cannot support the claimed quantitative scale: for example,
$k/\sqrt{\log k}$ is $o(k)$ but exceeds that scale. The report does not
rely on that insufficient inference. With $L=\log k$ and
$F=k\log L/L$, it substitutes the source's actual anchor estimates to
obtain

$$
\log S=O\!\left(\frac{k\log L}{L^2}+\sqrt{k}L+L^4\right)=o(F).
$$

The three ratios to $F$ are respectively $1/L$,
$L^2/(\sqrt{k}\log L)$, and $L^5/(k\log L)$, each tending to zero.
The approximation cost is $O(F)$ with absolute constants, and
$n\le2Sm$. This is an explicit completion of estimates already in the
source, not a repair using an unprovided theorem. Ceiling errors, the
forced prime cost, and interval overcounting retain their required
inequality directions.

For the first consequence the report separately bounds
$0\le\log n_k/k\le C\log\log k/\log k$. For the second it uses the
exact lcm identity and the permitted $\psi$ asymptotic to obtain
$\log(n_k/(k\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,k-1)))=-k+o(k)$.
Neither deduction strengthens an eventual estimate to an all-order one.
The cost objection therefore fails against the complete report, although
it correctly exposes why the source's weaker summary alone would be
insufficient.
