# problems/number_theory/E0952/claims

[[problems/number_theory/E0952/_index|..]]

[[problems/number_theory/E0952/claims/1997_01_01_gethner_stark|1997_01_01_gethner_stark]]: Gethner and Stark prove that no walk to infinity on the Gaussian primes, from
any starting point, has all its steps of length at most 2. This gives a
negative answer for every step bound up to 2.

[[problems/number_theory/E0952/claims/2019_08_27_das|2019_08_27_das]]: Das's arXiv note of August 2019 claimed that no infinite sequence of distinct
Gaussian primes off the axes has bounded steps. The author withdrew it in
September 2024, saying that the paths of its Theorem 3 miss primes.

[[problems/number_theory/E0952/claims/2024_01_16_stumpenhusen|2024_01_16_stumpenhusen]]: Stumpenhusen's three-page arXiv preprint of January 2024 claimed that no
bounded-step walk on the Gaussian primes reaches infinity. The author withdrew
it the next day, saying that the width of the moat was not correctly computed.

[[problems/number_theory/E0952/claims/2026_09_26_openai|2026_09_26_openai]]: OpenAI's release manuscript proves that for every step bound $D$ the
components of the distance-$D$ graph on the Gaussian primes have bounded size.
So no infinite bounded-step sequence of distinct Gaussian primes exists. The
Lean proof was built here.
