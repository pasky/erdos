# problems/unit_fractions/E0309/claims

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0309/_index|..]]

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0309/claims/1990_06_01_yokota|1990_06_01_yokota]]: Yokota's refereed 1990 theorem shows that the number of integers that are sums
of distinct unit fractions with denominators at most $N$ is at least
$(1/2-o(1))\log N$, so it is not $o(\log N)$. It is the first disproof, and
the site does not cite it.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0309/claims/1997_12_01_yokota|1997_12_01_yokota]]: Yokota's 1997 theorem that the number of integers that are sums of distinct
unit fractions with denominators at most $N$ lies between $\log N-5\log\log N$
and $\log N+1$, so it is not $o(\log N)$. It is refereed and credited by the
site.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0309/claims/1999_12_01_croot|1999_12_01_croot]]: Croot's 1999 Main Theorem: every integer up to the harmonic sum $H_N$ minus
$(\frac92+o(1))(\log\log N)^2/\log N$ is a sum of distinct unit fractions with
denominators at most $N$, so $F(N)=\log N+O(1)$. It is refereed and credited
by the site.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0309/claims/2002_10_01_yokota|2002_10_01_yokota]]: Yokota's 2002 Corollary 1: for large $N$, the number of positive integers that
are sums of distinct unit fractions with denominators at most $N$ is at least
$\lfloor H_N-(\pi^2/3+o(1))(\log\log N)^2/\log N\rfloor$. It is refereed and
credited by the site.
