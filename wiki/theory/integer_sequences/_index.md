# theory/integer_sequences

[[theory/_index|..]]

[[theory/integer_sequences/L123_odd_root_arc_colorings/_index|L123_odd_root_arc_colorings/]]: Coloring the N-th roots of unity by q arcs of argument gives dissociated
classes exactly when q times the least degree of a {-1,0,1} multiple of
the N-th cyclotomic polynomial is at least N; the normalized degree falls
along divisibility, so a positive infimum bounds every odd prime grid.

[[theory/integer_sequences/L124_flat_cyclotomic_degree_ratio/_index|L124_flat_cyclotomic_degree_ratio/]]: Open: the least degree of a {-1,0,1} multiple of the M-th cyclotomic
polynomial is at least a fixed positive fraction of M for all odd
squarefree M; a proof would bound the dissociated coloring number of
every distinct-prime tensor grid of Problem 774.

[[theory/integer_sequences/L20_roots_of_unity_analytic_sidon/_index|L20_roots_of_unity_analytic_sidon/]]: One constant bounds every trigonometric polynomial supported on roots of
unity by the value of some additive character of the discrete additive
group of the complex numbers; a step of the Problem 774 extraction route.

[[theory/integer_sequences/L210_odd_root_three_colorings/_index|L210_odd_root_three_colorings/]]: Open: for every odd squarefree N the N-th roots of unity split into three
dissociated sets; a proof would close the distinct-prime tensor route to a
counterexample for Problem 774 and answer Déchamps's covering question,
and a single order needing four classes would refute it.

[[theory/integer_sequences/L211_fractional_half_cover_obstruction/_index|L211_fractional_half_cover_obstruction/]]: A thirteen-element positive integer set has hereditary dissociation ratio 7/13, fractional covering number 37/19, and dissociated covering number three; exact finite certificates separate weighted half-extraction from two-colorability.

[[theory/integer_sequences/L21_roots_of_unity_proportional_dissociation/_index|L21_roots_of_unity_proportional_dissociation/]]: One integer denominator works for every finite set of complex roots of
unity: each contains an additively dissociated subset of proportional
size; the roots-of-unity analog of Problem 774's hypothesis.

[[theory/integer_sequences/L250_explicit_near_square_smooth_blocks/_index|L250_explicit_near_square_smooth_blocks/]]: For K >= 64 and 0 <= m <= K^(5/2)/128 the block m+1, ..., m+K contains
c(q-t)(q+t) with all three factors in [1, K], so k(n) in Problem 962 is
below max(64, (128n)^(2/5)); an explicit form of Erdős and Sárközy's 1979
exponent 2/5.

[[theory/integer_sequences/L251_averaged_near_square_smooth_blocks/_index|L251_averaged_near_square_smooth_blocks/]]: For K >= 64 and 0 <= m <= 2^(-15) K^(28/11) the block m+1, ..., m+K
contains c(q-t)(q+t) with all three factors in [1, K]; so k(n) in Problem
962 is below max(64, (2^15 n)^(11/28)), improving the exponent 2/5 of
Erdős and Sárközy to 11/28.

[[theory/integer_sequences/L252_nonresidue_blocks_without_smooth_members/_index|L252_nonresidue_blocks_without_smooth_members/]]: For a prime p = 3 (mod 4) with least quadratic nonresidue q, each of p-q+1,
..., p-1 has a prime factor at least q, so k(p) >= q-1 in Problem 962; an
exponent below 1/(4 sqrt(e)) for k(n) would improve Burgess's bound for
these primes.

[[theory/integer_sequences/L253_exponent_pair_smooth_blocks/_index|L253_exponent_pair_smooth_blocks/]]: Bourgain's exponent pair applied to the averaged near-square construction
gives a K-smooth integer in every block (m,m+K] with
m <= K^(97/38-delta), for large K depending on delta, and hence
k(n) <<_epsilon n^(38/97+epsilon) in Problem 962.

[[theory/integer_sequences/L269_simplex_affine_fiber_threshold/_index|L269_simplex_affine_fiber_threshold/]]: In the k-fold tensor of a q-point simplex over a prime-power index field, every affine fiber with nonzero coefficients is rationally independent for k<q and contains an explicit ternary relation for k>=q.

[[theory/integer_sequences/L271_repeated_simplex_extraction/_index|L271_repeated_simplex_extraction/]]: The integer relation lattice of a repeated power-of-two simplex is semistable, and its normalized determinant stays above one at a critical tensor scale, giving one hereditary dissociation constant through the stated stable-lattice and phase-entropy theorems.
