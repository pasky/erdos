***

**Claim.** Samuel Korsky's proof claim on the minimum
$g(n)=\min_{\pi\in S_n}S(\pi)$, submitted on 15 September 2026 to the
proof-claim tab of [[problems/number_theory/E0034/_index|Problem 34]], claims
an elementary construction giving, for every integer $k\ge1$,
$g(2\cdot6^k)\le31^k+35^k+2\cdot6^k\le4\cdot35^k$. Hence
$g(n)=O(n^{\log_635})$ along an infinite sequence, with
$\log_635=1.9842775\ldots<2$, which would refute the two expectations of the
site's commentary, $g(n)\ge n^{2-o(1)}$ and $g(n)\gg S(\iota)$ for the identity
$\iota$ (Konieczny's Questions 3 and 4). The proof is an external document
linked from the tab, and the site lists the entry as a partial proof claim.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Samuel
Korsky (account SamKorsky) on 15 September 2026, giving "GPT Astra" as the AI
used:

> Let $g(n)$ be the minimum number of distinct sums of nonempty consecutive
> intervals in a permutation of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$. We give an elementary
> construction showing that, for every integer $k\geq 1$,\[ g(2\cdot 6^k)\leq
> 31^k+35^k+2\cdot 6^k\leq 4\cdot 35^k. \]Thus $g(n)=O(n^{\log_6 35})$ along an
> infinite sequence, where $\log_6 35=1.9842775\ldots<2$, disproving the last
> two conjectures listed in the problem statement. Notes: The exponent is
> non-optimized; I pushed Astra to search for sharper constructions and was able
> to get the exponent down to $\sim1.86$, though I didn't try too hard. It would
> be very interesting to find the correct exponent - maybe it's even $3/2$!

**Why it is rejected.** It answers a variant, not the Statement.
[[problems/number_theory/E0034/_index|Problem 34]] asks whether every
permutation $\pi\in S_n$ has $S(\pi)=o(n^2)$, a question about the largest
values of $S(\pi)$. The claim answers instead the question the site's
commentary raises about the smallest value $g(n)$, whether
$g(n)\ge n^{2-o(1)}$ or even $g(n)\gg S(\iota)$. An upper bound on $g(n)$
says nothing about whether some permutation has $cn^2$ distinct consecutive
sums, so its mathematics answers the variant and does not settle this problem;
the value `disproved` is its answer to that variant.

**Standing.** Rejected, as answering a variant. The entry carries no comments
and no review on the site, which warns that listing on the tab is not a check
of the proof; this corpus has not read the external document.
