**Claim.** For the $g_k(N)$ of
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0866/_index|Problem 866]], with the
$b_i$ distinct integers of which one may be non-positive (the paper's
reading of the site's definition, Section 3, p. 2), W. van Doorn, *The
cardinality of a set containing the pairwise sums of a fixed number of
integers*, arXiv:2605.00040v1 (28 April 2026), proves
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/doorn_2026_cardinality_set_containing_pairwise_sums_fixed/theorem_1|Theorem 1]],
$g_3(N)=1$ for all $N\ge3$ (with $g_3(1)=g_3(2)=2$, and Theorem 2: integers
$0\le b_1<b_2<b_3$ suffice);
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/doorn_2026_cardinality_set_containing_pairwise_sums_fixed/theorem_3|Theorem 3]],
$g_4(N)=3$ for all $N\ge2$, the lower bound from the odd integers together
with $2N-2$ and $2N$;
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/doorn_2026_cardinality_set_containing_pairwise_sums_fixed/theorem_5|Theorem 5]],
$g_5(N)\ge4$ for $N\ge3$, from the odd integers together with $2N-4$,
$2N-2$ and $2N$; and
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/doorn_2026_cardinality_set_containing_pairwise_sums_fixed/theorem_8|Theorem 8]],

$$
g_5(N)<1.2\cdot10^8\quad\text{for all }N
$$

(the constant $113{,}591{,}719$, from the 1975 argument with explicit
constants and a Sidon-set bound of O'Bryant), so that $g_5$ is bounded
where the 1975 paper's $\log N$ lower bound holds only for the
positive-integer variant $h_5$ (Theorem 4). The paper also states, with a
proof sketch,
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/doorn_2026_cardinality_set_containing_pairwise_sums_fixed/theorem_9|Theorem 9]],
$g_k(N)\le h_k(N)<4N^{1-2^{2-k}}$ for $k\ge3$ and large $N$, a slight
sharpening of the 1975 exponent, and $h_4(N)\le2270$ for the positive
variant. Its Section 2 declares that ChatGPT (model GPT-5.3 Instant) was
used for brainstorming and produced the proof of Theorem 3 on its own, and
that the automated theorem prover Aristotle, from Harmonic, produced Lean
formalizations of every statement marked with a checkmark, improving the
$h_4$ bound on the way. The paper is compiled at
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/doorn_2026_cardinality_set_containing_pairwise_sums_fixed/_index|its source card]];
nothing is independently reviewed.

**Submission note.** Posted to the site's forum by Wouter van Doorn (account
Woett) on 4 May 2026:

> I have just uploaded my paper on $g_k(N)$. In it, the following bounds are
> proven: \begin{align*} g_3(N) &= 1 \text{ for all } N \ge 3, \\ g_4(N) &= 3
> \text{ for all } N \ge 2, \\ g_5(N) &< 1.2 \cdot 10^8 \text{ for all } N \ge
> 1, \\ g_k(N) &< 4N^{1 - 2^{2-k}} \text{ for all } k \ge 3 \text{ and all large
> enough } N. \end{align*}Furthermore, these results are all verified in Lean.
>
> The paper is my own work (mostly relying on the ideas by
> Choi-Erdős-Szemerédi), but specifically the $g_4$ result was obtained with the
> help of ChatGPT. Further noteworthy is that the bounds on $g_3$ and $g_5$
> seemingly contradict the earlier claims of $g_3 = 2$ and $g_5(N) \asymp \log
> N$. To reconcile this apparent contradiction, let us (as in my previous
> comment) define $h_k(N)$ as the smallest integer such that for all subsets $A
> \subseteq \{1, 2, \ldots, 2N\}$ with $|A| \ge N + h_k(N)$ there exist distinct
> positive integers $b_1, b_2, \ldots, b_k$ such that all $\binom{k}{2}$
> pairwise sums are in $A$.
>
> As it turns out, $g_k$ and $h_k$ are not the same (in fact, they seem to
> differ quite often), although we do in general have
> $$
> g_k(n) \le h_k(n) \le g_{k+1}(n)
> $$
> for all $k$ and $n$. And even though the stated Choi-Erdős-Szemerédi results
> do not necessarily all hold for $g_k$, they are all recovered for $h_k$.
>
> Furthermore, with the help of Aristotle I managed to make their $h_4 \ll 1$
> result explicit, while the above general bound for $g_k$ also holds for $h_k$.
> For completeness' sake, these are the best known bounds for $h_k$ currently:
> \begin{align*} h_3(N) &= 2 \text{ for all } N \ge 4, \\ h_4(N) &\le 2270
> \text{ for all } N \ge 1, \\ h_5(N) &\asymp \log N, \\ h_6(N) &\asymp \sqrt{N}
> (\text{which is also the correct order of growth for } g_6(N)), \\ h_k(N) &<
> 4N^{1 - 2^{2-k}} \text{ for all } k \ge 3 \text{ and all large enough } N.
> \end{align*}Finally, this problem is also mentioned in [Er72, p. 83], which
> can be added as a reference.

Posted to the site's forum by Wouter van Doorn (account Woett) on 26 February
2026:

> It is clear that the $b_i$ need to be distinct, and maybe one can add this
> requirement to the problem statement for completeness' sake. What is less
> clear however, is whether the $b_i$ need to be positive.
>
> In all theorem statements in [CES75], they refer to the elements in $A
> \subseteq \{1, 2, \ldots, 2N\}$ as positive integers, while the $b_i$ are just
> referred to as integers. This terminology is also used in [Er92c, p.41],
> suggesting that the $b_i$ are not required to be positive (although one can
> note that at most one can be non-positive, otherwise we have a negative sum).
>
> With this in mind, define $h_k(N)$ as the smallest integer such that for all
> subsets $A \subseteq \{1, 2, \ldots, 2N\}$ with $|A| \ge N + h_k(N)$ there
> exist distinct positive integers $b_1, b_2, \ldots, b_k$ such that all
> $\binom{k}{2}$ pairwise sums are in $A$. With $g_k(N)$ defined as in the
> problem statement (i.e. without the positivity requirement), in general we
> then have $h_{k-1}(N) \le g_k(N) \le h_k(N)$.
>
> Annoyingly, $g_k(N)$ and $h_k(N)$ are not the same function, and the lower
> bounds that Choi, Erdős and Szemerédi claim for $k = 3$ and $k = 5$ do not
> actually work for $g_k(N)$. In particular, the example (mentioned at the end
> of the remarks) of taking $A \subset \{1, 2, \ldots, 2N\}$ to be the union
> $$
> \{1, 3, \ldots, 2N-1\} \cup \{2, 4, \ldots, 2^{\lfloor \log_2 2N \rfloor} \}
> $$
> for a set for which supposedly no $b_1, b_2, \ldots, b_5$ exist is incorrect
> as stated; with $b_1 = -1$, $b_2 = 2$, $b_3 = 3$, $b_4 = 5$, $b_5 = 6$, one
> can check that all pairwise sums do belong to $A$ whenever $N \ge 6$. The
> example does work to show the lower bound $h_5(N) \gg \log N$, but at the
> moment I have no non-trivial lower bound for $g_5(N)$. As far as I know, it
> could be the case that we actually have $g_5(N) \ll 1$ (Edit 06-04-2026: which
> I might be able to show now!).
>
> Turning to $k = 3$, the truth is that $g_k(N)$ is equal to $1$ for all $N \ge
> 3$. Which is in contrast to the equality $h_3(N) = 2$ that Choi, Erdős and
> Szemerédi actually (unwittingly?) prove for all $N \ge 4$.
>
> To see why $g_3(N) \le 1$ (and therefore $g_3(N) = 1$) holds for all $N \ge
> 3$, let $A \subseteq \{1, 2, \ldots, 2N\}$ be any set with $|A| \ge N + 1$,
> and assume $N \ge 3$. Then $A$ contains $\{m, m+1\}$ for some odd $m \in \{1,
> 3, \ldots, 2N-1\}$ by the pigeonhole principle. If $A \setminus \{m \}$ only
> contains even integers, then $\{2, 4, 6\} \subseteq \{2, 4, \ldots, 2N\}
> \subset A$ and we can choose $b_1 = 0$, $b_2 = 2$, $b_3 = 4$. On the other
> hand, if $A$ contains an odd integer $2j+1 \neq m$, then we can choose $b_1 =
> j$, $b_2 = j+1$, $b_3 = m-j$, which are quickly checked to be distinct by the
> assumption $2j+1 \neq m$ and the fact that $m$ is odd. By the way, for $N = 2$
> one can note that the example $A = \{2, 3, 4\}$ shows $g_3(2) = 2$.
>
> I'll hopefully have more to say in the not too distant future, but I first
> wanted to clear up the above subtlety.

**Covers.** The exact values $g_3(N)=1$ ($N\ge3$) and $g_4(N)=3$
($N\ge2$), and the order of $g_5(N)$: bounded, with
$4\le g_5(N)<1.2\cdot10^8$ for $N\ge3$; a later release claims the smaller
constant $3{,}519{,}219$ on
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0866/claims/2026_07_08_erlbacher|its own page]].
Not covered: the value of $g_5$ (the paper's Section 7 reports no
counterexample to $g_5(N)\le5$ up to $N=15$ and does not exclude
$g_5(N)\le4$ for large $N$); the constants for $k=6$, whose order is
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0866/claims/1975_01_01_choi_erdos_szemeredi|Choi, Erdős and Szemerédi's]];
the order of $g_k$ for $k\ge7$; and the exponent for large $k$, which
Theorem 9 sharpens without closing.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki. Theorem 8 follows the 1975 argument
with explicit constants, but rests on the paper's own proof, not on the
1975 results recorded on their claim page.
