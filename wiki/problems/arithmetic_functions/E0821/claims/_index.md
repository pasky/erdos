# problems/arithmetic_functions/E0821/claims

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0821/_index|..]]

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0821/claims/1998_01_01_baker_harman|1998_01_01_baker_harman]]: Corollary 1 of Baker and Harman's refereed 1998 paper gives infinitely many
$n$ with more than $n^{0.7039}$ preimages under Euler's totient. This settles
the question for every $\epsilon\ge0.2961$.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0821/claims/2022_11_14_lichtman|2022_11_14_lichtman]]: Corollary 1.3 of Lichtman's 2022 preprint gives infinitely many $n$ with at
least $n^{0.7156}$ preimages under Euler's totient. This settles the question
for every $\epsilon\ge0.2844$ and is erdosproblems.com's best known bound.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0821/claims/2026_09_24_openai|2026_09_24_openai]]: The OpenAI release claims that for every $\varepsilon>0$ infinitely many $n$
have more than $n^{1-\varepsilon}$ preimages under Euler's totient. It derives
this from a count of primes whose predecessor is $x^\delta$-smooth.
