# problems/distance_problems/E0132/claims

[[problems/distance_problems/E0132/_index|..]]

[[problems/distance_problems/E0132/claims/1995_06_01_erdos_fishburn|1995_06_01_erdos_fishburn]]: Erdős and Fishburn's small-case classifications show that every five-point and
every six-point planar set has two distinct distances each occurring between
at most $n$ pairs. These are the cases $n=5$ and $n=6$ of the first question,
and the paper is refereed.

[[problems/distance_problems/E0132/claims/2025_05_07_clemen_dumitrescu_liu|2025_05_07_clemen_dumitrescu_liu]]: Theorems 1.2 and 1.3 of Clemen, Dumitrescu and Liu show that for $n\ge5$,
every convex $n$-point planar set, and every $n$-point set with small enough
first two convex layers, has a distance besides the diameter occurring at most
$n$ times. The paper is refereed in Acta Math. Hungar.

[[problems/distance_problems/E0132/claims/2026_01_28_zeraoulia|2026_01_28_zeraoulia]]: Zeraoulia's self-published note claims that every seven-point planar set has
two distinct distances each occurring between at most seven pairs, and that an
eight-point counterexample would have multiplicities $(1,9,9,9)$ with a unique
diametral pair.

[[problems/distance_problems/E0132/claims/2026_07_05_marchetto|2026_07_05_marchetto]]: Marchetto's note, with checking code, claims that every planar set of $n$
points with $7\le n\le13$ has two distinct distances each occurring at most
$n$ times. The cases $n=11$ and $12$ rest on Wei's 11-point 5-distance list,
and the rest are unconditional.

[[problems/distance_problems/E0132/claims/2026_07_25_ienjoymath|2026_07_25_ienjoymath]]: An anonymous note posted in the thread claims an independent proof, checked by
the claimant's script, of the first question for $n=7$, $8$, $9$, $10$ and
$13$. It also claims a $9n/7$ lower bound for the extremal problem of Clemen,
Dumitrescu and Liu.

[[problems/distance_problems/E0132/claims/2026_08_23_beller|2026_08_23_beller]]: Beller's forum proof claim states that every eight-point planar set has two
distinct distances each occurring between at most eight pairs, the case $n=8$
of the first question. It deduces this in Lean from three published results.

[[problems/distance_problems/E0132/claims/2026_09_25_jones|2026_09_25_jones]]: Jones's forum claim states that under a convex-layer condition the diameter
and the second-largest distance each occur at most $n$ times. It also bounds
the smaller multiplicity of the second-largest and smallest distances by
$4n/3$ plus a constant.
