***

**Claim.** Every finite simple bipartite graph with at most 26 vertices is
4-choosable: for every assignment of a set $L(v)$ of exactly four colours to
each vertex $v$ there is a proper colouring $c$ with $c(v)\in L(v)$ for every
$v$. Equivalently, the least number $n(4)$ of vertices of a bipartite graph
$G$ with $\chi_L(G)>4$ satisfies

$$
n(4)\ \ge\ 27 .
$$

**Covers.** A lower bound for the instance $k=4$ of
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0629/_index|Problem 629]]. The problem page records
$23\le n(4)\le40$: the lower bound from Erdős, Rubin and Taylor's inequality
$m(k)\le n(k)$ with Östergård's $m(4)=23$, the upper bound from Corollary 3.1
of Hanson, MacGillivray and Toft. This claim raises the lower bound to $27$,
also above the $25$ that Erdős, Rubin and Taylor's unproved footnote would
give, so that $27\le n(4)\le40$. It does not determine $n(4)$, and it says
nothing about $n(k)$ for $k\ge5$. The weaker bound $n(4)\ge26$ of
[[theory/combinatorics/L25_bipartite_four_choosability/_index|L25]] (tier 1),
a separate palette-covering argument, is implied and is not a premise.

**Credit.** The palette-minimization and layer-counting method is that of
[[../library/graph_coloring/hanson_1996_choosability_bipartite_graphs/_index|Hanson, MacGillivray and Toft]],
Lemmas 1--3 and Theorem 1. Plasma AI's proof derives the specializations it
needs in full and uses no unproved source premise. It reduces a
counterexample to a complete bipartite graph of order 26, forces exactly 17
colours and 26 pairwise distinct lists, and excludes every side split by an
explicit integer weighting of palette subsets. No literature novelty check is
claimed.

**Standing.** Tier 1. The whole-claim
[[theory/combinatorics/L78_bipartite_order_26_four_choosable/evidence/verify/order_26_examination|independent examination]]
and its
[[theory/combinatorics/L78_bipartite_order_26_four_choosable/evidence/verify/order_26_grade|separate passing grade]],
both dated 2026-10-14, record **refutation-failed** for four-choosability of
every bipartite graph through order 26 and for the consequence $n(4)\ge27$.
The grade keeps a disclosed preparation-concurrency breach, ruled not to void
mathematical independence, and two qualifications on weight support and
side-count metadata; neither refutes the theorem. No Lean proof is claimed.
Catalog acceptance is pending.
