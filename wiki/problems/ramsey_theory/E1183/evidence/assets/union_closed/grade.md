### Box, permutation and usable shape

The source's box induction and the report's alternative induction both work.
In the latter, for $q=2^{t-1}$ the lower-dimensional inequality is applied to
$y\mapsto f(a,y)f(a',y)$. Finite summation, Jensen and
Cauchy--Schwarz give

$$
\mathcal B_t(f)\ge
\mathbb E_{a,a'}(\mathbb E_y f(a,y)f(a',y))^q
\ge\bigl(\mathbb E_y(\mathbb E_a f(a,y))^2\bigr)^q
\ge(\mathbb E f)^{2^t}.
$$

The dimension decreases, the functions are nonnegative, and $q\ge1$;
$t=0$ and $t=1$ have the stated base/equality cases. The original source
route uses Cauchy--Schwarz before its slice induction; the report's order
really differs. Neither route uses an unproved conclusion from the lower
theorem.

For the color indicators, $\sum_c p_c^{2^t}\ge k^{1-2^t}$ also permits zero
color densities. Coordinate coincidences cost at most $t/(L+1)$. For each
remaining pair the grid-to-set map has disjoint nonempty generators
$X_i$ of sizes $h_i-\ell_i$, base size $u=r+\sum_i\ell_i$, and
$(u-r)+\sum_i a_i\le tL$. The permutation lemma follows by bijecting the
base, generators and complement, so its shape probability applies to this
cube. Repeated sizes and nonunique generator orderings cause no multiplicity
error.

The strongest selection objection is that degenerate pairs could dominate,
or that averaging is being silently conditioned or made uniform over shapes.
The report explicitly uses nonnegative weights of total at most one on
nondegenerate pairs. Consequently their weighted sum is at most the largest
admissible $\rho_\chi$. A nondegenerate pair exists when $L\ge1$, including
when the displayed general lower bound is nonpositive. Thus selection needs
neither a uniform shape distribution nor a shape good for every coloring.
For $L=tk^{2^t}$, the loss is strictly below $k^{-2^t}$ and the surviving
probability exceeds $(k-1)k^{-2^t}\ge k^{-2^t}$. The bounds
$u-r\le M-t$ and $1\le a_i\le M$ are precisely what slices consumes.

The auxiliary uniform-measure wording needs a nonempty grid in positive

### Slices, one common arrangement, gluing and the constant

Put $m=\lfloor(n-1)/M\rfloor$ and $s=\lfloor m/2\rfloor$, initially
with $s\ge t$, and
$\mathcal P=\{(j,A):s<j\le m,\ A\subseteq[s],\ |A|=t\}$.
For each pair $(j,A)$, freezing $K=[m]\setminus(A\cup\{j\})$ leaves exactly
$m-t-1$ frozen coordinates. Put
$R=\sum_{i<j,\ i\notin A}\beta_i$ and $r=R+1$. Since
$j\ge t+1$ and $mM\le n-1$,

$$
R\le(m-2)M,\qquad r+M\le(m-1)M+1\le n.
$$

The supplied shape extends the slice with $\beta_j=u-r+1$ and
$\beta_{a_{(i)}}=a_i$; the base is exactly $R+\beta_j=u$.
All new entries lie in $[M]$, including the endpoint $j=t+1$.
Different frozen assignments remain different complete vectors. This proves
the count $M^{m-t-1}$ without assuming simultaneous good extensions.

The report then counts incidences across **all** pairs, complete vectors and
permutations. Dividing
$|\mathcal P|M^{m-t-1}n!k^{-2^t}$ by $M^mn!$ gives one common arrangement
with at least $|\mathcal P|/(k^{2^t}M^{t+1})$ monochromatic cubes. Incompatible
good choices for different pairs do not defeat an incidence average.
Independence between cube events is never asserted.

I also tested the second possible failure: many cubes can share a top or
overlap, so counting them alone does not count family members. For
$H(j,B)=C_j\setminus Y_B$, $B,B'\subseteq[s]$ and $s<j\le j'$,
the blockwise identity
$H(j,B)\cup H(j',B')=H(j',B\cap B')$ puts every union in the selected cube
at the larger level. A color class of cubes therefore glues to an actual
monochromatic union-closed family. Its bases $H(j,A)$ are distinct:
the highest present nonempty block determines $j$, and the missing blocks
in $[s]$ determine $A$. This is the required injection into members, even
for cubes sharing a top. The loss is exactly a factor $k$, not a factor
depending on cube overlap.

For $n\ge4M(t+1)$, integrality gives
$n\le M(m+1)\le2M(s+1)$, hence $s\ge2t+1$, $n\le4Ms$ and $m-s\ge s$.
Each factor of $\binom st=\prod_{i=0}^{t-1}(s-i)/(t-i)$ is at least
$s/t$. Thus

$$
|\mathcal P|\ge\frac{n^{t+1}}{t^t(4M)^{t+1}},
\qquad
|\mathcal F|\ge
\frac{n^{t+1}}{k^{2^t+1}t^t(4M^2)^{t+1}}.
$$

$M\ge4t^2\ge t+1$ gives $n<4M^2$ and $c_{k,t}n^{t+1}<1$.
At the cutoff, $m=4t+3$ and $s=2t+1$; there is no floor gap.
This confirms the report's full polynomial derivation, rather than just its
last arithmetic line.

For the threshold, the exact expansion is

$$
\log_2c_{2,t}^{-1}

The report bounds the remainder by

$2t^3-t^2+t+1>0$. Therefore the total is at most $(t+2)^2 2^t$.
This covers every $t\ge1$, including $c_{2,1}=2^{-15}$ and threshold
$18$, and preserves threshold equality.

### Random tuples, nonempty unions, VC transfer and strict floors

The private elements of an independent $d$-tuple distinguish exactly
$2^d-1$ nonempty-index unions. They need not make the sets disjoint.
For a fixed tuple, independent fair colors on these distinct sets give
$2^{2-2^d}$, so the union bound over at most $2^{nd}$ ordered tuples is
$2^{nd+2-2^d}<1$. Dependence between different tuple events is harmless.
The strict hypothesis supplies an actual coloring with no bad tuple.

The report's common-core test
$\mathcal H=\{\{p\}\cup I:I\subseteq[d]\}$ is a direct test of the
interface: it is union-closed, omits the empty set, and shatters $[d]$,
while the sets $\{p,i\}$ overlap. It exposes the erroneous alternatives of
counting $2^d$ unions or requiring disjoint generators. The actual proof
passes the test. Binary union closure supplies every nonempty finite union;
shattering a $d$-set supplies singleton-trace members and thus a private
element tuple. Hence every nonempty monochromatic family in the selected
coloring has VC dimension at most $d-1$. The external finite cardinality
interface then applies exactly with $q=d-1$.

The empty-family VC convention is not defined in the excerpt. The report
explicitly bounds the empty family's size directly, and the minimax maximum
can use a nonempty family. No definition is silently changed. If $d>n$, the
binomial sum is $2^n$ and the bound holds directly. The strict hypothesis is
impossible for $d=1,2$ and positive $n$; at $n=1$ the first admissible $d=3$
also gives the trivial full sum. These checks close the apparent small-case
loopholes without adding an empty-member assumption.

For the explicit choice set $a=\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor\ge1$,
$b=\lfloor\log_2a\rfloor\ge0$. The integer inequalities
$2^{a+1}\ge n+1$, $2^{b+1}\ge a+1$, and $b+1\le a$ imply

$$
2^d\ge2(n+1)(a+1)>2n(a+1)+2\ge nd+2.
$$

The strict middle margin is $2a>0$. At $n=3$, $d=4$ and $16>14$;
$n=2$ is also covered. The binomial estimate uses zero terms beyond $n$
and $\binom nj\le n^j\le n^{d-1}$ for $0\le j<d$.
Finally $d\le D$ compares both positive coefficient and exponent in the
right direction. Thus the explicit bound is valid across every floor jump.
