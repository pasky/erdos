# problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0133/claims

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0133/_index|..]]

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0133/claims/1984_01_01_hanson_seyffarth|1984_01_01_hanson_seyffarth]]: Cayley graphs of cyclic groups on symmetric complete sum-free sets are
triangle-free graphs of diameter two with degree of order $\sqrt n$ along an
infinite sequence of $n$. So $f(n)/\sqrt n$ does not tend to infinity.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0133/claims/1994_01_01_furedi_seress|1994_01_01_furedi_seress]]: Theorem 6.1 of Füredi and Seress gives, for every large $n$, a triangle-free
graph of diameter two on $n$ vertices with maximum degree at most
$\frac{2}{\sqrt3}\sqrt n$ plus a lower-order term. So $f(n)$ has order
$\sqrt n$.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0133/claims/2017_03_12_haviv_levy|2017_03_12_haviv_levy]]: Theorem 1.5 of Haviv and Levy gives every large cyclic group $\mathbb Z_n$ a
symmetric complete sum-free subset of size $O(\sqrt n)$. Its Cayley graph is
triangle-free of diameter two with degree $O(\sqrt n)$, so $f(n)$ has order
$\sqrt n$.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0133/claims/2026_09_15_korsky|2026_09_15_korsky]]: A proof claim by Korsky on the site's proof-claim tab asserts that
$f(n)=(1+o(1))\sqrt n$, the sharp constant Alon conjectured, using a fibered
polarity graph. It is unreviewed, and the proof is in an external file.
