**Claim.** The answer to
[[problems/integer_sequences/E1057/_index|Problem 1057]] is yes: if $C(x)$
counts the Carmichael numbers in $[1,x]$, then

$$
\frac{\log C(x)}{\log x}\to1\qquad(x\to\infty),
$$

that is, $C(x)=x^{1-o(1)}$. The proof is a Lean 4 file of 393,097 lines
(about 17.7 MB) with no import statement, submitted under the handle JenW1N
to the bounty site Conjectures.io, which had published the problem as the
formal-conjectures declaration `Erdos1057.erdos_1057` with its answer left
open. The file's final theorem, `Bounty.target`, takes the type of that
declaration with the answer `True` and is closed by
`WeakZeroFree.target_of_selected_residue_bounds` applied to the proved
`candidate_selected_residue_bounds`, with no hypothesis left. Read from the
statements of its main declarations, the route is that of Alford, Granville and
Pomerance [AGP94]: Carmichael numbers are built as products of primes $p$ from a
set whose predecessors $p-1$ are smooth, a count of such products gives
$C(x)\ge x^{1-\varepsilon}$ for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$ and all large $x$
(`NearLinearLowerBound`), and the limit follows with the trivial $C(x)\le x$
(`target_of_lower_bound`). Its two analytic inputs are a zero-free half-plane
$\operatorname{Re}s>23/24$ for every primitive nonprincipal Dirichlet
$L$-function (`WeakZeroFree.actual_zero_free`), which it turns into the bound
$\psi(x,\chi)\ll x^{95/96}\log(qx)$ for the prime sum twisted by a primitive
character $\chi$ of modulus $q$
(`WeakZeroFree.primitive_twistedPsi_power_saving`), and a supply of primes with
smooth predecessors, which a large part of the file's own modules prove by
moment and Fourier estimates. A comment block left in the file from an earlier
stage calls it an incomplete formalization whose smooth-prime and zero-free
inputs are unproved; the final declaration takes neither as a hypothesis. No
write-up accompanies the proof, and the file names no author for it and declares
no AI system.

**The formal statement.** The task is the formal-conjectures declaration
`Erdos1057.erdos_1057`
([`FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1057.lean` at the catalog's commit of 2026-09-18](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/6fbb54f24ccc2e64dcfaffc28c58950e377110d2/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1057.lean),
the file's last change) with its open answer fixed to true: the limit of
`Real.log (carmichaelCounting x) / Real.log x` as the real $x$ tends to infinity
is $1$. There `carmichaelCounting x` is the number of natural numbers $n\le x$
with `IsCarmichael n`, and `IsCarmichael n`
([`FormalConjecturesForMathlib/NumberTheory/Carmichael.lean`](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/6fbb54f24ccc2e64dcfaffc28c58950e377110d2/FormalConjecturesForMathlib/NumberTheory/Carmichael.lean)
at the same commit) says that $n$ is a Fermat pseudoprime to every base $b\ge1$
coprime to $n$, that is, $n>1$ is composite and $n\mid b^{n-1}-1$ for all such
$b$, the usual definition. Since $1\le C(x)\le x$ for $x\ge561$, the limit
$\log C(x)/\log x\to1$ is exactly $C(x)=x^{1-o(1)}$, and the answer true is the
affirmative answer. So the formal target is the problem's question with the same
quantity, limit and polarity. The site's record prints the target with the
answer `True`. The task pins a catalog commit that the site builds from a public
catalog commit and an audit patch, which changes `1057.lean` only by writing
`IsCarmichael` as `_root_.IsCarmichael`; this corpus's replay reproduced that
commit exactly and found the target's type identical to its statement.

**The zero-free input.** `WeakZeroFree.actual_zero_free` is derived from
`OAI.ApplicationAudit.nonprincipal_LFunction_ne_zero`, in a module of the
file marked `PinnedNonprincipal`, which is not a module of the OpenAI
release. That theorem states that $L(s,\chi)\ne0$ for every nonprincipal
Dirichlet character $\chi$ and every $s$ with $\operatorname{Re}s>23/24$.
Its proof applies lemmas of the release's inlined modules directly, among
them the fixed-shell models `actual_fixedShellModels` of
`OAI.NumberTheory.DirichletL.RowCompletion.ZeroFreeRegion`. The file's copy
of that module keeps `CanonicalRowCompletion.dirichletTarget_unconditional`,
that no Dirichlet $L$-function vanishes in $\operatorname{Re}s>23/24$ apart
from the pole at $s=1$, but omits the release's theorem
`ShortDraft.dirichlet_LFunction_ne_zero`. The inlined tree is the release's
formalization of its zero-free half-plane work, whose stronger claimed
half-plane $\operatorname{Re}s>7/8$ is
[[../library/primes/openai_2026_quasi_riemann_hypothesis_zero_free_half_plane_7_8/theorem_1_1|Theorem
1.1 of *The Quasi-Riemann Hypothesis: A Zero-Free Half-Plane Re(s)>7/8*]].
The file uses only the weaker half-plane $\operatorname{Re}s>23/24$, names
that seven-eighths proof as the source of a few extracted numerical lemmas,
and does not inline its main theorem.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki is a premise: the file proves its
inputs in Lean. It inlines external Lean code, each piece marked by its
source module or a port note. The largest part is 286 modules
`OAI.NumberTheory.DirichletL.*` of the openai/math Lean release (Apache-2.0,
its first commit, of 6 October 2026). With that tree come 15 modules of Adam
Benenson's Lean proof of the Rellich--Kondrachov theorem (Apache-2.0,
"Authors: Adam Benenson"), a dependency the release's lakefile pins. The
file also ports 12 modules `OAI.NumberTheory.Ostmann.*` from the same
release commit (a zero-count argument for Dirichlet $L$-functions), and
modules of the PrimeNumberTheoremAnd project (Apache-2.0): complex-analysis
auxiliaries carrying Michael Stoll's copyright and elementary Mertens
estimates.

**Acceptance.** Formalized. This corpus's verification rebuilt the record's
solution file, unchanged, on the repository's pinned toolchain,
`leanprover/lean4:v4.34.1` with Mathlib at the revision
`lean/lake-manifest.json` pins. The kernel replayed, from an empty environment,
every declaration the target `Bounty.target` depends on, Lean core, Mathlib,
formal-conjectures and the inlined modules included, and accepted them all. The
target's axioms are exactly `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. Its
type, and every definition its statement reaches, are identical to the
formal-conjectures statement of the site's task, which **The formal statement.**
above reads as the problem's question, with `IsCarmichael` the usual definition.
Acceptance rests on this replay and that check.
