# problems/unit_fractions/E0283/claims

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0283/_index|..]]

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0283/claims/1963_03_17_graham|1963_03_17_graham]]: Graham's refereed 1963 theorem that every integer above $77$ is a sum of
distinct positive integers whose reciprocals sum to $1$. This is the case
$p(x)=x$ of the problem, with the exact threshold.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0283/claims/2018_01_18_alekseyev|2018_01_18_alekseyev]]: Alekseyev's theorem that $8542$ is the largest integer that is not a sum of
squares of distinct positive integers whose reciprocals sum to $1$, the case
$p(x)=x^2$ of the problem. It is a published book chapter, and its refereeing
is not recorded.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0283/claims/2025_08_31_van_doorn|2025_08_31_van_doorn]]: Van Doorn's manuscript reduces the binomial case $p(x)=ax^d+b$ to a finite
computation and carries it out for $x+b$ ($b\le5000$), $5x+b$ ($b<375$, prime
to $5$) and $x^2+b$ ($b\le800$). It is unrefereed, and part of it has a Lean
formalization.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0283/claims/2026_05_03_price|2026_05_03_price]]: An argument that GPT-5.5 Pro generated at Price's prompting proves the general
case of the problem, with $1$ replaced by any positive rational. The
erdosproblems.com curator accepted it, and its third-party Lean formalizations
have not been built locally.
