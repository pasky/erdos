# problems/covering_systems/E0202/claims

[[problems/covering_systems/E0202/_index|..]]

[[problems/covering_systems/E0202/claims/1968_01_01_erdos_szemeredi|1968_01_01_erdos_szemeredi]]: Erdős and Szemerédi proved in Acta Arithmetica (1968) that the largest number
of disjoint residue classes with distinct moduli at most $N$ is $o(N)$. This
proves the Erdős–Stein conjecture and is accepted on the refereed paper.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0202/claims/2002_08_30_croot|2002_08_30_croot]]: Croot proved in Acta Arithmetica (2003) that the largest number of disjoint
residue classes with distinct moduli at most $N$ lies between
$NL(N)^{-\sqrt2-o(1)}$ and $NL(N)^{-1/6+o(1)}$, where
$L(N)=\exp(\sqrt{\log N\log\log N})$. It is accepted on the refereed paper.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0202/claims/2005_01_01_chen|2005_01_01_chen]]: Chen proved in Acta Arithmetica (2005) that the largest number of disjoint
residue classes with distinct moduli at most $N$ is at most
$NL(N)^{-1/2+o(1)}$ for arbitrary moduli, where
$L(N)=\exp(\sqrt{\log N\log\log N})$. It is accepted on the refereed paper.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0202/claims/2013_01_01_de_la_breteche_ford_vandehey|2013_01_01_de_la_breteche_ford_vandehey]]: De la Bretèche, Ford and Vandehey proved in Acta Arithmetica (2013) that the
largest number of disjoint residue classes with distinct moduli at most $N$
lies between $NL(N)^{-1-o(1)}$ and $NL(N)^{-\sqrt3/2+o(1)}$, where
$L(N)=\exp(\sqrt{\log N\log\log N})$. It is accepted on the refereed paper.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0202/claims/2026_04_23_ho|2026_04_23_ho]]: Boon Suan Ho's 2026 manuscript, written with GPT-5.4 Pro, proves the sharp
asymptotic for the largest number of disjoint residue classes with distinct
moduli at most $N$. It is accepted on the erdosproblems.com credit after a
Lean formalization.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0202/claims/2026_04_30_chojecki|2026_04_30_chojecki]]: Przemek Chojecki posted an eight-page unsigned draft, dated 30 April 2026,
hosted on ULAM's site and credited to GPT-5.5 Pro. It claims the sharp upper
bound $f(N)\le NL(N)^{-1+o(1)}$ of Problem 202 by a spread-core route.
