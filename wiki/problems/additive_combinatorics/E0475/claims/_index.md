# problems/additive_combinatorics/E0475/claims

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0475/_index|..]]

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0475/claims/2005_08_10_bode_harborth|2005_08_10_bode_harborth]]: Theorem 2 of Bode and Harborth (Discrete Math. 2005, refereed) proves
Alspach's conjecture for $n-2$ lengths. So every set of $p-2$ nonzero residues
modulo a prime $p$, and the set of all $p-1$, has an ordering with distinct
partial sums.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0475/claims/2018_09_07_hicks_ollis_schmitt|2018_09_07_hicks_ollis_schmitt]]: Theorem 4.6 of Hicks, Ollis and Schmitt (J. Combin. Des. 2019), accepted and
refereed, proves Alspach's conjecture in $\mathbb Z_p$ for size $p-3$. So
every $(p-3)$-subset with nonzero sum has an ordering with distinct partial
sums.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0475/claims/2020_03_12_costa_pellegrini|2020_03_12_costa_pellegrini]]: Proposition 4.2 of Costa and Pellegrini (Arch. Math. 2020), accepted and
refereed, shows that every set of at most twelve nonzero residues modulo any
prime has an ordering with distinct partial sums. The proof uses the
Combinatorial Nullstellensatz.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0475/claims/2024_07_01_kravitz|2024_07_01_kravitz]]: Kravitz's 2024 preprint claims, as its Theorem 1.2, that for every prime $p$
every set of at most $\log p/\log\log p$ nonzero residues has an ordering with
distinct partial sums, by rectification to the integers. There is no journal
version.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0475/claims/2024_09_11_bedert_kravitz|2024_09_11_bedert_kravitz]]: Theorem 1.2 of Bedert and Kravitz (Israel J. Math. 2026), accepted and
refereed, proves that for every $c>0$ and every large prime $p$, every set of
at most $\exp(c(\log p)^{1/4})$ nonzero residues has an ordering with distinct
partial sums.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0475/claims/2026_02_17_pham_sauermann|2026_02_17_pham_sauermann]]: Pham and Sauermann (2026) claim a medium-range theorem that, together with the
small range of Bedert and Kravitz and the large ranges of Bedert, Bucić,
Kravitz, Montgomery and Müyesser, proves the conjecture for all large primes.
