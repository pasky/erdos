# problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0182/claims

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0182/_index|..]]

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0182/claims/1985_12_01_pyber|1985_12_01_pyber]]: Pyber proves that every $n$-vertex graph with at least $ck^2n\log n$ edges,
for a constant $c$, contains a $k$-regular subgraph, so the maximum is
$n^{1+o(1)}$ and the yes-or-no part is answered yes. It was refereed in
Combinatorica 5 (1985).

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0182/claims/1995_01_01_pyber_rodl_szemeredi|1995_01_01_pyber_rodl_szemeredi]]: Pyber, Rödl and Szemerédi give a random bipartite construction with
$cn\log\log n$ edges, for a constant $c$, and no $k$-regular subgraph for any
$k\ge3$, so the maximum is not linear in $n$. It was refereed in J. Combin.
Theory Ser. B 63 (1995).

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0182/claims/2022_04_26_janzer_sudakov|2022_04_26_janzer_sudakov]]: Janzer and Sudakov, Forum Math. Pi 11 (2023), prove in Theorem 1.2 that for
every $k$ there is a constant $C(k)$ such that average degree $C(k)\log\log n$
forces a $k$-regular subgraph. So the maximum is at most a constant times
$n\log\log n$, and the answer is yes.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0182/claims/2024_11_18_chakraborti_janzer_methuku_montgomery|2024_11_18_chakraborti_janzer_methuku_montgomery]]: Chakraborti, Janzer, Methuku and Montgomery prove that, for one absolute
constant $C$, average degree $Cr^2\log\log n$ forces an $r$-regular subgraph,
and that the $r^2$ dependence is sharp. So for fixed $k$ the maximum is of
order $k^2n\log\log n$ (Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 2026).

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0182/claims/2026_08_24_korsky|2026_08_24_korsky]]: A proof claim by Samuel Korsky, posted on the site's proof-claim tab, asserts
that $n^{1+o(1)}$ edges force an induced $k$-regular subgraph, hence a
$k$-regular one, which is the problem's yes-or-no part. It is unreviewed, and
the proof is in an external file.
