**Claim.** Let $q$ be a prime power, $n\ge1$ an integer,
$A_1,\ldots,A_n$ finite-dimensional vector spaces over $\mathbb F_q$, and
$C$ a linear subspace of $A_1\oplus\cdots\oplus A_n$. A word
$c=(c_1,\ldots,c_n)$ represents the $n$-element set
$S(c)=\{(i,c_i):1\le i\le n\}$, and distinct words represent distinct
sets. A $k$-sunflower consists of $k$ distinct sets whose pairwise
intersections all equal one set, which may be empty. For every integer
$k\ge3$ and every $a\in A_1\oplus\cdots\oplus A_n$, if the $n$-uniform
family

$$
\mathcal F=\{S(c):c\in a+C\}
$$

contains no $k$-sunflower, then

$$
\lvert\mathcal F\rvert=\lvert C\rvert<k^{2n}.
$$

More precisely, $\dim C<n\lceil\log_qk\rceil$ and
$\lvert C\rvert\le q^{n\lceil\log_qk\rceil-1}$; if $q\ge k$, then
$C=\{0\}$ and $\mathcal F$ has one member. The bound comes from a
subspace theorem: for every integer $s\ge1$, if $\dim C\ge sn$, there is
an $s$-dimensional subspace $U$ of $C$ on which each coordinate
projection is zero or injective, and for every $a$ the $q^s$ sets $S(c)$,
$c\in a+U$, form a $q^s$-sunflower.

**Covers.** The special case of
[[problems/set_systems/E0020/_index|Problem 20]] for affine linear word
families: the $n$-uniform families $\{S(c):c\in a+C\}$ above, over any
finite field $\mathbb F_q$ and with coordinate alphabets $A_i$ of any
finite dimension. For every $k\ge3$, every such family with at least
$k^{2n}$ members contains a $k$-sunflower, and the base $k^2$ does not
depend on the field or on the alphabet dimensions. The result does not
bound $f(n,k)$, which ranges over all $n$-uniform families: a family need
not have this form, and passing from an arbitrary word family to its
affine span can introduce new words and new sunflowers. Families outside
the class are not addressed, and the general question is not settled by
this claim.

**Credit.** The independent-representative criterion in the proof is the
classical linear version of Hall's condition; the proof gives it in full
and uses no external theorem. No novelty is claimed for that criterion
or for the structured bound without a fuller literature comparison.
