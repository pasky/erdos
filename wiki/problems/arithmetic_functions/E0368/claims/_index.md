# problems/arithmetic_functions/E0368/claims

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0368/_index|..]]

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0368/claims/1918_06_01_polya|1918_06_01_polya]]: Pólya's 1918 theorem, applied here to $n(n+1)$, says that the largest prime
factor of $f(n)$ tends to infinity when $f$ is a product of two distinct
linear factors. The theorem appeared in a refereed paper in Mathematische
Zeitschrift.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0368/claims/1935_01_01_mahler|1935_01_01_mahler]]: Mahler's 1935 theorem on the largest prime factor of $Dx^2-A$ with
$A\in\{\pm1,\pm2\}$, applied to $(2n+1)^2-1=4n(n+1)$, gives
$F(n)>(\log\log n)/(1+\varepsilon)$ for all large $n$. The source is a journal
paper.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0368/claims/1967_01_01_schinzel|1967_01_01_schinzel]]: Schinzel's theorem on the greatest prime factor of quadratic values along a
sequence gives infinitely many $n$ for which the largest prime factor of
$n(n+1)$ is at most $n^{O(1/\log\log\log n)}$. The theorem appeared in a
refereed paper in Acta Arithmetica.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0368/claims/2023_12_06_pasten|2023_12_06_pasten]]: Pasten's 2024 theorem on the largest prime factor of $xy(x+y)$, taken at $x=1$
and $y=n$, gives the lower bound $F(n)\gg(\log\log n)^2/\log\log\log n$, the
best known. The theorem appeared in a refereed paper in Inventiones
Mathematicae.
