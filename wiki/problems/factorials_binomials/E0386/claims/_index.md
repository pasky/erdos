# problems/factorials_binomials/E0386/claims

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0386/_index|..]]

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0386/claims/2026_09_29_martins|2026_09_29_martins]]: Pedro Martins's manuscript restricts the primes and the block length of any
binomial coefficient equal to a product of consecutive primes, and gives
finiteness for each fixed $k$ and block length. It does not decide whether
there are infinitely many solutions.

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0386/claims/2026_09_30_mysore|2026_09_30_mysore]]: Srihari Mysore's draft note claims that any $\binom n2$ equal to a product of
consecutive primes, other than $n=4,6,15,21,715$, has at least $1924$ prime
factors. The argument uses Dusart's prime-gap estimates and computer searches.
