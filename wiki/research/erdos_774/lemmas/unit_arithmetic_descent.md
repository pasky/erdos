# Arithmetic units, not equal norms, are needed for height descent

***

## Standing

The
[saturated prime staircase](prime_staircase_obstruction.md) supplies actual
root units with mandatory coordinates and full height-two kernel rank of
density tending to one. Consequently integrality, even with saturation,
does not rescue the high-density descent proposed here. The nonintegral
example and binary restrictions below retain their stated scopes.

The [prime-pivot example](prime_pivot_obstruction.md) leaves open a
high-density short-relation descent specific to roots of unity. This page
proves that **equal norms, even at every field embedding, cannot supply it**.
It then proves arithmetic restrictions that distinguish odd roots from the
example. Those restrictions do not yet establish descent or unbounded coloring.

The subsequent [binary-circuit lifting theorem](binary_circuit_lifting.md)
sharpens the distance-five bound to the exact distance nineteen on
distinct-prime grids, using singleton-layer descent and a graph-potential
argument. The simpler residue proof remains useful for isolating the role of
integrality.

## 1. An equal-modulus obstruction of density tending to one

Put
$$
\alpha=(1+i\sqrt{15})/4,\qquad
C_n=\{1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{n-1}\},\quad n\geq3.
$$
The primitive irreducible polynomial of $\alpha$ is
$f(t)=2t^2-t+2$; its two complex roots have modulus one.
The monic minimal polynomial $t^2-t/2+1$ is not integral, so
$\alpha$ is not an algebraic integer and in particular not a root
of unity. Its powers are distinct. Both field embeddings send every column
to a point of modulus one; the normalized trace inner product also gives
every column norm one.

The integer relation lattice of the indexed family $C_n$ has basis
$$
z_j=2e_j-e_{j+1}+2e_{j+2},\qquad 0\leq j\leq n-3.
\tag{1}
$$
Indeed an integer polynomial of degree at most $n-1$ vanishes at
$\alpha$ exactly when it is divisible by $f$. Primitivity and
Gauss's lemma make the quotient integral. Thus (1) is an integral basis,
and the rational rank is two. Each basis vector is a primitive height-two
circuit: a dependence on two powers would make some nonzero power of
$\alpha$ rational, necessarily $\pm1$ by modulus one, which
would make $\alpha$ a root of unity. The basis supports cover every
column and have connected intersection graph.

Every nonzero integer relation polynomial has **even lowest nonzero
coefficient**, because $f(0)=2$. Consequently no nonzero ternary
relation exists: $C_n$ is dissociated. Moreover, two ternary
representations of a nonzero sum cannot have different least used indices.
Their difference would be a relation with lowest coefficient $\pm1$.
The shared least index is therefore present in every representation: it is
a mandatory coordinate.

The [layer argument](height_descent.md) now gives
$$
D\subseteq C_n\otimes S_p\text{ dissociated}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
D\text{ contains no full }p\text{-fiber}.
\tag{2}
$$
For a ternary relation the layer sums agree. A common zero gives a trivial
relation by base dissociation. A common nonzero sum forces its mandatory
coordinate into every layer. Thus every fiber-proper two-coloring is proper.
Nevertheless the span of the height-two relations has density
$(n-2)/n\to1$. This example is **not a root configuration**.

## 2. Binary relation codes of odd roots

Let $C$ be a finite set of $n$ distinct odd-order roots, let
$$
L=\{a\in\mathbb Z^C:\sum_{x\in C}a_xx=0\},\qquad
k=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}L,
$$
and let $\overline L\subseteq\mathbb F_2^C$ be its reduction.
The kernel $L$ is saturated: if $2a\in L$, then $a\in L$.
Hence reduction induces an injection $L/2L\to\mathbb F_2^C$, and
$\dim\overline L=k$. Explicitly, an integral basis cannot become
dependent modulo two, since dividing such a dependence by two and reexpanding
in the basis would force all its coefficients to have been even.

Work in the number field generated by $C$, and reduce its algebraic
integers modulo a prime over two. All roots are units, and reduction is
injective on the finite group of odd-order roots in that field. For a direct
check of injectivity, a nontrivial odd-order root $z$ reducing to one
would give
$0=1+z+\cdots+z^{m-1}\equiv m\equiv1\pmod2$.

For the support $S$ of any word in $\overline L$, reduction of
its integer relation and of the complex-conjugate relation gives
$$
\sum_{x\in S}\bar x=0,
\qquad \sum_{x\in S}\bar x^{-1}=0.
\tag{3}
$$
There are no words of weight one or two: the reduced roots are nonzero and
distinct. For weight three, normalize the reduced roots to $1,a,b$.
Equation (3) gives $a+b=1$ and $ab=1$. They are exactly the
three cube roots of unity. Injectivity of odd-root reduction lifts their
ratios, so the original three roots form a regular three-gon and sum to zero.

Weight four is impossible for any $C$. If the four reduced roots are
$r_i$, their elementary symmetric functions satisfy $e_1=e_3=0$
by (3), since their product is nonzero. The polynomial with these four roots
would be
$t^4+e_2t^2+e_4$, whose derivative in characteristic two is zero.
It cannot have four distinct roots.

If $C$ is dissociated, weight three is excluded as well. Thus
$\overline L$ has minimum distance at least five. The radius-two
Hamming balls about its $2^k$ codewords are disjoint, proving
$$
2^k\left(1+n+\binom n2\right)\leq2^n.
\tag{4}
$$
In particular $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}C\geq \log_2(1+n+\binom n2)$. For
the even-$H$, $H I$-pivot invariant, the code is supported on the $n-k$ nonpivot
coordinates; the sharper bound is
$$
2^k\left(1+(n-k)+\binom{n-k}{2}\right)\leq2^{n-k}.
\tag{5}
$$

## 3. The remaining arithmetic obligation

The basis (1) reduces to individual middle-coordinate vectors of weight one,
violating even the simplest root-unit restriction. Equivalently,
$2x-y+2z=0$ among roots would make the unit $y$ divisible by
two in the ring of algebraic integers. Thus the obstruction in Section 1
cannot be embedded relation-preservingly into roots.

But (4) allows nullity density tending to one, and (5) only sharpens the
already known pivot half-density bound by a lower-order term. Neither proves
a root-specific high-density descent, let alone its required actual-color
matching. The reduced integral kernel must also not be confused with the
entire modular kernel of a nonsaturated column image. Equality holds when
the column image is saturated at two, meaning that its saturation quotient
has no two-torsion; full saturation is stronger than needed. No such
hypothesis is imposed by the binary-circuit lifting theorem.

These arguments were independently internally checked. No numerical search,
theorem-prover run, or external review is claimed.
