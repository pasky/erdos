# problems/unit_fractions/E0321/claims

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0321/_index|..]]

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0321/claims/1975_01_01_bleicher_erdos|1975_01_01_bleicher_erdos]]: Bleicher and Erdős's 1975 Lemma and count: the products of rapidly growing
primes up to $N$ have distinct reciprocal subset sums, so
$R(N)\ge\frac{N}{\log N}\prod_{j=3}^{k+1}\log_jN$.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0321/claims/1976_12_01_bleicher_erdos|1976_12_01_bleicher_erdos]]: Bleicher and Erdős's 1976 Theorem 3 bound for $\log S(N)$, combined with
$2^{R(N)}\le S(N)$, gives
$R(N)\le\frac{1}{\log2}\frac{N\log_rN}{\log N}\prod_{j=3}^{r}\log_jN$ when
$\log_{2r}N\ge1$.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0321/claims/2025_09_12_bettin_grenie_molteni_sanna|2025_09_12_bettin_grenie_molteni_sanna]]: The set $\mathcal U(N)$ in the proof of Bettin, Grenié, Molteni and Sanna's
Theorem 1 has distinct reciprocal subset sums and size of order $N/\log N$
times the iterated-logarithm product. This is the lower half of the order of
$R(N)$.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0321/claims/2026_07_15_young_zhu_luo|2026_07_15_young_zhu_luo]]: Young, Zhu and Luo's AI-assisted claim that the largest subset of
$\{1,\ldots,N\}$ with distinct reciprocal subset sums has size of order
$N/\log N$ times the iterated-logarithm product, via their bound for
$\log S(N)$. It is accepted.
