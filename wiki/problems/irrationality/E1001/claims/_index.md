# problems/irrationality/E1001/claims

[[problems/irrationality/E1001/_index|..]]

[[problems/irrationality/E1001/claims/1958_01_01_erdos_szusz_turan|1958_01_01_erdos_szusz_turan]]: Theorem III of Erdős, Szüsz and Turán's 1958 paper, which posed the problem,
proves that $\lim_{N\to\infty}S(N,A,c)$ exists and equals $12A\log c/\pi^2$
whenever $0<A<c/(1+c^2)$. In this range the approximation intervals do not
overlap.

[[problems/irrationality/E1001/claims/1962_05_01_kesten|1962_05_01_kesten]]: Theorem 2 of Kesten's 1962 paper proves that $\lim_{N\to\infty}S(N,A,c)$
exists for $Ac\le1$. It gives closed forms for
$c/(1+c^2)\le A\le\min(1/2,1/c)$ and for $1/2\le A\le1/c$, extending the
proposers' value in the sparse range.

[[problems/irrationality/E1001/claims/1966_01_01_kesten_sos|1966_01_01_kesten_sos]]: Kesten and Sós show in 1966 that the measure $S(N,A,c)$ converges as
$N\to\infty$, for every $A>0$ and $c\ge1$, without finding the value of the
limit. This is the existence half of the question.

[[problems/irrationality/E1001/claims/2006_01_01_xiong_zaharescu|2006_01_01_xiong_zaharescu]]: Xiong and Zaharescu reprove in 2006 that the limiting measure exists for every
$A>0$ and $c\ge1$. They give a formula for it as a finite alternating sum of
double integrals, from which the closed forms in the known ranges follow.

[[problems/irrationality/E1001/claims/2008_08_01_boca|2008_08_01_boca]]: Boca's 2008 paper proves, according to its abstract, that the limit of
$S(N,A,c)$ exists for all $A>0$ and $c>1$, and identifies it.
