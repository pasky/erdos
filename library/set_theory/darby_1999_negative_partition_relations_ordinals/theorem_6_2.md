***

**Source.** Carl Darby, *Negative partition relations for ordinals
$\omega^{\omega^\alpha}$* (1999), p. 221, with the reference list on p. 222.
The edition is identified on the
[[set_theory/darby_1999_negative_partition_relations_ordinals/_index|source card]].

**Statement.** $\omega^{\omega^2}\to(\omega^{\omega^2},3)^2$: however the pairs
from $\omega^{\omega^2}$ are split into two classes, there is a set of order
type $\omega^{\omega^2}$ with all its pairs in the first class or a set of
three elements with all its pairs in the second.

**Status in the paper.** After proving
[[set_theory/darby_1999_negative_partition_relations_ordinals/theorem_6_1|Theorem 6.1]],
the paper introduces "a chronologically ordered list" of other results it calls
recent (p. 221), and Theorem 6.2, headed "Theorem 6.2 (Darby)", is its first
item. No proof is given, and the reference list (p. 222) contains no work of
Darby's, so the paper does not say where a proof appears. The paragraph after
the list says
that Theorem 6.2 "combined with the results of this paper" exhibits
$\omega^{\omega^2}$ as neither a partition ordinal nor an anti-partition
ordinal. In the paper's sense (p. 205) a partition ordinal satisfies
$\alpha\to(\alpha,n)^2$ for every finite $n$ and an anti-partition ordinal
satisfies $\alpha\not\to(\alpha,3)^2$; $\omega^{\omega^2}$ satisfies the
relation above and, by Theorem 6.1,
$\omega^{\omega^2}\not\to(\omega^{\omega^2},6)^2$.

**Proof.** None in this paper. A published proof of the same relation is
Schipperus's
[[set_theory/schipperus_2010_countable_partition_ordinals/theorem_28|Theorem 28]]
at $\beta=2$, and Schipperus's paper reports (p. 1197) that Darby proved this
case independently.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the statement, the sentence that introduces the
list, the paragraph after it and the reference list were read clause by clause
on the page images (pp. 221--222). There is no proof to check. Nothing here is
independently reviewed.

**Dependencies.** None in the paper.

**Bears on.**

- [[../wiki/problems/set_theory/E0118/_index|#118]]: the positive half of the
  problem's counterexample $\alpha=\omega^{\omega^2}$ with $n=6$. This paper
  states it, proves only the negative half, and itself concludes that
  $\omega^{\omega^2}$ is neither a partition ordinal nor an anti-partition
  ordinal, which in its sense answers the problem's question no.

  stated here under Darby's name with no proof and no reference.
