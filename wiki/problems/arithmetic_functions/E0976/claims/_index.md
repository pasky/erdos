# problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/_index|..]]

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2001_05_01_heath_brown|2001_05_01_heath_brown]]: Heath-Brown proves that $n^3+2$ has a prime factor above $X^{1+10^{-303}}$ for
a positive proportion of $n\in(X,2X]$. So the answer to the first question is
yes for $t^3+2$.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2014_11_28_irving|2014_11_28_irving]]: Irving proves that $n^3+2$ has a prime factor above $X^{1+10^{-52}}$ for a
positive proportion of $n\in(X,2X]$. This improves Heath-Brown's exponent for
$t^3+2$.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2015_06_22_de_la_breteche|2015_06_22_de_la_breteche]]: De la Bretèche proves that if $f$ is an even monic irreducible quartic with
Galois group $V_4$, then $f(n)$ has a prime factor above $X^{1+c}$ for a
positive density of the $n\le X$. So the answer to the first question is yes
for these quartics.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2022_12_07_dartyge_maynard|2022_12_07_dartyge_maynard]]: Dartyge and Maynard prove that if $f$ is a monic irreducible quartic with
Galois group $C_4$ or $D_4$, then $f(m)$ has a prime factor at least $x^{1+c}$
for a positive proportion of $m\in(x,2x]$. So the answer to the first question
is yes for these quartics.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2023_08_19_carella|2023_08_19_carella]]: Carella's arXiv note claims that the greatest prime factor of
$\prod_{x\le n\le2x}(n^2+1)$ is at least $x^{3/2}$ as $x\to\infty$.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2024_04_05_pascadi|2024_04_05_pascadi]]: The proof of Pascadi's Theorem 1.1 shows that the greatest prime factor of
$\prod_{x\le m\le2x}(m^2+1)$ is at least $x^{1.30008}$ for all large $x$. This
answers the first question for $t^2+1$.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2025_05_01_grimmelt_merikoski|2025_05_01_grimmelt_merikoski]]: Grimmelt and Merikoski's arXiv preprint gives, for fixed coprime $a$ and
squarefree $h$ and all large $X$, an $m\in[X,2X]$ for which $am^2+h$ has a
prime factor above $X^{1.312}$. So the answer to the first question would be
yes for these quadratics.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2026_02_03_ermoshin|2026_02_03_ermoshin]]: Ermoshin's arXiv preprint proves that if $f$ is a monic irreducible cubic,
then $f(m)$ has a prime factor above $x^{1+c}$ for a positive proportion of
$m\in[x,2x]$. So the answer to the first question would be yes for these
cubics.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2026_04_16_bhalla|2026_04_16_bhalla]]: Bhalla's unpublished note derives, from an unproved hypothesis on prime values
of polynomials, that the greatest prime factor of $\prod_{m\le n}f(m)$ is at
least a constant times $n^d$ for every irreducible polynomial $f$ of degree
$d$.
