# problems/diophantine_problems/E0979/claims

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0979/_index|..]]

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0979/claims/1937_04_01_erdos|1937_04_01_erdos]]: In refereed work, Erdős proves that infinitely many $n$ have more than
$n^{c/(\log\log n)^2}$ representations as a sum of two squares of primes,
sharpened to $n^{c/\log\log n}$ in Part II. So $\limsup f_2(n)$ is infinite.

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0979/claims/1965_01_01_erdos|1965_01_01_erdos]]: In 1965 Erdős stated that he could also prove $\limsup f_3(n)=\infty$ for sums
of three cubes of primes, in unpublished work that seemed to need special
properties of primes. No proof by him has appeared.

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0979/claims/2026_08_17_kitamura|2026_08_17_kitamura]]: Kenta Kitamura gives an independent Lean 4 proof that the number of
representations of $n$ as a sum of three cubes of primes is unbounded, the
case $k=3$. It is registered as the formal-conjectures proof of that variant
and is not built here.

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0979/claims/2026_08_18_wang_zhang|2026_08_18_wang_zhang]]: Wang and Zhang's preprint proves unconditionally that some integers have
arbitrarily many representations as sums of three cubes of primes, the case
$k=3$. The proof goes through Hecke equidistribution for the Fermat cubic, and
the preprint is not refereed.
