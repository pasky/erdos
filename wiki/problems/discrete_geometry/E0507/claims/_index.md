# problems/discrete_geometry/E0507/claims

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0507/_index|..]]

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0507/claims/1981_12_01_komlos_pintz_szemeredi|1981_12_01_komlos_pintz_szemeredi]]: Komlós, Pintz and Szemerédi prove, in a refereed paper, that any $n$ points in
the unit square span a triangle of area at most
$\exp(c\sqrt{\log n})\,n^{-8/7}$. This gives the exponent $8/7$ for the disk,
since superseded by Cohen, Pohoata and Zakharov.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0507/claims/1982_02_01_komlos_pintz_szemeredi|1982_02_01_komlos_pintz_szemeredi]]: Komlós, Pintz and Szemerédi prove, in a refereed paper, that for all large $n$
some $n$ points of the unit square have every triangle of area at least
$c(\log n)/n^2$. This gives the lower bound $\alpha(n)\gg(\log n)/n^2$ for the
disk.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0507/claims/2017_03_08_ellmann|2017_03_08_ellmann]]: Ellmann's arXiv preprint places $n$ points in the unit circle with every
triangle of area at least of order $n^{-3/2}(\log n)^{-7/2}$. Its latest
version calls the method heuristic, and the bound rests on an unproved
uniformity assumption.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0507/claims/2020_06_05_agama|2020_06_05_agama]]: Agama's arXiv preprint asserts a lower bound of order $(\log n)/n^{3/2}$ and
an upper bound $n^{-3/2+\varepsilon}$ for the disk, and so an estimate of
$\alpha(n)$. It is rejected because OpenAI's release preprint records a
collinear triple in its lower bound.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0507/claims/2024_09_11_cohen_pohoata_zakharov|2024_09_11_cohen_pohoata_zakharov]]: Cohen, Pohoata and Zakharov prove, in a refereed Inventiones paper, that any
$n$ points in the unit square span a triangle of area at most $n^{-7/6+o(1)}$.
This gives the upper bound $\alpha(n)\ll n^{-7/6+o(1)}$ for the disk.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0507/claims/2026_09_25_openai|2026_09_25_openai]]: OpenAI's preprint claims that for every large $n$ some $n$ points in the unit
square have every triangle of area at least $cn^{-2+\eta}$, for an absolute
and extremely small $\eta$, which refutes the almost-$n^{-2}$ formulation. The
bound carries over to the disk.
