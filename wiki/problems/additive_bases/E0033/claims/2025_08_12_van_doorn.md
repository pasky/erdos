***

**Claim.** There is a set $A$ of positive integers such that every positive
integer is $n^2+a$ with an integer $n\ge0$ and $a\in A$, and, writing
$A(x)=\lvert A\cap\{1,\ldots,\lfloor x\rfloor\}\rvert$,

$$
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{A(x)}{\sqrt x}=2\varphi^{5/2}\approx6.66,
\qquad\varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}.
$$

The set is the union over $j\ge0$ of the integers in a window starting at
$\varphi^{2j}$ of length about $2\varphi^{j+1/2}$. It represents every positive
integer, so it qualifies for the first question of
[[problems/additive_bases/E0033/_index|Problem 33]], and the smallest possible
value of the limsup asked for there is at most $2\varphi^{5/2}$. This is
Theorem 1 of Wouter van Doorn's two-page note *The smallest set such that every
positive integer is the sum of a square and an element from this set*, in the
version uploaded on 12 August 2025 (the first preprint link above). The version
uploaded on 3 October 2025 (the second preprint link), the edition the
[[../library/additive_bases/doorn_2025_smallest_set_such_that_every_positive/_index|source card]]
records, narrows the windows to
$[\varphi^{2j},\varphi^{2j}+2\varphi^{j+1/2}-1)$ and proves the uniform bound
$A(x)<2\varphi^{5/2}\sqrt x$ for every $x>0$
([[../library/additive_bases/doorn_2025_smallest_set_such_that_every_positive/main_theorem|its Theorem]]);
a remark after the proof states that the limsup of this set is still
$2\varphi^{5/2}$
([[../library/additive_bases/doorn_2025_smallest_set_such_that_every_positive/limsup_sharpness|the remark]]).
The note says that it optimizes the construction Erdős gave and provides the
first known improvement on the upper bound.

**Submission note.** Posted to the erdosproblems.com forum by Wouter van Doorn
(account Woett) on 12 August 2025, in the problem's thread:

> The smallest possible value of $\limsup \frac{\left|A \cap \{1, 2, \ldots, N
> \} \right|}{N^{1/2}}$ is at most $2 \varphi^{5/2} \approx 6.66$, with
> $\varphi$ equal to the golden ratio. I have uploaded a proof here.

Posted to the same thread by the same account on 3 October 2025, in reply to
that post:

> I have very marginally improved the construction to get $\left|A \cap \{1, 2,
> \ldots, N\} \right| < 2 \varphi^{5/2} \sqrt{N}$ for all $N \in \mathbb{N}$,
> even though the limit superior is still the same.

**Covers.** An upper bound of $2\varphi^{5/2}\approx6.66$ for the smallest
possible limsup, the first question of
[[problems/additive_bases/E0033/_index|Problem 33]]. The limsup of this set
equals the bound, so the construction gives nothing smaller, and the claim does
not determine the smallest limsup. The liminf question is not covered; the
lower bound $4/\pi$ of
[[problems/additive_bases/E0033/claims/1993_07_01_cilleruelo|Cilleruelo]] and
[[problems/additive_bases/E0033/claims/1995_03_01_habsieger|Habsieger]] bounds
the smallest limsup from below.

**Claimant.** Wouter van Doorn, whose erdosproblems.com profile gives that name
for the account Woett, posted the bound with the note in his public GitHub
repository; the note names no assistance. erdosproblems.com marked both posts
as addressed by an update of the problem page, and the curator, Thomas Bloom,
replied to the second with thanks. The erdosproblems.com remarks (page last
edited 27 December 2025, accessed 2026-10-18) state that van Doorn has a
construction with $\lvert A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}\rvert/N^{1/2}<2\phi^{5/2}$ for
all $N$.

**Relation to the later bounds.** Two later constructions bound the smallest
limsup below this one, and both are pending:
[[problems/additive_bases/E0033/claims/2026_09_07_donteatllamas|the write-up of 7 September 2026]]
in the same thread, with $13\sqrt2/4\approx4.596$, and
[[problems/additive_bases/E0033/claims/2026_10_19_plasma_ai|Plasma AI's construction]],
with $9/2$.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Standing.** Claimed. The erdosproblems.com curator credits the construction
in the problem's remarks, but Bloom's catalog labeled the problem OPEN, so that
remark is not acceptance. The note has no journal publication, and no Lean
proof is known. The library's result pages check the theorem's proof and the
limsup equality step by step against the print; that is this corpus's own
reading, not an independent review. The problem's erdosproblems.com
proof-claims tab holds no claim (accessed 2026-10-18).
