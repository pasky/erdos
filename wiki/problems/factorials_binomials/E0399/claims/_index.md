# problems/factorials_binomials/E0399/claims

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0399/_index|..]]

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0399/claims/1937_01_01_erdos_oblath|1937_01_01_erdos_oblath]]: In a refereed paper (Acta Szeged 8 (1937)), Erdős and Obláth prove that
$n!=x^k\pm y^k$ has no solution with $x,y$ coprime and $xy>1$ when $k>2$ and
$k\ne4$, and none with $k=4$ for sufficiently large $n$.

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0399/claims/1973_05_01_pollack_shapiro|1973_05_01_pollack_shapiro]]: In a refereed paper (Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 26 (1973)), Pollack and Shapiro
are credited with showing that $n!=x^4-1$ has no solution. The monograph's
wider report, that the paper covers the whole coprime case $k=4$, is
unconfirmed.

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0399/claims/2025_04_07_barfield|2025_04_07_barfield]]: Jonas Barfield found that $10!=48^4-36^4$, a solution of $n!=x^k-y^k$ with
$xy>1$ and $k=4$, so the equation does have solutions. The bases share the
factor $12$, which puts the solution outside the coprime case Erdős and Obláth
had settled.

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0399/claims/2025_09_09_cambie|2025_09_09_cambie]]: Stijn Cambie observes, by a congruence modulo $8$, that $n!=x^4+y^4$ has no
solution with $x,y$ coprime and $xy>1$. The site's commentary credits the
remark to him.
