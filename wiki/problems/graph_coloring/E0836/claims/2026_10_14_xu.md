***

**Claim.** Linmiao Xu's development for
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0836/_index|Problem 836]] claims a negative answer
to the quadratic vertex bound and a positive answer to the linear
intersection bound, with chromatic number exactly $3$ and vertices counted
in the union of the edges. In the linked `Solution.lean`, the exported
theorems `Erdos836.Palomar.erdos_836.parts.i` and
`Erdos836.Palomar.erdos_836.parts.ii` state these answers for finite
hypergraphs. The file preserves copyright notices for The Formal
Conjectures Authors and Linmiao Xu; the registry's author field attributes
the submitted result to Linmiao Xu.

**Submission note.** The abstract of Linmiao Xu's Palomar entry
PALOMAR-2026-10-14-000004, version 1, registered on 14 October 2026, reads:

> A complete Lean 4 resolution of both parts of Erdős problem 836 (posed by
> Erdős and Shelah; Erdős and Lovász, 1975) on 3-chromatic intersecting
> r-uniform hypergraphs, together with structural bounds on the
> Erdős–Shelah codimension-one avoidance question. For Part (i), it
> formalizes Noga Alon's equitable-partition construction with (2r - 2) +
> C(2r - 2, r - 1) / 2 active vertices to disprove the O(r²) vertex bound
> for all r ≥ 2. For Part (ii), it adapts and generalizes Liam Price's
> minimal-blue bipartite double-counting argument to prove the universal
> bound r ≤ 2t + 1 when distinct edges meet in at most t vertices,
> establishing that every 3-chromatic intersecting r-uniform hypergraph
> has two distinct edges meeting in at least r/3 vertices, that linear
> intersecting hypergraphs are 2-colourable for r ≥ 4, and that avoiding
> pairwise intersection r - 1 forces minimum degree ≥ 3, degree ≥ 4 on
> multi-intersections, and |E(H)|, |V(H)| ≥ 9 at r = 4.

**Covers.** Both questions of the modified Statement. The first exported
answer uses finite examples with
$2r-2+\frac12\binom{2r-2}{r-1}$ incident vertices. For the second, the
exported theorem
`Erdos836.Palomar.r_le_two_mul_add_one_of_forall_card_inter_le` asserts
$r\le2t+1$ for a finite intersecting $r$-uniform hypergraph with no proper
$2$-coloring, when all distinct-edge intersections have size at most $t$.
Thus the largest intersection has size at least $\lfloor r/2\rfloor$,
which is at least $r/3$ for every $r\ge2$.

The displayed Lean targets quantify over finite vertex sets and finite edge
families. Their passage to the problem's unrestricted hypergraphs is an
ordinary classical compactness implication, not additional formal coverage:
a hypergraph with finite edges that has no proper $2$-coloring has a finite
edge subfamily with that property. Apply the finite intersection statement
there, and the resulting two edges also belong to the original hypergraph.
The finite counterexamples already disprove the first question on either
domain. This implication uses the compactness theorem for propositional
logic. Mere proper $3$-colorability does not suffice for the second answer;
the problem page explains that different reading.
