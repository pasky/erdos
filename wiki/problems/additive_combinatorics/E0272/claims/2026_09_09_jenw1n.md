**Claim.** Let $t(N)$ be the largest number of subsets of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$
whose pairwise intersections are all nonempty arithmetic progressions, the
quantity [[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0272/_index|Problem 272]] asks
for. The Lean proof establishes

$$
t(N)=\frac{N^2}{2}+O(N),
$$

through the bounds $\binom N2+1\le t(N)\le\binom N2+22051N$ for all large
$N$. This answers yes Szabó's linear-error question, the second of the two
questions in Section 6 of Szabó's 1999 paper
([[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0272/claims/1999_07_01_szabo|Szabó's claim page]]),
whether $t(N)=\binom N2+O(N)$, and sharpens Szabó's error term
$O(N^{5/3}(\log N)^3)$ to a linear one. The formal statement proved is the
variant `Erdos272.erdos_272.variants.szabo_strong` of the
formal-conjectures file for the problem, at the formal-conjectures commit the
bounty task pinned: with `IsArithInterSet N A` requiring
$A\subseteq\mathcal P(\{1,\ldots,N\})$ and every pair of distinct members to
intersect in a progression of some length $l>0$ (one- and two-element sets
counted as progressions, as erdosproblems.com's own $\binom N2+1$ example
presumes) and `maxArithInterCard N` the attained supremum of $|A|$, the
statement is that $t(N)-N^2/2=O(N)$ with real subtraction and a two-sided big-O.
The file's route: the lower bound from $\{1\}$ and all two- and three-element
sets containing $1$; for the upper bound, any admissible family with at least
$N^2/2$ members reduces, losing at most $2048N$ members, to a family with a
common point, which has at most $\binom N2+20001N$ members, or to a family with
a long common interval core, which has at most $\binom N2+20003N$, both by
private-witness and progression-matching counts. The solver is shown by
Conjectures.io as JenW1N; the 12,791-line file declares no author and no AI
system, and the library card
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/jenw1n_2026_erdos_problem_272_szabo_strong/_index|jenw1n_2026_erdos_problem_272_szabo_strong]]
records Conjectures.io's record, the verified statement, the acceptance timeline
and the file's provenance. The handle is Liam Kruer and Jensen Kohlmeyer's: the
bounty site's exposition of their JenW1N record on
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0196/claims/2026_09_09_kruer_kohlmeyer|Problem 196]]
names them as its authors, and Kruer's
[post of 5 October 2026](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/proof-claim:81681165220c4b07961cfd357bf6b996#post-9293)
on erdosproblems.com, announcing their Lean formalization of a Problem 790 claim
in the GitHub account JENW1N, is signed "(Jensen Kohlmeyer and Liam Kruer)".

**Covers.** The linear-error asymptotic $t(N)=N^2/2+O(N)$ alone. The exact
value of $t(N)$ for general $N$, which the erdosproblems.com question asks for
and which is reported only for $3\le N\le12$ (by computations posted on the
erdosproblems.com discussion thread in August 2025 for $N\le9$, and by Yang's
unrefereed preprint, which adds $N=10,11,12$; see
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0272/claims/2026_07_25_yang|Yang's claim page]]),
and Szabó's kernel conjecture, that every extremal family has a common
element, are not addressed; the bounty site's review note says as much. The
problem stays open on the erdosproblems.com question.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki: the proof is self-contained in its
Lean file and uses none of the earlier bounds.

**Acceptance.** Formalized. This corpus's verification rebuilt the record's
solution file, unchanged, on the repository's pinned toolchain,
`leanprover/lean4:v4.34.1` with Mathlib at the revision
`lean/lake-manifest.json` pins. The kernel replayed, from an empty environment,
every declaration the target `Bounty.target` depends on, Lean core, Mathlib and
formal-conjectures included, and accepted them all. The target's axioms are
exactly `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. Its type, and every
definition its statement reaches, are identical to the formal-conjectures
statement `Erdos272.erdos_272.variants.szabo_strong` of Conjectures.io's task,
which **Claim.** above reads as $t(N)=N^2/2+O(N)$. Acceptance rests on this
replay and that check.

The bounty site Conjectures.io verified the file with its Lean kernel on 9
September 2026; its certification is reported, not counted. Its report records a
static scan that found no imports, axiom declarations, `sorry`, `native_decide`
or unsafe options. The report also records that the statement was unchanged,
that the only axioms were `propext`, `Quot.sound` and `Classical.choice`, and
that a fresh isolated replay ran on 10 September 2026. The bounty site approved
the file in review on 11 September 2026 under its policy v2, certified the
record on 14 September 2026 and paid the bounty. Its review note states that the
approval concerns the unrestricted linear-error asymptotic. The note asserts
neither an exact extremal formula nor the kernel conjecture, and it calls itself
an eligibility decision rather than a guarantee of originality.

The bounty site's kernel check used a single kernel; its second kernel was not
run. The pinned catalog commit was unreachable on 2026-09-27, and the
formal-conjectures default branch states the variant identically; Conjectures.io
builds the pinned commit from a public catalog commit and an audit patch, and
the replay reproduced it exactly.

Not refereed: there is no write-up and no journal publication. On 2026-09-27
erdosproblems.com labeled the problem OPEN with no proof claim on its
tab, and the formal-conjectures default branch labeled the variant research
open.
