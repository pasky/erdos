# problems/set_theory/E1127/claims

[[problems/set_theory/E1127/_index|..]]

[[problems/set_theory/E1127/claims/1943_06_01_erdos_kakutani|1943_06_01_erdos_kakutani]]: Erdős and Kakutani (Bull. Amer. Math. Soc., 1943) prove that under the
continuum hypothesis the reals split into countably many rationally
independent sets. Such sets have no repeated distances, which gives the case
$n=1$ under CH.

[[problems/set_theory/E1127/claims/1972_09_01_davies|1972_09_01_davies]]: Davies (Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc., 1972) proves that if the continuum
hypothesis holds, the plane splits into countably many sets with all distances
distinct, and that such a partition, even of the line, implies the hypothesis.

[[problems/set_theory/E1127/claims/1987_11_01_kunen|1987_11_01_kunen]]: Kunen (Math. Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc., 1987) proves under the continuum
hypothesis that $\mathbb{R}^n$, for every finite $n$, splits into countably
many sets with all distances distinct. With Davies's converse, the statement
is independent of ZFC.
