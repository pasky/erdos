***

**Claim.** Dean Menezes, *The Three-Dimensional Erdős Box Problem Has Exponent
11/4*, arXiv:2609.22381v2 (7 October 2026, 5 pages), writes $z(n)$ for the
largest number of edges of a tripartite $3$-uniform hypergraph with $n$
vertices in each part and no box, a copy of $K^{(3)}_{2,2,2}$. Theorem 1.1
(p. 1): for each $q=2^m$ there is a box-free tripartite $3$-uniform hypergraph
with $q^4$ vertices in each part and $q^{11}$ edges. Corollary 1.2 (p. 1):
$z(n)=\Theta(n^{11/4})$. The lower bound $z(n)\ge q^{11}>(n/16)^{11/4}$ takes
$q=2^{\lfloor\log_{16}n\rfloor}$ and pads each part with isolated vertices; the
upper bound is
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1964_extremal_problems_graphs_generalized_graphs/theorem_1|Erdős's Theorem 1 of 1964]].
The construction (Construction 3.1, p. 3) takes $k=\mathbb F_q$,
$K=\mathbb F_{q^3}$, $\tau(s)=s^{q^2-q+1}$ and the trace $\mathrm{Tr}:K\to k$.
Each part is a copy of $K\times k$, and $(W,\omega)$, $(Y,\eta)$, $(Z,\zeta)$
form an edge when $\mathrm{Tr}(\tau(Y+Z)W)+\omega=E(Y,Z)+\eta+\zeta$, with the
offset $E(Y,Z)=\mathrm{Tr}(Y^{2q}\tau(Y+Z))$. Theorem 2.1 (p. 2) shows that
$\tau$ maps the fibers of $\delta(s)=\tau(s+1)+\tau(s)$ onto pairwise skew
affine lines over $k$; the argument needs characteristic $2$ (Remark 2.2). In a
box the four points $\tau(Y_i+Z_j)$ lie in an affine plane, so by Lemma 3.2
(p. 3) they sum to $0$ while the four offsets $E(Y_i,Z_j)$ do not sum to $0$.
Summing the four edge equations at one vertex of the first part then gives a
contradiction. Section 4.1 adds Corollary 4.1:
$z(n,K^{(3)}_{2,2,s})=\Theta(n^{11/4})$ for each fixed $s\ge2$ (the paper
writes $t$ for $s$), since a box-free hypergraph contains no
$K^{(3)}_{2,2,s}$.

In the problem's letters ($t$ the uniformity, $r$ the class size) the box is
$K_3(2)$. Each edge of a tripartite $3$-graph meets each part once, so the two
vertices of a class of a copy of $K_3(2)$ lie in one part and its three classes
lie in different parts: every copy is a box (an observation made here). The
hypergraph of Corollary 1.2 with $\lfloor n/3\rfloor$ vertices in each part,
plus isolated vertices, is therefore $K_3(2)$-free on $n$ vertices, and
$\mathrm{ex}_3(n,K_3(2))\ge z(\lfloor n/3\rfloor)=\Omega(n^{11/4})$. This is the
instance $(t,r)=(3,2)$ of the modified Statement,
$\mathrm{ex}_3(n,K_3(2))\ge n^{3-2^{-2}-o(1)}$, in the form $\Omega(n^{11/4})$.

**Covers.** The instance $(t,r)=(3,2)$ of the modified Statement of
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1158/_index|Problem 1158]], answered yes. It
is the first instance with $t\ge3$; the earlier lower bounds for $t=3$ reach
only $\Omega(n^{8/3})$. The instances $(2,2)$ and $(2,3)$ are the refereed
claims of [[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0714/_index|Problem 714]]. Nothing
for $t=3$ with $r\ge3$, for $t\ge4$, or for $t=2$ with $r\ge4$. The paper's open
questions 1 and 2 (Section 4.2) ask about $t=3$ with $r\ge3$ and about $r=2$
with $t\ge4$. The problem stays open.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki. The construction and the proof of
Theorem 1.1 are self-contained. Erdős's upper bound enters only the $\Theta$ of
Corollary 1.2, not the instance, which asks for the lower bound alone.

**Postings.** arXiv:2609.22381, v1 of 18 September 2026 (7 pages) and v2 of 7
October 2026 (5 pages). Both state Theorem 1.1 and Corollaries 1.2 and 4.1, and
this page follows v2. Version 2 drops the term $N(Y)$, the field norm of $Y$,
from v1's offset $E(Y,Z)=N(Y)+\mathrm{Tr}(Y^{2q}\tau(Y+Z))$. That changes the
hypergraph only by the relabeling $\eta\mapsto\eta+N(Y)$ of the second part (an
observation made here). Version 2 also merges v1's Lemmas 3.2 and 3.3 into one
direct computation, states Theorem 2.1 without v1's explicit lines
$c^2/N(c)+ck$, writes out the padding in Corollary 1.2, and drops v1's Section
4.2 on the octahedron problem for graphs.
