## Surface-by-surface grades

The conventions are simple undirected set-sized graphs and arbitrary
subgraphs, which need not be induced. Write $\mathbb N_0=\{0,1,\ldots\}$.
For a finite graph $F$, $\beta(F)$ is its minimum bipartizing edge-deletion
count; for infinite $G$ and $n\geq1$,

$$ h_G(n)=\max\{\beta(F):F\subseteq G,\ |V(F)|=n\}. $$

Proposition B asserts that every nondecreasing unbounded
$g:\mathbb N_0\to\mathbb N_0$ has one graph $G$ satisfying

$$
 |V(G)|=2^{\aleph_1},\qquad \chi(G)=\aleph_1,\qquad
 h_G(n)\leq(n-1)g(n)\quad\text{for every }n\geq1.
$$

Under $\diamondsuit$, a graph with the same bound and chromatic number can
instead have cardinality $\aleph_1$ and the source's Hajnal–Máté property.
CC is the separate statement that a fixed uniform linear budget on every
finite subgraph forces countable colorability when the ambient chromatic
number is at most $\aleph_1$. The review covers its equivalence with the
specified limit question, not its truth.

## Mathematical assessment of the attacks

### Definitions, deletion, packing and CC

The review checks that the set defining h_G(n) is nonempty and contained in
the finite integer interval from zero to binomial(n,2); its maximum is
therefore attained. Restricting a bipartizing edge set to a subgraph proves
monotonicity without an induced-subgraph assumption. These definitions are
well-defined without assuming CC.

For countable deletion, a countable coloring of the complement together with
a disjoint countable palette for the removed vertices would color the whole
graph countably. Restriction of an aleph_1-coloring gives the other inequality.
This verifies the exact chromatic number, without equating it with vertex
cardinality.

For the packing attack, if q disjoint copies are selected, every deletion set
for their induced union deletes at least b edges inside each copy. The edge
sets inside different copies are disjoint even when there are cross-edges.
With q=floor(n/v), padding gives

$$
 h_G(n)\geq b\lfloor n/v\rfloor
 \geq (b/v)(n-v)>2K(n-v)\geq Kn\qquad(n\geq2v).
$$

The strict step has n-v>0. The report attacks the real uniformity risk: witness
sizes may vary without bound. An uncountable disjoint family has a repeated
finite isomorphism type, since there are only countably many such types. Only
then is a fixed v selected. This is a proof of the needed uniformity, not an
assumption about all witnesses.

In the forward implication from CC, every stage before omega_1 has only
countably many previously used vertices, so the same conditional hypothesis
supplies another finite violator. In the reverse argument, a maximal disjoint
family exists by ZFC maximality; uncountability would contradict the supplied
infinitely many small-ratio orders by packing. Its countable vertex union
leaves the required linear complement. K<0 is impossible because h_G is
nonnegative. For K=0, any odd cycle can be padded to all larger orders, so the
hypothesis would force bipartiteness, again impossible here. These arguments
validate both the local remainder and the whole-limit equivalence. They
supply no proof of CC itself.

### The source interface and the exact graph sizes

The strongest source attack is validly posed: a bound on finite chromatic
numbers alone supplies no bound on how many edges must be deleted. The
review answers it using the actual Section 4 construction and Claim 4.1,
which give a common labeling on one graph. Its forest classes and tails without short odd
cycles are stronger data than the displayed theorem conclusion.

For S equal to the countable-cofinality ordinals below omega_2, the rank-beta
vertices are all natural-valued functions on S intersect beta. Distinct ranks
have distinct domains. Every domain has cardinality at most aleph_1, and the
domain at beta=omega_1+omega has cardinality aleph_1. Thus

$$
 2^{\aleph_1}=\aleph_0^{\aleph_1}
 \leq |V(G)|\leq\aleph_2\,\aleph_0^{\aleph_1}=2^{\aleph_1}.
$$

The equalities use ordinary ZFC cardinal arithmetic and Cantor's inequality,
not a continuum hypothesis. This independently checks the precise cardinality
claimed, rather than accepting an upper bound as equality.

One lower neighbor per label gives countably many lower neighbors per vertex;
coloring by rank with omega_1 colors avoids their countable palette. For the
lower bound, the recursively defined rho converts a purported countable
coloring into a coloring of the ladder indices. The club-guessing partition
argument and type-realization lemma then supply an eligible same-color edge.
The selected least eligible neighbor need not equal the original witness,
but eligibility itself forces its color to be k. The review checks precisely
this possible break in the chromatic proof.

The partition argument is sound even when a part merely has a nonstationary
set of successful guessers: intersect the offending club with a club avoiding
those guessers. A ladder contained in that intersection would have its limit
there, which is impossible. The intersection of the countably many resulting
clubs contradicts the original guessing property. This supplies the
specialization actually used in both chromatic arguments.

### Forest deletion, homomorphisms and diamond

A cycle in one label class has a vertex of greatest ordinal rank. Both cycle
neighbors are strictly lower, contradicting the single-lower-neighbor
property. An arbitrary orientation of outdegree one would not suffice; the
well-founded rank supplies the missing condition.

For a tail edge with label at least k, the k-th ladder blocks give the exact
Specker adjacency required by the homomorphism. Adjacent images are distinct.
An odd cycle whose image repeats vertices still yields an odd closed walk;
cutting at a repetition strictly decreases its odd length until an odd cycle
remains. Thus the source odd-girth bound transfers without an injectivity
assumption.

For a nondecreasing unbounded natural-valued g,

$$
 f(k)=\max(\{0\}\cup\{m\geq1:g(m)\leq k\})
$$

is finite, and n belongs to the defining set for f(g(n)). Hence the tail at
k=g(n) is bipartite on any n-vertex subgraph. The k lower labels contribute
at most k(n-1) edges: each is a forest on the same vertex set. This proves
the deletion bound for every positive n in the same graph, including n=1,
k=0, and arbitrarily long finite plateaus of g.

Under diamond, the reviewed Section 5 argument supplies simultaneous club
and coloring prediction, rather than applying the ZFC club-guessing theorem
at parameters that fail its cardinal-gap hypothesis. Coding a club and a
coloring into one subset of omega_1, and restricting to ordinals closed under
the coding maps and limit points of the club, gives the joint prediction
property used by the construction. The ordinal vertex set has size aleph_1;
the same least-eligible-neighbor argument prevents a countable coloring.

The omitted forest/tail analogue is explicitly supplied in the report. The
Hajnal–Máté assertion is also checked, not inferred from the source title.
With d_k=2s_k^2+1 and s_k>=1, the last zero in the relevant merge type follows
its first one. For a selected neighbor alpha at label k>=1 this implies

$$
 \alpha>\max C_\alpha[I_k]>\min C_\beta[I_k]
 >C_\beta(\max I_{k-1}).
$$

For every gamma<beta, only finitely many labels can produce a neighbor at
most gamma. An infinite lower-neighbor set consequently has order type omega
and is cofinal in beta. The order isomorphism onto omega_1 preserves that
cofinality. This addresses both the actual neighborhood condition and the
transfer of the source's vertex convention.

### The upper-bound and rate consequences

For each epsilon>0 the power budget is natural-valued, nondecreasing and
unbounded. Strictness follows from

$$
 (m-1)\lfloor m^\varepsilon\rfloor
 \leq(m-1)m^\varepsilon<m^{1+\varepsilon}\qquad(m\geq1).
$$

It does not require a strict rounding inequality. At m=1 it reads 0<1.
The logarithmic budget defines one graph independent of epsilon. The review
retains an explicit estimate

$$
 \lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil
 \leq 2+\frac{\ln n}{\ln2}
 \leq\left(2+\frac1{e\varepsilon\ln2}\right)n^\varepsilon,
$$

so the asserted dependence of the constant is justified. Its limiting-statement qualification is also correct: these upper bounds alone decide
neither divergence nor boundedness of the ratio.

For the rate claim, every integer tail of floor(r(m)) is bounded below when
r tends to positive infinity; only finitely many initial terms can be below
any fixed threshold. A nonempty bounded-below subset of the integers has a
minimum. Consequently

$$
 g(n)=\max\bigl(1,\min_{m\geq n}\lfloor r(m)\rfloor\bigr)
$$

is nondecreasing and tends to infinity. Eventually 1<=g(n)<=r(n), and then
(n-1)g(n)<nr(n). The report's test of initially negative and oscillating
rates is therefore substantive. It checks the property that distinguishes
convergence to infinity from mere unboundedness. Applying this to a proposed
common divergent lower rate disproves its uniform applicability. The report's
additional square-root-rate argument also correctly excludes lower bounds
with graph-dependent positive multiplicative constants. Neither statement
asserts that the target divergence actually holds.

### The signed-integer disproof and the surviving growth statements

The commission selects the original signed-integer claim surface. The report's
alternative readings therefore do not leave the grade ambiguous. Let

$$ g:\mathbb N_0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z,\qquad g(n)=n-2. $$

This function is nondecreasing and tends to infinity. Every nonempty graph
contains a one-vertex subgraph F, for which

$$ \beta(F)=0>-1=|V(F)|g(|V(F)|). $$

Thus no graph of chromatic number aleph_1 satisfies this all-order budget.
Indeed the generalized CC implication is true for this g, since any graph
satisfying its budget must be empty and is countably colorable. The witness
is a function refuting the universal assertion that the modified lemma is
false for every signed divergent budget. It is not a graph counterexample
to CC, and it is not merely a failure of the suggested minorant proof.

This is mathematically material to the third consequence. The fact that the
bad value occurs at one early index does not remove it from an all-order
hypothesis. The report correctly treats the second consequence differently,
because its conclusion is explicitly eventual. No finite initial segment was
discarded from the signed third claim in this grade.

The independent assertion about weaker growth hypotheses survives. The
logarithmic-budget graph is subquadratic. After any countable deletion it is
still uncountably chromatic and hence contains an odd cycle. An omega_1
recursion gives uncountably many disjoint odd cycles, and one finite length
ell occurs infinitely often. Padding repeated cycles yields

$$ h_G(n)\geq\lfloor n/\ell\rfloor\geq n/(2\ell)\quad(n\geq2\ell). $$

Together with the logarithmic upper bound this gives
log(h_G(n))/log(n) tending to one. Thus the report even handles the reading
of n^{1+o(1)} as an exponent equality, without pretending the upper estimate
alone proves it. These arguments are independent of the false signed-budget
universal assertion.

### Blow-ups and finite tails

For a selected odd cycle of length ell in H, a transversal cycle of its
s-fold blow-up is specified by one vertex from each class. There are exactly
s^ell such cycles. Each edge of this cycle blow-up occurs in exactly
s^{ell-2} of them. A deletion set making H[s] bipartite meets every selected
cycle; edges outside this cycle blow-up meet none. Double counting therefore
gives

$$ s^\ell\leq |D|s^{\ell-2},\qquad |D|\geq s^2. $$

The counts are symbolic integer identities for every positive s and every
odd ell>=3, not numerical evidence from sampled cases. The review's second
argument tests the same bound by a different mathematical route. For a cut,
write x_i for the first-color fraction in class i. The normalized number of
monochromatic edges is

$$
 \sum_{i\bmod\ell}\bigl(x_ix_{i+1}+(1-x_i)(1-x_{i+1})\bigr).
$$

It is affine in each coordinate. Successively moving each coordinate to a
minimizing endpoint cannot increase the value and ends at a coloring of an
odd cycle. That value is at least one. Hence every actual cut leaves at least
s^2 monochromatic edges, all of which a compatible bipartizing deletion set
must delete. The continuous cube is used in the valid lower-bound direction;
no fractional deletion set is substituted for an integral one.

With v=|V(H)| fixed, an embedding in the logarithmic-budget graph would require

$$ s^2\leq h_G(sv)\leq(sv-1)g(sv)\leq sv\,g(sv). $$

But $v g(sv)/s$ tends to zero. The estimate is in the original units, namely
s^2 edges and ratio s/v, and proves eventual exclusion for each fixed H in
the same graph. Extra ambient edges cause no difficulty. For the hypothetical
route, arbitrarily large blow-ups of one fixed non-bipartite base in each
ambient graph would violate any proposed constant linear bound. The base may
vary between ambient graphs; it cannot vary without control as s grows.
That precise conditional premise would imply CC and then the limit. The
review does not assert its existence.

For a fixed finite cutoff k, the lower labels are colored by a product of k
two-colorings. Pairing this finite tuple with a hypothetical countable tail
coloring gives a countable coloring of the whole graph, a contradiction.
Restriction gives the upper bound aleph_1. The report's empty-intersection
example is a decisive boundary check: every individual edge has a finite
label, so no edge survives all cutoffs, even though each finite cutoff has
uncountable chromatic number. No infinite-product or intersection transfer
is used in the accepted assertion.
