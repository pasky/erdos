# problems/diophantine_problems/E0407/claims

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0407/_index|..]]

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0407/claims/1988_03_01_tijdeman_wang|1988_03_01_tijdeman_wang]]: Tijdeman and Wang prove that every rational number beyond some constant has at
most four representations as $2^a3^b+2^c+3^d$, when representations with the
same three summands are identified. Four is best possible.

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0407/claims/1988_10_13_evertse_gyory_stewart_tijdeman|1988_10_13_evertse_gyory_stewart_tijdeman]]: Evertse, Győry, Stewart and Tijdeman prove that the number of representations
of $n$ as $2^a+3^b+2^c3^d$ is bounded by an absolute constant. This is the
first proof of Newman's conjecture.

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0407/claims/2023_08_09_bajpai_bennett|2023_08_09_bajpai_bennett]]: Bajpai and Bennett prove, with explicit constants, that a positive integer has
at most nine representations as $2^a3^b+2^c+3^d$, and at most four from 131082
on, when representations with the same three summands are identified.
