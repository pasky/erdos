**Claim.** For a sequence of distinct points on the circle of circumference $1$,
write $M_n^{(r)}$ and $m_n^{(r)}$ for the largest and smallest sums of $r$
consecutive gaps cut by the first $n$ points, so that the mean such sum is
$r/n$. Theorem 1.1 of S. Korsky, *A resolution of the de Bruijn–Erdős
consecutive-gap problem*, arXiv:2609.07196 (v2, 9 September 2026), recorded on
its [[../library/analysis/korsky_2026_resolution_debruijn_erdos_consecutive_gap_problem/_index|library card]]
with the
[[../library/analysis/korsky_2026_resolution_debruijn_erdos_consecutive_gap_problem/theorem_1_1|theorem's page]],
asserts absolute constants $c>0$ and $r_0$ such that for every $r\ge r_0$ and
every such sequence

$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\bigl(nM_n^{(r)}-r\bigr)\ge c\sqrt{\log r},\qquad
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\bigl(r-nm_n^{(r)}\bigr)\ge c\sqrt{\log r},\qquad
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{M_n^{(r)}}{m_n^{(r)}}\ge1+\frac{\log r}{100\,r}.
$$

Taking the infimum or supremum over sequences of distinct points, and writing
$\Lambda_r^{\mathrm{dist}}$, $\lambda_r^{\mathrm{dist}}$ and
$\mu_r^{\mathrm{dist}}$ for the constants over that family, these say that
$\Lambda_r^{\mathrm{dist}}-r$, $r-\lambda_r^{\mathrm{dist}}$ and
$r(\mu_r^{\mathrm{dist}}-1)$ all tend to infinity. Together with the upper bound
$\mu_r\le1+c'\log r/r$ of Clément and Steinerberger, proved by sequences of
distinct points and so valid for $\mu_r^{\mathrm{dist}}$ as well as $\mu_r$, the
third bound fixes the order of $\mu_r^{\mathrm{dist}}-1$ at $\log r/r$. These
are the three parts of the modified Statement of
[[problems/analysis/E1221/_index|Problem 1221]], restricted to sequences of
distinct points. The problem's constants $\Lambda_r$, $\lambda_r$ and $\mu_r$
are taken over all sequences, and since $\Lambda_r\le\Lambda_r^{\mathrm{dist}}$,
$\lambda_r\ge\lambda_r^{\mathrm{dist}}$ and $\mu_r\le\mu_r^{\mathrm{dist}}$,
these inclusion inequalities alone do not transfer the lower bounds to the
larger family. Equality of the constants would suffice, but is unnecessary
for a separate proof over all sequences. The author also notes that each
one-sided bound alone implies that the ratio excess is unbounded, the weaker
form of the third part.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Samuel
Korsky (account SamKorsky) on 8 September 2026, giving "GPT Astra" as the AI
used:

> We prove all three de Bruijn--Erdős conjectures. Writing $M_n^{(r)}$ and
> $m_n^{(r)}$ for the largest and smallest $r$-spans after $n$ points, we
> obtain\[ \limsup_n(nM_n^{(r)}-r),\ \limsup_n(r-nm_n^{(r)})\gg\sqrt{\log r},
> \qquad \limsup_n\frac{M_n^{(r)}}{m_n^{(r)}}\ge 1+\frac{\log r}{100r}. \]The
> proof compares forward and backward walks by $kr$ places at nearby insertion
> times, converting span bounds into estimates for point counts in short
> intervals. Larcher’s finite-prefix discrepancy bound yields the ratio result.
> For the one-sided bounds, the fact that the average $r$-span is exactly $r/n$
> converts one-sided span control into $L^1$ control; averaging the same
> comparison and applying Halász’s planar $L^1$ discrepancy theorem then forces
> the stated $\sqrt{\log r}$ growth. Note that the one-sided bounds already
> implies the third conjecture, but the stronger estimate for the ratio here is
> actually sharp asymptotically (matching upper bounds of Steinerberger and
> Clement). Notes: I plan to revise the document to make it more human-readable;
> the only reason I posted now in its half-baked form is because I have an arxiv
> pre-print proving only the third limit coming out today or tomorrow night. The
> full resolution follows from similar ideas (Astra was able to derive the
> one-sided inequalities quickly after I fed in my existing argument for the
> third limit). Thus I expect the problem to otherwise fall to Erdős hunters
> shortly, hence the rush.

**Method, as the paper describes it.** Counts of points in short arcs at
nearby insertion times are compared by walking $kr$ places forward and
backward along the cyclic order, which turns control of the $r$-spans into
control of those counts. For the ratio bound the comparison is closed by a
finite-prefix discrepancy inequality extracted from Larcher's proof of
Schmidt's theorem. For the one-sided bounds the identity that the average
$r$-span equals $r/n$ converts one-sided span control into an $L^1$ bound, and
Halász's planar $L^1$ discrepancy theorem then forces the $\sqrt{\log r}$
growth. Version 1 of the preprint (7 September 2026) carried the ratio bound
only; version 2 added the two one-sided bounds. The problem page's research
folder holds reconstructions and complete local reproofs. The all-sequence
ratio bound with coefficient $1/200$ is
[[theory/analysis/L163_all_sequence_span_ratio/_index|L163]], and the two
all-sequence one-sided bounds are
[[theory/analysis/L164_all_sequence_span_deviations/_index|L164]], each with
a separate whole-statement examination and passing grade. These records
examine the supplied local proofs and do not accept this exact source claim
with coefficient $1/100$.

**Scope.** This is a full claim to the intended, mean-normalized problem,
credited to Korsky as on the erdosproblems.com proof-claim tab. The theorem as
printed assumes distinct points. The extension to coincident points is a
technical completion of his approach, supplied by the local proofs below;
classifying the problem claim as full does not enlarge the source theorem's
hypotheses or assert equality of the two families' constants.

**Covers.** All three divergence questions in the modified Statement:
$\Lambda_r-r$, $r-\lambda_r$ and $r(\mu_r-1)$ tend to infinity. Korsky's
Theorem 1.1 supplies the claimed resolution for distinct-point sequences;
the local completion covers all sequences. Neither formulation asserts the
simultaneous positive divergence of the literal printed first two expressions.

**Technical completion and local verification.**
[[theory/analysis/L163_all_sequence_span_ratio/_index|L163]] and
[[theory/analysis/L164_all_sequence_span_deviations/_index|L164]] give complete
local proofs using Korsky's span-to-count mechanism and closing arguments,
with coincident terms allowed. Their conjunction
[[theory/analysis/L165_consecutive_gap_growth_conjunction/_index|L165]] yields,
for absolute $c>0$ and all sufficiently large $r$,

$$
\Lambda_r-r\ge c\sqrt{\log r},\qquad
r-\lambda_r\ge c\sqrt{\log r},\qquad
r(\mu_r-1)\ge\frac{\log r}{200}.
$$

Plasma AI supplies the coincident-point extension, the complete local
finite-list and planar discrepancy proofs with constants, and their
composition. The finite-list proof follows Larcher's range splitting; the
planar proof follows Halász's theorem as presented by Bilyk. These complete
Korsky's argument and check it locally. Each input has its own
whole-statement examination and distinct passing grade. L165's
[whole-conclusion examination](../../../../theory/analysis/L165_consecutive_gap_growth_conjunction/evidence/verify/whole_statement/report.md)
received a [distinct PASS grade](../../../../theory/analysis/L165_consecutive_gap_growth_conjunction/evidence/verify/whole_statement/grade.md)
on 2026-10-16 UTC, warranting tier 1 for the conjunction and its exact supplied
mean-normalized formulation. The reviews cover the supplied local proofs,
not independent reading of the original publications. They do not certify
Korsky's stronger $1/100$ coefficient, optimal constants, attainment, equality
of the two sequence families' constants, historical interpretation, or Lean.
