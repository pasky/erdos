# problems/arithmetic_functions/E0649/claims

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0649/_index|..]]

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0649/claims/2025_01_16_sampaio|2025_01_16_sampaio]]: No integer $n$ has greatest prime factor $19$ while $n+1$ is a power of $2$,
so the statement fails for the pair $19$ and $2$. The erdosproblems.com
remarks record this observation of Sampaio and extend it to primes above $13$
with $2$ as a primitive root.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0649/claims/2025_01_16_tong|2025_01_16_tong]]: For every prime $p$ there are infinitely many primes $q$ such that no integer
$n$ has greatest prime factor $p$ while $n+1$ has greatest prime factor $q$.
This is the elementary disproof that the erdosproblems.com remarks record and
credit to Tong.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0649/claims/2026_02_07_alexeev|2026_02_07_alexeev]]: There are infinitely many primes $q$ such that no $n\ge2$ has $P(n)P(n+1)=2q$,
with $P$ the greatest prime factor. This 2020 Romanian Master of Mathematics
argument was formalized in Lean by ChatGPT, Aristotle and Boris Alexeev, and
accepted after the Lean proof was built here.
