***

**Claim.** The answer to [[problems/distance_problems/E0090/_index|Problem 90]]
is no, with the exponent $1.043171$. Theorem 1.1 of Eric Naslund, *An Exponent
of 1.043 for the Unit Distance Problem*, dated 8 October 2026 in the author's
repository, states that there are finite sets $U_j\subset\mathbb R^2$ with
$|U_j|\to\infty$ and

$$
\frac{u(U_j)}{|U_j|^{1.043171}}\to\infty,
$$

where $u(U)$ counts the unordered pairs of points of $U$ at Euclidean distance
one; in particular $u(n)\ge n^{1.043171}$ for arbitrarily large $n$ (p. 1).
The first `preprint` link is the version of 8 October that the registry entry
cites, and the second its revision of 9 October (101 pages), which this page
was read against; Theorem 1.1 is the same in both, and the page numbers here
are the revision's. A fixed positive gain along an unbounded sequence of $n$
exceeds $C/\log\log n$ for every fixed $C$ once $n$ is large, so the bound the
problem asks about fails. The theorem asserts nothing for every large $n$.

The paper says the proof uses no unproved hypothesis, such as the generalized
Riemann hypothesis, but is computer-assisted (p. 2): finite facts about the
tower, a census of primes up to norm $4\cdot10^{13}$ (Proposition 5.12, p. 48),
rigorous enclosures of 576 $L$-functions of degrees 4 and 8 at 21 real points
near $1$ (Proposition 6.5, p. 58) and the final margin at $\delta=0.043171$
(Section 11, pp. 84--89) are certified by exact computation and interval
arithmetic in supplementary programs in the repository. A replay program reruns
the certificate (Subsection 1.8, p. 8) with two limits: it recomputes the
$L$-values at three of the abscissae and checks the others against their stored
hashes, and it repeats the census only on sample ranges, checking the census
bins against an independent classification. The construction is the number-field
method of OpenAI and Sawin, with unit distances from elements of relative norm
one in quadratic extensions $K/F$ where $F$ has both real and complex places;
the fields come from an infinite pro-$2$ tower over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{241})$
ramified only above $2$, $3$ and $5$, with Frobenius conditions at the two
primes above $29$ and at one prime above $41$. The relative zeta value is
bounded by $C_{\rm eff}=0.0422764$ (Theorem 4.2, p. 32) through the dihedral
quotients of the tower group and a field $E_{W''}$ of degree $8192$ contained in
every field of the tower. Table 1 of the revision (p. 2) lists the exponents
claimed between Sawin's $1.014114$ and its own, the largest being the author's
unpublished $1.0358324$. The version of 8 October instead names as the largest
exponent previously claimed the author's earlier manuscript *An Exponent of
1.04273 for the Unit Distance Problem*, in the same repository, and says it
keeps that manuscript's method and most of its construction; the revision drops
the citation and sets aside only "preliminary versions of the present work". The
paper's AI Methodology statement says that agents developed the mathematics,
wrote the programs and wrote the paper under the author's supervision: Claude
Opus 5.5 in Claude Code produced the refinements of the zeta bound, the
certificates and the paper, an agent run of the Muse Spark system found the
Frobenius condition above $41$, and the preliminary versions were produced with
Claude Opus 5.5, ChatGPT Sol 5.6 and ChatGPT Astra 6.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki. The paper calls itself self-contained
apart from the standard results it cites, among them the Golod--Shafarevich
criterion, and its supplementary programs, which import programs of the
author's earlier manuscripts in the same repository.

**Formalization.** The registry's selected theorem
`UnitDistanceSqrt241Submission.target_of_wide_zeta_bound`, in the 158-line
challenge file `lean/ChallengeZeta241.lean` at the pinned commit, which imports
only Mathlib, proves a weaker, conditional form of Theorem 1.1: if

$$
\frac{\log\operatorname{Re}\zeta_{E_W}\bigl(1+\tfrac1{4411}\bigr)}{8192}
+\frac1{4411}\Bigl(\frac{\ell-\gamma-\log(4\pi)}4
-\frac{\operatorname{Re}(\zeta_{E_W}'/\zeta_{E_W})(2)}{8192}\Bigr)
<0.050969,
$$

with $\ell=\tfrac94\log2+\tfrac12\log3615$, $\gamma$ Euler's constant and $E_W$
the field of degree $8192$ written out in the challenge file (the paper's
$E_{W''}$), then there are finite sets $U_j\subset\mathbb C$ with
$|U_j|\to\infty$ and the number of unordered pairs at distance one in $U_j$,
divided by $|U_j|^{1.04315}$, tending to infinity. Under that inequality,
$(H_W)$, the Lean theorem disproves the problem's bound with the exponent
$1.04315$. $(H_W)$ is not proved in Lean: the paper's Remark 4.38 (pp. 43--44)
says that a supplementary program bounds its left side by $0.0509172$ from the
enclosures of Proposition 6.5, and that the formalization does not cover the
census beyond norm $1000$, the signed kernel or the general shell weights. Its
Status subsection (Subsection 1.5, p. 6) concludes that the formalization does
not prove Theorem 1.1, even conditionally. The registry entry records Lean
`v4.35.0-rc2`, the Mathlib revision it pins, the permitted axioms `propext`,
`Quot.sound` and `Classical.choice`, checks by two further kernels, and an
automated review by a language model with a neutral outcome and no warnings; the
project's metadata reports no `sorry` and no `native_decide`, gives its review
status as self-assessed, and says the Lean was produced with AI tools, Claude
Opus 5.5 in Claude Code and Codex, under the author's direction. Version 1 of
the entry, registered on 1 October 2026
([record](https://palomar-registry.org/entry?id=PALOMAR-2026-10-01-000018&version=1)),
proved the exponent $1.0427$ from a different inequality for the Kummer field
$E$ of degree $512$, alongside the 1.04273 manuscript. This corpus has neither
built nor audited the development, and no statement-fidelity review of its
challenge file exists here, so it gives no `formalized` evidence.

**Acceptance.** None documented. The paper is not refereed and is not on
arXiv. Its Status subsection (p. 6) reports ten fresh-context reviews of the
dihedral refinements, the signed kernel, the shell weights and the
intermediate certificates, one of which found a corrected error in an
intermediate certificate, and three of the whole manuscript, all by AI agents
and not human peer review; it says that no reviewer recomputed the octic
$L$-values and that the finite computations for the cap above $41$ have had no
full independent review. The corpus has not replayed the certificate. The
claim is therefore claimed; the problem's standing rests on
[[problems/distance_problems/E0090/claims/2026_05_20_openai|OpenAI's accepted claim]]
and does not change.
