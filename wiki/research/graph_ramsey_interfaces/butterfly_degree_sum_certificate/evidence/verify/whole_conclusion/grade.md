## Weakest steps and strongest attacks

### Finite realization and graph conventions

The report tests the supplied construction rather than inferring a graph from
rational densities. With $M=10000$, weights
$(w_A,w_B,w_C,w_D,w_E)=(4273,2471,709,1274,1273)$, $q=4352$ and $r=3011$,
its part $i$ is the tagged finite set
$\{i\}\times[ w_i ]\times\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z\times[s]$.
All factors are positive integers, the weights sum to $M$, and total order is
$N=M^2s$. This is the frozen labeling made explicitly disjoint.

Complete pairs are $AB,BC,AD,AE$. For $AC$, part names choose the difference
$z_C-z_A$ once; membership in $[0,q-1]$ defines an unordered pair. A fixed
$A$ endpoint has $q$ distinct allowed residues in $C$, each with $w_Cs$
vertices. A fixed $C$ endpoint has residues $z_C-u$ for the same $q$
distinct values of $u$, hence $qw_As$ neighbors. The $DE$ argument uses $r$.
No within-part edges or repeated pair classes occur, so the graph is simple
and undirected.

The report's strongest attack is substantive: $z_A=9999,z_C=0$ gives an
allowed forward difference $1$, but reversed difference $9999$ is not in
the interval. Requiring both orientations to pass the same test would break
the calculation. The frozen rule instead names the parts, so reverse
neighborhoods use the negated interval and retain the same cardinality.
The attack fails against the actual rule. The phrase about equal degrees
on each side of $DE$ means constancy within each side: the opposite-part
counts are $3011\cdot1273s$ and $3011\cdot1274s$, respectively. The displayed
formulas already make that distinction; the report does not silently replace

$$

This defeats the attempted failure at the largest remainder $t=U-1$;
at multiples of $U$ the first strict comparison also remains strict.
The cleared numerator

### Minimum, asymptotic consequence and reconstruction boundary

The report checks the supplied definition's domain. For a triangle-free
eligible graph, each edge $uv$ has disjoint endpoint neighborhoods, hence
$d(u)+d(v)\le n$. Summing gives $\sum d(v)^2\le ne$; the squared-difference
inequality $(\sum d(v))^2\le n\sum d(v)^2$ then gives
$4e^2\le n^2e$, contradicting $e>n^2/4$. There are finitely many labeled
graphs and the complete graph is eligible for $n>2$. Thus every relevant
triangle maximum and the minimum over graphs exist. That minimum is exactly
the largest threshold valid for every graph in the class.

The constructed graph therefore gives $h(n)<bn$ with $b=183/125$.
Since $433^2=187489<187500=12\cdot125^2$, positive square roots give
$b<c=2(\sqrt3-1)$. For $\delta=c-b>0$, any real error
$\varepsilon(n)\to0$ eventually has $|\varepsilon(n)|<\delta/2$.
The proposed bound would then give $h(n)/n\ge c-\varepsilon(n)>b$,
a contradiction. Signed or oscillating errors do not avoid this. Equivalently,
$\limsup h(n)/n\le b<c$ excludes the proposed lower asymptotic bound.

finite labels, edge rule, both directional counts, support classification,

The report expands those deductions; it does not supply a missing realization
theorem or a new padding construction. Its triangle-existence argument is an

contract requires for definitional material. It imports no established
external theorem. Moreover, the exhibited graph directly defeats the proposed
universal degree-sum guarantee even before that guarantee is expressed as a
minimum. Checking this definition's domain is not a repair of a failed central
construction. No essential omitted lemma was identified. The broader request
to estimate $h(n)$ receives an upper bound only; no optimality conclusion
