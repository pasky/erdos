# problems/additive_bases/E0033/claims

[[problems/additive_bases/E0033/_index|..]]

[[problems/additive_bases/E0033/claims/1965_01_01_moser|1965_01_01_moser]]: Moser's 1965 proceedings paper proves that every additive complement of the
squares has counting function $A(N)$ with $\liminf A(N)/\sqrt N>1.06$, the
first yes to the liminf question. The proceedings volume is not shown to be
refereed, so the claim is pending.

[[problems/additive_bases/E0033/claims/1993_07_01_cilleruelo|1993_07_01_cilleruelo]]: Cilleruelo's 1993 theorem shows that a set completing the squares up to $N$
has at least $(4/\pi+o(1))\sqrt N$ elements. So every additive complement of
the squares has liminf at least $4/\pi$, which answers the liminf question
yes.

[[problems/additive_bases/E0033/claims/1995_03_01_habsieger|1995_03_01_habsieger]]: Habsieger's 1995 theorem gives an explicit lower bound for an additive
complement of a polynomial's values up to $N$, with constant $4/\pi$ for the
squares. So every additive complement of the squares has liminf at least
$4/\pi$.

[[problems/additive_bases/E0033/claims/2025_08_12_van_doorn|2025_08_12_van_doorn]]: Wouter van Doorn's golden-ratio construction, posted on the erdosproblems.com
forum on 12 August 2025, gives a complement of the squares with limsup
$2\varphi^{5/2}\approx6.66$, so the smallest possible limsup is at most that.
The erdosproblems.com remarks credit it, but Bloom's catalog labeled the
problem OPEN, so the claim is pending.

[[problems/additive_bases/E0033/claims/2026_09_07_donteatllamas|2026_09_07_donteatllamas]]: An unsigned three-page write-up, posted on the erdosproblems.com forum on
7 September 2026 by the user donteatllamas and made with the help of GPT,
builds a complement of the squares on the scales $3^k$ with limsup at most
$13\sqrt2/4\approx4.596$. No review is known, so the claim is pending.

[[problems/additive_bases/E0033/claims/2026_10_19_plasma_ai|2026_10_19_plasma_ai]]: Plasma AI constructs an explicit complement of the squares whose counting function has upper limit 9/2 against the square root, so the smallest possible limsup is at most 9/2. Author-recorded at tier 0; catalog acceptance is pending.
