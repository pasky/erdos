**Claim.** For every two-coloring $c:\mathbb R^2\to\{-1,1\}$ of the plane,
with no measurability or other regularity assumed, there is at most one
positive length $r$ such that $c$ has no monochromatic equilateral
triangle of side $r$. Equivalently, for every real $x>0$ with $x\ne1$,
every two-coloring of the plane contains a monochromatic equilateral
triangle of side $1$ or of side $x$. Under every two-coloring, every set
of three distinct points that is not equilateral, collinear triples
included, has a monochromatic congruent copy, obtained by a translation
and a rotation. Hence for all but at most one triangle $T$ there is a
monochromatic congruent copy of $T$, and an exceptional $T$, if there is
one, is equilateral, with a side that depends on the coloring.

**Submission note.** The Palomar registry's description of entry
PALOMAR-2026-10-19-000008, registered on 19 October 2026 by a maintainer of the
repository, whose `formalization.yaml` names Jacob Parish and Andrew Turner as
its responsible maintainers:

> A Lean proof that in every two-coloring of the plane any three distinct points
> that are not the vertices of an equilateral triangle, collinear points
> included, have a monochromatic congruent copy and at most one equilateral side
> length is missed, so at most one triangle up to congruence, degenerate or not,
> has no monochromatic copy. Alternating strips show that one equilateral
> triangle can be missed. This answers Erdős Problem 173 as Bloom states it and
> settles the conjecture of Erdős, Graham, Montgomery, Rothschild, Spencer and
> Straus that every non-equilateral triangle has a monochromatic copy, collinear
> triples included; it is new to our knowledge. The step from arbitrary to
> measurable colorings adapts OpenAI's rigidity theorem for wild character laws,
> extended here from the algebraic numbers to the algebraic closure of ℚ(x) in ℂ
> for every real x, and its transfer from proper to weak measurable colorings.

**Covers.** The whole Statement of
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0173/_index|Problem 173]], whether or not
three distinct collinear points are counted as a triangle. The one
exception is needed: as the problem page records, the coloring of the
plane by alternating strips has no monochromatic equilateral triangle of
one side length. The claim does not classify the colorings that miss an
equilateral side length.

**Credit.** The reduction to two equilateral side lengths is Lemma 1.3
and Corollary 1.4 of
[[../library/discrete_geometry/jelinek_2009_monochromatic_triangles_two_colored_plane/corollary_1_4|Jelínek, Kynčl, Stolař and Valla]],
who take it from Erdős, Graham, Montgomery, Rothschild, Spencer and
Straus; the two-scale lead reconstructs its eight-point configuration
in coordinates. The rigidity and transfer steps build on OpenAI's
manuscript
[[../library/discrete_geometry/openai_2026_euclidean_plane_not_five_colorable/_index|*The Euclidean plane is not five-colorable*]]
(OpenAI Math Release, September 23, 2026), whose subject is the chromatic
number of the plane. Its Sections 2 and 3 give a proof that every wild
rotation-invariant character law on the algebraic plane is Haar measure,
using a selection step confined to number fields, and its Lemma 4.1 and
Proposition 4.2 transfer proper colorings to weak measurable ones by
projection onto the continuous vectors. Plasma AI supplies the rest. At
algebraic ratios the manuscript's number-field step applies as stated; at
transcendental ratios a function-field finite-cover theorem gives the
unit-vector template that replaces it. With that template, the
manuscript's other lemmas, their field hypotheses checked for the new
field, extend its rigidity theorem to $F_x(i)$, where $F_x$ is the real
closure of $\mathbb Q(x)$ in $\mathbb R$, for every real $x>0$. Plasma
AI also proves that the two-scale question on that countable field is
equivalent to the whole-plane question, adapts the manuscript's
projection to two colors and three-point configurations by thresholding
the quadratic $1+ab+ac+bc$, excludes two unequal scales for measurable
colorings by a Bessel bound for $x\ge3/2$ or $x\le2/3$ and a circle
argument inside that window, and composes these into the whole
conclusion. The circle argument
uses the Lebesgue density theorem and Federer's criterion for finite
perimeter (H. Federer, *Geometric Measure Theory*, 1969, 4.5.11), the
latter checked at statement level. Neither the manuscript's
five-color theorem nor its Lean formalization is a premise. Two AI models
found Plasma AI's proof on October 16 and 17, 2026, working in alternating
steps: GPT-6 Astra in Codex and Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code, as the
formalization repository's README records.

**Formalization.** The repository
[plasma-ai/erdos-173](https://github.com/plasma-ai/erdos-173/tree/4a368a652753fab53272f7a747755876f04bc2cc)
formalizes the result in Lean 4 (`v4.35.0-rc3`, Mathlib `v4.35.0-rc3`) at
commit 4a368a6, with a prose blueprint that follows the development;
[[../library/discrete_geometry/plasma_ai_2026_monochromatic_triangles_two_colorings_plane/_index|its source card]]
digests both. `Challenge.lean` states five theorems. Three copy
formal-conjectures' statement of Problem 173 at its commit of 2026-10-14,
verbatim apart from their attribute lists: `Erdos173.erdos_173`, that any
two triangles without a monochromatic congruent copy are congruent, with its
variants `right_triangle` and `equilateral_exception`. Two are the
repository's own: `Erdos173.erdos_173.variants.non_equilateral`, that every
non-equilateral triangle has a monochromatic congruent copy, and
`Erdos173.erdos_173.variants.three_distinct_points`, the same for any three
distinct points that are not the vertices of an equilateral triangle,
collinear ones included. Triangles in the first four are Mathlib's
`Affine.Triangle`, with affinely independent vertices, and in all five a copy
may be reflected and relabelled. `Solution.lean` proves the five, and
`comparator.json` permits only `propext`, `Quot.sound` and
`Classical.choice`. The rigidity step uses the Lean form of OpenAI's theorem
from `openai/math`, generalized over a carrier field with the template
property, which the formalization proves for the algebraic closure of
$\mathbb Q(x)$ at every transcendental $x$; the circle argument replaces
Federer's criterion by an elementary argument with nested squares. The
repository's `formalization.yaml` names Jacob Parish as the formalization's
author and records that it was written with Claude Code. The repository is
registered on the Palomar registry as `PALOMAR-2026-10-19-000008`, version 1,
first registered on 19 October 2026 (the `record` link; trust level high,
automated review outcome neutral), and the entry pins commit 4a368a6.

repository's tree at 4a368a6, at its own pinned toolchain,
`leanprover/lean4:v4.35.0-rc3` with Mathlib at `v4.35.0-rc3`, in a sandbox

depends only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`
([[../library/discrete_geometry/plasma_ai_2026_monochromatic_triangles_two_colorings_plane/evidence/verify/replay_receipt|the replay receipt]], 2026-10-19). The
[[../library/discrete_geometry/plasma_ai_2026_monochromatic_triangles_two_colorings_plane/evidence/verify/statement_fidelity/_index|graded fresh-context review of the statements]],

found all five faithful. `erdos_173` covers the Statement for triangles of
three non-collinear points, the reading of formal-conjectures and of Mathlib's
`Affine.Triangle`, and `three_distinct_points` covers any three distinct

covers the Statement under either reading. The compared statements allow
