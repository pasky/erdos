# problems/discrete_geometry/E0210/claims

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0210/_index|..]]

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0210/claims/1951_05_01_motzkin|1951_05_01_motzkin]]: Motzkin proves that $n$ points in the plane, not all on a line, determine a
number of ordinary lines that tends to infinity with $n$. This answers the
first question.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0210/claims/1958_01_01_kelly_moser|1958_01_01_kelly_moser]]: Kelly and Moser prove that $n$ points in the plane, not all on a line,
determine at least $3n/7$ ordinary lines, a bound attained at $n=7$.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0210/claims/1993_02_01_csima_sawyer|1993_02_01_csima_sawyer]]: Csima and Sawyer prove that $n$ points in the plane, not all on a line,
determine at least $6n/13$ ordinary lines once $n\ge8$.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0210/claims/2012_08_23_green_tao|2012_08_23_green_tao]]: For all large $n$, Green and Tao determine the least number of ordinary lines
spanned by $n$ points not all on a line: $n/2$ for even $n$ and
$3\lfloor n/4\rfloor$ for odd $n$. Both values are attained, and the result
proves the Dirac–Motzkin conjecture.
