## Restatements and assignment fidelity

For an integer $m\ge2$ and real $t>0$, let
$\gamma=2^{1/m}$ and $a_n=\lfloor t\gamma^n\rfloor$ for
$n\in\mathbb N_0$. The first conclusion says that for every integer lower
bound $L$ and finite $F\subset\mathbb Z$, there is $H\in\mathbb N_0$
such that every integer $N\ge H$ is the sum of a finite set of distinct
values in

$$
\{a_n:n\in\mathbb N_0,\ n\ge L\}\setminus F.
$$

A finite set of deleted occurrences can also be avoided, simultaneously with
$F$, by requiring a later first index. The threshold is allowed to depend on
the parameters and deletions. Negative $L$ does not extend the sequence to
negative indices; it merely imposes no further restriction on $\mathbb N_0$.
Deleted noninteger objects are irrelevant because every sequence value is an
integer. These clarifications preserve the assigned statement.

The second conclusion fixes $\gamma=\sqrt2$ before choosing
$\alpha,\beta>0$. For every such pair, the interleaving

$$
I(2n)=\lfloor\alpha\gamma^n\rfloor,\qquad
I(2n+1)=\lfloor\beta\gamma^n\rfloor
$$

is eventually complete over finite sets of positions. Thus it proves
$\exists\gamma\in(1,2)\ \forall\alpha,\beta>0$ with the catalog's
irrational-ratio condition, and in fact does not need that condition. The
report preserves this quantifier order and separately explains the
distinct-value reading. It makes no assertion for every base in $(1,2)$.

The original commission charged the reviewer to refute the entire D1

## Mathematical grading and reconstruction

### The shift and the source hypotheses

The root argument works for composite as well as prime $m$. If
$\gamma=p/q$ in lowest positive terms, $p^m=2q^m$ forces $q=1$.
No positive integer has $m$th power $2$ for $m\ge2$. Monotonicity of
positive integer powers gives $1<\gamma<2$. The reciprocal $1/\gamma$
is irrational, and $t>0$ permits the cancellation in the source ratio.

Let $\delta=\gamma-1>0$ and $M=\max(F\cup\{0\})$. The report's
sufficient condition

$$
j\ge\max\{0,L\},\qquad
t\gamma^j>\max\{M+1,1/\delta\}
$$

can be satisfied: $(1+\delta)^j\ge1+j\delta$ is unbounded. For all
$n\ge j$, the real increment is $t\gamma^n\delta>1$, hence
$a_{n+1}\ge a_n+1$; also $a_j\ge M+1$. The tail is positive,
injective and avoids every value of $F$. Small $t$, large $m$, empty $F$
and large prescribed $L$ alter this choice without creating an exception.

These deductions validate the frozen proof's stated large-enough shift and
strictly increasing tail. The report makes its elementary growth and floor
inferences explicit; it does not introduce a new completeness lemma. The
foundations used are ordinary integer divisibility, the Archimedean property
of the real numbers, real powers, floors and finite sets. No theorem about
the distribution of fractional parts is required.

### The indexed-to-value transfer and finite deletions

With $u=t\gamma^j$ and $v=t\gamma^{j+1}$, the external theorem applies
because $u,v>0$ and $u/v=1/\gamma$ is irrational. Its terms are exactly
$a_{j+mr}$ and $a_{j+1+ms}$. Both index maps are injective. An intersection
would require $m(r-s)=1$, impossible for integer $m\ge2$.

Consequently even overlapping exponent sets $R,S$ in the external theorem
give a finite set of distinct original indices. Injectivity on the entire
chosen tail, including between the two classes, then preserves the sum on
passing to its set of values. The unused residue classes for $m>2$ create
no obligation: representations from a subset of the available tail are
sufficient. The shift is chosen once before the source threshold and all
target integers, so there is no target-dependent tail or quantifier exchange.

For deleted occurrences $D\subset\mathbb N_0$ finite, requiring
$j\ge1+\max(D\cup\{-1\})$ is an instance of the already arbitrary
first-index condition. Applying the source theorem after this shift proves
the deletion assertion afresh. It does not infer strong completeness from
ordinary completeness.

The frozen proof already states the two residue classes, their disjointness,
the strictly increasing tail, and the resulting distinct-value conclusion.
The report's finite-image sum identities and deletion specialization expose
those inferences. They supply no omitted essential lemma or replacement proof.

### The existential consequence

At $m=2$, the single-ray result with $t=\alpha$ supplies the required
finite set of ray indices $B$. The map $n\mapsto2n$ places it inside the
interleaving without changing the sum. No term from the $\beta$ ray is
used, so coincidences between rays and the value of $\alpha/\beta$ do
not matter. The base $\sqrt2$ is fixed independently of both coefficients.

This is precisely the frozen proof's inclusion argument. The report checks
its quantifiers and its indexed and value conventions, rather than applying
the base-$2$ source theorem directly to $(\alpha,\beta)$ at a new base.
No additional premise or missing transfer lemma is supplied by the grader.
The acceptance order is the single-ray conclusion first, then this consequence.

## Grading the attacks and witnesses

The strongest attempted failure is substantive. At $m=2,t=1$, the first
two values are both $1$, so irrationality of the source ratio alone does
not justify distinct-value sums. The report also gives a genuine
counterexample to the generic indexed-to-value shortcut: two copies of each
$3^k$ represent every nonnegative integer by ternary digits, whereas one
copy of each value cannot represent $2\cdot3^k$. A larger power is too
large, and

$$
\sum_{i=0}^k3^i=\frac{3^{k+1}-1}{2}<2\cdot3^k.
$$

Both witnesses are exact. They test a real failure mode but do not meet the
frozen proof's injective-tail conditions. The report explains why its attack
fails at those conditions, rather than dismissing multiplicity as harmless.

The deletion attack is also valid: removing $1$ from the complete sequence
$1,2,4,\ldots$ leaves only even subset sums. This defeats an unsupported
ordinary-to-strong inference and the excluded case $m=1,t=1$; it does
not defeat reapplication of the theorem after each shift. The $t=0$
control correctly yields only zero sums and tests the positivity hypothesis.

For any proposed threshold independent of $F$, choose an integer $K\ge1$
above it and delete $\{1,\ldots,K\}$. Remaining nonnegative values are
zero or exceed $K$, so they cannot sum to $K$. This verifies the report's
rejection of that uniform strengthening. It does not claim that every
parameter must separately affect every possible threshold.

For the consequence, the report attacks quantifier reversal and a wrong-base
source application, and answers each with the explicit fixed base and source
coefficients. Its inherited multiplicity risk is separately addressed by
using only the first ray. These are attacks on both actual conclusions, not
finite samples standing in for their universal proofs. Their arithmetic and
scope were rederived in this grading. No computational mathematical leg is
required or claimed.
