**Claim.** Let $A$ be the set of squarefull positive integers, those in which
every prime factor occurs to exponent at least two, $1$ included, and let
$r(n)$ count the ordered pairs $(a,b)\in A^2$ with $a+b=n$; the write-up's
Problem 1.1 restates the question of
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0943/_index|Problem 943]] as whether
$r(n)=n^{o(1)}$. Theorem 1.2 of the 24-page write-up *Erdős Problem 943 on
sums of two squarefull numbers*, dated 18 March 2026 (the first link above),
states that for every $\varepsilon>0$ and every $n\ge1$

$$
r(n)\ll_\varepsilon n^{2/5+\varepsilon}.
$$

The proof (Section 3) writes each summand uniquely as $x^2y^3$ with $y$
squarefree and sorts the solutions of $x_1^2y_1^3+x_2^2y_2^3=n$ into dyadic
boxes $x_i\sim X_i$, $y_i\sim Y_i$. With $y_1,y_2$ fixed, the solutions inject
into the representations of one integer $M\le n^{4/3}$ by a form $X^2+dY^2$
with $d$ squarefree, which number at most $6\tau(M)$ by an ideal count in
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$ (Lemma 2.2 and Proposition 3.1). With $x_1,x_2$
fixed, the pairs $(y_1,y_2)$ are primitive points of height at most $n^{1/3}$
on a diagonal cubic, whose number is $O_\varepsilon(n^\varepsilon)$ by
Heath-Brown's uniform count (Proposition 3.2). So a box holds
$\ll_\varepsilon n^{\varepsilon}\min(X_1X_2,Y_1Y_2)$ solutions, and
$(X_1X_2)^2(Y_1Y_2)^3\le n^2$ makes that minimum at most $n^{2/5}$. Sections 4
to 10 study the coprime subcount, the representations with $(a,b)=1$: they
give reformulations, bounds for a fixed squarefree cube factor of the first
summand and for dyadic congruence boxes, and a second-moment barrier, and pose
as Problem 10.1 a first-moment estimate that would make the coprime subcount
$n^{o(1)}$. The write-up states that no reduction of $r(n)$ to this model is
known, since dividing a representation by $\gcd(a,b)$ need not leave both
summands squarefull (Remark 4.1). Section 11 surveys the literature of 2025
and 2026.

**Covers.** A partial result toward the answer yes: the bound
$1_A\ast1_A(n)\ll_\varepsilon n^{2/5+\varepsilon}$ in place of the
$n^{o(1)}$ the question asks for. Whether $1_A\ast1_A(n)=n^{o(1)}$ is not
settled. The write-up proves no sharper bound on the full count, and its
results on the coprime subcount do not bound it.

**Claimant and assistance.** The write-up prints Przemyslaw Chojecki as its

**Relation to Plasma AI's claim.**
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0943/claims/2026_10_19_plasma_ai|Plasma AI's claim page]]
records the same bound for the same count, proved in
[[theory/number_theory/L24_powerful_sum_bound/_index|L24]], an
author-recorded result of later date; that page names this write-up as a
pending claim with the same bound. L24 uses a norm count of the same kind for
fixed cube factors. In place of the diagonal-cubic count it uses the counts of
either summand's two parameters, which determine the representation, and
combines three bounds in each dyadic box, so it takes no input from
Heath-Brown's preprint.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki. Beyond the divisor bound and the lemmas
the write-up proves, its proof rests on one cited theorem, Theorem 1.3 of D. R.
Heath-Brown's preprint *Counting square-full solutions to $x+y=z$*
(arXiv:2601.07817), the write-up's Lemma 2.3; that preprint is not filed in the
library.
