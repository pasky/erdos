**Claim.** Let $p>q\geq 2$ be coprime and call $n$ representable if it is a
sum of integers $p^kq^l$ no one of which divides another. For each of the
pairs $(p,q)=(5,2)$, $(9,2)$ and $(5,3)$ there is an infinite sequence of
non-representable numbers greater than $1$ whose terms are pairwise coprime
and each coprime to $pq$; in particular there are infinitely many
non-representable $n$ coprime to $pq$, and infinitely many pairwise coprime
ones, the two readings of the second question of
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E1110/_index|Problem 1110]]. These are
exactly the pairs that Yu and Chen [YuCh22] left open when they proved that
there are infinitely many coprime non-representable numbers for $q>3$, for
$q=3$ with $p\neq 5$, and for $q=2$ with $p\notin\{3,5,9\}$, so together
with their theorem the second question is answered yes for every coprime
pair other than $\{2,3\}$. The claimant, the forum user
Apiros3, posted the claim on the erdosproblems.com proof-claims tab on
2026-08-05 as a partial proof; the claim's tab names the AI systems GPT-5.6 Sol
and GPT-5.5 as the tools that took part in writing the formalization, and the
repository's own statement says the
formalization was generated with OpenAI models and that its owner accepts
responsibility for errors without claiming credit for model-generated work.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Apiros3
(account apiros3) on 5 August 2026, giving "GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-5.5" as the AI
used:

> GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-5.5 were used in the proof formalization. There are
> infinitely many coprime non-representable numbers. Yu and Chen reduced this
> problem to three cases (p,q) = (5,2), (9,2), (5,3), and we give a proof for
> these three. The proof is by picking a small seed c < p and then repeatedly
> forming larger coprime non-representable numbers of the form p^a + c q^M. The
> proof sketch is as follows: - Any representation is just a finite antichain of
> grid points (as no terms divide each other) - If an antichain sum in the
> rectangle 0 <= i <= a, 0 <= j < M is congruent to p^a mod q^M, then it is
> exactly p^a. - If N = p^a + c q^M has a representation, then c q^M < (p-1)p^a
> forces (a,0) to be a grid point. Removing this and dividing by q^M implies c
> is representable, a contradiction. The cases are each proven by seeds: -
> (5,2): c = 3 - (9,2): c = 5 - (5,3): c = 2

**The argument.** A representation is a finite antichain of lattice points
$(i,j)$ in the coordinatewise order, with $p^iq^j$ the summands. For
coprime bases $p,q>1$, coordinatewise comparison is equivalent to
divisibility of these terms. The pinned development's
[`LowRowSyncProvider`](https://github.com/Apiros3/erdos1110/blob/6d66854a3d940490c25a802b9aed54fe896ec8ec/Erdos1110/Providers.lean)
includes the hypothesis $p^a<q^M$: for $M\geq1$, an antichain in
$0\leq i\leq a$, $0\leq j<M$ whose sum is congruent to $p^a$ modulo
$q^M$ is then exactly $\{(a,0)\}$. The theorem
`lowRowSyncProvider_core` in
[`Embedding.lean`](https://github.com/Apiros3/erdos1110/blob/6d66854a3d940490c25a802b9aed54fe896ec8ec/Erdos1110/Embedding.lean)
assembles this from the residual induction in `LowRowResidual.lean` and
the singleton lemma in `LowRows.lean`.

The unrestricted rectangle sentence in the quoted submission sketch is
false: $p=5$, $q=2$, $a=M=1$ and the singleton antichain $\{(0,0)\}$
give a sum $1\equiv5\pmod2$ that is not $5$. This witness violates
$p^a<q^M$, so it does not refute the pinned lemma. Nor does the size
inequality below alone supply that missing hypothesis.

For the embedding, take positive $a,M$ in the two-sided window

$$
p^a<q^M,
\qquad cq^M<(p-1)p^a,
$$

and set $N=p^a+cq^M$, where $c$ is non-representable. The second
inequality gives $N<p^{a+1}$, so every point in a representation of $N$
has first coordinate at most $a$. Split the representation into rows
below $M$ and rows at least $M$. The low-row sum is congruent to $p^a$
modulo $q^M$, and the first inequality makes the singleton lemma
applicable. The low rows are therefore exactly $\{(a,0)\}$. Subtracting
$p^a$ and shifting every remaining second coordinate down by $M$ preserves
the antichain condition and gives a representation of $c$, a contradiction.
This is the argument of `embeddingNonrepProvider_of_lowRowSync` in the
pinned module. It uses both window inequalities.

The seeds are $c=3$ for $(5,2)$, $c=5$ for $(9,2)$ and $c=2$ for
$(5,3)$. Each is positive, smaller than $p-1$, non-representable and
coprime to $pq$; every prime factor of $c+1$ divides $pq$. Each resulting
$N$ is prime to $pq$, since $N\equiv cq^M\pmod p$ and
$N\equiv p^a\pmod q$. For pairwise coprimality, let $D$ be the product
of the previously chosen terms. The development's `PeriodProvider` and
`PowerWindowProvider` choose $a,M$ in the displayed window with
$p^a\equiv q^M\equiv1\pmod D$. Thus $N\equiv c+1\pmod D$, and
$\gcd(N,D)=1$ because $\gcd(D,pq)=1$. The construction in
[`Sequence.lean`](https://github.com/Apiros3/erdos1110/blob/6d66854a3d940490c25a802b9aed54fe896ec8ec/Erdos1110/Sequence.lean)
iterates this step. These are the source's mathematical interfaces; the
development has not been built or independently audited here.

A comment in the erdosproblems.com thread of 2026-10-02 observes that
the same seed device extends to every coprime pair other than $(4,3)$ and
$(6,5)$, with $c$ the smallest prime not dividing $pq$ when $pq$ is even and
$c=t-1$ for $t$ the least prime factor of $pq$ when $pq$ is odd; that remark
is a thread comment, not part of the claim.

**Covers.** The second question of the problem, infinitely many coprime
non-representable numbers, in the pairwise-coprime reading and hence in the
coprime-to-$pq$ reading, for the three pairs $(5,2)$, $(9,2)$ and $(5,3)$,
and through Yu and Chen's theorem, which the Lean development also proves,
for every coprime pair $p>q\geq 2$ with $\{p,q\}\neq\{2,3\}$. The first
question, the density of the non-representable numbers, is not addressed.

**Formalization.** The repository `Apiros3/erdos1110` (Apache License 2.0,
Lean toolchain `v4.32.1`; the pinned commit of 2026-10-02 is the repository's
only commit) defines `Erdos1110Conclusion p q` as the existence of a sequence
of non-representable numbers greater than $1$, each coprime to $pq$ and
pairwise coprime, and declares the theorems
`Erdos1110.exceptional_5_2_unconditional`,
`Erdos1110.exceptional_9_2_unconditional`,
`Erdos1110.exceptional_5_3_unconditional` and their conjunction
`Erdos1110.exceptional_cases_unconditional`, which its README calls the new
contribution. It also proves Yu and Chen's range in Lean, unconditionally:
`yuChenRange_unconditional` discharges the pairs with $q>3$, with $q=3$ and
$p>6$, and with $q=2$ and $p>10$ through counting bounds that give the
representable numbers density zero (the `representableDensityZero` theorems
of `Density/YuChen.lean`), and the pairs $(4,3)$ and $(7,2)$ through
explicit constructions; `erdos1110_unconditional` and
`Erdos1110.erdos1110_unordered` assemble these with the three exceptional
pairs into the conclusion for every coprime pair of bases at least $2$ other
than $\{2,3\}$, with no hypothesis beyond coprimality, and
`erdos1110_setConclusion_unordered` and
`erdos1110_valueSetConclusion_unordered` restate it as an infinite pairwise
coprime set, the form of the formal-conjectures statement. The forum claim's
write-up link, a Markdown file under `docs/`, is not in that commit, and the
README describes the manuscript as in preparation, so the repository is the
only retained posting. This corpus has not built or audited the development;
it is a `formalization` link and not `formalized` evidence.
