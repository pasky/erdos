# problems/number_theory/E1096/claims

[[problems/number_theory/E1096/_index|..]]

[[problems/number_theory/E1096/claims/1998_04_01_erdos_komornik|1998_04_01_erdos_komornik]]: Erdős and Komornik's 1998 theorem: the gaps of the ordered sums of distinct
powers of $q$ tend to zero for every $q\in(1,2^{1/4}]$ other than the square
root of the second Pisot number. It is the first resolution, refereed and
credited by erdosproblems.com.

[[problems/number_theory/E1096/claims/2011_03_23_akiyama_komornik|2011_03_23_akiyama_komornik]]: Akiyama and Komornik's theorem: the gaps of the ordered sums of distinct
powers of $q$ tend to zero for every $q\in(1,2^{1/3}]$, which closes the point
Erdős and Komornik had left out. It was refereed and published in the Journal
of Number Theory (2013).

[[problems/number_theory/E1096/claims/2011_11_10_feng|2011_11_10_feng]]: Feng's theorem, refereed in JEMS 2016: the gaps tend to zero for every
$q\in(1,\sqrt2)$ whose square is not a Pisot number. With Siegel's smallest
Pisot number this answers the question for every $q$ below about 1.151.

[[problems/number_theory/E1096/claims/2026_04_16_acosta_de_leon|2026_04_16_acosta_de_leon]]: Acosta De León's two-page note of 16 April 2026, posted on a data repository
and linked from the problem's thread, deduces the answer for every $q$ below
the square root of the smallest Pisot number from Feng's Theorem 1.4. It is
pending.
