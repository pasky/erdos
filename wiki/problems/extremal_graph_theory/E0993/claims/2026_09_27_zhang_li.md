***

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Tong Zhang
(account TongZhang) on 27 September 2026, giving "GPT6.0 astra" as the AI used:

> The proof consists of two parts, covering \(n\le60\) and \(n\ge59\). For
> \(n\le60\), we derive inequalities from the structural properties of forests.
> For \(n\ge59\), we assign weights to independent sets according to their
> cardinality, reducing the required coefficient inequalities to probabilistic
> estimates.

The Palomar registry's description of entry PALOMAR-2026-10-11-000012:

> A Lean 4 and Mathlib formalization of the theorem that the independence
> sequence of every finite forest is unimodal: the numbers of independent sets
> with 0, 1, 2, ... vertices weakly increase up to some index and weakly
> decrease after it. This is the statement listed as Erdős Problem 993; later
> sources attribute the question to a 1987 paper of Alavi, Malde, Schwenk and
> Erdős. The first claimed proof for all finite forests is by T. Zhang and W.
> Li (self-archived preprints, September 2026). This repository formalizes a
> second proof, by W. Li, K. Vallier and T. Zhang: it adapts the public
> arXiv:2610.07943v1 and follows version 2.15 of that manuscript, deposited with
> restricted access and under revision. The Lean proof is computer-assisted and
> in two parts (at most 24 vertices; at least 25). About 13,700 finite
> computations are evaluated by the Lean kernel, without native_decide, on the
> three standard axioms. AI systems produced and reviewed all of the Lean; no
> human has read the Lean proofs. No manuscript cited is peer reviewed. No
> priority and no first formalization is claimed.

**The claim.** For every finite forest $F$ the sequence
$i_0(F),i_1(F),\ldots,i_{\alpha(F)}(F)$ of its independent-set counts by
size is unimodal: the statement of
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0993/_index|Problem 993]] in full (T. Zhang
and W. Li, *Unimodality of forest independence polynomials*, Zenodo,
27 September 2026, doi:10.5281/zenodo.22999166, CC BY 4.0; submitted to the
erdosproblems.com proof-claims tab the same day by the first author, naming
GPT6.0 astra as a tool). The authors' summary splits the proof in two: forests
on at most $60$ vertices are handled by inequalities derived from the structure
of forests, and forests on at least $59$ vertices by weighting independent
sets by their size, which reduces the needed comparisons of adjacent
coefficients to probabilistic estimates; a thread comment by the first
author explains the latter as a conditional-binomial representation of the
size of a random independent set in the hard-core model, with the weights
the marginal occupation probabilities, and credits the paper of Fang, Lu,
Nevo, Yao and Zheng
([[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0993/claims/2026_09_17_fang_lu_nevo_yao_zheng|their claim page]])
as the inspiration. The Zenodo record cites the code of the
computer-assisted part at a fixed revision of the repository
`zhangzenozhang-jpg/forest-unimodality-arxiv-verification` (the first `code`
link), and its update of 30 September 2026 states that the paper's arXiv
submission was withdrawn on 28 September 2026, before it was publicly
announced, pending a revision for clarity.

**The revision and the formalization.** A second version, *Unimodality of
forest independence polynomials*, version 2.2 by W. Li, K. Vallier and
T. Zhang (Zenodo record 23182492, 6 October 2026, CC BY 4.0; posted on arXiv
the same day as arXiv:2610.07943v1, 81 pages, whose comments field calls it
a provisional manuscript being rewritten before journal submission and
discloses substantial AI contributions), presents what its description
calls a second proof, with the threshold lowered to $25$
vertices: forests on at least $25$ vertices by the paper's Sections 2--5 and
Appendix B, and forests on at most $24$ vertices by exact counting (its
Section 6, by hand except for exact rational evaluations of two formulas at
$43$ parameter triples); the thread comment of 6 October 2026 announcing it says the
revision removes part of the computer-assisted verification and
reorganizes the proof, and that the authors intend a shorter and less
computational version; the record cites its supplementary code at a fixed
revision of `zhangzenozhang-jpg/forest-unimodality-v2.2-supplement` (the
second `code` link). Its Lean 4 development
(`selfreferencing/erdos993-forest-unimodality-lean` at the pinned commit,
Lean v4.28.0 with Mathlib v4.28.0, 1,352 modules in the closure, Apache
2.0) names `erdos993_v22_final` in `Erdos993Lean/Analytic/V22/Final.lean`
as its main theorem, stated for acyclic simple graphs on every vertex count;
its README says the development follows Sections 2--5 and Appendix B for the
large forests and replaces Section 6 for the small ones by $249$
kernel-checked rational certificates of a linear relaxation; its README
reports no `sorry` and the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`
and `Quot.sound` together with Lean's compiler axioms `Lean.ofReduceBool`
and `Lean.trustCompiler`, from $411$ compiled finite checks. Those are the
repository's own statements; no build or audit of the development by this
corpus, and no comparison of the formal statement with the problem's
wording, is recorded.

**The registered formalization.** A later version of the second proof,
version 2.15 (Zenodo record 23269638, revision r2 of 10 October 2026, which the
record describes as a 137-page working manuscript under revision by its authors
and says corrects the $k=1$ case in the proof of Lemma 7.4, with the Lean commit
unchanged), is deposited with restricted access and all rights reserved, its
file available from the authors on request; this corpus has not read it.
Its Lean 4 formalization by K. Vallier and T. Zhang
(`selfreferencing/erdos993-palomar` at the pinned commit of 10 October 2026,
Lean v4.35.0-rc2 with the matching Mathlib, Apache 2.0) was registered on
the Palomar registry on 11 October 2026 as PALOMAR-2026-10-11-000012 (the
`record` link). Its `Challenge.lean` states `Erdos993.erdos_993_forest`,
that the independence sequence of every acyclic simple graph on a finite
vertex type is unimodal, and the tree case `Erdos993.erdos_993`; its README
says that the two definitions and the two statements are those of the forest
and tree statements in formal-conjectures pull request #4192, renamed.
`Solution.lean` proves both. The README reports that the Lean adapts
arXiv:2610.07943v1 and follows version 2.15, in two parts, forests on at
most $24$ vertices and on at least $25$; that every finite computation,
about 13,700 in all, is evaluated by the Lean kernel through
`decide +kernel`, with no `native_decide`, so that the axioms are `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` only; that AI systems produced all of
the Lean and its certificate data; and that no human has read the Lean
proofs, nor yet all of the manuscript's proofs. The registry's record lists
the development above as superseded by this one, and reports that its two
kernel checkers, nanoda and con-ron, accepted both theorems at the pinned
commit against the challenge with only those three axioms permitted, and an
automated review by a language model (`codex:gpt-6-sol`) with outcome
neutral. This corpus has not built the repository or compared its statement
with the problem's wording.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki as a premise: the paper of Fang, Lu,
Nevo, Yao and Zheng is credited as the inspiration for the method, not used
as an input, and the claim covers every forest itself.
