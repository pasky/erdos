# One more dimension in the unit-congruence bound

***

This note improves a universal bound but does not resolve Erdős 963.
The notation $d(P)$ denotes maximum dissociation dimension, not
the minimum size of an inclusion-maximal dissociated subset.

## Extension theorem

**Theorem.** Let $q\ge4$ be even, and let $O$ be a finite set of
positive integers in one residue class $a\pmod q$, where
$\gcd(a,q)=1$. If $d(O)\le q+1$, then

$$
|O|\le2^{d(O)-1}.
\tag{1}
$$


The case $d(O)\le q$ follows from
[[research/erdos_963/affine_coherence|affine-coherent extraction and congruence transfer]].
Indeed, if the count fails, extraction gives an affine-coherent
$(d(O)+1)$-subset, and the transfer lemma makes it coherent and
hence dissociated. We prove the remaining case $d=q+1$.

Split $O=A\sqcup B$ into its two residue classes modulo $2q$.
If one is empty, the same transfer argument, now modulo $2q$,
proves (1). Assume both are nonempty.

Suppose $D\subseteq A$ is dissociated and $|D|=d$, and put
$S=\sum D$. Every $b\in B$ has a ternary representation on $D$.
If its coefficient sum is $s$, reduction modulo $2q$ gives

$$
s\equiv q+1=d\pmod{2q},\qquad -d\le s\le d.
$$

The only possibilities are $s=d$ and $s=2-d$. The former
representation is all-plus. In the latter, positivity excludes
all-negative representations, leaving exactly one positive and
$d-1$ negative coefficients. Thus

$$
b=S\quad\hbox{or}\quad b=2x-S\quad(x\in D).
\tag{2}
$$

At most one value of the second kind is positive. Consequently,
if $d(A)=d$, then $|B|\le2$. The same assertion holds with the
two fibers interchanged.

If both fibers have at least three elements, they therefore have
dimension at most $d-1=q$. Apply the already proved unit-class
bound modulo $q$ to each: their total size is at most $2^{d-1}$.
It remains to treat a fiber $B$ of size one or two.

If $d(A)\le d-1$, then
$|O|\le2^{d-2}+2\le2^{d-1}$, so suppose $d(A)=d$.
When $B=\{b,c\}$, equation (2) shows that every maximum
$d$-subset of $A$ contains

$$
x=(b+c)/2.
$$

Deleting $x$ lowers the dimension of $A$ to at most $d-1$.
Applying the unit-class bound to $A\setminus\{x\}$ gives

$$
|O|\le2^{d-2}+3\le2^{d-1},
$$

since $d\ge5$.

## The singleton fiber

Let $B=\{b\}$. By a *circuit* we mean a nonzero ternary relation
whose support contains no smaller support of a nonzero ternary
relation. Consider all circuits on $O$ of support size at most $d+1$.

Every such circuit contained in $A$ is balanced: its coefficient
sum is divisible by $2q$, and its absolute value is at most
$d+1=q+2<2q$. A circuit through $b$, oriented as
$b=\sum_{x\in A}\varepsilon_xx$, has at most $d$ other
coordinates. The same congruence and positivity argument as in
(2) gives exactly two possible forms:

$$
b=\sum_{x\in E}x,\quad |E|=d,
\qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
b=x-\sum_{y\in E'}y,\quad |E'|=d-1.
\tag{3}
$$


If $b>\max A$, only the first form is possible. If $b<\max A$,
the first form is impossible as well: circuit minimality makes its
$d$ summands dissociated, and adjoining $\max A>b=\sum E$
would give a dissociated $(d+1)$-set. Thus all circuits through
$b$ have the same coefficient sum on $A$, namely $d$ when
$b>\max A$ and $2-d$ when $b<\max A$.

Let $R\subseteq\mathbb Z^O$ be generated by the circuits just
considered, and put $H=\mathbb Z^O/R$. Two integer functionals
annihilate $R$: the actual weights and the function $h$ equal to
one on $A$, with

$$
h(b)=d\quad(b>\max A),\qquad
h(b)=2-d\quad(b<\max A).
$$

These functionals are rationally independent, since $A$ contains
at least two distinct weights. Hence $\operatorname{rank}H\ge2$.
In particular $\dim_{\mathbb F_2}(H/2H)\ge2$.

The actual odd weights give a character $\lambda:H\to\mathbb F_2$
equal to one on every coordinate column. Choose a character
$\chi\notin\{0,\lambda\}$. Its values partition $O$ into two
nonempty classes $O_0,O_1$. Both have dimension at most $d-1$.
For if a dissociated $d$-set lay in $O_0$, adjoining a point of
$O_1$ would produce a circuit of size at most $d+1$ using that
point; applying $\chi$ contradicts that relation. For a $d$-set
in $O_1$, apply $\chi+\lambda$ instead.

The classes are still subsets of the original unit class modulo
$q$. The established bound at dimension at most $q=d-1$ gives
$|O_0|,|O_1|\le2^{d-2}$. This proves (1).

## A recursive universal estimate

For $d\ge1$ put

$$
B_d=\min\left\{
2^{d+\lfloor\log_2(d+1)\rfloor-1}-1,
2^{\lceil\log_2d\rceil-1}(2^d-1)
\right\},
$$

where the second term is omitted for $d=1$. These are the
previously proved positive-set bounds: the first follows by
applying the binary-tree bound to $\{0\}\cup P\cup(-P)$,
and the second is the coherent-support bound.

Set $U_0=0$, $U_d=2^d-1$ for $1\le d\le4$, and, for $d\ge5$,

$$
q_d=2^{\lceil\log_2(d-1)\rceil},\qquad
U_d=\min\{B_d,\ q_d2^{d-2}+U_{d-1}\}.
\tag{4}
$$

Then every finite positive real set of dimension at most $d$ has
at most $U_d$ elements, and every finite real set of dimension
at most $d$ has at most $2U_d+1$ elements.

To prove this, use the established integer realization and divide
by the largest common power of two. The resulting positive set
has a nonempty odd part. Its even part has dimension at most
$d-1$, because one odd element can be adjoined to every
dissociated even subset. Partition the odd part into its $q_d/2$
unit classes modulo $q_d$. The theorem bounds each by $2^{d-1}$,
since $d\le q_d+1$. This proves the second term of (4), and
$B_d$ supplies the first. Symmetrization proves the real-set bound.

The first new values are

$$
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
d&5&6&7&8&9\\\hline
U_d&47&127&383&895&1919\\
2U_d+1&95&255&767&1791&3839.
\end{array}
$$

In particular every $96$-element real set contains a dissociated
six-set. This still does **not** prove the required six-set for
every $64$-element real set. Equivalently, the estimate $U_5=47$
does not give the target positive bound $U_5=31$.

The remaining unit-congruence gap begins at $d=q+2$. A ternary
representation across the two modulo-$2q$ fibers can then omit
a coordinate of a maximum set, and the full-support argument
(2), as well as the singleton circuit argument, no longer applies.

### Sharpening by the new affine theorem

The subsequent [[research/erdos_963/affine_eight|eight-point affine theorem]]
improves the unit-class count, for $4\le d\le q+1$, to $7\,2^{d-4}$. Thus (4)
can be strengthened: keep $U_0=0$ and $U_d=2^d-1$ for $1\le d\le4$, and replace
its recursion by

$$
\boxed{U_d=\min\{B_d,\ 7q_d2^{d-5}+U_{d-1}\}
\qquad(d\ge5).}
\tag{5}
$$

The proof is the same parity split. The first values are now

$$
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
d&5&6&7&8&9\\\hline
U_d&43&127&351&799&1695\\
2U_d+1&87&255&703&1599&3391.
\end{array}
$$

In particular every $88$-element real set has a dissociated
six-set, but the requested threshold is $64$, still unproved.

The same note proves the unmodified unit-class count through $d\le q+2$ for even
$q\ge6$, through $d\le q+3$ for even $q\ge10$, and through
$d\le\lfloor4q/3\rfloor$ for even $q\ge56$. Those additional congruence ranges
can further lower the odd-part term at some dimensions; they do not establish
the bound for unrestricted odd sets. The concrete endpoint $q=4,d=6$ was left
unresolved by these arguments; it is settled, with bound $31$, by
[[research/erdos_963/congruence_grid|the grid theorem]].

## Current strengthened recursion

The later [[research/erdos_963/affine_fourteen|fourteen-point theorem]] gives
the affine bound $13\,2^{d-5}$ for $d\ge5$. For even $q\ge6$ the unit-class
argument above propagates that same bound through $d=q+1$: both dropped classes
contribute $13\,2^{d-6}$, and the two-point exception contributes at most
$13\,2^{d-6}+3\le13\,2^{d-5}$. The already proved case $d\le q$ follows directly
from the affine theorem. The $q=4,d=5$ endpoint retains its proved bound
fourteen, not thirteen.

Combining these facts with [[research/erdos_963/positive_seven|the sharp
positive dimension-three bound]] and the grid theorem gives the following
current recursion. Set

$$
(U_0,U_1,U_2,U_3,U_4,U_5,U_6)=(0,1,3,6,14,42,104),
$$

and for $d\ge7$ set

$$
\boxed{U_d=\min\{B_d,\ 13q_d2^{d-6}+U_{d-1}\},
\qquad q_d=2^{\lceil\log_2(d-1)\rceil}.}
\tag{6}
$$

Then $|P|\le U_{d(P)}$ for positive sets, and
$|A|\le2U_{d(A)}+1$ for arbitrary real sets.

For the new starting values, the established odd bounds through
dimension four are $1,2,4,8$. The sharp positive bound $U_3=6$
therefore gives $U_4=8+6=14$. Splitting an odd dimension-five
set modulo four gives at most $28$ elements, hence
$U_5=28+14=42$. The grid theorem bounds each modulo-four
class of a dimension-six odd set by $31$, so its size is at
most $62$ and $U_6=62+42=104$. Each use of the even-part
bound is justified by adjoining an odd element after normalization.
For $d\ge7$, there are $q_d/2$ odd classes, each bounded
by $13\,2^{d-5}$, proving (6).

In particular,

$$
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}
d&3&4&5&6&7&8&9\\\hline
U_d&6&14&42&104&312&728&1560\\
2U_d+1&13&29&85&209&625&1457&3121.
\end{array}
$$

Thus every $86$-element real set has a dissociated six-set,
and every $210$-element real set has a dissociated seven-set.
The required thresholds $64$ and $128$ remain unproved.
