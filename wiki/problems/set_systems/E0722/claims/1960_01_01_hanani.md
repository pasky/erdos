***

**Claim.** The Theorem of Section 3 of Hanani's *On quadruple systems*
(p. 148) states that a system $S(3,4,n)$, a family of $4$-subsets of an
$n$-element set containing every $3$-subset exactly once, exists if and only
if $n\equiv2$ or $4\pmod 6$. The congruence is the divisibility condition of
[[problems/set_systems/E0722/_index|Problem 722]] for $(r,k)=(3,4)$,
$\binom{4-i}{3-i}\mid\binom{n-i}{3-i}$ for $i=0,1,2$, so the answer is yes
for $(3,4)$ and every $n$, not only large $n$. The necessity is the counting
argument of the paper's Section 1; the sufficiency is proved by induction on
$n$, through recursive constructions that build a system on $n$ points from
systems on smaller admissible numbers of points. The paper settles the
quadruple case of Steiner's problem of 1853, open for more than a century;
Erdős recounts in [Er81], Part VI, that Hanani told him of the result in
1955 and that he urged its publication.

**Covers.** The case $(r,k)=(3,4)$ of the problem, for every $n$: the
divisibility conditions, $n\equiv2$ or $4\pmod 6$, suffice for a Steiner
system $S(3,4,n)$. The paper says nothing about any other pair $(r,k)$; the
cases $(2,4)$ and $(2,5)$ are
[[problems/set_systems/E0722/claims/1961_06_01_hanani|Hanani's block designs]],
and the general case is
[[problems/set_systems/E0722/claims/2014_01_15_keevash|Keevash's existence of designs]].

**Acceptance.** Refereed: H. Hanani, On quadruple systems, Canad. J. Math.
**12** (1960), 145–157, received 9 February 1959. The record gives the year
only, so the page is dated to the first day of that year. The erdosproblems.com
commentary credits the case $(3,4)$ to Hanani under its key [Ha61]. The
erdosproblems.com reference record resolves that key to his 1961 paper on
$2$-designs, and [Er81] also attaches the case to the 1961 paper. This 1960
paper is the one that proves the case. The curator credits the problem's PROVED
label to Keevash and names this case in the progression that ends with Keevash.
That does not settle the problem on this result, so the page lists no `reviewed`
evidence. The proof has not been reconstructed or independently reviewed in this
corpus.
