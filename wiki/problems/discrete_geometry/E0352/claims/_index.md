# problems/discrete_geometry/E0352/claims

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0352/_index|..]]

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0352/claims/1978_01_01_erdos|1978_01_01_erdos]]: Erdős states, leaving the proof to the reader, that a planar set of infinite
measure contains a triangle of every prescribed area. Later sources add
unbounded sets of positive measure, so the question is answered for these
sets.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0352/claims/2002_01_01_freiling_mauldin|2002_01_01_freiling_mauldin]]: Mauldin's 2002 chapter reports Freiling and Mauldin's theorem that a planar
set with no triangle of area greater than $1$ has outer measure at most
$4\pi/\sqrt{27}$. For convex sets it answers the question yes.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0352/claims/2013_01_01_freiling_mauldin|2013_01_01_freiling_mauldin]]: Mauldin's 2013 survey reports that Erdős's constant $4\pi/\sqrt{27}$ is the
sharp threshold when the set is the union of the interiors of at most three
compact convex sets. The general case is open.
