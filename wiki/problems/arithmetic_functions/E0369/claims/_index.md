# problems/arithmetic_functions/E0369/claims

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0369/_index|..]]

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0369/claims/1976_08_01_eggleton_selfridge|1976_08_01_eggleton_selfridge]]: Eggleton and Selfridge give, for every $\epsilon>0$, infinitely many runs
$n,\dots,n+4$ of five consecutive integers that are all $n^\epsilon$-smooth.
This settles the question and erdosproblems.com's first reading for $k\le5$.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0369/claims/1998_04_01_balog_wooley|1998_04_01_balog_wooley]]: Balog and Wooley prove that for every fixed $u>1$ infinitely many $n$ are
followed by about $\log\log\log\log n$ consecutive $n^{1/u}$-smooth integers.
This settles the problem as worded and its first reading, and the second
reading only for infinitely many $n$.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0369/claims/2017_10_05_bober_fretwell_martin_wooley|2017_10_05_bober_fretwell_martin_wooley]]: Theorem 2.1 of Bober, Fretwell, Martin and Wooley gives, for every
$\epsilon>0$ and $k$ and all large $n$, $k$ consecutive $n^\epsilon$-smooth
integers in $[n-n^\theta,n]$ for some $\theta<1$.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0369/claims/2026_03_26_yang|2026_03_26_yang]]: Yang shows that for every $\epsilon>0$ and $k$ and all large $N$ the interval
$[N/2,N]$ holds $k$ consecutive integers $m$ that are each
$m^\epsilon$-smooth. This is erdosproblems.com's second strengthening, and the
erdosproblems.com curator accepted it on the forum.
