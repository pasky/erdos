# problems/additive_combinatorics/E0789/claims

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0789/_index|..]]

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0789/claims/1962_01_01_erdos|1962_01_01_erdos]]: Theorem IV of Erdős (Mat. Lapok 1962, refereed) shows that an admissible
sequence has $A(x)<Cx^{5/6}$, which gives $h(n)<Cn^{5/6}$, the first upper
bound for Problem 789. The paper contains no $(n\log n)^{1/3}$ lower bound.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0789/claims/1966_01_01_straus|1966_01_01_straus]]: Straus's 1966 theorem (J. Math. Sci.) shows that an admissible subset of
$\{1,\ldots,N\}$ has at most a constant times $N^{1/2}$ elements, which gives
$h(n)\ll n^{1/2}$. It is refereed and known here through the 1991 reproof and
the 1999 account.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0789/claims/1974_04_01_choi|1974_04_01_choi]]: Estimate (1) of Choi (J. Number Theory 1974, refereed) states that any $n$
nonzero integers contain an admissible subset of size $\gg(n\log n)^{1/3}$.
This is the best refereed lower bound for $h(n)$ and refines Erdős's
$n^{1/3}$.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0789/claims/2026_09_12_korsky|2026_09_12_korsky]]: Samuel Korsky makes an AI-assisted full claim that every set of $n$ integers
has an admissible subset of size at least $c(n\log\log n/\log n)^{1/2}$, which
with Straus's bound would fix $h(n)$ up to logarithms. The write-up is on a
file-sharing service, with a third-party Lean proof.
