# problems/unit_fractions/E0297/claims

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0297/_index|..]]

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0297/claims/2024_03_25_steinerberger|2024_03_25_steinerberger]]: Steinerberger proves that, for all large $N$, at most $2^{0.93N}$ subsets of
$\{1,\ldots,N\}$ have reciprocal sum at most one. So the number with
reciprocal sum exactly one is not $2^{N-o(N)}$.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0297/claims/2024_04_10_liu_sawhney|2024_04_10_liu_sawhney]]: Liu and Sawhney prove that the number of subsets of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ with
reciprocal sum one is $\exp(\gamma N+o(N))$, where $\gamma\approx0.631573$ is
defined by an integral equation.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0297/claims/2024_04_24_conlon_fox_he_mubayi_pham_suk_verstraete|2024_04_24_conlon_fox_he_mubayi_pham_suk_verstraete]]: Conlon, Fox, He, Mubayi, Pham, Suk and Verstraëte prove that the number of
subsets of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ with reciprocal sum one is $2^{cN+o(N)}$, for an
explicit constant $c\approx0.91117$.
