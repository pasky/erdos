# problems/graph_coloring/E0740/claims

[[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/_index|..]]

[[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/claims/1977_06_01_rodl|1977_06_01_rodl]]: Rödl (Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 1977) proves that large finite chromatic number
forces a complete graph or a triangle-free subgraph of large chromatic number.
This gives the case $\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_0$ with $r\le4$.

[[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/claims/1988_09_01_komjath_shelah|1988_09_01_komjath_shelah]]: Komjáth and Shelah (J. Symbolic Logic 1988) prove it consistent with ZFC that
some $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph has only countably chromatic triangle-free
subgraphs. So the statement for every cardinal is not a theorem of ZFC.

[[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/claims/2026_08_16_dottedcalculator|2026_08_16_dottedcalculator]]: A forum proof claim of August 2026, posted under the username DottedCalculator
with a write-up generated by GPT 5.6 Sol, asserts the affirmative answer for
$\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_0$ and every $r$. It is AI-written and unreviewed.

[[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/claims/2026_09_06_land|2026_09_06_land]]: Johan Land's September 2026 manuscript claims to construct a graph of
chromatic number $\aleph_1$ all of whose triangle-free subgraphs are countably
colorable, which would answer the question negatively. It is AI-assisted, and
its Lean formalization has not been built.

[[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/claims/2026_09_07_steiner|2026_09_07_steiner]]: Corollary 1.4 of Steiner's preprint (arXiv, September 2026) states that every
infinite graph of infinite chromatic number has, for every odd $g$, a subgraph
of infinite chromatic number and odd girth at least $g$. This is the case
$\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_0$.
