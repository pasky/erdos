***

**Claim.** The preprint *The hypercube Ramsey number has linear order* of the
OpenAI mathematics release (dated 23 September 2026; its author line reads
OpenAI, and the release's README says that its manuscripts were produced by
an internal OpenAI model, which it does not name, and are at different
stages of verification, not all with Lean formalizations) states as its
Theorem 1.1 that there is an absolute constant $C>0$ with

$$
R(Q_n)\le C\,2^n\qquad\text{for every integer }n\ge0,
$$

where $Q_n$ is the graph on $\{0,1\}^n$ whose edges join vectors differing
in one coordinate and $R(H)$ is the least $M$ such that every red-blue
coloring of the edges of $K_M$ contains a monochromatic copy of $H$, not
necessarily induced. This is the displayed statement of
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0181/_index|Problem 181]], and the manuscript
names the question of Burr and Erdős, whether the cubes form an $L$-set, as
what it resolves. The manuscript is carded at
[[../library/ramsey_theory/openai_2026_hypercube_ramsey_number_has_linear_order/_index|openai_2026_hypercube_ramsey_number_has_linear_order]],
with the theorem paged at its
[[../library/ramsey_theory/openai_2026_hypercube_ramsey_number_has_linear_order/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]]
page. With the two-block lower bound $R(Q_n)\ge3\cdot2^{n-1}-1$
that the introduction records, the theorem determines the order of
$R(Q_n)$; the manuscript says it gives no numerical value of $C$ and does
not decide whether $R(Q_n)/2^n$ converges. Its introduction places the
result after the bounds of Graham, Rödl and Ruciński, Shi, Fox and Sudakov,
Conlon, Fox and Sudakov, Lee and Tikhomirov, the last three recorded on the
problem page, and notes that the fixed-degeneracy theorem of Lee does not
reach the cube because the degeneracy of $Q_n$ is $n$.

**Method and read depth.** By the introduction, the proof works with a
hypothetical sequence of counterexamples having two disjoint host sides of
size $N$ each with $N/2^n\to\infty$ at no prescribed rate, derives
discrepancy from the absence of a cube in both colors, excludes a cluster
structure, and embeds the cube through a tiling by subcubes fixed on
initial coordinates, with piece sizes from retained patch masses, with a
regime guide and a dependency diagram organizing eighteen sections. Claims
checked for Theorem 1.1, for Lemma 2.1 (the reduction to a counterexample
sequence), for the lower-bound paragraph of Section 1.1 and for the
statements of the staging results the card lists; the proof (Sections 2--18)
was read for its structure only, no step was checked, and nothing here is
review.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the manuscript's Section 3 supplies
the finite tools its proof uses, resting on standard results it cites.

**Formalization.** None of the theorem in the release; a third-party Lean
proof, described below, has not been built here. The release's Lean tree at
the pinned revision holds `OAI/Combinatorics/Ramsey/Hypercube.lean`, the first
`formalization` link above, imported by the root module, with no catalog entry
and no comparator challenge. It defines the cube graph, and the Ramsey number
through copies that need not be induced. It proves the elementary bounds
$2^n\le R(Q_n)\le2^{2^n+2^n}$. It also proves the manuscript's Lemma 2.1
(`counterexample_sequence_of_not_linear`): if no $C>0$ has $R(Q_n)\le C2^n$
for every $n$, there are dimensions tending to infinity and red-blue
colorings between two sides of size $N\le n2^n$, with $N/2^n\to\infty$,
containing no monochromatic cube. It does not state or prove Theorem 1.1.
Nothing was built or audited here.

The theorem is proved in Lean outside the release, in Yao Xu's repository
[`hypercube-ramsey-lean4`](https://github.com/cadamcat/hypercube-ramsey-lean4/tree/ad206e1bf8240c28b538dfe72f10364cbc1591da)
(account cadamcat), linked here at its tag v1.0.0 of 8 October 2026. Its README
names Yao Xu as author and credits the theorem to this preprint, and says the
work was developed with AI assistance (Claude Code, Codex and Antigravity). Its
`HypercubeRamsey/Main.lean`, the second `formalization` link, proves
`Erdos181.erdos_181`, that some $C>0$ has $R(Q_n)\le C\,2^n$ for every $n$,
with $R(Q_n)$ written `diagonalGraphRamsey (hypercube n)` over a copy of the
formal-conjectures definitions; the repository's checks compare that statement
with a challenge file holding the formal-conjectures statement and definitions.
The README notes that copies need not be induced, as in the manuscript, and says
the repository proves that this Ramsey number equals the release's, defined as
a least positive size, so the bound is not vacuous. The proof takes the cube,
its Ramsey number, finite Ramsey bounds and Lemma 2.1 from the release's Lean
library at the pinned revision and formalizes the rest of the manuscript's
argument; it builds on Lean v4.34.1, with the release's compatibility patches
applied to the library's pinned packages. The repository's
[verification record](https://github.com/cadamcat/hypercube-ramsey-lean4/blob/35fa13445f3ab7a1934f0e293cb49f86b83d13da/docs/verification.md),
at its commit of 11 October 2026, reports for the tag a fresh build on 8
October 2026 with no `sorry`, the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and
`Quot.sound` only, a replay in a fresh Lean kernel, and a Comparator check of
the theorem against the challenge file; and, on 10–11 October 2026, a second
Comparator run in which the independent checker nanoda also accepted the proof.
Both Comparator runs used the tool's development stand-in for its sandbox. On
11 October 2026 formal-conjectures (the `record` link) moved `erdos_181` from
`research open` to `research solved` and linked this theorem as its formal
proof, keeping its own proof `sorry`; the formal-conjectures catalog links a
formal proof and does not referee it. This corpus has not built the development,
printed its axioms or audited its definitions, so the formalization is a link,
not a warrant.

**Standing.** Claimed. The manuscript is a release preprint with no journal
record and no independent review known to this corpus, and the release's tree
has no Lean proof of Theorem 1.1; the third-party Lean proof under
Formalization has not been built or audited here. The erdosproblems.com page for
Problem 181, accessed 2026-09-18, showed OPEN with an empty proof-claim tab. A
refereed version, a documented independent acceptance, or a build of that Lean
proof here with an audit of its statement (`formalized` evidence) would move the
claim to accepted; until then the problem's standing is claimed through this
page.
