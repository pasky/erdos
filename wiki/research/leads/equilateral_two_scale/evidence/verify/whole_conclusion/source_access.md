## OpenAI plane-coloring manuscript

The [[../library/discrete_geometry/openai_2026_euclidean_plane_not_five_colorable/_index|canonical source card]]
identifies *The Euclidean plane is not five-colorable*, September 23,
2026 Math Release, external release revision `adc7f1241`, and records the
Apache-2.0 terms. Its PDF and matching transcription are retained at
`library/discrete_geometry/openai_2026_euclidean_plane_not_five_colorable/`,
with the common stem `openai_2026_euclidean_plane_not_five_colorable`.
These canonical files supply the lawfully retained proof text; there is
no duplicate source transcription among the research assets.

The examined scope is the transcription's original lines 83--545:
Sections 2 and 3 and the supplied part of Section 4 through Proposition
4.2, together with selected bibliography. The review derives the
relative-singularity, tower, multiple-average, number-field selection and
continuous-factor interfaces needed by the native argument. The new
finite-function-field selection replaces the step confined to number
fields. The grade covers these deductions and their stated specialization,
not the manuscript's full five-color theorem or its separate Lean
formalization.

## Finite triangle forcing

The edition is Jelínek, Kynčl, Stolař and Valla, *Monochromatic triangles
in two-colored plane*,
[arXiv:math/0701940v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0701940v1), January 31,
2007. The [[../library/discrete_geometry/jelinek_2009_monochromatic_triangles_two_colored_plane/_index|source card]]
also identifies the later Combinatorica publication; the review used the
arXiv edition, not that later typesetting. Its consumed inputs are the
direct-motion and degenerate-triangle conventions, Lemma 1.3 and
Corollary 1.4, including the finite forcing proof.

The report's historical transcription locators are lines 48--54 and
72--94. That restricted transcription is not distributed in the public
copy, and no republication license is inferred from its availability to
the examiners. The exact public edition linked above supplies the source;
the [[../library/discrete_geometry/jelinek_2009_monochromatic_triangles_two_colored_plane/corollary_1_4|result card]]
states its interface. The native circle proof and independent review
reconstruct the forcing configuration in coordinates and justify its
positive-measure use. Their argument depends on those written coordinates,
not on an unread figure or a copied source proof.

## Empty measure-theoretic boundary

The edition is P. Lahti, *Federer's characterization of sets of finite
perimeter in metric spaces*,
[arXiv:1804.11216v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.11216v1), April 27, 2018.
The [[../library/analysis/lahti_2018_federer_characterization_finite_perimeter/_index|source card]]
fixes that edition; its title page is dated May 1, 2018. The supplied TeX
excerpt included the Euclidean introduction on p. 1, Theorem 1.1 on p. 2,
the standing metric hypotheses on p. 3, the boundary definition and
formulas (2.2)--(2.3) on p. 5, and (2.4) on p. 6, with required macros
and bibliography. These statements and their hypotheses were examined;
the criterion's and perimeter representation's original proofs were not.

The [[../library/analysis/lahti_2018_federer_characterization_finite_perimeter/theorem_1_1|retained theorem interface]]
states the exact boundary convention, finite-perimeter criterion,
representation and relative isoperimetric inequality. In the Euclidean
application, an empty measure-theoretic boundary gives finite and then
zero perimeter on each ball, so one color is null there; nested balls
give a globally null or conull color. This application is checked in the
whole review. The cited theorems remain external boundaries. No restricted
source transcription or TeX excerpt is copied into these assets.
