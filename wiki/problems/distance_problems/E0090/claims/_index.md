# problems/distance_problems/E0090/claims

[[problems/distance_problems/E0090/_index|..]]

[[problems/distance_problems/E0090/claims/2026_05_20_alon_bloom_gowers_litt_sawin_shankar_tsimerman_wang_wood|2026_05_20_alon_bloom_gowers_litt_sawin_shankar_tsimerman_wang_wood]]: Theorem 1.1 of Alon, Bloom, Gowers, Litt, Sawin, Shankar, Tsimerman, Wang and
Matchett Wood, arXiv:2605.20695, gives planar sets with a fixed-power excess
of unit pairs. It is a human-digested proof of OpenAI's result, and its status
is claimed.

[[problems/distance_problems/E0090/claims/2026_05_20_openai|2026_05_20_openai]]: Theorem 1.1 of OpenAI's report Planar Point Sets with Many Unit Distances
gives $n$-point planar sets with $n^{1+\delta}$ unit pairs, for a fixed
$\delta>0$ and infinitely many $n$. It is accepted on the companion's check
and the curator's credit.

[[problems/distance_problems/E0090/claims/2026_05_20_sawin|2026_05_20_sawin]]: Theorem 1 of Sawin's arXiv:2605.20579 gives, for arbitrarily large $n$, an
$n$-point planar set with at least $n^{1.014114}/C$ unit pairs, for an
absolute constant $C$. This is a second, quantitative disproof, and it is
claimed.

[[problems/distance_problems/E0090/claims/2026_05_26_anon|2026_05_26_anon]]: Theorem 1.1 of a 13-page manuscript with no author line gives
$u(n)\ge n^{1+c_0\log\log\log n/\log\log n}$ along an infinite set of $n$.
This refutes the problem's bound with a gain tending to zero, and the result
is claimed.

[[problems/distance_problems/E0090/claims/2026_06_02_emmerich|2026_06_02_emmerich]]: Proposition 2 of Emmerich's report re-optimizes the parameters of Sawin's
criterion, keeping Sawin's prime set, and states $u(n)>n^{1.0152}$ for
arbitrarily large $n$. The result is claimed.
