# Evidence

[[research/leads/complete_bipartite_size_ramsey/_index|..]]

***

The [entry point](main.py) checks the finite diagnostics of
[the lead](../_index.md). Run from the repository root:

```sh
uv run --no-sync python wiki/research/leads/complete_bipartite_size_ramsey/evidence/main.py
```

It needs networkx and numpy from the evidence group and takes under a minute.
Arithmetic is exact: Python integers for counts and loads, and NumPy uint32
for colourings of graphs with at most 15 edges. It checks:

- the exact witness inventory, dimensions, binary alphabet, and vertex
  domain, including that the complete-host witness concerns $K_{17}$ and
  $K_{3,3}$;
- the capacities of Lemma 2 that the lead tabulates, from exact binomials;
- each stored colouring of a complete bipartite host, for $n=2$ the hosts
  $K_{3,6}$ and $K_{4,6}$ and for $n=3$ the hosts $K_{5,40}$, $K_{6,40}$,
  $K_{7,28}$, $K_{8,28}$, $K_{9,22}$, $K_{10,20}$, $K_{11,18}$, $K_{12,16}$,
  $K_{13,15}$ and $K_{14,14}$, twice: by the red and blue loads of every
  $n$-set of columns, and by the common red and blue columns of every $n$-set
  of rows;
- the counting inequality of Lemma 2 for $K_{3,7}$, $K_{4,7}$ and $K_{7,29}$;
- a colouring of every $K_{a,b}$ with $a\le b$ and $ab\le20$ ($n=2$) or
  $ab\le202$ ($n=3$), so that the cheapest complete bipartite Ramsey hosts
  have $21$ and $203$ edges;
- $b(2)=5$: a colouring of $K_{4,4}$, and the absence of five pairwise
  compatible rows of length $5$, which covers every colouring of $K_{5,5}$;
- the $313$ isomorphism classes of graphs with minimum degree at least $3$
  and at most $14$ edges, each with a good colouring checked against every
  4-cycle, and their completeness: in every cell of vertex and edge counts,
  the sum of $v!/|\mathrm{Aut}(G)|$ equals the number of labelled graphs,
  counted independently through degree sequences; and $K_6\to C_4$ over all
  $2^{15}$ colourings;
- the stored colouring of $K_{17}$, by common neighbourhoods and by every pair
  of disjoint $3$-sets;
- the $30$ affine hyperplanes of $\mathbb F_2^4$, generated by their defining
  linear equations: every translation permutes the family, every $4$-set
  has both loads at most $3$, and every four rows have fewer than four common
  points in either colour. The plane $\{0,1,2,3\}$ has both loads exactly
  $3$, refuting divisibility by $4$ for arbitrary translation-invariant
  families. The capacity at $(a,n)=(16,4)$ is $78$.

The colourings are in [`assets/witnesses.json`](assets/witnesses.json). A
SAT solver found them; nothing in them is trusted until the entry point checks
it. The hyperplane construction is generated directly by the checker and
proved in the lead. Negative controls reject a corrupted colouring, a
nonbinary row, a missing or duplicated host witness, a smaller replacement
for the stated $K_{17}$ target, and a missing host class.

The completeness count for $n=2$ is independent of the atlas and extension
enumeration. For a prescribed labelled degree sequence, delete its first
vertex and sum the counts of the residual sequences over every possible set
of its neighbours. Negative degrees contribute zero, and the empty sequence
contributes one. Sorting residual sequences merely relabels vertices, so it
does not change their counts. Summing over sorted degree sequences with
minimum degree $3$ and sum $2m$, weighted by the number of their distinct
permutations, counts every labelled graph in the $(v,m)$ cell exactly once.
The generated representatives are deduplicated by isomorphism; their orbit
sizes sum to that independent count in every cell. Thus none is missing.
The degree-removal argument in the lead reduces every possible host with
at most $14$ edges to one of these cells or to the empty graph.

For the complete bipartite minima, symmetry permits $a\le b$. Below an
edge threshold $q$, only $a\le\lfloor\sqrt q\rfloor$ need checking, and
the witness for $b=\lfloor q/a\rfloor$ covers every smaller $b$ by deleting
rows. When $a\le2n-2$, a column split with both colour classes smaller than
$n$ can be repeated arbitrarily. These reductions explain the exhaustive
scope even though the stored witness list is finite.

The entry point does not check the solver verdict that $K_{9,23}$ has no good
colouring, the theorem $r(K_{3,3})=18$ behind $K_{18}$, or the propositions of
the lead. The [discovery script](produce.py) writes the SAT instance for a
complete bipartite or complete host to the ignored `output/` folder and, given
`--solve`, runs `kissat` from the path and writes any colouring it finds
there; its unsatisfiable verdicts are solver verdicts without a checked
certificate. For example:

```sh
uv run --no-sync python wiki/research/leads/complete_bipartite_size_ramsey/evidence/produce.py kab 9 23 3 --solve 600
```

The encoding has one Boolean variable per edge, with true meaning red.
Every candidate copy contributes two clauses excluding its all-red and
all-blue assignments. In the bipartite case, auxiliary prefix-equality
variables put the rows in nonincreasing lexicographic order. Permuting rows
preserves avoidance, so this removes no feasible colouring class. Repeated
rows remain allowed. In the complete-host case, the encoding uses every
unordered pair of disjoint $n$-sets. The recorded $K_{9,23}$ verdict came
from Kissat 4.0.4; this encoding and that solver's verdict do not replace a
checked unsatisfiability certificate.
