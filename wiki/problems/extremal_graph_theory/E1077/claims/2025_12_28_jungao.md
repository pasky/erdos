***

**Claim.** The wording of
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1077/_index|Problem 1077]] is false, and
the size of the almost-regular subgraph it should have asked for is
$n^\alpha$. The witness: the complete bipartite graph $K_{a,n-a}$ with
$a\approx n^\alpha$ has about $n^{1+\alpha}$ edges, and every $D$-balanced
subgraph of it with an edge has at most $(D+1)a$ vertices, because each of
its edges has an end on the small side and the degree bounds cap the large
side at $D$ times the small side. For $0<\alpha<\tfrac12$ that bound is
below $n^{1-\alpha}$ for large $n$, so no $D$-balanced subgraph on more
than $n^{1-\alpha}$ vertices has an edge, for every $\epsilon>0$ and every
$D$; since the question quantifies over every $\epsilon,\alpha>0$, this
refutes it as a whole, and the problem page recomputes the count with
$a=\lceil2n^\alpha\rceil$. The same graph shows that in any question of
this shape the subgraph can have at most a constant times $n^\alpha$
vertices. The comment itself states the exponent and the two bounds and
does not say that it refutes the printed wording; reading it as the
disproof of the exponent $1-\alpha$ is the site's acceptance (the
curator's reply of 31 December 2025 and the commentary) and the problem
page's own count. The comment's second part, not part of the disproof, is
a lower bound of the same order: Theorem 1.3 of the preprint of Jiang and
Longbrake (arXiv:2507.03261) gives a $6$-almost-regular subgraph of
average degree of order $n^\alpha/\log n$, and the comment reports that
its proof, followed carefully, gives at least $n^\alpha/2$ vertices. The
problem page records this lower bound as resting on a preprint and a forum
reading of its proof, with the logarithm unresolved on the sources read
there.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the count is elementary.

**Acceptance.** Reviewed: the site's curator, Thomas Bloom, who took no part in
the comment, adopted it. The thread marks the comment as addressed by a site
update, and the page was last edited 8 January 2026. The commentary credits
JunGao with the exponent $\alpha$ and names the complete bipartite graph as the
witness for the upper bound. It also states its best guess at the intended
question, with $n^\alpha$. The site labeled the problem DISPROVED (LEAN). The
community database lists the problem's informal status as disproved as of its
last update on 29 December 2025.

Not refereed: the comment is a forum post, and the preprint it relies on for the
lower bound had no journal record on 2026-09-18.
