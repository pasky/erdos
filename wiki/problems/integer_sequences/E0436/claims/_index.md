# problems/integer_sequences/E0436/claims

[[problems/integer_sequences/E0436/_index|..]]

[[problems/integer_sequences/E0436/claims/1962_01_01_lehmer_lehmer_mills_selfridge|1962_01_01_lehmer_lehmer_mills_selfridge]]: Theorem 1 of Lehmer, Lehmer, Mills and Selfridge (Math. Comp. 16 (1962)) lists
the thirteen primes with no three consecutive cubic residues. Every other
prime has such a run starting at or below 23532, and this bound is attained
for infinitely many primes.

[[problems/integer_sequences/E0436/claims/1991_01_01_hildebrand|1991_01_01_hildebrand]]: Theorem 1 of Hildebrand (Michigan Math. J. 38 (1991)) proves that for every
$k$ the least pair of consecutive $k$th power residues modulo $p$ is bounded
in $p$. So $\Lambda(k,2)$ is finite, and the first question of Problem 436 has
answer yes.

[[problems/integer_sequences/E0436/claims/2026_10_19_plasma_ai|2026_10_19_plasma_ai]]: Assuming the three-function case of the logarithmically averaged Elliott
conjecture, $\Lambda(k,3)$ is finite for every odd $k$; author-recorded at
tier 0, conditional on that open conjecture, catalog acceptance pending.
