# problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1012/claims

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1012/_index|..]]

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1012/claims/1961_12_01_ore|1961_12_01_ore]]: Theorem 4.3 of Ore (Ann. Mat. Pura Appl. 1961, refereed) says that a graph on
$n$ vertices with at least $\binom{n-1}2+2$ edges has a Hamilton circuit,
sharp by $K_{n-1}$ with a pendant edge. This gives $f(0)=1$.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1012/claims/1971_09_01_bondy|1971_09_01_bondy]]: Theorem 2 of Bondy (Discrete Math. 1971, refereed) says that a graph of order
$n$ and size at least $\frac12(n^2-5n+14)=\binom{n-2}2+4$ has a cycle of
length $n-1$, sharp for $n>4$. This gives $f(1)=1$.

[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1012/claims/1972_05_01_woodall|1972_05_01_woodall]]: Woodall's Corollary 11.1 (Proc. London Math. Soc. 1972) gives a circuit of
every length from 3 to $n-k$ in a graph on $n\ge2k+3$ vertices with the
problem's edge count. So $f(k)\le2k+3$, and the edge count is sharp there.
