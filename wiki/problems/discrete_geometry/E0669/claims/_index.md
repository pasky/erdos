# problems/discrete_geometry/E0669/claims

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0669/_index|..]]

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0669/claims/1974_02_01_burr_grunbaum_sloane|1974_02_01_burr_grunbaum_sloane]]: Burr, Grünbaum and Sloane place $n$ points on a cubic curve so that at least
$1+\lfloor n(n-3)/6\rfloor$ lines pass through exactly three of them. With the
pair count, this makes both limits for $k=3$ equal to $1/6$.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0669/claims/2012_08_23_green_tao|2012_08_23_green_tao]]: Green and Tao prove that for all large $n$, at most
$\lfloor n(n-3)/6\rfloor+1$ lines contain exactly three of $n$ points in the
plane. With the cubic-curve construction, this is the exact value of $f_3(n)$.
