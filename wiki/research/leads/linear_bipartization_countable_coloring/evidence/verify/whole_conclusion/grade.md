## Restatements and scope

### Target 1

For every finite real $C\ge0$ and every set-sized undirected simple
graph $G$, assume that for every finite subgraph $F\subseteq G$,

$$
\beta(F)\le C|V(F)|,
$$

where $\beta$ counts the minimum number of edges, not vertices, whose
deletion leaves a bipartite graph. Then a proper map from $V(G)$ to a
countable palette exists. There is no hypothesis on the cardinality
or chromatic number of $G$. Singular cardinals are included.

The framework is ZFC. Sums of nonnegative terms over arbitrary sets
are suprema of finite subsums. The intermediate uniform trace-energy
bound concerns infinite earlier traces only; finite traces are
handled by their finite predecessor sets. No finite chromatic bound
is asserted. This is a universally quantified implication, not a
claim that an additional hypothetical configuration exists.

### Target 2

For every set-sized simple graph $G$ with $\chi(G)=\aleph_1$, define

$$
h_G(n)=\max\{\beta(F):F\subseteq G,\ |V(F)|=n\},\qquad n\ge1.
$$

Then

$$
\forall K>0\ \exists N\in\mathbb N\
 \forall n\ge N,\qquad h_G(n)>Kn.
$$

The threshold may depend on $G$ and $K$. The graph may have any
vertex cardinality. This is a full limit, not unboundedness or a
subsequence statement.

The least profile exists and is attained for fixed $n$, since its
possible values are a nonempty finite set of integers. It is the
strongest relevant interpretation of the supplied deletion-bound
wording: every admissible bound dominates it.

The separately consumed converse reduction is also in scope:
failure of the limit gives a countable $S$ and finite $K>0$ such
that $G-S$ is $2K$-linear and still has chromatic number $\aleph_1$.
Conversely, a C-linear graph of that chromatic number would violate
the limit. This proves the stated equivalence with the restricted
CC lemma. It does not assert a converse equivalence with Target 1
at unrestricted chromatic number.

### Target 2: three consequential checks

**Every countable-deletion remainder.** Deleting countably many
vertices preserves chromatic number exactly $\aleph_1$. Target 1,
graded first, therefore gives a finite obstruction to $2K$-linearity
