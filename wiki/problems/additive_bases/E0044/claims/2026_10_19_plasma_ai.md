***

**Claim.** For every integer $N\ge1$, every Sidon set
$A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ and every $\epsilon>0$, there are arbitrarily
large integers $M$ and a set $B\subseteq\{N+1,\ldots,M\}$ such that
$A\cup B$ is a Sidon set of size at least

$$
\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}-\epsilon\right)M^{1/2}.
$$

Here a Sidon set is one whose sums $a+b$ over unordered pairs, repeated
elements included, are pairwise distinct.

**Covers.** [[problems/additive_bases/E0044/_index|Problem 44]] asks for
size at least $(1-\epsilon)M^{1/2}$ for every $\epsilon>0$. The claim gives
the coefficient $1/\sqrt2$ in place of $1$, and so answers the question yes,
for every $N$ and $A$, at each $\epsilon>1-1/\sqrt2$. For
$0<\epsilon\le1-1/\sqrt2$, that is for the coefficient $1$ itself, the
question remains open.

**Credit.** The extension specializes
[[../library/additive_bases/singer_1938_theorem_finite_projective_geometry_some_applications_number_theory/theorem_p380|Singer's prime-power difference-set theorem]],
consumed as an external premise at statement level; its proof is not
reconstructed. Deleting finitely many conflicting differences from a Singer
set and separating the three kinds of pair sums are Plasma AI's. No novelty
is claimed.

**Standing.** [[theory/additive_combinatorics/L31_finite_sidon_prefix_extension/_index|L31]]
is author-recorded at tier 0, without independent examination or a Lean
proof. Catalog acceptance is pending.
