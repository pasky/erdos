# problems/distance_problems/E0659/claims

[[problems/distance_problems/E0659/_index|..]]

[[problems/distance_problems/E0659/claims/2014_06_08_sheffer|2014_06_08_sheffer]]: Sheffer's survey lists $\phi(n,4,3)=O(n/\sqrt{\log n})$, the answer yes, from
the triangular lattice, which contains two-distance rhombi. The bound is true
by Grayzel's theorem, but this argument does not prove it.

[[problems/distance_problems/E0659/claims/2026_01_13_grayzel|2026_01_13_grayzel]]: Grayzel shows that for every $n\ge2$, $n$ points of a box in the lattice of
points $(a,b\sqrt2)$ have $O(n/\sqrt{\log n})$ distances while every four
determine at least three, so the answer is yes. The site's curator confirms
it, and a Lean proof assumes Bernays' asymptotic.

[[problems/distance_problems/E0659/claims/2026_01_29_feng|2026_01_29_feng]]: In the Aletheia agent's claimed proof, the $n$ points of the ring of integers
of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$ nearest the origin have $O(n/\sqrt{\log n})$
distances and contain no square, equilateral triangle or pentagon trapezoid,
so the answer is yes.
