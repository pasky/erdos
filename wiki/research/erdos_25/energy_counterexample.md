# research/erdos_25/energy_counterexample

***

## Statement and scope

**Proved.** There are finite systems of distinct singleton moduli, with
the original activation rule, and cutoffs $Q\to\infty$ such that
$$
 \mathcal E:=\sum_q e_q\log q\longrightarrow2\log2,
 \qquad
 H_B(Q)-\beta H_Q\sim(\log Q)^{1/21}\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{1}
$$
Here $B$ is the delayed excluded union, $\beta$ is the full periodic
union measure, and $e_q$ are the full first-kill measures in increasing
modulus order. All the moduli are at most $Q$.

Consequently there is no absolute $C$ for which
$$
 H_B(X)\le\beta H_{\lfloor X\rfloor}
              +C\left(1+\sum_{q\le X}e_q\log q\right)
 \tag{2}
$$
holds for every finite distinct-modulus singleton system. This accounts
for the entire preparatory sieve, not just the late first-kill cells.
The construction also refutes a uniform additive $O(\log\log X)$
error and the proposed rank-sensitive error $C_r=\exp(o(r))$.

This is **not** an infinite counterexample to Problem 25. Its normalized
harmonic excess tends to zero. A uniform additive $o(\log X)$ estimate
and the constant first-kill entropy bound remain open.

The [rank-filtered refinement below](#rank-filtered-residue-channels)
increases the attainable lower-bound exponent to every
$\gamma<(7\,2^{1/7}-3\,3^{1/3}-3)/4=0.100469\ldots$,
still with energy tending to $2\log2$. It does not contradict the
weaker square-root energy target.

## A sparse divisor-residue channel

Let $L=2^k$ and $Q>L$ be integers. For each odd residue
$r\in\{1,\ldots,L-1\}$, put
$$
 D_r=\{q\in\mathbb N:L<q\le Q,\ q\equiv r\pmod L\},
 \qquad
 b_r=\mu\left(\bigcup_{q\in D_r}q\widehat{\mathbb Z}\right).
$$
We claim that some such $r$ satisfies
$$
 b_r\ll L^{-1/2}(\log(2Q))^{\sqrt2-1}.
 \tag{3}
$$
The implied constant is absolute.

For every odd prime $p\le Q$, independently choose a nonnegative
integer $V_p$ with
$$
 \Pr(V_p=v)=(1-1/p)p^{-v},
 \qquad M=\prod_{\substack{p\le Q\\p\ne2}}p^{V_p}.
$$
These are precisely the finite-dimensional Haar valuation laws. Every
$q\in D_r$ is odd and has all prime factors at most $Q$, so
$$
 b_r=\Pr(\exists q\in D_r:q\mid M).
$$
For each realization of $M$, at most
$\min(\varphi(L),\tau(M))$ unit residues modulo $L$ occur among its
divisors in $(L,Q]$. Here $\tau$ is the number of positive divisors.
Consequently
$$
 \frac1{\varphi(L)}\sum_{r\text{ odd}\bmod L}b_r
 \le\mathbb E\min\left(1,\frac{\tau(M)}{\varphi(L)}\right)
 \le\varphi(L)^{-1/2}\mathbb E\sqrt{\tau(M)}.
 \tag{4}
$$

The divisor moment factors over the independent valuations. For $p\ge3$,
$$
 (1-1/p)\sum_{v\ge0}\sqrt{v+1}\,p^{-v}
 =1+\frac{\sqrt2-1}{p}+O(p^{-2}).
$$
The remainder is bounded uniformly in $p$: the terms $v\ge2$ have sum
$O(p^{-2})$. Comparison with the local factors of
$\prod_{p\le Q}(1-1/p)^{-(\sqrt2-1)}$ therefore gives
$$
 \mathbb E\sqrt{\tau(M)}
 \ll\prod_{p\le Q}(1-1/p)^{-(\sqrt2-1)}
 \ll(\log(2Q))^{\sqrt2-1}.
 \tag{5}
$$
The last bound is the elementary Euler-product estimate proved in
[coprime entropy](coprime_entropy.md#elementary-prime-estimates).
Equations (4)--(5), together with $\varphi(L)=L/2$, prove (3).
No distribution theorem for primes in arithmetic progressions is used.

From now on choose
$$
 Q=\left\lfloor\exp(L^{21/20})\right\rfloor
$$
and choose the least odd $r$ minimizing $b_r$. This is a finite,
deterministic choice: each $b_r$ is the exact proportion of covered
residues in a finite common period. The minimum satisfies (3). Since
$\sqrt2-1<5/12$, we obtain
$$
 b_r\ll L^{-1/2+(21/20)(5/12)}=L^{-1/16}.
 \tag{6}
$$

## The finite congruence system

First install the $k$ classes
$$
 C_j=[2^{j-1}-1]_{2^j},\qquad1\le j\le k.
 \tag{7}
$$
These are disjoint full cylinders: membership is equivalent to
$v_2(n+1)=j-1$. Their full survivor set is the single fiber
$[-1]_L$, of measure $1/L$.

Set
$$
 \alpha=L-1-r\in[0,L-2],
$$
and then install one class
$$
 [\alpha]_q\qquad(q\in D_r).
 \tag{8}
$$
All new moduli are odd and greater than $L$, whereas the old moduli
are the distinct powers of two at most $L$. Thus all moduli are
globally distinct and all old classes precede the new ones in increasing
modulus order. The residues in both (7) and (8) are normalized; in
particular $0\le\alpha<q$ for every new class.

The full union in (8) is the common translate by $\alpha$ of the
multiples union generated by $D_r$, hence has measure $b_r$.
Because all its moduli are odd, this event is independent of the
2-adic fiber $[-1]_L$. The final full survivor measure is therefore
exactly
$$
 \delta=1-\beta=\frac{1-b_r}{L}.
 \tag{9}
$$

## Exact delayed survivors and activation

The positive roots omitted by the delayed versions of the old classes
are
$$
 2^h-1,\qquad1\le h\le k-1.
$$
They are not covered by another old class, since the full old cylinders
are disjoint. Every new modulus exceeds $L$, so no new class is active
at these roots. The remaining old delayed survivors are the positive
integers in $[-1]_L$.

The first positive point of this fiber is $L-1$. For every later one,
write
$$
 n=Lt+L-1,\qquad t\ge1.
$$
If $n\le Q$, then
$$
 q=n-\alpha=Lt+r
$$
satisfies $L<q\le n\le Q$ and $q\equiv r\pmod L$, so $q\in D_r$.
Moreover $n=q+\alpha$ is precisely the first active endpoint of its
new class. This checks activation without any change of cutoff or
delay convention.

Thus the delayed survivor set $A$ satisfies the exact identity
$$
 A\cap[1,Q]=\{2^h-1:1\le h\le k\},
 \qquad
 H_A(Q)=\sum_{h=1}^k\frac1{2^h-1}<2.
 \tag{10}
$$

## Bounded total first-kill energy

Since the old full cylinders are disjoint, their first-kill measures
are $2^{-j}$. Their energy is
$$
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{old}}
 =\log2\sum_{j=1}^k\frac{j}{2^j}
 =\left(2-\frac{k+2}{L}\right)\log2.
 \tag{11}
$$
The sum of all the new first-kill measures is exactly the increment
in full union measure, namely $b_r/L$. All new moduli are at most
$Q$, so (6) gives
$$
 0\le\mathcal E_{\mathrm{new}}
 \le\frac{b_r}{L}\log Q
 \ll L^{-1/16+1/20}=L^{-1/80}.
 \tag{12}
$$
Together (11)--(12) prove $\mathcal E\to2\log2$.

On the other hand, (9)--(10) give
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 H_B(Q)-\beta H_Q
 &=\delta H_Q-H_A(Q)\\
 &\ge(1-b_r)L^{1/20}-2,
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{13}
$$
because $H_Q\ge\log(Q+1)>L^{21/20}$. In fact, using
$H_Q=\log Q+O(1)$ and $b_r=o(1)$, this excess is
$$
 (1+o(1))L^{1/20}
 =(1+o(1))(\log Q)^{1/21}.
 \tag{14}
$$
This proves (1), disproves (2), and disproves a uniform additive
$O(\log\log X)$ error.

## Consequence for the rank-sensitive target

The later [prime-only amplification](prime_additive_excess.md)
supersedes the rank-constant obstruction in this section: no finite
rank-only additive bound exists, already at rank one. The construction
here still has the distinct additional feature of bounded first-kill
energy, and the calculations below remain valid.

The proved rank truncation in
[rank and moment obstructions](rank_and_moment_obstructions.md#a-rank-sensitive-additive-target-now-refuted)
states that, with
$$
 R=\left\lceil8\log\log(3Q)\right\rceil,
$$
the union $B_{>R}$ of classes whose moduli have $\Omega(q)>R$ has
$H_{B_{>R}}(Q)=O(1)$, uniformly over finite singleton families.
Explicitly its bound is
$$
 H_{B_{>R}}(Q)
 \ll(3/2)^{-R}(\log(3Q))^{5/2}=O(1).
$$

Delete those high-rank classes from the construction and write
$B_{\le R}$ and $\beta_{\le R}$ for the remaining delayed union and
full union measure. Removing classes can only decrease Haar measure,
and loses at most $O(1)$ harmonic mass. Consequently
$$
 H_{B_{\le R}}(Q)-\beta_{\le R}H_Q
 \ge H_B(Q)-\beta H_Q-O(1)
 \gg L^{1/20}.
 \tag{15}
$$
Since $R=(42/5)\log L+O(1)$, any universal bounds
$$
 H_B(X)\le\beta H_{\lfloor X\rfloor}+C_R
 \quad\text{for all systems with }\Omega(q)\le R
$$
must therefore satisfy, along this sequence,
$$
 C_R\ge\exp\bigl((1/168+o(1))R\bigr).
 \tag{16}
$$
In particular $C_R=\exp(o(R))$ is impossible. This does not contradict
the bounded-rank natural-density theorem, which does not assert such
a uniform additive error.

## Why this does not resolve Problem 25

The normalized excess is
$$
 \frac{H_B(Q)-\beta H_Q}{H_Q}
 =\frac{1-b_r}{L}-\frac{H_A(Q)}{H_Q}
 \sim\frac1L\longrightarrow0.
$$
Thus these finite systems do not produce a fixed positive logarithmic
excess. Also, combining gadgets while retaining their preparatory
classes for unbounded $k$ retains every class in (7). Those classes
already have full Haar union measure one; their delayed survivor set
is contained in $\{2^h-1:h\ge1\}$ and has natural density zero.
Any additional deletions preserve that zero density. This direct
combination therefore cannot give the required infinite counterexample.

The spectral conductor-energy inequality remains valid. What fails is
its proposed uniform conversion to harmonic discrepancy. Neither a
uniform additive $o(\log X)$ bound nor the constant first-kill entropy
target is refuted by this construction.

## Two linear entropy charges also fail

The same example refutes linear charging of the **unnormalized**
first-kill relative entropy either by the reciprocal sum or by the
first-kill energy. This does not refute the constant normalized entropy
target.

Let $I$ index the new odd-modulus classes, and put
$c_i=H_{E_i}(Q)/H_Q$. Their exact endpoint deletion gives
$$
 m:=\sum_{i\in I}H_{E_i}(Q)
   =\frac{\log Q-\log L+O(1)}L\sim\frac{H_Q}{L},
 \qquad \sum_{i\in I}e_i=\frac{b_r}{L}.
$$
The log-sum inequality, together with $b_r\ll L^{-1/16}$, yields
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 H_Q\sum_i c_i\log_+(c_i/e_i)
 &\ge m\log\frac{mL}{H_Qb_r}\\
 &\gg L^{1/20}\log L.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{17}
$$
On the other hand, summing along the single residue channel gives
$$
 \sum_i\frac1{q_i}\asymp 1+\frac{\log Q}{L}
 \asymp L^{1/20},
$$
while (11)--(12) show $\sum_i e_i\log q_i\to2\log2$. Thus neither
of these costs can bound (17) up to an absolute linear factor, even
after adding an absolute constant. The lower bound after normalization
by $H_Q$ is only $\gg(\log L)/L$, which tends to zero; it remains
compatible with a universal bound for $\sum_i c_i\log_+(c_i/e_i)$.

## Optimizing the divisor moment

The exponent $1/21$ can be slightly improved without changing the
construction. For every fixed $0<\theta<1$, replace the square root
in (4) by the $\theta$th power. The local factors are
$$
 (1-1/p)\sum_{v\ge0}(v+1)^\theta p^{-v}
 =1+(2^\theta-1)/p+O_\theta(p^{-2}),
$$
so the same proof gives
$$
 b_r\ll_\theta L^{-\theta}(\log(2Q))^{2^\theta-1}.
$$
Choose $Q=\lfloor\exp(L^a)\rfloor$ with $a>1$. The new energy is
then at most a constant times
$$
 L^{a2^\theta-\theta-1}.
$$
It tends to zero if $a<(1+\theta)/2^\theta$, while the harmonic excess
is asymptotic to $L^{a-1}$. Elementary differentiation gives
$$
 \max_{0<\theta<1}\frac{1+\theta}{2^\theta}
 =\frac{2}{e\log2},\qquad
 \theta_{\rm max}=\frac1{\log2}-1.
$$
Thus for every fixed
$$
 0<\gamma<1-\frac{e\log2}{2}
$$
there are systems with $\mathcal E\to2\log2$ and excess
$\asymp(\log Q)^\gamma$. All activation and distinctness checks are
unchanged. This remains sublogarithmic and does not give an infinite
counterexample. The [prepared-block entropy bound](prepared_blocks_entropy.md)
also shows that this mechanism's normalized entropy is small, even
after optimizing its parameters.

## Rank-filtered residue channels

**Theorem.** For every
$$
 0<\gamma<\gamma_*:=
       \frac{7\,2^{1/7}-3\,3^{1/3}-3}{4}
       =0.100469471\ldots,
 \tag{18}
$$
there are finite distinct-singleton systems with cutoffs $Q\to\infty$
and original activation such that
$$
 \mathcal E\longrightarrow2\log2,\qquad
 H_B(Q)-\beta H_Q\ge(\log Q)^\gamma.
 \tag{19}
$$
Only a lower bound for the excess is claimed here, unlike the
asymptotic equivalence in (1).

Write $L=2^k$, $H=\log Q$, and fix $c>1$, to be chosen below.
Put
$$
 R=\lfloor c\log H\rfloor,\qquad I(c)=c\log c-c+1.
$$
For each odd residue $r\pmod L$, define
$$
 D_r=\{q:L<q\le Q-L,\ q\equiv r\pmod L,\ \Omega(q)\ge R\},
 \quad b_r=\mu(M(D_r)),\quad m_r=\sum_{q\in D_r}\frac1q.
 \tag{20}
$$
We choose $Q$ so that $\log L/\log H$ tends to a fixed
positive constant. All estimates below are in this regime.

### The rank-sensitive divisor moment

Let $V_p$, for odd primes $p\le Q$, have the Haar geometric laws,
and set $M=\prod_{3\le p\le Q}p^{V_p}$. The number $N_R(M)$
of divisors of $M$ with $\Omega\ge R$ satisfies
$$
 N_R(M)\le2^{-R}\sum_{d\mid M}2^{\Omega(d)}
          =2^{-R}\prod_{3\le p\le Q}(2^{V_p+1}-1).
$$
At most $\min(\varphi(L),N_R(M))$ odd channels in (20) can be
represented among its divisors. Therefore
$$
 \frac1{\varphi(L)}\sum_r b_r
 \le\varphi(L)^{-1/3}\mathbb E N_R(M)^{1/3}
 \ll L^{-1/3}H^A,\qquad
 A=3^{1/3}-1-\frac{c\log2}{3}.
 \tag{21}
$$
For the last estimate, independence and the uniform local expansion give
$$
 (1-1/p)\sum_{v\ge0}(2^{v+1}-1)^{1/3}p^{-v}
 =1+\frac{3^{1/3}-1}{p}+O(p^{-2}).
$$
The remainder is uniform for $p\ge3$, since $2^{1/3}<3$.
The elementary Euler-product bound then gives the power
$H^{3^{1/3}-1}$; the factor $2^{-R/3}$ supplies the remaining
term in $A$.

### Enough channels retain reciprocal mass

Set $Y=(Q-L)^{1/R}$ and
$$
 S=\sum_{R^2<p\le Y}\frac1p=\log H-O(\log\log H).
$$
The last equality follows from
$\sum_{p\le x}1/p=\log\log x+O(1)$, itself a consequence of the
two-sided elementary Euler-product estimates used earlier.
Products of $R$ distinct primes from this interval are odd, at
most $Q-L$, and greater than $L$: their minimum logarithm is
greater than $2R\log R$, whereas $\log L=O(\log H)$.

Sampling $R$ primes independently with weights $1/(pS)$, the
collision probability is at most
$$
 {R\choose2}S^{-2}\sum_{p>R^2}p^{-2}=O(S^{-2})=o(1).
$$
Unique factorization therefore gives
$$
 \sum_r m_r\ge(1-o(1))\frac{S^R}{R!}
                  =H^{1-I(c)+o(1)}.
 \tag{22}
$$
No prime distribution in residue classes is used here.

For definiteness let $s=S^R/(2R!)$, so (22) is at least $s$
for all sufficiently large $Q$. Define
$\mathcal G=\{r:m_r\ge s/L\}$.
The other channels contribute at most $s/2$, because there are
$L/2$ odd channels. Uniformly $m_r\le CH/L$, by summing the
progression $r+tL$, $t\ge1$. Hence
$$
 |\mathcal G|\gg Ls/H=L H^{-I(c)+o(1)}.
$$
Choose the least-footprint channel in $\mathcal G$. Equation (21)
then proves the simultaneous estimates
$$
 m_r\ge \frac{H^{1-I(c)+o(1)}}L,\qquad
 b_r\ll L^{-1/3}H^{A+I(c)+o(1)}.
 \tag{23}
$$
The extra $H^{I(c)+o(1)}$ in the footprint is the cost of
restricting to large-mass channels; it is not discarded.

### Installing the filtered channel

Use the binary preparation (7), leaving the full fiber $[-1]_L$,
and put $\alpha=L-1-r\in[0,L-2]$. Append
$[\alpha]_q$ for $q\in D_r$. All new moduli are odd and exceed
the old moduli. They are distinct, and every residue is normalized.
Each distinct endpoint
$$
 n=q+\alpha\le Q
$$
lies in the old survivor fiber and is active at its own modulus.
Its reciprocal mass, summed over $D_r$, is
$$
 \sum_{q\in D_r}\frac1{q+\alpha}=m_r-O(1/L);
$$
indeed $\sum_{q>L,\ q\equiv r\ (L)}\alpha/q^2=O(1/L)$.
Other appended congruences may also cover these points, which only
helps the union count.

The new union has density $b_r$ on the odd-prime coordinates and
is independent of the binary fiber. Thus its incremental measure is
$b_r/L$, and its entire first-kill energy is at most
$$
 \mathcal E_{\rm new}\le b_rH/L.
 \tag{24}
$$
The old energy tends to $2\log2$. Its delayed survivor harmonic
mass is at most $H_Q/L+3$: the progression $[-1]_L$ costs
at most $H_Q/L+1$, and the inactive roots $2^j-1$ cost less
than two. Its harmonic discrepancy is consequently at least $-3$.
It follows that the final discrepancy is at least
$$
 -3+m_r-O(1/L)-\frac{b_rH_Q}{L}.
 \tag{25}
$$

Choose a fixed $\eta>0$ and
$Q=\lfloor\exp(L^{1/\eta})\rfloor$. Then
$L=H^{\eta+o(1)}$. By (23)--(24), the new energy tends to zero
if
$$
 \eta>\eta_{\rm crit}(c):=\frac34(1+A+I(c))
       =\frac{3\,3^{1/3}-c\log2+3I(c)}4.
 \tag{26}
$$
Under this condition, (23) and (25) give an excess lower bound
$H^{1-I(c)-\eta+o(1)}-O(1)$.
The supremum of its exponent allowed by (26) is
$$
 1-I(c)-\eta_{\rm crit}(c)
 =\frac{4-3\,3^{1/3}+c\log2-7I(c)}4.
$$
This expression is maximized at $c=2^{1/7}$, since its derivative
has the sign of $\log2-7\log c$. Substitution gives (18).
For every smaller $\gamma$, choose $\eta$ with strict margins
in (26) and $1-I(c)-\eta>\gamma$. This proves (19).

The examples do not refute the
[square-root energy estimate](spectral_sieve.md#a-weaker-energy-target-unresolved).
For these parameters $\eta>1/2$, while the trivial upper bound
for positive excess is
$$
 (H_B(Q)-\beta H_Q)_+\le(1-\beta)H_Q\le H_Q/L
                                      =H^{1-\eta+o(1)}=o(\sqrt H).
$$
Nor do these finite systems produce positive normalized logarithmic
excess. The general conditioned-tail and square-root estimates remain
unresolved.

## One fixed family can have finite energy and unbounded additive excess

**Proposition.** There is one infinite distinct-singleton system whose
delayed excluded union $B$, full Haar union measure $\beta$, and
increasing-order first-kill masses $e_q$ satisfy
$$
 d(B)=\beta<\frac14,\qquad
 \sum_q e_q\log q<\infty,\qquad
 \limsup_{X\to\infty}(H_B(X)-\beta H_X)=\infty.
 \tag{27}
$$
Its survivor set has natural density $1-\beta>3/4$, so this is
**not** a counterexample to Problem 25. It rules out bounded additive
amortization even for one fixed family of finite total energy; it does
not rule out the required $o(\log X)$ bound.

The finite input is the proved construction (1): for some absolute
$C_*$, every $T>0$ admits a finite singleton family
$\mathcal F$, all of whose moduli are at most a cutoff $X$, with
$$
 \mathcal E(\mathcal F)\le C_*,\qquad
 D_{\mathcal F}(X):=H_{B_{\mathcal F}}(X)
                         -\beta_{\mathcal F}H_X>T.
 \tag{28}
$$
Only this established finite input is needed below.

### The exact affine lift

Let an already installed finite system have full union $U_{\rm old}$
of measure less than one and common period $P$. Choose a surviving
residue $b\in[0,P)$, and let $L$ be a multiple of $P$.
Replace every class $a\pmod q$ in a fresh finite family by
$$
 La+b\pmod{Lq}.
 \tag{29}
$$
This residue is normalized, and the lifted full union lies in
$[b]_L$, hence avoids $U_{\rm old}$. Its actual delayed union is
exactly $LB_{\mathcal F}+b$: since $0\le b<L$, the inequality
$Ln+b\ge Lq$ is equivalent to $n\ge q$.

The lifted footprint is $\beta_{\mathcal F}/L$. If all lifted
moduli follow the old ones, its first-kill masses are $e_q/L$,
so its energy contribution is
$$
 \frac{\mathcal E(\mathcal F)+\beta_{\mathcal F}\log L}{L}
 \le\frac{C_*+\log L}{L}.
 \tag{30}
$$
At $Y=LX+b$, with $\theta=b/L\in[0,1)$,
$$
 0\le \frac1L H_{B_{\mathcal F}}(X)
                   -H_{LB_{\mathcal F}+b}(Y)
 \le\frac1L\sum_{n\ge1}
                 \left(\frac1n-\frac1{n+1}\right)=\frac1L.
$$
Also $H_Y-H_X\le\log(Y/X)\le\log L+1$. Consequently
$$
 H_{LB_{\mathcal F}+b}(Y)-\frac{\beta_{\mathcal F}}L H_Y
 \ge\frac{D_{\mathcal F}(X)-\log L-2}{L}.
 \tag{31}
$$

### Induction and protection of earlier cutoffs

Suppose the first $j-1$ blocks and increasing cutoffs $Y_i$
have been chosen. Their finite delayed union $B_{<j}$, of footprint
$\beta_{<j}$, has a bounded harmonic discrepancy: for a finite
constant $C_{j-1}$,
$$
 H_{B_{<j}}(N)-\beta_{<j}H_N\ge-C_{j-1}\qquad(N\ge1).
 \tag{32}
$$
This follows from periodic partial summation and the finitely many
activation exceptions. Use $C_0=0$ for the empty prefix.

Choose $L_j$, a multiple of the old period, sufficiently large that
$$
 \begin{gathered}
 L_j>Y_{j-1}\quad(j>1),\qquad
 L_j^{-1}\le2^{-j-3},\qquad
 (C_*+\log L_j)/L_j\le2^{-j},\\
 L_j^{-1}\le2^{-j}/H_{Y_i}\qquad(1\le i<j).
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{33}
$$
The old footprint is below $1/4$, so an old surviving residue
$b_j$ exists. Apply (28) with
$$
 D_{\mathcal F_j}(X_j)>
       L_j(j+C_{j-1}+3)+\log L_j+2,
$$
lift by (29), and put $Y_j=L_jX_j+b_j$.
Each new modulus is at least $L_j>Y_{j-1}$, whereas all old
moduli are at most $Y_{j-1}$. Thus the global moduli are distinct
and strictly ordered block by block. Their full block unions are
disjoint. Equations (31)--(32) give
$$
 H_{B_{\le j}}(Y_j)-\beta_{\le j}H_{Y_j}>j+3.
 \tag{34}
$$

In the resulting infinite family,
$$
 \beta=\sum_j\frac{\beta_{\mathcal F_j}}{L_j}<\frac14,
 \qquad
 \sum_qe_q\log q
  =\sum_j\frac{\mathcal E(\mathcal F_j)
                         +\beta_{\mathcal F_j}\log L_j}{L_j}
  \le\sum_j2^{-j}<\infty.
$$
Future moduli exceed $Y_j$, so none changes deletion below that
cutoff. Their future Haar cost is controlled as well:
$$
 (\beta-\beta_{\le j})H_{Y_j}
 \le H_{Y_j}\sum_{k>j}L_k^{-1}
 \le\sum_{k>j}2^{-k}=2^{-j}.
$$
Equation (34) therefore gives
$H_B(Y_j)-\beta H_{Y_j}>j+3-2^{-j}$.

Finally, the whole delayed block $j$ lies in
$\{L_jn+b_j:n\ge1\}$, with at most $N/L_j$ points below
$N$. Hence
$$
 |(B\setminus B_{\le J})\cap[1,N]|
      \le N\sum_{j>J}L_j^{-1}.
$$
Finite-prefix periodicity and this summable tail prove $d(B)=\beta$.
This completes (27), including original activation and natural-density
