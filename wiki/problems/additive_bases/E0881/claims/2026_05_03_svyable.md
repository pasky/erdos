***

**Claim.** On 2026-05-03 Svyable posted in the thread of
[[problems/additive_bases/E0881/_index|Problem 881]] the manuscript *Infinite
Deletions from Strongly Minimal Additive Bases*, which claims a complete
answer; the post links the ChatGPT conversation that generated the write-up and
a Perplexity conversation used as its referee. For $k=1$ the answer is yes:
removing a sparse infinite subset from a basis of order $1$ leaves a basis of
order $2$ (Proposition 7). For every $k\ge2$ the answer is no (Main Theorem
6): some asymptotic basis $A$ of order $k$, minimal at order $k$ both in
the ordinary sense and under infinite deletions, has $A\setminus B$ not an
asymptotic basis of order $k+1$ for every infinite $B\subset A$, and
$|A\cap[0,x]|=O_k(x^{1/k})$. formal-conjectures states the question for every
order $k$ and every minimal basis (`erdos_881`, the `record` link), with a
basis of order $k$ meaning that all large integers are sums of exactly $k$
elements, so one counterexample at some $k\ge2$ answers it no. The
erdosproblems.com definitions page instead allows sums of at most $k$ elements.
The source card is
[[../library/additive_bases/svyable_2026_infinite_deletions_strongly_minimal_additive_bases/_index|svyable_2026_infinite_deletions_strongly_minimal_additive_bases]].

**Submission note.** Posted to the erdosproblems.com forum by Svyable on 3 May
2026:

> Let \(A\subset\mathbb N\) be an asymptotic additive basis of order \(k\), and
> suppose that \(A\) is strongly minimal under infinite deletions in the sense
> that, for every infinite \(B\subset A\), the set \(A\setminus B\) is not an
> asymptotic basis of order \(k\). Must there exist an infinite \(B\subset A\)
> such that \(A\setminus B\) is an asymptotic basis of order \(k+1\)?
>
> We give a complete answer. For \(k=1\), the answer is yes. For every
> \(k\ge2\), the answer is no. More strongly, for every \(k\ge2\), we construct
> a set \(A\subset\mathbb N\) such that \(A\) is an asymptotic basis of order
> \(k\), is ordinarily minimal at order \(k\), is strongly minimal under
> infinite deletions at order \(k\), and satisfies the stronger property that
> \(A\setminus B\) is not an asymptotic basis of order \(k+1\) for every
> infinite \(B\subset A\). The examples may be chosen thin, with\[ A(x) =
> \lvert A \cap [0, x] \rvert = O_k(x^{1/k}) \]which is optimal up to constants
> for bases of order \(k\).

**Why it is rejected.** It answers a variant, not the Statement. The
manuscript, like formal-conjectures' `erdos_881`, reads a basis of order $k$
as a set of which all large integers are sums of exactly $k$ elements, and it
answers that variant no for every $k\ge2$ (Main Theorem 6).
[[problems/additive_bases/E0881/_index|Problem 881]] reads the question, as
its Formulation states, with the erdosproblems.com definition of sums of at most
$k$ elements. The manuscript's set does not contain $0$, and neither its
minimality at order $k$ nor its failure at order $k+1$ carries over from
exactly $k$ (and $k+1$) summands to at most $k$ (and $k+1$), so the
counterexample settles no case of the question as the problem page reads it.
The case $k=1$, the manuscript's Proposition 7, is the same under both
definitions, but it is elementary and not what the claim contributes: a basis
of order $1$ contains every large integer, and deleting a sufficiently sparse
infinite subset of it leaves every large integer a sum of at most two
remaining elements. The manuscript's mathematics thus answers the exactly-$k$
variant and does not settle this problem; the value `disproved` is its answer
to that variant.

**Standing.** Rejected, as answering a variant. The erdosproblems.com curator
wrote the same day that the proof appears to have been generated entirely by GPT
and asked for the use of AI to be disclosed. A reader posted the same day an AI
check, a ChatGPT conversation, reporting several major gaps. The check finds
that the manuscript counts sums of exactly $k$ elements, so that it could settle
at most that reading and not erdosproblems.com's; that the clause of the witness
lemmas placing the witnesses in any prescribed subinterval (the 'Moreover'
clause of Lemma 8, and its analogue for the booster witnesses) is false as
stated, since a witness is a sum of $k$ chosen elements, while the construction
relies on it; that the booster step's scale inequalities fail for $k\ge21$; that
the recursion uses the next scale before choosing it and lacks a uniform
estimate for its induction; and that the finite-booster normal form theorem is
not proved. The thread records no reply to these points. No refutation of the
result in its own reading is recorded, and it is not refereed, reviewed or
formalized.
