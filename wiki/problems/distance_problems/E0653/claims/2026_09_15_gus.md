**Claim.** The answer to
[[problems/distance_problems/E0653/_index|Problem 653]] is yes: for every
$\delta>0$ and every sufficiently large $n$ there is a set of $n$ points in
the plane whose $n$ counts $R(x_i)$ of distinct distances to the other points
take at least $(1-\delta)n$ different values, so $g(n)\ge(1-o(1))n$; with the
trivial $g(n)\le n$ this gives $g(n)=(1-o(1))n$. The claimant is the solver
recorded by the bounty site Conjectures.io under the name gus; the proof is a
Lean 4 file of about 28,600 lines whose header names no author and no AI
system, and which declares adapted material from the Prime Number Theorem and
More project, ported to the pinned Lean and Mathlib under the Apache License
2.0. The AI system behind the proof, if any, is undisclosed. The record links
an exposition of the proof, linked above, dated 17 September 2026 and written
by Liam Kruer and Jensen Kohlmeyer, which says it was prepared by Conjectures
with Codex assistance from the accepted Lean submission and that the
erdosproblems.com Lean verification covers the formal source, not the prose. The
result is known on the erdosproblems.com proof-claims tab through an entry of 27
September 2026, linked above, submitted by the erdosproblems.com curator, Thomas
Bloom, naming conjectures.io as the claimant with the system recorded as
Unknown; Bloom's note says that Bloom has not verified or examined the proof,
posts the entry so that others are aware of it, does not endorse the
Conjectures.io program, and objects that it is not transparent which AI system
is used.

**The formal statement.** Conjectures.io attacked the formal-conjectures
statement `Erdos653.erdos_653` ([`FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/653.lean` at
the formal-conjectures commit of
2026-09-18](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/6fbb54f24ccc2e64dcfaffc28c58950e377110d2/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/653.lean))
with its open answer fixed to true: there is a function $o$ with $o(n)\to0$ such that
eventually $(1-o(n))\,n\le$ `maximalDistinctDistancesFrom n`, where the
latter is the supremum, over $n$-point finite subsets $X$ of the Euclidean
plane, of the number of distinct values of the per-point count of distinct
distances from a point of $X$ to the points of $X$. That count includes the
zero self-distance and so equals $R(x_i)+1$ for every point, which leaves the
number of distinct values unchanged, and the proof file proves this equality;
the ordering of the $R(x_i)$ in the problem's wording carries no content for
that number; the supremum is a maximum since $g(n)\le n$; and the
eventually-with-$o(n)\to0$ form is the asymptotic wording, finitely many $n$
being absorbable into $o$. So the formal statement is the problem's
question clause for clause. The proof file's last declaration, `target`,
spells that type out rather than naming the formal-conjectures theorem; the
file's own `Point`, `pinnedCount`, `diversity` and `Erdos653` live in a local
namespace and are bridged to the formal-conjectures definition, and the file
redefines no catalog or Mathlib name.

**The construction.** The points are $(2s)\times(2s)$ blocks of shifted
integer grids whose rational offsets come from distinct primes congruent to
$3$ modulo $4$, so that a point's count of distinct distances is governed by a
coarse degree of its block and the blocks' counts fall into disjoint
intervals; skew and jitter parameters spread the counts inside each block to a
$(1-\delta)$ fraction of distinct values; a ported estimate for primes in
arithmetic progressions feeds the sparsity bounds; and a padding step, adding
a far point that shifts every count by one, reaches every $n$. The theorem
`target` is discharged from `official_target_of_squareFamilies` applied to
the construction `squareFamilies`. The exposition presents the same
construction in another parametrization: $K=t^2$ blocks with $t=2(s+1)$, each
an $L\times L$ grid, offset by Gaussian integers built from primes congruent
to $3$ modulo $4$.

**Acceptance.** Formalized. This corpus's verification rebuilt the record's
solution file on the repository's pinned toolchain, `leanprover/lean4:v4.34.1`
with Mathlib at the revision `lean/lake-manifest.json` pins, after proof-only
changes for lemmas that Mathlib renamed or removed since Conjectures.io's
toolchain; the target's statement is unchanged. The kernel replayed, from an
empty environment, every declaration the target `Bounty.target` depends on, Lean
core, Mathlib and formal-conjectures included, and accepted them all. The
target's axioms are exactly `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. Its
type, and every definition its statement reaches, are identical to the
formal-conjectures statement of Conjectures.io's task, whose reading as the
problem's question is under **The formal statement.** above: the per-point count
includes the self-distance $0$, which shifts every count by one and leaves the
number of distinct values unchanged. Acceptance rests on this replay and that
check.

Conjectures.io's certification is reported, not counted. The erdosproblems.com
Lean kernel verified the submission on 15 September 2026. Its review approved
the record on 16 September 2026 under its policy v3. Conjectures.io certified
the record on 17 September 2026 and paid the bounty. According to
Conjectures.io's review record, the exact accepted proof and task passed its
production verification. The record also says that the theorem establishes the
intended asymptotic diversity of distance counts, and that counting the
self-distance does not alter it. It says that two independent agent assessments
of the same model family agreed, covering formal semantics and prior-source
eligibility. It also says that no fresh replay was run on a second kernel, so
its verdict rests on one kernel implementation. The permitted axioms were
`propext`, `Quot.sound` and `Classical.choice`.

That certification is Conjectures.io's own. No refereed publication exists. The
erdosproblems.com page labeled the problem OPEN. The formal-conjectures catalog
marks the statement research open (commit of 2026-09-18, linked above).

The corpus also read the proof file as text. A text scan found no `sorry`,

The target, header and key declarations were checked against Conjectures.io's
