**Claim.** For every simple graph $G$ with $\chi(G)=\aleph_1$, whatever
the cardinality of its vertex set,

$$
\forall K>0\ \exists N\ \forall n\ge N:\qquad h_G(n)>Kn,
$$

so $h_G(n)/n\to\infty$. Here $h_G(n)$ is the least bound in the Statement
of [[problems/set_theory/E0111/_index|Problem 111]], the largest number of
edge deletions that an $n$-vertex subgraph of $G$ needs to become
bipartite; every admissible bound is at least this one. The threshold $N$
may depend on $G$ and $K$. The second question of the Statement therefore
has a positive answer.

The proof goes through a countable-coloring theorem: for every finite
$C\ge0$, every graph each of whose finite subgraphs $F$ can be made
bipartite by deleting at most $C|V(F)|$ edges has countable chromatic
number, at every vertex cardinality, singular cardinals included.
Deleting countably many vertices from a graph of chromatic number
$\aleph_1$ leaves chromatic number $\aleph_1$, so the theorem yields
$\aleph_1$ pairwise vertex-disjoint finite subgraphs $F$ each needing more
than $2K|V(F)|$ deletions. One isomorphism type recurs uncountably often,
and packing its copies gives $h_G(n)>Kn$ for every large $n$.

On the first question, the behaviour of $h_G(n)$: for every nondecreasing
unbounded $g:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$ there is a graph $G$ with
$|V(G)|=2^{\aleph_1}$, $\chi(G)=\aleph_1$ and

$$
h_G(n)\le(n-1)\,g(n)\qquad(n\ge1),
$$

and under $\diamondsuit$ there is such a graph with $|V(G)|=\aleph_1$.
With $g(n)=\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$, one graph of chromatic number $\aleph_1$
has $h_G(n)\ll n^{1+\varepsilon}$ for every $\varepsilon>0$. This confirms
in ZFC the conjecture of Erdős, recorded in the remarks on erdosproblems.com,
that the Erdős–Hajnal–Szemerédi bound $h_G(n)\ll n^{3/2}$ can be improved
to $\ll n^{1+\varepsilon}$ for every $\varepsilon>0$.

**Covers.** The definite question of
[[problems/set_theory/E0111/_index|Problem 111]], whether
$h_G(n)/n\to\infty$ for every graph $G$ of chromatic number $\aleph_1$:
answered yes, as a limit over all sufficiently large $n$ rather than along
a subsequence, for graphs of every vertex cardinality. The first question,
the behaviour of $h_G(n)$, is open-ended and not a part. On it the claim
supplies only the upper bounds stated above. It determines no rate of divergence and no exact order of
$h_G(n)$ for any particular graph, and asserts no optimality of the bound
$(n-1)g(n)$.

**Credit.** The countable-coloring theorem, its layered-measure proof and
the reading of the upper bound are Plasma AI's. Section 2 of the Erdős
Problem a Day report on Problem 111
(<https://www.erdosproblemaday.com/report/111>, dated 26 July 2026) also
proves the reduction of the limit to a finite-density statement; the argument
here does not rely on it. The graph of the upper bound is the one
built in Chris Lambie-Hanson's ZFC proof of
[[../library/graph_coloring/lambiehanson_2020_growth_rate_chromatic_numbers_finite_subgraphs/theorem_a|Theorem A]]
of *On the growth rate of chromatic numbers of finite subgraphs* (Adv.
Math. 369, 2020), and under $\diamondsuit$ the Hajnal–Máté graph of his
[[../library/graph_coloring/lambiehanson_2020_growth_rate_chromatic_numbers_finite_subgraphs/theorem_b|Theorem B]].
The bound is read off the edge labelling of his Claim 4.1; his
construction uses the odd girth of Specker graphs and Shelah's
club-guessing theorem as that paper cites them.

**Formalization.** The repository
[plasma-ai/erdos-111](https://github.com/plasma-ai/erdos-111/tree/de72caf0e388c729697b6813f4ea90bfe1617c7b),
at commit de72caf, formalizes the claim in Lean 4 (`v4.35.0-rc3`, Mathlib
`v4.35.0-rc3`). `Challenge.lean` states it as `Erdos111.erdos_111`, for every
graph whose least color cardinal `chromaticCardinal G` is $\aleph_1$, with
`h G n` the largest number of deletions an $n$-vertex subgraph needs to become
bipartite; it also states the countable-coloring theorem,
`Erdos111.countably_colorable_of_linear`, and the limit for every graph with no
coloring by $\mathbb N$, `Erdos111.tendsto_of_not_countably_colorable`.
`Solution.lean` proves the three, and `comparator.json` permits only `propext`,
`Quot.sound` and `Classical.choice`. The proof follows the repository's
blueprint, which reorganizes the reviewed argument: closure over a set in place
of elementary submodels, a chain that starts from the empty set, and limits of
laws along an ultrafilter in place of weak-star compactness. The repository's
`formalization.yaml` names Jacob Parish as the formalization's author, records
that it was written with Claude Code, and gives its review status as
self-assessed, with a Comparator check run on macOS without Comparator's
sandbox. The repository's README credits the proof to two models working in
alternating steps, GPT-6 Astra in Codex and Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code, and
the Lean to Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code. The repository is registered on
the Palomar registry as `PALOMAR-2026-10-19-000007`, version 1, first registered
on 19 October 2026 (the `record` link; trust level high, automated review
outcome neutral), and the entry pins commit de72caf. The source card
[[../library/set_theory/plasma_ai_2026_bipartizing_finite_subgraphs_uncountably_chromatic_graphs/_index|plasma_ai_2026_bipartizing_finite_subgraphs_uncountably_chromatic_graphs]]
digests it.
