# problems/unit_fractions/E0290/claims

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0290/_index|..]]

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0290/claims/2024_11_05_van_doorn|2024_11_05_van_doorn]]: Van Doorn's 2024 arXiv paper proves that the denominator of a block of
consecutive reciprocals starting at $a$ first drops at an index at most linear
in $a$, and that the drop comes after at least order $\log a$ terms.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0290/claims/2026_08_31_van_doorn|2026_08_31_van_doorn]]: A 2026 preprint by van Doorn claims that the limit inferior of
$(b(a)-a)/\log a$ equals $1/(1+c)\approx0.546$, the lower value of the
author's 2024 bounds. A language model is credited for one step.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0290/claims/2026_09_24_van_doorn|2026_09_24_van_doorn]]: A 2026 note by van Doorn claims that for almost all $a$ the first denominator
drop comes after more than $\exp(\tfrac12\sqrt{\log a\log\log a})$ terms.
Hence no power of $\log a$ bounds $b(a)-a$ on a set of positive density.
