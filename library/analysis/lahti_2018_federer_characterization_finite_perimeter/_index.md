# Lahti (2018): Federer's characterization of sets of finite perimeter

[[analysis/_index|..]]

[[analysis/lahti_2018_federer_characterization_finite_perimeter/theorem_1_1|theorem_1_1]]: Finite codimension-one boundary measure implies finite perimeter in the
stated metric setting. In the Euclidean plane the criterion and the
perimeter representation imply that a measurable set with empty
measure-theoretic boundary is null or conull.

***

Panu Lahti, *Federer's characterization of sets of finite perimeter in metric
spaces*, arXiv:1804.11216v1 (27 April 2018). The title page is dated 1 May
2018. The version used here is the arXiv v1 PDF; its page numbers and labels
are used below.

The paper proves the finite-perimeter criterion in complete metric spaces
with a doubling measure and a $(1,1)$-Poincare inequality. If the
codimension-one Hausdorff measure of a measurable set's measure-theoretic
boundary in an open set is finite, then its perimeter in that open set is
finite (Theorem 1.1, p. 2). The Euclidean case, due to Federer, is recalled
on p. 1 with the reference *Geometric Measure Theory* (1969), Section
4.5.11. The metric proof uses fine potential theory for $p=1$; that proof
is not consumed here.

The [[analysis/lahti_2018_federer_characterization_finite_perimeter/theorem_1_1|criterion and its Euclidean application]]
record the boundary definition (2.3), the perimeter representation (2.4),
and the relative isoperimetric inequality (2.2). They give an explicit
local-to-global deduction: a measurable subset of the plane with empty
measure-theoretic boundary is null or conull. Empty topological boundary
is not the hypothesis, and no regularity of the coloring is assumed
beyond measurability.

**Reading.** The introduction's Euclidean criterion, Theorem 1.1, the
boundary definition and formulas (2.2)--(2.4), and the standing metric
hypotheses were checked at statement level in the v1 TeX and PDF pages
1--3 and 5--6. The corresponding Euclidean specialization is recorded on
the result page. Neither Federer's original proof nor Lahti's proof of
the metric extension was read. No independent proof verification is
asserted.
