# problems/discrete_geometry/E1121/claims

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E1121/_index|..]]

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E1121/claims/1945_11_01_goodman_goodman|1945_11_01_goodman_goodman]]: Goodman and Goodman proved in 1945 that finitely many disks in the plane that
no disjoint line separates lie in one disk whose radius is the sum of their
radii, as Erdős conjectured. The proof is refereed and credited by
erdosproblems.com, and a Lean formalization is linked.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E1121/claims/1947_12_01_hadwiger|1947_12_01_hadwiger]]: Hadwiger proves that for a nonseparable system of plane convex curves, the
perimeter, diameter and circumradius of the convex hull are at most the sums
of those of its members. The circumradius inequality contains the circle
covering theorem.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E1121/claims/2015_07_17_bezdek_litvak|2015_07_17_bezdek_litvak]]: Theorem 6.1 of Bezdek and Litvak's paper Packing convex bodies by cylinders
bounds the circumradius of the hull of nonseparable disks by the sum of their
radii, using an integral-geometric lemma. This gives a second proof of the
circle covering theorem.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E1121/claims/2016_02_02_bezdek_langi|2016_02_02_bezdek_langi]]: Bezdek and Lángi prove that a nonseparable family of positive homothets of an
o-symmetric convex body with ratios $\tau_i$ is covered by a translate of the
body scaled by $\sum_i\tau_i$. For the disk this is the circle covering
theorem.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E1121/claims/2016_05_13_akopyan_balitskiy_grigorev|2016_05_13_akopyan_balitskiy_grigorev]]: Akopyan, Balitskiy and Grigorev prove that a nonseparable family of positive
homothets of a convex body $K$ with ratios $\tau_i$ is covered by a translate
of $\frac{\sigma+1}{2}(\sum_i\tau_i)K$, with $\sigma$ the asymmetry of $K$.
The disk has $\sigma=1$, giving the circle covering theorem.
