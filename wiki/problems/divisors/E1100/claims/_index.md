# problems/divisors/E1100/claims

[[problems/divisors/E1100/_index|..]]

[[problems/divisors/E1100/claims/1989_03_01_erdos_tenenbaum|1989_03_01_erdos_tenenbaum]]: Erdős and Tenenbaum (1989) prove that for almost all $n$ the number of coprime
consecutive divisor pairs exceeds a power of $\log n$, so its ratio to
$\omega(n)$ tends to infinity. They also show that its maximum up to $x$ is at
least $\exp\bigl(c\log x/(\log\log x)^2\bigr)$ for some $c>0$.

[[problems/divisors/E1100/claims/2026_07_31_korsky|2026_07_31_korsky]]: Korsky's manuscript, prepared with GPT-5.6 Pro, claims that the maximum of the
coprime consecutive divisor count up to $x$ is
$\exp(\Theta(\log x/\log\log x))$ with an explicit lower constant, answering
Erdős's maximal-order questions in the negative.

[[problems/divisors/E1100/claims/2026_08_04_ross|2026_08_04_ross]]: Does not improve the best known bound ($g(k)\ge F_{k+1}\gg\varphi^k$, from
Chevyrev, Searles and Slinko 2013). Ross claims that for squarefree $n$ with
$k$ prime factors, the maximum number of coprime consecutive divisor pairs is
at least $c\varphi^k/\sqrt k$.
