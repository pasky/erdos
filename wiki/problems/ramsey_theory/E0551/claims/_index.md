# problems/ramsey_theory/E0551/claims

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0551/_index|..]]

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0551/claims/1973_02_01_bondy_erdos|1973_02_01_bondy_erdos]]: Theorem 4 of Bondy and Erdős (1973) gives $R(C_k,K_n)=(k-1)(n-1)+1$ whenever
$k\ge n^2-2$, the first infinite range of the identity. It is refereed and
credited by the site's commentary.

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0551/claims/2004_04_27_nikiforov|2004_04_27_nikiforov]]: Theorem 1 of Nikiforov's preprint arXiv:math/0404501v1 (journal version
Combin. Probab. Comput. 14 (2005)) gives $R(C_k,K_n)=(k-1)(n-1)+1$ whenever
$n\ge4$ and $k\ge4n+2$, a linear threshold. It is refereed.

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0551/claims/2018_07_17_keevash_long_skokan|2018_07_17_keevash_long_skokan]]: Theorem 1.1 of Keevash, Long and Skokan (IMRN 2021) gives an absolute constant
$C$ with $R(C_k,K_n)=(k-1)(n-1)+1$ whenever $k\ge C\log n/\log\log n$, so the
identity holds for all large $n$. Finitely many pairs remain unchecked.

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0551/claims/2026_09_25_openai|2026_09_25_openai]]: The OpenAI release's preprint of 25 September 2026 proves
$R(C_k,K_n)=(k-1)(n-1)+1$ for every $k\ge n\ge3$ other than $k=n=3$, where
$R(C_3,K_3)=6$, by a reduction to 3,099 finite pattern instances. It is
accepted on its built Lean proof.
