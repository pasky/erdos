# problems/ramsey_theory/E1182/claims

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E1182/_index|..]]

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E1182/claims/1980_01_01_burr_erdos_faudree_rousseau_schelp|1980_01_01_burr_erdos_faudree_rousseau_schelp]]: Burr, Erdős, Faudree, Rousseau and Schelp (Ars Combinatoria 1980) prove
$F(n)\ge(17n+1)/15$ for $n\ge4$ and $F(n)<(27/4+\varepsilon)n(\log n)^2$, and
bound $f(n)$ between $n^{3/2}(\log n)^{1/2}$ and $n^{5/3}(\log n)^{2/3}$ up to
constants. They also tabulate both functions for $n\le6$.

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E1182/claims/1996_12_01_brandt|1996_12_01_brandt]]: Brandt's 1996 preprint (Freie Universität Berlin, A 96-24) claims that almost
every $168$-regular graph $H$ of order $n$ has $R(K_3,H)>2n$. This would give
$F(n)<84n$ for large $n$ and answer the closing question of Problem 1182 no.

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E1182/claims/2007_06_27_sudakov|2007_06_27_sudakov]]: Sudakov's refereed 2007 theorem $R(K_s,G)\ge c(m/\log m)^{(s+1)/(s+3)}$ for
every graph $G$ with $m$ edges gives $f(n)=O(n^{3/2}\log n)$ at $s=3$. So the
exponent of $f(n)$ is $3/2$, and only a factor $(\log n)^{1/2}$ remains.

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E1182/claims/2026_09_10_gu|2026_09_10_gu]]: A full proof claim under Qiyuan Gu's name on the erdosproblems.com tab (10
September 2026), with a Zenodo manuscript, asserts that the least $R(K_3,G)$
over $m$-edge graphs $G$ has order $m^{2/3}/(\log m)^{1/3}$, and deduces that
$f(n)$ has order $n^{3/2}(\log n)^{1/2}$.

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E1182/claims/2026_09_28_kataria|2026_09_28_kataria]]: A partial proof claim by Pravar Kataria and Claude Fable 5.1 on the
erdosproblems.com tab (29 September 2026), with a note in a GitHub repository,
asserts $F(n)\ge n+\lfloor(n-1)/6\rfloor$ for $n\ge4$ and $F(n)\le5.03n$ for
large $n$. These unreviewed bounds would improve the constants $17/15$ and
$84$.
