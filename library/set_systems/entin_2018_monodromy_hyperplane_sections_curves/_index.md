# Monodromy of hyperplane sections of curves and decomposition statistics over finite fields

[[set_systems/_index|..]]

***

Alexei Entin, *Monodromy of Hyperplane Sections of Curves and Decomposition
Statistics over Finite Fields*,
[arXiv:1805.05454v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05454v3), 31 March 2019.
The paper first appeared on arXiv in May 2018 and was published in
*International Mathematics Research Notices* **2021** (14), 10409–10441.
The edition read here is arXiv v3; no comparison with the journal edition
has been made.

Theorem 1 counts the Frobenius conjugacy classes of hyperplane sections
of a quasireflexive curve whose components over the finite field are
absolutely irreducible. The section domain requires exactly as many
**distinct geometric intersection points** as the curve's degree. It is
not a condition merely on the rational points of the intersection.
The main term is the conjugacy-class proportion in the product of the
component symmetric groups; the relative error is of order
$q^{-1/2}$, uniformly at fixed ambient dimension and total degree.

Section 2 defines quasireflexivity using a simple tangent on each
component of the whole curve. Proposition 2.1 identifies it with
reflexivity in characteristic different from two. Its proof invokes the
generic order of contact theorem and the Segre–Wallace criterion.

**Read status: claims checked; proofs read for structure only.**
Theorem 1 with its definitions, and Section 2 through Proposition 2.1
and its short proof, were read in the v3 TeX. The main counting and
monodromy proofs, the cited contact and separability proofs, and the
later applications remain unread. This reading supplies no
independent proof certification.

**Bears on.** The plane-curve specialization underlies
[[set_systems/asgarli_2025_blocking_sets_union_plane_curves/theorem_3_2|the Frobenius count used for blocking sets]],
and therefore the conditional curve approximation route to
[[../wiki/problems/set_systems/E1159/_index|Problem 1159]].
It does not provide the required approximation of arbitrary minimal
blocking sets.
