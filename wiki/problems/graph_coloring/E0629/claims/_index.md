# problems/graph_coloring/E0629/claims

[[problems/graph_coloring/E0629/_index|..]]

[[problems/graph_coloring/E0629/claims/1980_01_01_erdos_rubin_taylor|1980_01_01_erdos_rubin_taylor]]: Erdős, Rubin and Taylor's paper introducing choosability states that the least
number of vertices of a bipartite graph that is not $2$-choosable is $6$, and
gives general bounds on $n(k)$. It appeared in conference proceedings with no
record of refereeing.

[[problems/graph_coloring/E0629/claims/1996_01_01_hanson_macgillivray_toft|1996_01_01_hanson_macgillivray_toft]]: In a refereed paper, Hanson, MacGillivray and Toft prove that every complete
bipartite graph that is not $3$-choosable has at least $14$ vertices. The
lines of the Fano plane as lists on $K_{7,7}$ show that $14$ is attained, so
$n(3)=14$.

[[problems/graph_coloring/E0629/claims/2026_10_19_plasma_ai|2026_10_19_plasma_ai]]: Every bipartite graph with at most 26 vertices is 4-choosable, so $n(4)\ge27$; tier 1 on an independent examination and a separate passing grade, with catalog acceptance pending.
