# problems/discrete_geometry/E0505/claims

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0505/_index|..]]

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0505/claims/1933_01_01_borsuk|1933_01_01_borsuk]]: Borsuk proves that every bounded planar set of diameter $1$ is the union of
three sets of smaller diameter, the instance $n=2$ of the question. The paper
is refereed in Fund. Math., and the formal-conjectures statement files credit
it.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0505/claims/1955_01_01_eggleston|1955_01_01_eggleston]]: Eggleston proves that every set of diameter $1$ in $\mathbb R^3$ is the union
of four sets of smaller diameter, the instance $n=3$ of the question. The
result is refereed, and the site and the formal-conjectures statement file
credit it.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0505/claims/1993_07_01_kahn_kalai|1993_07_01_kahn_kalai]]: Kahn and Kalai answer the question no. Finite sets built from equal cuts of a
complete graph need at least $(1.2)^{\sqrt n}$ parts for large $n$, and exact
counts give failures at $n=1325$ and every $n>2014$.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0505/claims/2013_05_12_bondarenko|2013_05_12_bondarenko]]: Bondarenko gives a two-distance set of $416$ points on the unit sphere in
$\mathbb R^{65}$ that cannot be split into $83$ parts of smaller diameter, so
at least $84$ are needed where Borsuk's assertion allows $66$. The result is
refereed in Discrete Comput. Geom.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0505/claims/2014_11_06_jenrich_brouwer|2014_11_06_jenrich_brouwer]]: Jenrich and Brouwer give a two-distance set of $352$ points in
$\mathbb R^{64}$ whose subsets of smaller diameter have at most five points.
So at least $71$ parts are needed where Borsuk's assertion allows $65$.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0505/claims/2026_05_27_grinsztajn|2026_05_27_grinsztajn]]: Grinsztajn's public, unpublished note claims there are $321$ points in
$\mathbb R^{63}$ whose subsets of smaller diameter have at most five points,
so that at least $65$ parts are needed where Borsuk's assertion allows $64$.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0505/claims/2026_08_12_ji|2026_08_12_ji]]: Ji's arXiv submission claims the $321$-point, $65$-part counterexample in
$\mathbb R^{63}$, generated by ChatGPT and checked by the submitter. It was
withdrawn two days later because the construction had been posted earlier.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0505/claims/2026_09_23_openai|2026_09_23_openai]]: OpenAI shows that the rank-one projectors of four-dimensional space, with the
Frobenius metric, form a compact set of diameter $\sqrt2$ in a
nine-dimensional space that no ten sets of smaller diameter cover. The proof
is kernel-checked in Lean.
