**Claim.** Let $A$ be the set of integers whose base-$3$ digits are all $0$
or $1$ and $B$ the set of integers whose base-$4$ digits are all $0$ or $1$.
Then the lower density of $A+B$ is $0$: for every $\epsilon>0$ there are
arbitrarily large $x$ with $\lvert (A+B)\cap[1,x]\rvert<\epsilon x$. This
answers the question of
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0125/_index|Problem 125]] in the negative.
The Lean statement proved is `erdos_125.variants.positive_lower_density`,
the negation of `0 < (A + B).lowerDensity`, together with
`lower_density_zero : (A + B).lowerDensity = 0`, in the formal-conjectures
file at the linked commit. The paper of Tsoukalas et al. (Science, 2026) proves
the statement in its Appendix B.4, recorded on the library page
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/tsoukalas_2026_advancing_mathematics_research_ai_driven_formal_proof_search/erdos_125|Erdős #125]],
and gives as its Lean proof the file
`APNOutputs/ErdosProblems/erdos_125.variants.positive_lower_density.lean` of
DeepMind's repository `google-deepmind/alphaproof-nexus-results`, which proves
the same statement, `answer(False) ↔ 0 < (A + B).lowerDensity`, as
`target_theorem_0`.

**Argument, in outline.** The lemma names of the Lean texts, and the summaries
on the thread, indicate a Dirichlet approximation aligning the scales $3^k$ and
$4^m$, a gap in $A+B$ forced at each aligned scale, and a sparse sequence of
scales at which the count $\lvert (A+B)\cap[1,x]\rvert$ shrinks by a constant
factor each time, so that it falls below $\epsilon x$ infinitely often. Thomas
Bloom's reconstruction on the thread (post 5114, 2026-03-30) suggests that the
argument generalizes to any bases $d_1,\dots,d_r$ with $\sum 1/(d_i-1)<1$. Nat
Sothanaphan's reply (post 5119, the same day, crediting GPT-5.4 Thinking)
sketches that generalization for $r\geq 2$ bases, repetition allowed, through
the simultaneous Dirichlet approximation theorem. The proof has not been
reconstructed in this corpus.

The repository's file runs through different lemmas from the formal-conjectures
copy, and its lemma names and statements indicate the following. A gap lemma
(`A_B_gap`) shows that no integer strictly between $(3^k-1)/2+(4^m-1)/3$ and
$\min(3^k,4^m)$ lies in $A+B$, since the elements of $A$ below $3^k$ are at most
$(3^k-1)/2$ and those of $B$ below $4^m$ at most $(4^m-1)/3$. The irrationality
of $\log 4/\log 3$ (`log_ratio_irrational`) gives, by a Dirichlet-type
approximation (`dirichlet_approx`), exponents with
$3^k\leq 4^m\leq(1+\epsilon)3^k$, so that the gap is a fixed fraction of the
scale. A scale step (`scale_step`)
then shows that a bound $\lvert(A+B)\cap[0,N)\rvert\leq CN$ at one scale yields
the bound $\tfrac{11}{12}CN'$ at a larger scale $N'$, and induction
(`density_multi_scale`) gives, for every $d$, some $N$ with
$\lvert(A+B)\cap[0,N)\rvert\leq(11/12)^dN$; hence (`density_tends_to_zero`) the
count falls below $\epsilon N$ for some $N$ at every $\epsilon>0$, which is the
negation of positive lower density. The paper's informal proof (B.4,
pp. 34--35 of arXiv v2) follows the same route with the factor $0.99$.

The same claimant's first step,
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0125/claims/2026_02_25_deepmind|DeepMind's earlier result]],
showed that $A+B$ has no positive density; the present result improves it
and does not rest on it logically.

**Standing.** The claimant is Google DeepMind (AlphaProof Nexus), which the
site's commentary credits with both results; the result was posted on
2026-03-30 by George Tsoukalas, who reports that a DeepMind prover agent found
the Lean proof autonomously. The paper of Tsoukalas et al., *Advancing
mathematics research with AI-driven formal proof search* (Science 394 (2026),
234--239; arXiv:2605.22763), settles the provenance: the agent is AlphaProof
Nexus, the paper's system, and the paper calls post 5110 its original
communication of the result. `authors` lists the paper's twenty-one authors in
their published order. The site's curator, Thomas Bloom, marked the problem
DISPROVED (LEAN), records in the commentary that DeepMind proved the lower
density is $0$, and reconstructed the argument on the thread; Giuseppe Melfi,
an author of the problem's literature, confirmed on the thread (post 5169,
2026-04-01) that the result rules out the remaining scenario with positive
lower density. That curator acceptance is the `reviewed` evidence. The
`refereed` evidence is the paper's publication in Science on 2026-10-08, whose
Appendix B.4 in the arXiv version gives the informal proof; the Science text
and its supplement could not be read here (the publisher's site refused access), and the
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/tsoukalas_2026_advancing_mathematics_research_ai_driven_formal_proof_search/_index|library card]]
says what was checked against the published version.

Three Lean texts hold proofs: the pinned formal-conjectures copy of 2026-03-30;
the paper's file in DeepMind's results repository; and Boris Alexeev's
repository copy, whose header declares itself a formalization of this result
with informal author a DeepMind prover agent and formal authors the agent and
George Tsoukalas. The results repository describes itself as the Lean proofs
generated by AlphaProof Nexus for the paper of that name, with the
`ErdosProblems/` folder serving its section on Erdős problems; it was created
on 2026-05-13, and the one commit touching the file, "Results for AlphaProof
Nexus", was authored on 2026-05-13 and committed on 2026-05-19 from the GitHub
account `aferr`. The file carries a Google LLC copyright header and
`EVOLVE-BLOCK` markers around its lemmas and its proof, names no person,
imports the formal-conjectures utilities, contains no `sorry`, and runs to
22,480 bytes and 370 lines. No comment or commentary on the site refers to it.
None of the three has been built or audited in this corpus, so the page lists
no `formalized` evidence; the Lean qualification in the site's label refers to
the first and third. Whether $A+B$ has positive upper density is a separate
question that the formal-conjectures statement file, at its
[commit of 2026-09-18](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/6fbb54f24ccc2e64dcfaffc28c58950e377110d2/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/125.lean),
keeps open.
