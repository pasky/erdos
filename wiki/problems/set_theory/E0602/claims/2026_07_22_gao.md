***

**Claim.** Lezhe Gao's manuscript *Peeling Hypergraphs: A Structural
Sufficient Condition for Property B*, a Zenodo deposit of 22 July 2026 filed
on the proof-claims tab of [[problems/set_theory/E0602/_index|Problem 602]] on
1 October 2026, calls a finite hypergraph peeling if its edges can be ordered
so that each edge brings at least two vertices not in any earlier edge, or one
such vertex together with a vertex of an earlier edge. It proves that every
peeling hypergraph has Property B and characterizes peeling hypergraphs by
acyclic assignments of vertices to edges; for graphs the peeling ones are
exactly the forests.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Lezhe Gao
(account lezhe) on 1 October 2026, giving "DeepSeek & GPT-5.5" as the AI used:

> This paper introduces peeling hypergraphs, a structural sufficient condition
> for Property B. A hypergraph is peeling if its edges can be ordered so that
> each edge introduces at least two new vertices, or one new vertex while also
> touching earlier ones. The main theorem gives an equivalent characterization
> via acyclic vertex-to-edge assignments. Peeling is shown to generalize
> forests for graphs, with cycles as minimal obstructions. The paper does not
> resolve Erdős Problem #602, as it focuses on finite hypergraphs; it is a
> partial result offering a new combinatorial criterion for Property B. Notes:
> This paper exclusively studies finite hypergraphs. It does not address the
> infinite almost-disjoint family setting of Erdős Problem #602. No compactness
> argument, transfinite construction, or reduction from the infinite case to
> the finite case is provided. Therefore, this submission is not a proof of,
> nor a genuine partial solution to, the original infinite problem. It is
> submitted solely as a related finite structural criterion for Property B, in
> case it stimulates further work on the infinite version.

**Why it is rejected.** It does not address the Statement.
[[problems/set_theory/E0602/_index|Problem 602]] asks whether every family of
countably infinite sets, any two meeting in a finite set of size other than
$1$, has a $2$-coloring of its union with no set monochromatic. The claim
addresses instead a criterion for Property B of finite hypergraphs, and, as
its author says, gives no compactness argument, transfinite construction or
reduction from the infinite case, so its mathematics settles no instance of
this problem. The value `proved` is its answer to that criterion.

**Standing.** Rejected, as not addressing the Statement. The entry carries no
comments and no review, and this corpus has not checked the manuscript.
