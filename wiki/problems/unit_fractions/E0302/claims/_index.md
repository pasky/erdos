# problems/unit_fractions/E0302/claims

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0302/_index|..]]

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0302/claims/2025_03_25_cambie|2025_03_25_cambie]]: Cambie's construction, recorded in the site's commentary, of a set of density
$5/8$ with no relation $1/a=1/b+1/c$ among distinct elements. It shows
$f(N)\ge(5/8+o(1))N$, so $f(N)$ is not $(1/2+o(1))N$.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0302/claims/2025_08_11_van_doorn|2025_08_11_van_doorn]]: Van Doorn's note proves that every subset of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ of size at least
$9n/10+\log(n)^3+1$ contains distinct $x,y,z$ with $1/x+1/y=1/z$. So
$f(N)\le(9/10+o(1))N$ for Problem 302.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0302/claims/2026_07_20_schuh|2026_07_20_schuh]]: A partial proof claim of 20 July 2026 that $f(N)\le(373/420+o(1))N$ for
Problem 302, filed by the account 15Redstones and credited to Robert Schuh
with two AI systems. The argument is an unsigned text on a paste site.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0302/claims/2026_08_16_khanukov|2026_08_16_khanukov]]: An AI-assisted partial proof claim by Dmitry Khanukov for Problem 302, first
released on 16 August 2026 and filed on the site on 13 September 2026. It
states $f(N)\ge(5/8+\delta)N$ for large $N$ and
$\limsup f(N)/N\le140803024/163562355$.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0302/claims/2026_09_25_kitamura|2026_09_25_kitamura]]: An AI-assisted Lean 4 development of 25 September 2026 by Kenta Kitamura
declares $\limsup f(N)/N\le0.8461739827964010$ for Problem 302. The route is a
priority recurrence over smooth blocks with kernel-checked certificates.
