**Claim.** Let $a_n>0$ with $a_n\to0$ and $\liminf_{n\to\infty}a_{n+1}/a_n>0$;
the sequence need not be monotone and its ratios need not converge. Theorem 1.1
(p. 1) of Kan Jiang and Lifeng Xi, *The Erdős similarity problem for null
sequences with adjacent ratios bounded below*, arXiv:2610.11449v1, posted
2026-10-08 (10 pages, the only version), states that for every $\eta\in(0,1)$
there is a compact set $E\subseteq[0,1]$ of Lebesgue measure greater than
$1-\eta$ such that for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$ and every
$s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}$ some $n\ge1$ has $x+sa_n\notin E$. So $E$
contains no set $aA+b$ with $a\ne0$ for $A=\{a_n:n\ge1\}$, of either sign of
$a$, which answers the question of
[[problems/analysis/E0120/_index|Problem 120]] yes for that $A$. The proof first
passes to a subsequence $b_j=a_{n_j}$ with $\alpha\le b_{j+1}/b_j\le1/2$ for a
constant $\alpha>0$ (Lemma 2.1, p. 3, which uses no monotonicity). It then
proves a periodic hitting statement (Proposition 2.2, p. 3): for every
$\tau\in(0,1)$ an open $1$-periodic set of density at most $6\tau$ meets
$x+tb_n$ for every real $x$ and every $t\in[1,2]$. Removing from $[0,1]$ the
dilations $\pm2^kH_k$, $k\in\mathbb{Z}$, of the sets $H_k$ that the proposition
gives for $\tau_k=\eta4^{-|k|}/64$, each of density at most $6\tau_k$, gives the
theorem (pp. 3--4). The proposition is proved in Sections 3–5 by the finite-tree
construction of the OpenAI release's dyadic and geometric manuscripts, with
grids of mesh comparable to $b_j$, index windows on the edges of a complete
ordered tree, independent random selector and terminal tables, and an
open-neighborhood repair of the exceptional centers. The lower ratio bound
limits the number of representative dilations to an exponential in the window
length (estimate (5.1)), and the upper bound separates the grids. Corollary 6.1
(p. 9) adds a single compact set of measure greater than $1-\eta$ for any
countable family of such sequences at once, with infinitely many distinct points
of every affine copy of each outside it. Section 6 (pp. 9–10) notes that the
estimate (5.1) fails for $b_n=2^{-2^n}$, a limitation of the estimate and not a
claim of universality, and that the whole conjecture would follow from the case
of positive null sequences with $a_{n+1}/a_n\to0$. Read depth: the whole of v1;
the proof was read through but not checked step by step.

**Covers.** The question, answered yes for every set $A=\{a_n:n\ge1\}$ with
$a_n>0$, $a_n\to0$ and $\liminf a_{n+1}/a_n>0$. It follows for every set that
contains an affine copy of such a sequence, since the same $E$ avoids it; the
paper's closing remark uses the same observation. Equivalently, the theorem
reaches every set $A$ with an accumulation point $p$ approached from one side
by points of $A$ whose distances to $p$ shrink by at most a fixed factor at
each step. The geometric progressions $\{q^n:n\ge1\}$, $0<q<1$, are the case
of constant ratio $q$, with the same form of avoiding set: the release's
[[problems/analysis/E0120/claims/2026_10_05_openai|geometric-progression claim]]
and its instance $q=1/2$, the
[[problems/analysis/E0120/claims/2026_09_25_openai|accepted dyadic case]], are
special cases. So are the decreasing sequences with $a_{n+1}/a_n\to1$ of Eigen
and Falconer. Corollary 6.1 also gives one avoiding set for countably many
ratios $q$ at once, which the release's geometric manuscript does not assert.
Not covered: the bounded infinite sets at none of whose accumulation points
such a sequence exists. Among positive null sequences these are exactly the
sequences whose terms, listed in decreasing order, have
$\liminf a_{n+1}/a_n=0$, such as $2^{-2^n}$ and $1/n!$; by the paper's closing
remark the whole conjecture reduces to these. Uncountable sets of that kind
include the Cantor sets whose dissection ratios tend to $0$. The general
question is not addressed.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki. The paper credits the finite-tree
construction and the geometric case to the two release manuscripts, which it
cites as unpublished, and writes the adapted proof out in full (p. 2), so the
theorem does not rest on either release claim.
