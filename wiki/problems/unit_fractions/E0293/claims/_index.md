# problems/unit_fractions/E0293/claims

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0293/_index|..]]

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0293/claims/2025_12_26_van_doorn_tang|2025_12_26_van_doorn_tang]]: Van Doorn and Tang's Theorem 1.1 (Math. Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc. 2026)
gives $v(k)\ge e^{ck^2}$ for an absolute constant $c$ and every $k\ge1$. The
upper bound $v(k)\le c_0^{(2/5+o(1))2^k}$ comes from the Elsholtz-Planitzer
count.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0293/claims/2026_09_16_van_doorn|2026_09_16_van_doorn]]: A claimed, AI-generated note by van Doorn and GPT-6 Astra Pro, with an
author-side Lean file, states that for large $k$ every integer between $2$ and
$\exp\exp\sqrt{k/(2c_0)}$, where $c_0=14/\log2$, occurs as a denominator in
some $k$-term decomposition of $1$. So $v(k)$ exceeds that bound.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0293/claims/2026_09_25_openai|2026_09_25_openai]]: Corollary 1.3 of the OpenAI mathematics release of 25 September 2026 states
$e^{e^{k/600}}\le v(k)\le1+k^{2^{k-1}}$ for large $k$, with the liminf and
limsup of $\log\log v(k)/k$ between $\log2/257$ and $\log2$. It is accepted as
a partial answer.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0293/claims/2026_10_10_van_doorn|2026_10_10_van_doorn]]: Wouter van Doorn's forum post of 10 October 2026 claims that his write-up on
Problem 304, with a greedy start that never takes $1/b$, gives
$v(k)>e^{e^{ck}}$ for some $c>0$. The write-up does not carry out this step,
so the claim is a sketch; it is partial and pending.
