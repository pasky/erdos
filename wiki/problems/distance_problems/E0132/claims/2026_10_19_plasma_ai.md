**Claim.** Let $A\subset\mathbb R^2$ be a set of $n\ge2$ points. A class
is the set of unordered pairs of $A$ at one occurring distance, and $R(A)$
is the number of classes with at most $n$ pairs, that is, the number of
distances which occur at least once but between at most $n$ pairs. For a
nonzero real traceless symmetric $2\times2$ matrix $S$, a class is
$S$-split if it contains two pairs with difference vectors $d,e$ such that
$d^{\mathsf T}Sd\ne e^{\mathsf T}Se$. Let $\kappa(A)$ be the least number of
$S$-split classes over all such $S$, and $M(A)$ the largest number of points
of $A$ on one circle of positive radius. Then

$$
R(A)\ge\frac{n-1-2\kappa(A)\bigl(M(A)+2\bigr)}{4}.
$$

**Covers.** Both questions of the modified Statement of
[[problems/distance_problems/E0132/_index|Problem 132]], for the sets on
which some quadratic form splits few classes. Whenever the right side
exceeds $1$, the set has two such distances, and along any sequence of sets
on which $n-2\kappa(A)(M(A)+2)$ tends to infinity, so does $R(A)$. In the
case $\kappa(A)=0$, where some nonsimilarity affine map preserves every
distance equality, the bound is $(n-1)/4$, so every such set with $n\ge6$
has two. The bound gives nothing when every form splits many classes, as
for sets invariant under a rotation of order three, where every class is
split by every form. Both questions remain open in general.
