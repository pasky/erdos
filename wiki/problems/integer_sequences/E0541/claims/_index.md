# problems/integer_sequences/E0541/claims

[[problems/integer_sequences/E0541/_index|..]]

[[problems/integer_sequences/E0541/claims/1974_02_14_erdos_szemeredi|1974_02_14_erdos_szemeredi]]: Erdős and Szemerédi (Publ. Math. Debrecen, 1976) prove Graham's conjecture for
$p$ nonzero residues modulo every sufficiently large prime $p$, a refereed
result credited by erdosproblems.com. It is partial: it leaves out small
primes and the residue $0$.

[[problems/integer_sequences/E0541/claims/2009_02_27_gao_hamidoune_wang|2009_02_27_gao_hamidoune_wang]]: Theorem 1.1 of Gao, Hamidoune and Wang (J. Number Theory, 2010) shows that $n$
integers in $[0,n-1]$ taking at least three values have two nonempty zero-sum
subsequences modulo $n$ of distinct lengths. It is refereed and credited by
erdosproblems.com.

[[problems/integer_sequences/E0541/claims/2009_03_18_grynkiewicz|2009_03_18_grynkiewicz]]: Theorem 3.4 of Grynkiewicz (European J. Combin., 2011) shows that a sequence
of $n$ elements of a finite abelian group of order $n$ whose nonempty zero-sum
subsequences all have one length has at most two distinct terms. The result is
refereed.

[[problems/integer_sequences/E0541/claims/2025_12_31_alexeev|2025_12_31_alexeev]]: Boris Alexeev produced a Lean 4 proof of the prime-modulus statement with
Aristotle and ChatGPT and posted it in the erdosproblems.com thread on 31
December 2025. This corpus built and audited a later revision, so the claim is
accepted.
