**Claim.** Let $d\ge2$ and $M\ge2$ be integers with
$\gcd(j^d-1:2\le j\le M)=1$. Then every integer
$k\ge1+(2^d-2)(M^d-1)$ is the number of cubes in a dissection of the unit
$d$-cube into homothetic cubes of positive side length. For every odd
$d\ge3$ the gcd condition holds with $M=\lfloor\sqrt{3d}\rfloor+1$, so

$$
c(d)\le1+(2^d-2)\bigl((\lfloor\sqrt{3d}\rfloor+1)^d-1\bigr)
\qquad(d\ge3\ \text{odd}),
$$

and $c(d)/d^d\to0$ as $d\to\infty$ through odd integers. Hence no constant
$C>0$ gives $c(n)\ge Cn^n$ for all large $n$: the answer to whether
$c(n)\gg n^n$ is no.

**Covers.** The definite part `n_to_n` of
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0769/_index|Problem 769]], answered in the
negative. The gcd condition is proved only for odd $d$, so no bound is
given in even dimensions, including those with $d+1$ prime. The claim does
not determine the order of growth of $c(n)$ or whether
$\log c(n)/(n\log n)$ has a limit; the open-ended request for good bounds
is not a part. The earlier pending claims of
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0769/claims/2026_07_14_snyder|Star Fleet Math]],
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0769/claims/2026_07_24_zeng|Zeng]] and
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0769/claims/2026_08_05_korsky|Korsky]]
answer the same part the same way; none of them is a premise here.
