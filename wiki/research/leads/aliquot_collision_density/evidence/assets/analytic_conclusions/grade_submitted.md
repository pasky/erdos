$$
\sum_{\substack{k\le y\\k\text{ Z-rough}}}

Higher powers of q are counted once in both sides. The accumulated error is
$O_Z(\log\log y)$, since
$\sum_{q\le y}1/(q-1)\le\log(1/m_y)+1$.

### B and the two arithmetic interfaces

The most serious union attack is that each sparse piece can contain values
far above its lower cutoff, creating unexpectedly dense intermediate intervals.
The report answers exactly that risk. Failure of tightness supplies arbitrarily
large witnesses at every integer threshold k. For a fixed L,

$$
\#\{n\le x:s(n)<x/L\}
\le (L^{-1/2}+m_{\sqrt L})x+O_L(1),
$$

because any remaining n has $n>x/\sqrt L$ and no prime divisor at most
$\sqrt L$. Choose L to make the coefficient at most $\eta/4$, and then
choose witnesses $x_k\ge k^2Lx_{k-1}$ absorbing the fixed error. The sets
$A_k=\{m\ge x_k/L:r_{x_k}(m)>k\}$ retain capture at least
$\eta x_k/2$, while $|A_k|<x_k/k$.

For every sufficiently large y, take the last k with $x_k/L\le y$. Later
sets contribute nothing; counting every element of every earlier set is an
upper bound even when its values are enormous. Therefore

$$
\frac{|\bigcup_jA_j\cap[1,y]|}{y}
\le\frac Lk+\frac{Lk x_{k-1}}{x_k}
\le\frac{L+1}{k}\longrightarrow0.
$$

This establishes natural density zero at all large y. It does not assume
bounded values, disjoint pieces, or a rate of tightness. The witness construction
is valid at each stage because arbitrarily large witnesses remain available;
integer parts handle real witness points. Its image target has positive values,
so the convention at zero is harmless.

The sufficiency direction, (3) implies (1), needs an upper-output interface.
Finite divisor summation gives

$$
\sum_{n\le x}\frac{\sigma(n)}n
=\sum_{d\le x}\frac1d\left\lfloor\frac xd\right\rfloor<2x.
$$

Inputs with $s(n)>Lx$ thus number at most $2x/L$. With epsilon and L fixed,
the surviving capture from a density-zero target below Lx is
$\sqrt{Kx\,|A\cap[0,Lx]|}=o(x)$. Taking the upper density and only then
letting epsilon decrease and L increase proves the needed implication. Arbitrary
x-dependent exclusions are allowed.

The two model attacks have real force. For $g(n)=n^2$ the fibers have size
one but the sparse squares capture all inputs; the upper-output interface
fails. For $f(n)=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor$, a density-zero target captures at
most $(2y+1)|A\cap[1,y]|=o(x)$ with $y=\lfloor\sqrt x\rfloor$, whereas
all but $O_T(1)+O(\sqrt x)$ inputs lie in fibers exceeding any fixed T; the
lower-output interface fails. These are valid counterexamples to the respective
unsupported general-map transfers, not to s. The actual arithmetic argument
establishes both interfaces independently.

The finite-fiber arrows are also complete. Strict slack in the limiting
statement gives eventual heavy-input mass below epsilon. Removing those fibers
bounds every retained multiplicity by T. Conversely a heavy-value set has
cardinality less than $x/T$, exactly the target-size uniformity needed in (4).
The review's additional direct check of (3) implies (2) is sound:
$F_x(T)\le\varepsilon+\sqrt{K/T}$, using surviving counts and the heavy-value
cardinality bound. It requires no hidden control of output sizes. All four
conditions, their quantifiers and the corrected-route consequence are covered.
