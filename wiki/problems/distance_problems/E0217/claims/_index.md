# problems/distance_problems/E0217/claims

[[problems/distance_problems/E0217/_index|..]]

[[problems/distance_problems/E0217/claims/1983_01_01_pomerance|1983_01_01_pomerance]]: Erdős's 1983 lecture credits to Pomerance a five-point configuration: a unit
equilateral triangle, its circumcenter and a point on the unit circle about
one vertex. Its distances have multiplicities 4, 3, 2 and 1.

[[problems/distance_problems/E0217/claims/1987_01_01_palasti|1987_01_01_palasti]]: Palásti (Studia Sci. Math. Hungar. 1987) gives seven points in the plane in
general position that determine six distinct distances, the $i$-th occurring
$i$ times. This answers the instance $n=7$ yes.

[[problems/distance_problems/E0217/claims/1989_01_01_palasti|1989_01_01_palasti]]: Palásti (Discrete Math. 1989) gives six points with no three on a line, no
four on a circle, no point equidistant from three others and no equilateral
triangle, whose five distances occur 1 to 5 times. This answers the instance
$n=6$ yes.

[[problems/distance_problems/E0217/claims/1989_09_01_palasti|1989_09_01_palasti]]: Palásti (Period. Math. Hungar. 1989) gives examples on the triangular lattice
for every $n$ from 3 to 8, of which the eight-point example is new. This
answers the instance $n=8$ yes.
