**Claim.**
[[../library/ramsey_theory/yuan_2021_anti_ramsey_numbers_paths/theorem_1|Theorem 1]]
of Yuan, *Anti-Ramsey numbers for paths* (arXiv:2102.00807v3, p. 1; the
title as the arXiv record gives it, where the PDF prints the singular *The
anti-Ramsey number for paths*), states that for $n\ge k\ge5$ and
$\ell=\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor$,

$$
\mathrm{AR}(n,P_k)=\max\Bigl\{\binom{k-2}{2}+1,\
\binom{\ell-1}{2}+(\ell-1)(n-\ell+1)+\epsilon\Bigr\},
$$

with $\epsilon=1$ for odd $k$ and $\epsilon=2$ for even $k$, where
$\mathrm{AR}(n,G)$ is the largest number of colors in an edge-coloring of
$K_n$ with no rainbow copy of $G$. This is the path question of
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E1105/_index|Problem 1105]] answered yes, term for
term; in the 1975 parameters $k=2t+3+\epsilon_0$ it is Conjecture 2 of
Erdős, Simonovits and Sós, whose announced proofs never appeared. The proof
reduces to connected Turán numbers for paths and uses stability theorems of
Füredi, Kostochka, Luo and Verstraëte, stated in Section 2 as Corollary 5
(odd $k\ge9$) and Corollary 6 (even $k\ge6$), which hold for every number
of vertices, so the range is the full $n\ge k\ge5$. The preprint qualifies
these inputs itself: the Remark after Corollary 6 (p. 3) says that case (d)
of Corollary 6, the equality case $e(G)=h(n,k-1,\ell-1)$, is not proved in
the two cited papers, that "We can prove this with a little more effort",
and that it follows easily from the stability results of Ma and Yuan
(arXiv:2010.13667); footnote 1 (p. 2) asserts, without proof, that the
cited Theorem 2.3 for $2$-connected graphs without long cycles extends to
connected $P_k$-free graphs. The even-$k$ case therefore rests in part on
stability inputs the preprint asserts rather than proves in the sources it
cites, a gap the author declares and the acceptance below does not
address.

**Covers.** The path half: the exact formula for all $n\ge k\ge5$. The
refereed
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E1105/claims/1984_03_01_simonovits_sos|Simonovits and Sós 1984]]
covers paths on at least $13$ vertices for $n>ct^2$ before it, and the
cycle half is the accepted
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E1105/claims/2005_09_01_montellano_ballesteros_neumann_lara|Montellano-Ballesteros and Neumann-Lara 2005]].

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the proof's inputs are the cited
Turán and stability theorems, not held here.

**Acceptance.** Reviewed: the site's curator, T. F. Bloom, labeled the problem
PROVED. In the commentary, the curator says that Yuan [Yu21] has announced a
proof of the path formula for all $n\ge k\ge5$ (page last edited 29 January
2026, accessed 2026-09-18). The curator is independent of the author. For the
path half, the label can rest on no other source, since Simonovits and Sós cover
only $n>ct^2$. The community database lists the problem as proved as of its last
update, 1 February 2026. The thread (two comments of 19 January 2026) concerns a
literature identification, and the proof-claim tab is empty. Not refereed: the
paper is an arXiv preprint. Its v1 is dated 1 February 2021, the date the page
is named by. Its latest version, v3, is dated 9 February 2021. Its listing has
no journal reference, it has no Crossref record, and its seven citing records
include no published version (2026-09-18).

**Formalization.** Two outside Lean developments, linked above, prove the
path formula for all $n\ge k\ge5$, the statement of Theorem 1. Linmiao Xu's
development, registered in the Palomar registry as PALOMAR-2026-10-13-000006
on 13 October 2026 and pinned at its registered commit of 11 October 2026,
proves the formal-conjectures declaration `erdos_1105.parts.ii`. Its record
names this preprint as an adapted source, and its route runs through
representative-graph exchanges, a finite host-window reduction, degree-core
elimination and 2-connected circumference bounds. The registry ran the proof
through two kernels and records a model review by GPT-6 Sol through Codex
with a neutral outcome, which is not an independent review.
`Erdos1105.lean` of Boris Alexeev's repository plby/lean-proofs, a file whose
proofs were posted on 23 August 2026, pinned at the commit of 15 September
2026, proves the same formula over its own definition of $\mathrm{AR}(n,G)$.
Its notes of 23 August 2026 say that the path argument follows the
representative-graph approach of Theorem 1 and that the connected-path
extremal and stability inputs are proved, not assumed; the repository's
source list credits the informal argument to GPT-5.6 Sol and the formal
proof to Codex and GPT-5.6 Sol. Both report only the axioms `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. If they build as reported and their
statements are faithful, the formula does not rest on the stability inputs
that the preprint asserts rather than proves. Both prove the path half that
this page settles, so they are links here and not claims of their own. This
corpus has not built either or audited its statement, so no `formalized`
evidence is listed.

**Read depth.** Theorem 1, the definitions and the quoted Turán inputs
