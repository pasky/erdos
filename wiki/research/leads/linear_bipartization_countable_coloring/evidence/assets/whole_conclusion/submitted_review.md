## Restatement

### Target 1: arbitrary-cardinality coloring

For every finite real C>=0 and every set-sized undirected simple graph G,
assume

$$
\forall F\subseteq G\text{ finite},\qquad
\beta(F)\le C|V(F)|,
$$

where beta(F) is the minimum number of **edges** whose removal makes F
bipartite. Then there is a proper coloring of G by a countable palette:
chi(G)<=aleph_0. Neither |V(G)| nor chi(G) is bounded in the hypothesis.
The assertion includes all singular vertex cardinalities. It asserts
no finite upper bound on chi(G).

The proof is examined in ZFC. Arbitrary sums of nonnegative terms mean
suprema of finite subsums. The intermediate trace bound applies only to
vertices with infinitely many neighbors in their preceding model;
finite traces are dealt with separately in the coloring argument.

### Target 2: the full E111 implication

For every simple graph G with chi(G)=aleph_1, of any vertex cardinality,
let h_G(n) be the largest beta(F) of an n-vertex subgraph. Then

$$
\forall K>0\ \exists N\in\mathbb N\ \forall n\ge N,
\qquad h_G(n)>Kn.
$$

This is the full limit h_G(n)/n to infinity. The threshold N may depend
on G and K. A subsequence or mere unboundedness would not suffice.
The least profile exists because its possible values for fixed n form
a nonempty finite set of integers. The supplied problem wording permits
a deletion bound; any such bound is at least the least profile, so this
conclusion also applies to that reading.

The additional consumed reduction is checked: failure of the limit
would give a countable S and a finite K>0 such that G-S is 2K-linear
and still has chromatic number aleph_1. Conversely, a C-linear graph of
chromatic number aleph_1 would violate the limit. This establishes the
asserted equivalence for the restricted CC lemma with chi(G)<=aleph_1;
no converse equivalence is claimed for Target 1 with unrestricted
chromatic number.

## Weakest steps and independent derivations

### Target 1

**1. A successor must preserve a fixed entire old law.** This is the
pivotal assertion, Lemma S. For a finite collection of future patterns,
fix all old and infinite-trace-root signs through one joint Radon law
Lambda. The finite-trace roots remain free. Finite convex separation
reduces feasibility to every weighted expected minimum over those free
signs. A finite minimizing choice attains that minimum while retaining
Lambda; no conditional-distribution theorem is needed.

For integer weights a_i, replicate k a_i copies inside the next model
but outside the old model. Add L neighbors from the old trace of each
infinite-trace root. These stars introduce old roots but no new vertices
as L grows. The new-vertex budget is therefore

$$
Cq+Ck\sum_i a_i|S_i|,\qquad q=|R_X|+|R_Y|.
$$

For fixed k, the total copied-edge cost is bounded by
W=k sum_i a_i |E_(P_i)|. Almost surely, each of the finitely many trace
minority counts b_y^L stays bounded. Once L-2b_y^L>W for every such y,
any minimizing coloring must use the prescribed opposite trace signs.
Thus the limiting pattern cost is at least k times the disputed free-root
minimum. Nonnegative convergence, division by k, and then k to infinity
give the required weighted bound with no q term. Real weights follow by
continuity. Closed finite-coordinate inequalities and compactness then
give all future inequalities simultaneously.

The independent exact-cost expression and the reversed separation attack
are in derivations Section 7. They verify the order of limits, root and
copy disjointness, budget accounting, and retention of the same marginal.
No essential inference in this step remained unsupported.

**2. Uncountable traces and arbitrary products must not create a false
simultaneous event.** For a trace T, every finite subset has expected
minority count at most C. Among countable subsets, a sequence approaching
the supremum has a countable union attaining it. Include an infinite
countable subset to ensure a unique eventual sign almost surely. Adding
any individual u outside that union cannot increase the maximal expected
minority count, so its probability of disagreeing with the derived sign
is zero. Summing over the countable subset gives at most C; all remaining
summands are zero, in the finite-subsums sense.

Consistent finite laws determine a Radon law by positive extension on
continuous cylinder functions and Riesz representation. Countable
marginals preserve each derived-sign identity. Compactness is applied
to a fixed full marginal, which is closed, rather than to the graph of
an arbitrary Borel function. At a model limit, a pattern's finitely many
roots already occur at one earlier stage, so its J inequality persists.
The exact hypotheses and null-set distinctions are checked in derivations
Sections 4, 6 and 8. No separability or common conull event over all
vertices is assumed.

**3. The measure must yield an actual countable coloring at every
cardinality.** The Hilbert partition assigns a vector a rational
threshold gap and one coordinate sign, not a basis index or support
set. Equal labels have energy at least a positive eta depending on
the label. Consequently a vertex has only finitely many same-label
neighbors in earlier layers: directly for a finite trace, and by the
energy bound for an infinite trace. Greedy coloring applies to the
edges between layers. A separate countable color inside each layer
handles its remaining edges; the product of three countable labels is
countable.

Every layer has cardinality strictly below kappa. A continuous chain
of length cf(kappa) preserves this inequality at every proper limit,
including singular kappa. A minimal-cardinality counterexample would
therefore have countably colorable layers and would be colored by the
construction, a contradiction. This rederives the induction without
assuming its conclusion at the same cardinal. Derivations Sections 2,
3 and 9 provide the complete checks.

### Target 2

**1. The coloring theorem is used on every countable-deletion remainder.**
Removing a countable S from a graph of chromatic number aleph_1 preserves
that chromatic number. Otherwise a countable coloring of the remainder
and disjoint fresh colors for S would color the original graph. Target 1
therefore produces a finite graph with beta(F)>2K|F| in each such
remainder. Recursion through omega_1 gives disjoint witnesses; every
proper stage has removed only countably many vertices. This uses no
assumption that the whole graph has size aleph_1.

**2. Variable finite witnesses must yield one repeatable size.** There
are countably many finite isomorphism types. An uncountable family of
disjoint finite witnesses contains infinitely many copies of one type,
with fixed v vertices and b>2Kv deletions required. This avoids assuming
any uniform bound on the sizes originally obtained from Target 1.

**3. The lower bound must hold at every sufficiently large integer.**
For every n>=2v, use floor(n/v) disjoint copies and pad to exactly n
vertices. Each copy requires its own b deleted edges, even in the
presence of cross edges. Therefore

$$
h_G(n)\ge b\left\lfloor\frac nv\right\rfloor
 \ge \frac bv(n-v)>2K(n-v)\ge Kn.
$$

A maximal disjoint family gives the converse remainder statement: if
h_G(n)<=Kn infinitely often, that family must be countable, and its
vertex union meets every finite obstruction to 2K-linearity. These
arguments are fully retained in derivations Section 10. The claimed
limit and the supplied formulation agree.
