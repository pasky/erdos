## Core construction: adjudication of the reasoning

Let $\omega(n)$ count distinct prime factors on positive integers, with
$\omega(1)=0$. The report's restatement uses

$$
R_F(x)=\frac{\#\{n\ge1:x<n+\omega(n)<x+F(x)\}}{F(x)}.
$$

It faithfully preserves the existence of one positive $F$ on $[1,\infty)$,
$F(x)\to\infty$ at every real location, and
$F(n)/\omega(n)\to0$ along one fixed set of natural density one, together
with bounded $R_F$ at all sufficiently large real locations. The numerator
counts inputs, including collisions of shifted values. The smallness limit
is an integer assertion; the width limit and count bound are real assertions.
These are not interchanged.

### External premises and foundations

The consumed source is Erdős, Pomerance and Sárközy, *On locally repeated
values of certain arithmetic functions, IV*, *The Ramanujan Journal* **1**
(1997), 227–241, Lemma 1 on printed pp. 229–230,
DOI 10.1023/A:1009723712317. The two supplied statement forms agree on every
consumed clause. For $X\ge2$, the specialization is $m=1$, $h=0$ and
$f=\omega$: the size condition holds, the residue class contains every
integer, and the condition on primes dividing $m$ is vacuous. Distinct prime
factors of coprime factors are disjoint, so $\omega$ is nonnegative and
additive. The maximum defining $K$ is nonempty and equals $1$;
$A(X)=\sum_{p\le X}1/p$. The absolute source constant is independent of
$X$. These checks give precisely the external interface used by the report:

$$
\sum_{1\le n\le X}(\omega(n)-A(X))^2
\le C X(A(X)+1),\qquad X\ge2.
$$

The report neither assumes a normal-order theorem in place of this estimate
nor promotes the source's reading-depth label into an independent proof.
The supplied record says Claims checked, with printed statement and remarks
read, and the proof on pp. 230–232 read without a step-by-step check. The
reviewer's and grader's actual access is narrower: the two supplied statement
forms and that record. No print image or source proof has been independently
examined here.

The positive-$K$ omega instance is the expressly permitted external premise.
The strict-zero defect discussed below does not occur in this instance:
$K=1$ and $A(X)\ge1/2$. Accepting its use is ordinary reliance on the
identified source at the commissioned interface, not a proof of that instance
from the zero-function calculation or a certification of the unrestricted
source statement. No repair of the general statement is silently adopted.

Euler's divergence $A(X)\to\infty$ is the other expressly permitted

### Sparse inputs, crowded locations and endpoints

Use the report's notation $I_j=[2^j,2^{j+1})\cap\mathbb N_{>0}$,
$X_j=2^{j+2}$, $A_j=A(X_j)$ and $H_j=\lceil A_j^{3/4}\rceil$.
The potentially consequential ceiling and uniformity steps are sound:

$$
\frac{H_j}{A_j}\le A_j^{-1/4}+A_j^{-1}\to0,
\qquad
\frac{|D_j|}{2^j}
\le4C(A_j^{-1/2}+A_j^{-3/2})\to0.
$$

The second inequality follows because each bad input contributes more than
$H_j^2$ to the moment sum. Also $A_j\le1+(j+2)\log2$, hence
$H_j+1=o(2^j)$. One cutoff therefore enforces both $H_j\le A_j/2$ and
$2^{j+1}+H_j+1<X_j$ for every subsequent index. No effective numerical
cutoff, finite computation or exchange of limits is needed.

The strongest attempted core refutation attacks a real weakness of a naive
argument: few bad inputs can still create arbitrarily crowded windows.
Here they are not discarded. For one integer image $m=n+\omega(n)$,

$$
u<m<u+H_j+1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m-H_j\le u\le m-1.
$$

There are $H_j$ integer endpoints before restricting to $I_j$. The report's
looser $H_j+1$ bound is valid even when many inputs share $m$. Thus, with
$E_j=\{u\in I_j:b_j(u)>H_j+1\}$,

$$
(H_j+2)|E_j|\le\sum_{u\in I_j}b_j(u)
\le(H_j+1)|D_j|.
$$

This proves sparsity of the locations that must receive a different width.
It does not prove those locations infinite, which the core does not need.

For $u\notin E_j$, nonnegative $\omega$ forces every input counted in the
integer window below $X_j$. For good inputs, the two endpoints and
$|\omega(n)-A_j|\le H_j$ imply

$$
u-A_j-H_j<n<u+2H_j+1-A_j.
$$

That interval has length $3H_j+1$ and at most the claimed $3H_j+2$ integer
inputs. The bad inputs contribute at most $H_j+1$. These classes exhaust
the count; no monotonicity or injectivity of the shifted map was assumed.

At $u=\lfloor x\rfloor$ off $E_j$, the whole real window is contained in
$(u,u+H_j+1)$, giving $R_F(x)\le4+3/H_j\le7$. This inclusion remains
valid at $x=u$, immediately below $u+1$, and at a dyadic boundary assigned
to its new cell. At an exceptional cell, or an early cell assigned the same
large-width branch, $F(x)=2(u+1)$ and

$$
1\le n<x+F(x)<3(u+1),
\qquad
R_F(x)\le\frac{3(u+1)-1}{2(u+1)}<\frac32.
$$

Inputs beyond $X_j$ can occur in this larger window, but the latter estimate
does not use $D_j$ or the source moment range. This disposes of the attempted
range counterexample without losing exceptional locations. It also proves
the report's global bound for the stated initial choices, not merely its
required eventual bound.

### Density, limits and the target implication

A density attack can concentrate every exceptional point at the beginning of
its block. The report still controls a cutoff $2^k\le N<2^{k+1}$: if
$|T_j|\le\varepsilon2^j$ for $j\ge J$, then

$$
\left|\bigcup_jT_j\cap[1,N]\right|
\le C_J+\varepsilon\sum_{j=J}^k2^j
\le C_J+2\varepsilon N.
$$

Here $T_j\subseteq I_j$, and $C_J$ covers the finite prefix. Divide by $N$
and let the arbitrary $\varepsilon$ tend to zero. Neither summable error
proportions nor distribution inside a partial block is assumed.

This argument is applied separately to $E_j$ and $D_j\cap I_j$. That second
application is essential: sparsity of crowded output locations alone does
not control $\omega$ at the integer argument of the width. On the fixed
complement $S$ of these two unions and the finite prefix,

$$
F(n)=H_j,\qquad \omega(n)\ge A_j/2>0,
\qquad \frac{F(n)}{\omega(n)}\le2H_j/A_j\to0.
$$

The bound is uniform within each good part of a block, so it gives ordinary
convergence along that one $S$, not just statistical averages or a different
exceptional set for each tolerance. The zero denominator at $n=1$ is excluded
in the finite prefix. At every real location in a late block, either width
branch is at least $\min(H_j,2^{j+1})$, which tends to infinity. The width
limit therefore does not discard $E$.

The definitions have no circular dependence: $H_j$ is fixed by $A_j$, then
$D_j$, $b_j$ and $E_j$ are finite objects, and only then is $F$ defined.
The large-window estimate is separate from the short-window rule defining
$E_j$. One and the same $F$ has all the required properties. Its bounded
normalized counts exclude a diverging sequence of real locations, and also
of integer locations. This is the negation of the universal-width assertion
for $f=\omega$ under the supplied interpretation; it does not classify any
other function.

## Ancillary findings

### The literal stronger-width exclusion

The report correctly separates two readings of the final remark. A statement
that the proof does not establish a stronger admissibility condition is a
scope limitation. A statement that this particular $F$ actually fails that
condition requires evidence about its exceptional set.

The report's diagnostic is valid. For $Y\ge e$ and $s=1+1/\log Y$,
$p^{s-1}\le e$ when $p\le Y$. The elementary inequality
$-\log(1-t)\ge t$ for $0<t<1$, expansion of a finite product of convergent
positive geometric series, unique factorization, and integral comparison give

$$
A(Y)\le e\sum_{p\le Y}p^{-s}
\le e\log\prod_{p\le Y}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}
\le e\log\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-s}
\le e\log(1+\log Y).
$$

There is no unproved infinite Euler product or imported prime asymptotic in
this chain. It yields $H_j=O((\log(j+2))^{3/4})$. Since
$\log\log n\ge\log(j\log2)$ on $I_j$, the quotient
$H_j/\log\log n$ tends to zero uniformly outside $E$. If $E$ is infinite,
its integer members have an unbounded subsequence on which
$F(n)/\log\log n=2(n+1)/\log\log n\to\infty$. Conversely, a finite $E$
leaves only the first, small-width branch in the tail. Consequently the
report's exact diagnostic equivalence holds:

$$
F(n)=o(\log\log n)\text{ through all integers}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad E\text{ is finite}.
$$

The frozen proof gives upper bounds on $|E_j|$, without establishing
$E_j\ne\varnothing$ for unboundedly many indices. The missing interface
for its literal exclusion is therefore precisely

$$
\forall J\ \exists j\ge J\ \exists u\in I_j:
 b_j(u)>H_j+1.
$$

The report does not settle that assertion. An empty exceptional set being
compatible with the displayed upper bounds is not a claim that the actual
omega construction has an empty set. Thus the report correctly records a
proof defect rather than a disproof. Its new diagnostic exposes the missing
lemma; it does not supply that lemma or silently repair the remark. No proof
of a stronger-width theorem is introduced by this grade.

The core construction works with finite or infinite $E$, so this unresolved
ancillary assertion is not an unproved premise of the core. The **pass**
accepts the report's scoped **proof defective; statement unresolved** finding
and its diagnostic, while leaving that literal assertion unproved.
