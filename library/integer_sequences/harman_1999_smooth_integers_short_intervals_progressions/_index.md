# Integers without large prime factors in short intervals and arithmetic progressions

[[integer_sequences/_index|..]]

***

Glyn Harman, *Integers without large prime factors in short intervals and
arithmetic progressions*, Acta Arithmetica 91, no. 3 (1999), 279–289. The
selected text is the eleven-page
[journal PDF](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa91/aa9137.pdf).

No explicit copyright notice or redistribution license was found in the selected
PDF, inspected 2026-10-15. Public access does not establish redistribution
rights. No file of this source is held.

## Scope

Theorem 1 treats intervals of length $x^{1/2}$. Theorem 2, printed p. 280,
considers $y=x^\gamma$ for $3/7<\gamma<1/2$. It defines $\theta(\gamma)$
by an infimum over known exponent pairs and, subject to
$\theta(\gamma)<\gamma$, obtains

$$
\Psi(x+y,u)-\Psi(x,u)\gg y
\qquad\text{for }u\geq x^{e^{-1/2}\theta(\gamma)+\epsilon},
$$

where $\epsilon>0$. Here $\Psi(x,u)$ counts positive integers at most $x$
whose prime factors are all at most $u$. The simpler choice
$\theta=(3-5\gamma)/2$ in display (1.4) is explicitly conditional on the
exponent-pair conjecture. The theorem does not state the unconditional result
throughout the displayed $\gamma$ range without the exponent-pair condition.
Theorem 3 treats arithmetic progressions with separate hypotheses.
