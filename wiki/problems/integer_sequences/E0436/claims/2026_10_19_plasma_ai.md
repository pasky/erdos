**Claim.** For $1$-bounded multiplicative functions put
$\mathbb D(f,g;x)^2=\sum_{p\le x}(1-\operatorname{Re}f(p)\overline{g(p)})/p$,
and write $\mathbb E^{\log}_{n\le X}$ for the logarithmic average over
$n\le X$. Assume the three-function case of the logarithmically averaged
Elliott conjecture, in the form of Conjecture 1.3(ii) of Tao and
Teräväinen's 2019 structure paper: if $g_1,g_2,g_3$ are $1$-bounded
multiplicative functions and one of them satisfies
$\inf_{|t|\le X}\mathbb D(g_j,\chi(n)n^{it};X)\to\infty$ as $X\to\infty$
for every Dirichlet character $\chi$, then
$\mathbb E^{\log}_{n\le X}g_1(n)g_2(n+1)g_3(n+2)\to0$. Then
$\Lambda(k,3)<\infty$ for every odd $k$. Under the same assumption, every
completely multiplicative $f:\mathbb N\to\mu_k$ with $k$ odd takes the value
$1$ at $n$, $n+1$ and $n+2$ for some $n$. The step from such functions to $\Lambda(k,3)$ is unconditional:
for odd $k$, $\Lambda(k,3)$ equals the supremum, over all completely
multiplicative $f:\mathbb N\to\mu_k$, of the least $n$ with
$f(n)=f(n+1)=f(n+2)=1$, taken as $\infty$ when there is none.

**Covers.** The second question of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0436/_index|Problem 436]], whether
$\Lambda(k,3)$ is finite for every odd $k$, answered yes conditionally on the
assumption above, which is an open conjecture. Unconditionally the claim
adds no case: $k=3$ is settled by the accepted
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0436/claims/1962_01_01_lehmer_lehmer_mills_selfridge|Lehmer, Lehmer, Mills and Selfridge claim]],
and every odd $k\ge5$ remains open. No bound on $\Lambda(k,3)$ is given, so
the growth question is not addressed, nor is the first question.

**Credit.** The assumption is Tao and Teräväinen's conjecture, recorded with
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/tao_2019_structure_correlations_multiplicative_functions_at_almost/_index|their 2019 structure paper]].
The proof uses the Chebotarev density theorem, the fixed-modulus
Vinogradov–Korobov bound for Dirichlet $L$-functions, the fixed-modulus prime
number theorem in arithmetic progressions with Mertens' estimates, and the
fundamental lemma of sieve theory, all at their published statements. The
realization of prescribed character values at primes, the identity for
$\Lambda(k,3)$, the sieve argument for functions at bounded pretentious
distance from a Dirichlet character, and the conditional deduction are
Plasma AI's.

**Standing.** Author-recorded at tier 0, with no independent review and no
executable evidence. The assumption is unproved, so the claim derives
nothing for the problem's standing. Catalog acceptance is pending.
